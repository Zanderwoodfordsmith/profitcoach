## Presenter script

### Offering Facilitators Guide – Module 07

Last Update 15.June.22

**Handouts Required:** Neats Worksheet

### Slide 1

The success of your business is dependent on the sales of your products.

In today’s crowded and competitive market, it’s hard to distinguish yourself from the marketing “noise” and your competition.

Therefore, how you “show” your product becomes a key factor in your ability to grab attention and sell your product.

### Slide 2

Think of your business as a value delivery system.

Everything you do should be focused on the value you deliver to your customers.

This then is the focus of your offerings

### Slide 3

An offering is usually static. It defines your long term inventory

An offer is dynamic, it can be temporary and short term

### Slide 4

Quite simply your offerings are the products you are marketing to your customers,

These will be any tangible or intangible good or service that is the result of a process and that is intended for delivery to a customer or end user

Your products can be goods, services or ideas (IP).

### Slide 5

Surprisingly, many business owners I talk to have difficulty in explaining what their products are or do.

It’s not that they don’t know of course, they are intimately familiar with them. It’s just that they haven’t thought through how to explain their value to their prospects.

If you don’t have a written list, it is vitally important that you create one.

List everything that you expect someone to pay you money for. Even if you only have one product, write it down.

Pay attention to the categories shown here and test for each product against each category.

If you have more that one response to each category then for our purposes you most likely have more than one virtual product, even if it is provided by one actual product.

For example if your product serves two customer segments (equals two target markets) then you will most likely have a different marketing strategy or different value proposition for each of the segments. i.e. you now have two virtual products.

The same applies to each category.

We will use this list to develop your offering strategy.

Work with your client to come up with a list of all their products.

Foe each product determine how many different target markets it can serve. Rach one is classified as a separate product.

### Slide 6

This involves taking your product list and providing more defining details of each product.

First make sure the product is clearly defined in name or title.

Then identify the list of features for each product. The features are usually the things you get excited about and want to shout about. Unfortunately they are also the things the prospective customer doesn’t care about until after the buying decision is made.

Review the list of features, think like a customer and ask of each one “so what?”

If the answer doesn’t proved a real benefit to a customer, then transcribe it into a benefit that does. Usually there are far fewer benefits than features because more than one feature can provide the same benefit.

Work with your client to come up with a list of benefits using the “neats” worksheet.

### Slide 7

The purpose of your offering(s) is to persuade someone to buy by giving them a reason to buy that overcomes all their reasons not to buy.

And to do that, an offering should be designed to help your prospective customer make a decision.

And to do that requires some careful creativity and an understanding of buying psychology.

### Slide 8

This is a list of the possible inclusions when creating your offering.

Product name may be a simple title or a branding name.

Product mix refers to collections of related products that are generally bought together.

Depth refers to any quality or style upgrades. (Apple watch straps).

Width describes you product range.

Support is what you provide after the sale in terms of warranty, repair, return, etc.

Pricing is you base price.

### Slide 9

Use some or all these approaches to help you create the best possible offerings.

Work with your client to initiate the words and phases to build an offereing. Set it as an action to complete for the next session.

### Slide 11

It can greatly improve your conversion rate if you provide some supporting information as proof of value,

This can be incorporated into your website, your product packaging, marketing material, elevator pitch, live interviews. The list goes on.

Ask your client to share what they currently have and/or what they think is possible.

### Slide 12

A basic offer is just the standard price you are asking for your product as defined in the offering.

However you may wish to create special offers to boost sales and revenue.

There are several ways to consider an offer.

- Simple price reduction or discount.
- Bundling products together with a package price advantage
- Quantity price reduction
- Adding bonus features
- Added to higher cost purchases
- Referral rewards
- Payment terms
- Offers can be time limited with stated end time
Work with your client to create at least one offer.
