### Slide 2

We need to look at who you are creating value for and who are your most important customers

A simple and popular way to analyse your customers is the so called ABCD segmentation and it look like this …

Your **A** customers will produce good profits with not much demand on your time.

They are probably nice people and they definitely pay on time.

Your **B** customers are also good to deal with but you probably have to work a bit harder with them and likely not to be making as much profit for the effort. This will possibly be your biggest group of cuistomers.

Your **C** customers will be taking up a lot more time and effort to keep them happy with possibly lower profits. They can be more awkward to work with and may not be the best payers.

Your **D** customers contribute little or nothjing to your business and are probably taking your valuable time away from more fruitful customers. They are often the squeeky wheel

### Slide 3

Look after you're **A** customers. Neglecting them can lead to the perception of indiference, and you could lose them at a decision point. Don't just communicate with them when you are selling something.

Work with you **B** customers with the intention of helping them become A clients. This can be an effort but can also be worthwhile. As they likely form the core of your business you still need to look after them.

When it come to the **C** customers, try and avoid having too many of these, and make the ground rules clear and be sure to press for on time payment.  Try to get them to become **B** customers

Get rid of your **D** customers or get them to change quickly. They are drawing you away from servicing your better customers and putting you at risk.

### Slide 4

Recognising who your customers are is a very significant contribution to your business success.

There are many way to segment different types of customers. You may only choose to focus on one specific type or you may have several.

Customer segments define the different groups or people the organisation aims to reach or serve.

One important factor is that you can’t address the different segments or types of customer with the same customer relationship strategies. What works for one segment will not necessarily work for another

There are different ways to segment and analyse your customers.

What differentiates the customer segments is:

- They have different needs and will justify a distinct offer you need to craft for that particular need.
- They are reached through different distribution channels and the correct selection could be critical to reaching them.
- They require a different type of relationship from you depending on the perceived value of the product.
- They have substantially different profitability which will determine the acquisition cost you are prepared to invest.
Some organisations serve two or more very diversified or independent segments.

### Slide 9

**A customer journey** – is the sequence of all the interactions a buyer has with your business, from discovering you to making a purchase and should include the customer care beyond purchase.

The customer journey is best represented in some form of diagram that shows the various stages of the customer’s interaction with your business. This is known as a **customer journey map or** CJM. This is different from a sales tool because it totally customer-centric

Mapping the customer journey is all about seeing the business from the customer's perspective with the intention of improving the customer experience with you.  The map tracks every touchpoint between the company and your business. Touchpoints should include actions on the part of the business and on the part of the company, including thoughts and emotions.

This is the most effective approach to getting insight into the customer’s actions, thoughts and feelings which guides you to opportunities for improvement and earning long term loyalty,

Any company that cares about its customers should have a Customer Journey Map.

### Slide 10

### Here are five customer journey stages

There can be many more but this is a simple example

### Awareness

Customers learn about your product or service through a word-of-mouth recommendation, seeing an advertisement or doing their own research.

### Interest

You have the customer’s attention and they’re considering buying your product or service. At this stage, the customer might be reading reviews, comparing prices or hunting for a promotion to sweeten the deal.

### PURCHASE

The customer has decided to take action and make a purchase. This isn’t the end of the line though! For one thing, a customer can decide to buy something and then change their mind because of an unsatisfactory experience with the buying process.

### Experience

Customers receive the product or service and begin to use it. At this stage, customer support becomes very important. Make it easy for customers to address any issues, whether it’s by talking directly to a representative or using self-service resources, such as an FAQ page or a tutorial video.

### Loyalty

If the product or service 'wows' customers, your brand begins to earn their loyalty. If customers like you enough, they’ll become brand advocates who spread the word about your company. At this stage, reward and retain your customers by providing customer loyalty programmes, offering promotions and, above all, continuing to deliver a product and user experience that meets expectations.

### Slide 14

Customer service is the assistance and advice provided by a company to those people who buy or use its products or services.

It also includes the processes and people that support that service.

If you business makes customer focus a top priority, it can set your business apart from the competition. Especially in the UK where customer service is notoriously poor.

Customer service doesn’t just happen when customers reach out with a problem. It should start whenever people reach out to the company for any reason, even before they have decided to buy from you.

The golden rule is that good customer service meets or exceeds customer expecations.

There is a report -  Zendesk’s Customer Experience Trends Report, 2020 revealed that roughly half of customers say they would switch to a competitor after just one bad experience. In the case of more than one bad experience, the number snowballs to 80%.

So, what do they expect?

Quick replies on a channel of their choice

What do they dislike?

Being bounced around between different departments or individuals.

Having to repeat the story when they are transferred.

Waiting on hold.

Poor music sound on hold.

The three most important practices:

Empathy

Rapid solving of problems

Showing that the customer matters

Customer Service sklills:

- Language and tone
- Active listening
- Clear communication
- Interpersonal skills
- Attention to detail
- Attentiveness
- Collaboration skills
If a prospect is reaching out to get more information about a product and the agent is understanding, efficient, and helpful that prospect is going to remember the experience as a positive one.

The golden rule of customer service comes down to understanding what a customer needs and doing what you can to make them happy.
