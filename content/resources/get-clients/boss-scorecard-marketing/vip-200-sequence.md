## The Two Week Sequence

Four messages. Fourteen days. Each one has a different job.

The versions in the source material were written by an agency selling LinkedIn services. Below is the same structure rewritten for a Profit Coach talking to a UK business owner doing £200K to £5M.

## The four jobs

| **Day** | **Message** | **Its job** | **Target** |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | They accept | They see your posts for 48 hours | 30 to 40% acceptance |
| 1 | Problem opener | Start the conversation | 20 to 30% reply |
| 2 | Forgot to mention | Add proof, hand over the asset |  |
| 8 | The nudge | Make them read the first two | Highest reply of the four |
| 15 | Loose breakup | Pull the replies nothing else pulls |  |

If you only send message one, you will conclude LinkedIn is dead. Most of the meetings come from three and four.

## Message 1: the problem opener

**Its job:** name a problem they already feel, then reframe it so they realise it is not the problem they thought it was.

**The anatomy, four lines:**

- Who they are, in seven words or fewer
- The problem, phrased as what most owners like them experience
- The reframe. "The issue isn't X, it's Y"
- A soft ask, and a hint that you have something specific
Under 75 words. One real question. No link, no calendar, no use of the word coaching.

## Message 1, Version A: profit isn't following revenue

Hi Sarah, Most owners running an engineering business your size tell me revenue is growing but profit isn't following it. The issue is rarely sales. It's that nobody has ever scored the nine areas underneath the business to find where the money is actually leaking. Worth a chat? I've got two ideas for a business your size.

**Use when:** they are visibly growing. New hires, new site, expansion posts.

## Message 1, Version B: the owner is the bottleneck

Hi Sarah, Most construction owners at your size aren't short of work. They're short of themselves. Everything still routes through you. That isn't a time management problem, it's a systems one, and it's usually three specific things. Want me to tell you which three?

**Use when:** they post about being flat out, hiring, or working weekends. This is the highest-frequency pain in our data at 19 percent.

## Message 1, Version C: they can't name what's broken

Hi Sarah, Most owners I meet know something is capping their growth. Very few can name which thing, so they end up fixing the loudest problem instead of the most expensive one. I use a 13 question scorecard that names it and puts a number on the gap. Want the link?

**Use when:** you have no track record yet. The scorecard is the credibility. New coaches start here on day one.

## What you change and what you leave alone

**Change every time** Their first name. Their sector. Their size band. Which of the three versions fits what you saw on their profile.

**Change once, then never again** Your proof line, if you use one.

**Leave exactly as written** The four line shape. The word "most" in line two. The reframe in line three. The single soft ask at the end.

The temptation is to improve it. Don't. When a message is working your job is volume, not edits.

## Message 2: the forgot to mention

**Its job:** add one piece of proof, then hand over the valuable thing. This is where the V in RSVP lands.

Two sentences. It should read like something you remembered afterwards, not message two of a sequence. Send it three days after message one, whether or not they replied.

## Message 2, Profit Coach versions

**Matches Version A**

Forgot to mention, the last engineering owner I did this with found £180K sitting in his pricing, not his pipeline. Would it help to see what we changed?

**Matches Version B**

Forgot to mention, we took a £900K owner from 58 hours a week to 41 in five months without dropping revenue. Want me to show you what came off his plate first?

**Matches Version C**

Forgot to mention, the average score on this is around 4 out of 10, and almost everyone guesses the wrong area. Would it make sense to send you yours?

## Message 3: the nudge

Sarah?

That is the entire message. Their first name and a question mark.

It is the highest performing follow up in the whole sequence. It makes them scroll up and read the two messages they skipped, which is the only thing that has ever worked.

Do not add to it. No "just bumping this". No "hope you're well". No emoji. Send it on day eight.

## Message 4: the loose breakup

**Its job:** take the pressure off and reframe on their goal, not your offer. This pulls replies nothing else pulls. "Let's talk." "Try me next quarter." "Actually, speak to my FD."

**Matches Version A**

Totally get it if the timing's off. Just know that when getting profit to move without adding headcount does become the priority, I'd be glad to help. All the best either way, Sarah.

**Matches Version B**

No worries if now isn't the moment. When getting the business to run without you does move up the list, I'm here. Wishing you a good quarter either way.

**Matches Version C**

Sounds like this isn't a priority right now and that's fair enough. If it changes, the 13 questions are yours, no charge and no call needed. All the best.

## Six ways coaches break this

- **Adding a calendar link.** Instantly turns a conversation into a pitch.
- **Asking two questions.** Every extra question drops the reply rate. One only.
- **Writing four paragraphs.** Nobody reads a LinkedIn novel. Under 75 words.
- **Using the word coaching.** Owners want a system, not a relationship. Say what they get, not what you are.
- **Stopping after message one.** Most of the meetings come from three and four.
- **Editing a template that's working.** If it's above 20 percent, put volume behind it, don't tinker.
