The 7 quality tools are problem-solving tools. They:

- help you identify and prioritise problems quickly and more effectively
- support decision making
- give you simple but powerful tools for continuous improvement
- give you a way to communicate problems and solutions across the business
- help you pull useful information out of the data you collect.

## Planning your data collection

- What question do we need to answer?
- How will we recognise and communicate the answers?
- What data analysis tools will we use, and how will we communicate the results?
- What type of data do we need to answer the question?
- Where in the process can we get the data we need?
- Who in the process can give us the data?
- How can we collect it from people with the least effort and error?
- When is the data to be provided?
- How much will it cost to collect?
- What extra information do we need for future analysis?

## The 7 quality tools explained

### Check sheets

A simple and effective way of gathering information. A check sheet:

- keeps the data you collect consistent
- can be filled in while doing the normal job
- makes data collection and analysis simpler
- highlights trends
- spots problems.

How to use one:

1. Decide on the format, for example a table or a tally chart.
2. Decide which factors need to be measured.
3. Create a format that makes it simple to collect data on all of them.
4. Leave space for comments. They often give valuable insight.
5. Prepare instructions and train the people collecting the data.
6. Test the check sheet before full use, so you can iron out problems.
7. Audit the process and validate the results.

### Pareto chart

Commonly known as "ABC analysis" or "the 80:20 rule". For example, 80% of problems come from 20% of the causes. The data categories are arranged in order of frequency, starting with the most frequent.

- It is one of the most effective yet simple tools available.
- It identifies the most significant problem to work on first.
- It is an effective ongoing improvement tool.
- It separates the vital few from the trivial many.

### Scatter diagram

A graph that helps you spot possible relationships between two different sets of variables. It shows what happens to one variable when another changes, and it is a way of testing possible cause and effect relationships.

### Histogram

A histogram has much in common with the Pareto chart, and can be vertical or horizontal. It is a visual way of showing data, and it is easier to display and read large amounts of data this way than in tables. It is a picture of how the process behaved over a given period of time.

Why use one? It helps you make sense of data, lets you see patterns that are hard to spot in tables of numbers, and is a simple way of communicating data.

### Control chart

A statistical tool used to tell the difference between variation caused by common causes and variation caused by special causes. It is a picture of how stable or unstable the process is over time, with the data shown in the order it happened.

Why use one? To judge how the process performed over a period of time, to give everyone a common language for discussing process performance, and to check whether changes have improved the process.

### Cause and effect diagram

A tool used alongside brainstorming to identify, sort and display the possible causes of a specific problem. It is also known as a fishbone or Ishikawa diagram. It shows the relationship between the outcome and the factors that influence it.

### Flowcharts

Flowcharts show the steps in a process, for example the flow of materials or the order of operations. You can use them to compare planned changes with what actually happens, and to start process improvement activities.

Why use them? They give an overview of the process at a glance, show how each step relates to the others, show where to collect data and where to put controls, and help identify the customers of the process.

## Summary

The 7 quality tools are the basis for problem solving. Using just any tool isn't enough. What matters is knowing how and when to use each one.
