## Chaotic teams

- They rush headlong into tasks without thinking about the process.
- Objectives are unclear.
- They assume everyone knows the objectives.
- Planning is poor.
- The leader's role is unclear.
- Ideas are voiced but not listened to or developed. There's a lot of talking over and interrupting, and communication tends to be selfish and undisciplined.
- Success is patchy.
- They tend to explain away failure.
- It's fun, for a short while.

## Formal teams

- They are likely to over-react and become too formal in their procedures and roles.
- Strong leadership often seems to be the answer to the problems of the chaotic stage, and the leader is often criticised for not being strong enough during that stage.
- Roles are formal and specific (timekeeper, secretary and so on).
- Flexibility is poor.
- They don't make use of what their members can do.

## Mature teams

- They are more flexible with procedures, and can break the rules without slipping back into chaos.
- Roles are flexible. Members act according to the task and the contribution they can make.
- The leader is less directive.
- Everyone shares responsibility.
- The atmosphere is relaxed and co-operative.
- Everyone is involved and contributes according to their strengths and abilities.
- Members understand and trust each other.
- Listening is good. Ideas are listened to and developed.
- Members aren't frightened to contribute, and nobody is ridiculed for failing.

## Synergy: 1 + 1 = 3

Individuals working together in a team will produce more than the sum of the same individuals working alone.

## The roles people play

**Leaders** co-ordinate the activities of others, including the review process. They clarify goals and promote decision making, motivate and encourage the team, listen to members' opinions and check that everyone has understood.

**Doers** are very action-oriented. They like to get tasks done, care a lot about achieving objectives, and bring drive and energy to the team.

**Thinkers** carefully think through problems and ideas. They are usually very analytical and enjoy planning and problem solving.

**Carers** are very interested in building good team spirit. They are sensitive to people's feelings and morale, and work to ease tensions and build good relationships.

## Two simple team tools

**Why, why?** Ask "why?" five times to find the root cause of a problem:

- Why did the machine stop? Because the fuse blew due to an overload.
- Why was there an overload? Because the bearing lubrication was inadequate.
- Why was the lubrication inadequate? Because the lubrication pump wasn't working properly.
- Why wasn't the pump working properly? Because the pump axle was worn out.
- Why was it worn out? Because sludge got in.

By asking "why?" five times, you can find the root cause of the problem, and so the real solution: fitting a strainer to the lubrication pump. Without it, you might simply have replaced the fuse.

**Five Ws and one H.** For problem solving, ask:

- **Who** is having the problem?
- **Why** are you doing things this way?
- **Where** is the problem happening?
- **What** is going wrong?
- **When** is the problem happening?
- **How** is it happening this way?

For planning, ask:

- **Who** is going to do what?
- **Why** is that necessary?
- **Where** do you start?
- **What** do you have to achieve?
- **When** do you need to be finished?
- **How** can you achieve the objective?

## Giving feedback

- Remember the purpose.
- Make sure the other person is ready to receive your feedback.
- Be specific.
- Focus on the positive parts of their behaviour.
- Give them a chance to consider it and respond.
- Don't overload them.

## Receiving feedback

- Make sure you are ready to listen.
- Remember that the feedback is there to help you learn and develop.
- Put it in your own words to make sure you have understood.
- After each piece of feedback, think about what you have heard.
- Thank the person who gave it.

## What effective teams look like

Team members:

- communicate openly and frankly with each other, both giving and receiving feedback
- can compete and co-operate at the right times
- are committed to quality and to achieving goals
- support other team members, and share responsibility for solving problems and making decisions
- support the team leader

The team:

- sees conflict as healthy and necessary, in a climate of trust, openness and understanding
- cares about its own development
- has a friendly, good-natured atmosphere
- is exciting to work with
