A tactical implementation plan (TIP) comes from a shared understanding of why the business needs to change or improve. It is the common tool that makes clear to everyone the actions needed to reach the business goals and targets.

The business goals and targets are set from these events and sources of information:

- Business challenges
- The value planning process
- Value stream maps
- Business goals and targets
- Benchmarking
- Business assessments
- Diagnostic assessment

A tactical implementation plan holds the actions that came out of the gap analysis, which let you move from the current state to the future state.

## What is a tactical implementation plan?

A set of objectives that support the business operating plan, with:

- a set of measurable goals that support the objectives
- a timed schedule for reaching the goals
- a visible way of communicating what is to be done
- accountable people for delivering each objective
- clear milestones for the objectives

## Why have one?

To plan and carry out actions well, you need a standard, structured scheduling tool.

Poor, unstructured planning can lead to these problems.

**Trying to improve everything at once:**

- It needs a large number of people.
- It needs many teachers to keep the message the same.
- It is stressful, because of the number of changes.
- It increases the risk to customer delivery.

**Unco-ordinated changes focused on processes or systems:**

- It is hard to see any benefit until they are all finished.
- It is hard to link the improvements in the value stream.
- The longer timescale needs a higher level of focus.
- The focus is on specifics, not the whole value stream.

A TIP makes clear to everyone exactly what has to be achieved, when and by whom, and lets you review progress against the schedule. It helps you:

- define the agreed, compelling business need
- give focus to drive the improvements
- encourage regular reviews of where the schedule stands
- review quality, cost and delivery measures
- gain ownership of the goals and actions
- set the direction for each business team
- communicate and get people involved

## How to use the TIP

The TIP is built to show the tasks that must be completed in a set timescale, and to make progress on each task visible so you can review it regularly.

## Creating a tactical implementation plan

- Each objective has a set of tasks to support it.
- There are specific goals to achieve.
- Each TIP has an owner who is accountable for updating and reviewing it.
- Accountable people from the workstreams sign up to the plan (level 1).
- Each task has a start and finish date.
- Each task has an accountable person (level 1 or 2).
- TIPs are an essential enabler in the change process.
- Quality is more important than quantity.
- Sign-off from senior management secures commitment.
- Make sure the detail in the TIP is reviewed often.

Highlight missed milestones as soon as they are known, then follow the standard escalation process.

Actively encourage the structured use of TIPs in **your** business.
