While I was working in a major public sector organisation, one of the internal consultants I had been mentoring for a while asked me a question. How was it that we always seemed to focus on exactly the right area to fix the issues that were hurting the operation, even when it wasn't obvious what the issues were, let alone what the solutions needed to be? Was this some kind of witchcraft?

He had completed his Green Belt and was working on his Black Belt, but the Operational Diagnostic had not been covered in enough detail for him to understand how powerful and important it is. Nor does the belt system, or any other kind of study, give any of us that rare commodity: experience.

He only had the experience of applying what he'd learned in one environment. As a wise man once said to me, "Isn't ten years' experience doing the same thing simply one year's experience repeated ten times?"

## The step-by-step approach

I explained the step-by-step approach we take when carrying out the Operational Diagnostic. It is based on three things: common sense; a proven structure that lets you identify the root causes of issues; and experience across different sectors and industries, which feeds into the solutions needed to fix those root causes.

I also assured him there was no witchcraft involved.

Whatever the industry or sector, one key activity is always the starting point: the Operational Diagnostic. This critical activity gives insight into the key business issues, the root causes of those issues and the areas for improvement. It forms the basis of all the improvement work that follows.

It goes without saying that without it, no improvements could be made, no benefits would be delivered and there would be no change to the bottom line (which is usually the be-all and end-all for most big companies).

Unfortunately, for most smaller businesses, this activity only comes as part of a larger piece of consultancy work, where it is used to estimate benefits, plan the work and sell more services into the business.
