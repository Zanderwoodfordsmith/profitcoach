Kaizen Jitsu means "the art of improvement".

The name comes from the Japanese "kai" (change) and "zen" (good), which together mean change for the good, or improvement, and "jitsu", which means art.

After all, it's pretty pointless making changes in any organisation if, once you walk away, the organisation simply goes back to the old way of doing things. (Does this sound familiar?)

There is a way of making change in any organisation that wins the full buy-in of all the staff, while subtly but steadily changing the culture into one where the changes are embraced, sustained and improved on in future.

This has to be done during the change process, though, not as an afterthought or a bolt-on "cultural change" or "people change" process, as some of the big consultancy players do.

In the words of the old Japanese saying, "If the student hasn't learned, the teacher hasn't taught." Or, in my words: "If the changes didn't stick, you didn't do your job." (Assuming you're the person who made the changes.)
