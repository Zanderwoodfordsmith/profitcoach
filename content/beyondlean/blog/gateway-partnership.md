Why would you need a Gateway, or partnership, process? Why does your company need to change at all, especially if you're doing very well right now?

## Why change the way you do things?

- In today's global economy only two states are possible: expansion or contraction. You drive forwards or you slip backwards.
- Standing still is no longer an option.
- Your competitors will be moving forward. The world around us moves forward every day.
- Just to keep what you have, you need to grow and move forward at the same rate.
- To really get ahead, you need to do something different.

So you start an improvement programme.

## A foundation of quicksand

![Improvements standing on a foundation of quicksand](/beyondlean/blog/gateway-partnership/quicksand.jpg)

You get some small early wins, but after the first burst of enthusiasm things start to slip, because people aren't involved and haven't bought in. It seems to be left to a handful of individuals to drive the change.

It is almost impossible to build anything lasting on a foundation of quicksand.

For anything to endure, it must be built on a solid foundation. That is every bit as true for changing an organisation as it is for a building. If you were investing large sums of money in a new factory or office block, you would make damn sure those foundations were going to hold the thing up.

## Gateway gives you that foundation

![Gateway as the foundation, resting on mutual trust and respect](/beyondlean/blog/gateway-partnership/gateway-foundation.jpg)

Gateway gives you the foundation to build your organisational change on. Gateway means working together for the mutual benefit of all:

- Mutual trust and respect between the shop floor and management.
- A spirit based on the simple but critical understanding that the welfare of shop floor employees and of management depend on each other.
- Working together in this stable environment adds to the success of the company.
- Employees come to see, and take pride in, the fact that they contribute directly to the company's growth.
- In doing so, they are also improving their own tomorrows.

## So what is Gateway?

- It is a process that gives employees, unions and management the chance to shape the future of your company together.
- You decide, as a team, what does and doesn't go into each Gateway package. If a package isn't balanced, and doesn't give a win for both management and employees, it doesn't go forward.
- It is a process designed specifically for cooperation and success for all.
- It is a process designed to help secure jobs for all employees in the long term.

## How Gateway works

The Gateway process is a highly flexible framework for managing changes in culture and working practices that support the long-term business objectives. It gives you a partnership approach to designing, carrying out and communicating business change.

**Involvement is the cornerstone of the process.**

All significant business change that involves employees should be channelled through the Gateway process.

Gateway develops over time. Although the design allows for a long-term plan, decisions on each Gateway step are taken one at a time. For example, the content of Gateway four is only decided 75% of the way through Gateway three, using the long-term plan to choose the activities that suit the time.

![The Gateway process, from company targets and vision to a decision on each proposal](/beyondlean/blog/gateway-partnership/gateway-process.jpg)

## Roles within the process

**Gateway steering team.** This is made up of key senior managers, a senior trade union official and the site convenor (for example three management members, one senior trade union official, one convenor and one employee relations manager). The steering team meets monthly. It sponsors the process, reviews how well it is working against the original objectives and owns the overall timescales.

**Gateway implementation team.** This is made up of the company's senior operational managers, the site's senior shop stewards and the convenor. It has four company managers, four trade union representatives, a facilitator and a chairperson (who also sits on the steering group). Other experts are called into the team as needed. The implementation team designs its own management review process and is responsible for:

- proposing tasks to the steering team
- prioritising tasks into the Gateway process
- designing Gateway activities, involving stakeholders and employees
- communicating the output to both the management team and the trade union, plus employees as appropriate

## Operating principles

The key drivers of the Gateway process are the operating principles. These are an agreed set of guidelines that every Gateway proposal must line up with. They are normally agreed at a facilitated session early in the roll-out.

Here is an example set of operating principles:

In support of the company's objectives, and to promote long-term job security, management, union and employees will work on the basis of these key principles:

1. We will have systems, processes and behaviours that build trust and empower our teams. This will be supported by the right training.
2. We will give all our employees the chance to be involved in the future development of our company.
3. The process of change will be open and transparent at all stages.
4. Our leaders, through their behaviours and actions, will visibly support and embrace cultural change.
5. We will work consistently to the agreed and correct policy or process.
6. We will make the best use of the available skills, talents and time of each individual.
