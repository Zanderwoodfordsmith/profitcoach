One of the clients I've been coaching recently asked me how I seem to know how certain people will behave and react to things before they even happen.

There is no magic here, and we aren't talking about the so-called "soft skills". This is a science rather than an art, whatever some people seem to think. It's about being able to read people well, and then being able to influence them based on their personality type and what drives them. Here we'll look at the first part of good stakeholder management.

Managing stakeholders well is one of the most skilled and difficult parts of any improvement project. The subject is massive, but it is so important that I decided to pull out some of the key points, hints and tips that I think you can put to use straight away. Almost always, how well you master this skill decides whether a project succeeds or fails.

## Two behavioural modes

Here is the first useful thing to watch for in your stakeholders: the different behavioural modes. There are two.

**Default mode.** This is the one you may have to look hard to see. The default mode holds all of a person's deep-seated beliefs and prejudices, their goals and drivers, and their basic intentions, good or bad. You spot someone's default behavioural mode not by listening to what they say, but by noticing **how** they say it and how they behave. Once you know what you're looking for, you will be able to spot it, and that is the key to knowing exactly how to deal with them.

**Assumed mode.** This is the mode the person wants you to believe is their default. It often hides the intentions, beliefs and goals they don't want you to see. The old saying "take people as you find them" isn't always the best one to follow. Of course, some people are completely open and only ever show their default mode, but you need to be able to tell the difference. People tend to use the assumed mode when they are adjusting to their "audience", or before trust has been firmly established. (It's the one two-faced people show you before they stab you in the back.)

Always remember: the eyes are the window to the soul.
