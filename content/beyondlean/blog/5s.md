## 5S can form the basis of your production system

5S is in place in all world-class organisations. It aims to:

- Remove waste from the workplace.
- Reduce non-value-added activities.
- Provide an environment where continuous improvement is embraced.
- Improve safety.
- Increase quality.

Putting 5S into a workplace involves an initial improvement of an area but, more importantly, it involves creating a disciplined system to keep the improved condition over time. It creates a foundation to build stability, and it is key to building a successful continuous improvement environment.

## Step 1 (Seiri): Sort

Clear out and classify. Identify:

- what you know you definitely need all the time
- what you know you definitely need occasionally
- what you think you might need in the future
- what you think you no longer need.

Never assume. Make sure everyone is in agreement. Check that what you think becomes what you know.

If there is a question over an item, give it a red tag, which means it needs further review. When in doubt, throw it out.

Remember: redundant items cost money to store. If you don't need it, don't keep it.

### Eliminating waste: action at the source

Seven steps to follow:

1. Determine the extent of the dirt and leakages.
2. Undertake a major clean.
3. Investigate the reasons why.
4. Determine the worst locations within the site or area.
5. List the reasons in detail.
6. Decide on an effective action policy.
7. Budget and schedule an implementation plan (if necessary).

## Step 2 (Seiton): Set in order

Configure: put everything in its correct place.

- Organise storage for all equipment: what, where and how many.
- Frequency of use defines where things live in relation to where they are used. If something is used often, keep it close. If it is used rarely, store it away from the work.
- The shop must have clear walkways, work areas, rest areas and so on.
- Consider using different coloured floor areas to highlight walkways, work areas and storage areas.
- All essentials, such as fire extinguishers and safety equipment, must be visible and easy to reach.

The place must be:

- appropriate to how it is used
- well maintained
- set up so tools are easy to find
- clearly labelled, for both tools and their locations.

A place for everything and everything in its place.

## Step 3 (Seiso): Shine

Clean and check.

- Regular cleaning schedules are needed.
- Create a clean and tidy working environment and maintain it.
- Define responsibility zones for cleaning, and make roles and responsibilities clear.
- Develop regular routines for keeping the place clean (for example, a five-minute 5S).

Cleaning must become an activity which is:

- ongoing
- monitored
- a source of pride
- seen as a value-added contribution.

Cleanliness is the basis of quality. Once the workplace is clean, it must be kept that way.

## Step 4 (Seiketsu): Standardise

Conformity and good housekeeping.

- Decide what counts as an abnormality and make it visible to the operator.
- Design clear, standard labels for locations, tools and machine conditions, and put them in standard positions.
- Provide indicators where limits can be exceeded.
- Draw position markers in places where items are removed and returned.

Agree and set:

- standards of cleanliness
- procedures for maintaining those standards
- standard marking and labelling of all items
- standard methods for showing limits, identifying locations and so on.

Decide what needs doing regularly to keep each area in order, and how often: daily, weekly or monthly. Use this to write standard 5S checklists, and decide who is responsible for each one.

The system must be controlled and maintained.

## Step 5 (Shitsuke): Sustain

Custom and practice: stick to the system rules.

- Develop and keep habits through training and discipline, at all times.
- Use visual rather than verbal communication to train people in new procedures.
- Involve everyone in developing the standard documents (for example, check sheets).

It is important to maintain discipline:

- Incorrect practices must be identified.
- Correct practices have to be taught and demonstrated.
- Make sure everyone understands and agrees.

To keep it going, develop a 5S audit sheet and set an audit schedule. Divide the workplace into areas and give each one a person responsible for 5S. Involve operators, supervisors and managers in regular 5S audits (possibly monthly).

Without training and discipline, the other steps will fail.

## What 5S does for your business

**For your culture**, it:

- creates awareness of the principles
- helps people see the link between how the workplace is organised and how efficiently it runs
- starts your improvement work with one fully cleaned and organised "model area"
- develops standard procedures for maintenance, and a system to enforce them
- improves morale and gets more people involved.

**For your operation**, it:

- reduces cost
- improves operating conditions
- reduces equipment operating errors
- improves process reliability
- promotes safety
- increases equipment availability
- improves how customers see you.
