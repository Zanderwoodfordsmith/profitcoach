## Make the foundation strong

Lasting success with Lean depends totally on putting it in on a solid foundation.

Business stability is essential. The basics must be in place.

So what needs to be in place in any business, in any industry, before Lean can succeed?

![The Stability phase checklist: discipline, terms of payment, measures, organisational model, takt time and work sequence](/beyondlean/blog/stability-first/stability-checklist.jpg)

## Discipline

1. Get the people there.
2. Get the people there on time.
3. Get the people doing what they should be doing when they are there.

You do this through standard disciplinary procedures, sickness and absence procedures and strong consequence management.

In simple terms, the policies must be in place and followed, so that they act as enablers for any business improvement.

## Terms of payment

Use a simple but very effective model with your suppliers and customers to keep your cash flow as fluid as possible.

## Install measures

What are the key performance indicators (KPIs) for your business? How do you know for certain whether you are winning or losing? "If you can measure it, you can improve it."

A basic performance management system is all you need. You can build on it later, once you have stability.

## Organisational model

Success with Lean relies on a good communication structure and a solid, well-structured organisational model. That includes sensible reporting lines and the right ratio of leaders to workers.

## Takt time

One of the cornerstones of success with Lean is working to customer demand, or working to takt.

Takt is a German word. It describes the beat of the conductor's baton. It is the principle that all activity in a business is kept in time by a pulse, set by customer demand.

Takt time relates customer demand to the time available:

**Takt time = production time available ÷ customer demand**

Time is deducted for:

- lunch and tea breaks
- team briefings
- TPM (maintenance) breaks
- clean-down time

For example:

- 8 hours x 60 = 480 minutes
- Time available = 480 minutes, minus 20 minutes of breaks, minus 10 minutes of TPM = 450 minutes
- If customer demand is 500 units a week, daily demand = 500 ÷ 5 = 100 products a day
- Takt time = 450 minutes ÷ 100 = 4.5 minutes, or 270 seconds

In this example, a finished product must come off the end of the line every 4.5 minutes. This is sometimes called the production "drumbeat".

Takt lets you work out your delivery schedule adherence, which gives you a clear view of how closely you are performing to customer demand.

## Work sequence

With your takt time set, you can arrange your sub-products and operations into the most logical sequence, with some measures in place so everyone knows what is expected of them and in what time.
