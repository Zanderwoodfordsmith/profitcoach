Poka yoke, or mistake proofing, means taking steps to make sure errors or abnormalities can't happen.

It can involve checklists, quality checks, part or tool design, machine changes, setting tolerance limits and so on.

## Three types of poka yoke

1. **Contact type:** uses shape, size or other physical properties to detect whether a feature is there or not. For example, things that only fit one way.
2. **Constant number type:** if a fixed number of actions or movements are not made, an error signal is triggered. For example, having to complete the data entry fields when taking an order.
3. **Performance sequence type:** makes sure steps are done in the right order. For example, a checklist for pre-flight checks, or completing forms in a logical way.

Poka yokes are either:

- **Shut-out type:** they stop an error being made.
- **Attention type:** they highlight that an error has been made.

Shut-out is clearly better than attention, because it prevents waste and doesn't rely on someone noticing the error signal.

The ideal poka yokes are:

- inexpensive
- simple and easy to put in place
- specific to the need
- developed by every employee.

## The three golden rules

![The three rules of error proofing](/beyondlean/blog/error-proofing/three-rules.jpg)

An error-proofing system should take into account three simple rules:

- Don't accept a defect from your supplier.
- Don't make a defect yourself.
- Don't pass a defect on to your customer.

Ideally, design the product so that it can't be assembled incorrectly.

## Everyday examples

- The unleaded fuel filler opening on a car's fuel tank
- The cut-off switch on a kettle
- The ball cock in a toilet cistern
- Spell check on a computer
- The overflow outlet on a sink
- Phone cord plugs

## Errors and defects

To understand error proofing, it helps to understand the difference between a defect and an error.

- A defect is a product that deviates from specification or doesn't meet customer expectation.
- An error is any deviation from a specified process. Errors can be made by machines or people, and can be caused by errors that happened earlier.
- All defects are created by errors.

This means that if errors can be prevented, no defects will be created. Error proofing finds and removes the errors that cause defects. Keep using it to find and prevent new errors.

## The 10 most common types of error

Error proofing can only affect the errors that could cause defects. Not all errors cause defects. Here are the ten most common errors it is designed to correct or eliminate:

1. **Processing omissions:** leaving out one or more process steps.
2. **Processing errors:** not carrying out an operation according to the standard work procedure.
3. **Errors setting up the workpiece:** using the wrong tooling, or setting the machine incorrectly for the current product.
4. **Missing parts:** not all parts included in the assembly, welding or other processes.
5. **Wrong part or item:** the wrong part installed in an assembly.
6. **Processing the wrong workpiece:** the wrong part machined.
7. **Operation errors:** carrying out an operation incorrectly, or using the wrong revision of a standard process or specification sheet.
8. **Adjustment, measurement and dimension errors:** errors in machine adjustments, test measurements, or the dimensions of a part coming in from a supplier.
9. **Errors in equipment maintenance or repair:** defects caused by incorrect repairs or component replacement.
10. **Errors preparing blades, jigs or tools:** damaged blades, poorly designed jigs or the wrong tools.

## How to use poka yoke

1. Identify the operation or process that needs mistake proofing. Target areas with high numbers of errors, or where even a single error is very costly.
2. Use the 5 whys or cause and effect analysis to get to the root of the problem.
3. Decide whether to use a shut-out or an attention method. There may be technical or financial reasons why you have to choose attention.
4. Decide whether a contact, constant number or sequence method is best. This will depend on the nature and purpose of the activity.
5. Design a suitable poka yoke.
6. Test it to see if it works. Try to avoid large expense before you finish this step: use mock-ups or make-dos.
7. Once you have a working method, make sure you have the right tools, checklists, software and so on for it to work consistently and correctly.
8. Train everyone to use it.
9. After it has been running for a while (how long depends on how often the activity happens), review performance to make sure the errors have been eliminated.

Then take whatever steps are needed to improve on what you have done.

## How it helps

- Making mistakes impossible prevents waste and makes processes run more smoothly.
- It enforces operating procedures and sequences.
- It builds quality in at the source, instead of checking for it after the fact.
- It removes the choices that lead to wrong actions.

Don't worry about getting it right. Just get it going.
