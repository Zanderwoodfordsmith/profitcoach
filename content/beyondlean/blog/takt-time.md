Takt is a German word meaning "beat" or drumbeat, like a metronome. It means matching the pace of production to customer demand.

Sticking to takt will ensure customer satisfaction through 100% on-time delivery. It also makes sure you don't deliver too many or too few when the customer doesn't want them. If the customer wants 200 items a week, then we need to deliver 200 items a week, not 800 items at the end of the month. If we do it that way, the customer may have staff waiting around for four weeks.

## Benefits

- It links production activity to actual customer demand.
- It gives production a drumbeat, which makes planning and forecasting better.
- It keeps all production activity in step, from the first process to final assembly. This is "flow".

## Definitions

- **Takt:** the pace at which the customer needs products.
- **Cycle time:** the time in which a process cycles to produce products.
- **Lead time:** the total production time from start to finish, including inventory time between processes.

Don't get them confused.

![How takt time, cycle time and lead time relate](/beyondlean/blog/takt-time/takt-cycle-lead-time.jpg)

## How to calculate takt time

Takt relates customer demand to the time available:

Takt = production time available / customer demand

Deduct time for:

- lunch and tea breaks
- team briefings
- TPM breaks
- clean-down time.

Takt time is one of the fundamentals the Toyota Production System was built on.
