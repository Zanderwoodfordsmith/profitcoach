A client once asked me why he needed to leave his office and go to the workplace to carry out "this gemba thing". He was quite new to his role as a senior manager at one of the major banks. After a little persuasion, he reluctantly went and spent half a day among his teams in the gemba.

When he came back, he asked for a short debrief, and he was nothing short of enthused about how much he'd learned about his operation, its strengths and weaknesses, and the people who worked for him. My favourite quote was: "I've learned more in the last three and a half hours about this operation than in the previous four weeks of so-called management meetings."

## What it means

Gemba management is a style of management based on being highly visible at the gemba (the place where value is added), and on actively promoting and using Lean Six Sigma tools and techniques in day-to-day work.

## The shadow you cast

An organisation is a shadow of its leader.

If you show belief in the new ways of working, the people you lead will see this as acceptable behaviour and try to copy it. However, if you simply pay lip service to the change, guess what the people who work for you will do?

Your staff may not always do as you ask, but they will always try to do as you do.

What kind of shadow will you cast?
