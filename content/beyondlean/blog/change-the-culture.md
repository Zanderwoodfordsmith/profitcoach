Many of my clients and colleagues are forever asking, "How do we make a Lean Six Sigma project last?" This is by far the hardest part to achieve, and it is all about the people.

Based on my personal scars and experience, any improvement activity is 20% about the tools and 80% about the people. It is people who decide whether it lasts. So what decides whether change "sticks" or not?

The beliefs, behaviour and actions of individuals all form part of the "culture" of an organisation, and it is this culture that decides whether your project, programme or transformation lasts.

For anything to stay in place, it has to be accepted as "the norm", or put quite simply, "the way we do things around here".

The change must become part of the culture of the organisation. If it doesn't, it will always be "the stuff we have to do as well as our day job", and it will be dropped at the first opportunity.

## What do we mean by culture?

Some associated words and phrases:

- Way of life, customs, traditions
- Society, background, ethnicity
- Conduct, behaviour, habits, manner

Some definitions:

- "...relating to the cultivation of the mind or manners, especially through artistic or intellectual activity..."
- "...the norms and values that people live by..."
- "...unwritten rules that define what is expected of people and how they should behave..."
- "...the way we do things around here..."

## What do we need to change the culture?

First, we must begin to change the **behaviour, values and beliefs** of the leadership. To do this well takes skill in building rapport, influencing and selling, because the first step in any change of any kind is desire.

Then we need to find **change agents** within the organisation who show a "pioneering spirit". We need to define a picture and feel of the **desired future**, and plan a **strategy and roadmap** to make it happen.

**Involve** as many **people** as possible, who will define it, create a plan and take the actions needed to make it real.

And finally: effort, skill and patience.

## Creating the desire

How do we create the desire that will let us begin to change the behaviour, values and beliefs of the leadership? First, answer these questions:

- Who has a need that I or we can and must satisfy? It may be a business or a specific person.
- Who do you know who is respected by the people you need to influence?
- Who do you know who you suspect might be respected by the people you need to influence?
- If you don't know anyone, what research will you need to do to find out?
- What chains or links between people can you create?
- What value could you create for the person or people in the chain?

So we've covered **what** we need to do to make change stick, but to be honest, that's the easy bit.

Now we get into **how** we go about changing the beliefs, behaviours and actions of the key people connected to the change programme. This can only be done by influencing, negotiating, showing the benefits and getting the stakeholders to **want** to do things differently. In other words, creating the desire.

Some say these are the "soft skills", the "fluffy stuff" or "people skills", and that some people have them as a natural ability but they can't really be taught to people who don't "have it".

That's absolute garbage. This is an exact science, as exact a science as physics. Its common name is sales.

It's how we use these exact techniques to build solid relationships, with high levels of rapport and trust, so that our stakeholders actively want to show the behaviours and actions we need. Those in turn shape the culture into one that embraces the changes as the new norm.
