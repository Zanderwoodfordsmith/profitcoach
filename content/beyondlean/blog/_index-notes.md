## The 6 featured posts, and why

I picked posts in Paul's own voice, built on his own client stories, that speak to an owner rather than teach a tool.

1. **standardisation-gone-wrong.** Three of his own stories (the doughnut queue, the bank that made every team's board identical, the contact centre that cut cost to serve by 30%). It shows judgement and humour, not dogma.
2. **lean-and-six-sigma-dont-work.** His core belief in one piece: get the basics right first, then improve. Memorable lines ("You can give a ferret a toolbox...", the two gardeners) and a plain promise: about 20% better from standardisation alone.
3. **kanban-is-it-the-answer.** He tells a boss "you're not ready" and asks four basic questions any owner can ask of their own business. It builds trust because he turned down the shiny fix.
4. **gemba-management.** Short and vivid: a bank manager learns more in half a day with his teams than in four weeks of meetings. One action an owner can take tomorrow.
5. **why-lean-transformations-fail.** Speaks straight to the owner as the person whose behaviour decides whether change works, with a simple daily habit (an hour at the gemba). Dated 2006, so it also shows a long track record.
6. **what-lean-six-sigma-can-do-for-your-business.** Answers the questions owners actually ask ("we already make money", "we work flat out and still make no profit") in plain terms.

Not featured, but strong: the three case studies are the best proof on the site. I left them out of the six because they are written up from his slide decks, not in his own words. Once Paul approves the wording, the airline catering case study is a good swap for gemba-management.

## Decisions I made (overturn any of these)

- **Source text.** I worked from the live site (the current versions), and used the 2022 export where the live page was garbled.
- **Dates.** A date is the earliest date the old site's own RSS feeds show for that page, or the date printed on the page (newsletter #001: 1 July 2006). Some are when a page was re-announced, not first written. A3 reports (2021-06-30), value stream mapping (2021-07-27), business strategy (2021-07-15) and the tactical implementation plan (2021-05-26) are older pages that only show a 2021 date. Gateway, Stability, Kaizen and the three case studies show no date, so theirs is empty. Zander may prefer to hide dates on old posts.
- **readMinutes.** Body words divided by 200, rounded, minimum 1.
- **Videos.** Where a page embedded one of Paul's YouTube videos, the post has one line: "Watch Paul's video: [title](link)". I checked every video: all are public and on his own channel. The Kaizen Jitsu video is no longer available, so that reference is gone.
- **Images.** Only Paul's own diagrams and slides. Several old export images had been overwritten by newer slides with the same file name, so each one was viewed before use. The four case study images were rendered from his PDFs, choosing slides with no client or staff names.
- **Case studies.** No prose existed, only slide decks. I wrote each one up from the slide facts alone, in a plain "we" voice. No clients are named, and the snack manufacturer figures are labelled as the diagnostic's estimates, not results.
- **Merges.** Each of these old pages was a duplicate or a fragment, so it forwards to the same post:
  - lean-six-sigma-culture.html (a copy of change-culture.html) goes to change-the-culture.
  - lean_manufacturing.html (an older copy of the benefits Q&A) goes to what-lean-six-sigma-can-do-for-your-business.
  - business-management.html (the original roadmap page, now showing a copy of the home page on live) goes to standard-business-roadmap.
  - lean-manufacturing-results.html (the short Evaluation phase) is folded into standard-business-roadmap.
  - lean-5s.html (slide notes) is folded into 5s.
- **Titles.** Lightly reworded where the old title was keyword-stuffed or would clash. Lean_Basics-why-lean-does-not-work.html became "The real reason Lean transformations fail", so it doesn't sit next to "Lean and Six Sigma don't work".
- **Pronouns.** In the newsletter post, "he" for the senior manager became "they".
- **Internal links.** I only rewrote links the old pages already had. There are two exceptions:
  - standard-business-roadmap links to the Gateway post where the old page said "see the Culture section of this site".
  - kaizen opens with a link to standard-business-roadmap, because "the phase" makes no sense without it.
- **Removed everywhere.**
  - Course, belt, certification, discount, product, training pack and PayPal wording and links.
  - "GUARANTEED", shouting caps and "!!".
  - "Coming soon" promises.
  - Links to other old-site pages.
  - Em and en dashes.
- **Spelling.** UK spelling throughout. "Taichi Ohno" is corrected to Taiichi Ohno.
