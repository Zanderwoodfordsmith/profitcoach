Waste is the use of any material or resource beyond what the customer requires and is willing to pay for.

Lean aims to identify and eliminate waste to improve the performance of the business.

Shigeo Shingo identified seven forms of waste, plus one more: the eighth waste, the under-use of people. The seven forms of waste are shown below.

![The seven wastes](/beyondlean/blog/the-seven-wastes/seven-wastes.jpg)

## 1. Overproduction

- Producing sooner, faster or in greater quantities than the customer actually demands.
- Making too much, too early or "just in case".
- Overproduction gets in the way of a smooth flow of goods or services.
- It takes the focus away from what the customer really wants.
- It leads to excess inventory.

Caused by:

- MRP push rather than kanban pull
- large batch sizes
- the feeling that it looks better to be busy
- poor use of people
- lack of customer focus.

Why it is a waste:

- it costs money
- it uses resources ahead of plan
- it creates inventory
- it hides inventory and defect problems
- it takes up space.

## 2. Inventory

Any raw material, work in progress (WIP) or finished goods which are not having value added to them.

Caused by:

- a production schedule that isn't level
- inaccurate forecasting
- too much downtime or set-up time
- push instead of pull
- large batches
- unreliable suppliers.

Why it is a waste:

- it adds cost
- it needs extra storage space
- it needs extra resource to manage
- it hides shortages and defects
- it can become damaged
- its shelf life can expire.

## 3. Motion

Motion is the movement of people. Wasted motion happens when people move more than the process needs them to. It adds cost.

Caused by:

- no standard operating procedure
- poor housekeeping
- a badly designed cell
- inadequate training.

Why it is a waste:

- it interrupts the flow of production
- it increases production time
- it can cause injury.

## 4. Waiting

People or parts that wait for a work cycle to be completed. Ask:

- Where are the bottlenecks?
- What are the major causes of lost machine availability?
- What are we doing to improve machine availability?
- Do people wait on machinery?

Caused by:

- shortages and an unreliable supply chain
- lack of multi-skilling and flexibility
- downtime and breakdowns
- ineffective production planning
- quality, design and engineering issues
- "black art" processes.

Why it is a waste:

- stop-start production
- poor workflow continuity
- it causes bottlenecks
- long lead times
- failed delivery dates.

## 5. Transportation

Unnecessary movement of parts between processes:

- complex material flow paths
- processes that are not closely coupled
- wasted floor space
- unnecessary material handling
- potential damage to products.

Caused by:

- a badly designed process or cell
- poor value stream flow
- complex material flows
- sharing of equipment.

Why it is a waste:

- it increases production time
- it uses resources and floor space
- poor communication
- it increases work in progress
- potential damage to products.

## 6. Over-processing

Processing beyond the standard the customer requires. By processing more efficiently, we use less resource to achieve the same customer satisfaction.

Caused by:

- out-of-date standards
- the attitude "we've always done it like this"
- not understanding the process
- lack of innovation and improvement
- lack of standard operating procedures.

Why it is a waste:

- it uses resources
- it increases production time
- it's work above and beyond the specification
- it can reduce the life of a component.

## 7. Not right first time (scrap, rework and defects)

A defect is anything the customer would judge as not good enough to pass the quality standard.

- Defects reduce customer satisfaction.
- Defects have to be put right.
- Putting them right costs time, effort and materials.
- Defects in the field will lose customers.
- Right first time is the key.

Caused by:

- processes that are out of control or not capable
- lack of skill, training and on-the-job support
- inaccurate design and engineering
- machine inaccuracy
- "black art" processes.

Why it is a waste:

- it adds cost
- it interrupts the schedule
- it uses resources
- it creates paperwork
- it reduces customer confidence.

## The eighth waste

The under-use of people. People are your greatest asset: learn to get the best out of them. Two questions to ask:

- How could we better involve people in continuous improvement?
- How could we make better use of people's time?
