Yes, you heard me right: there is a 9th waste. We all know the 8th waste is the under-use of our people. But what about the 9th waste: the under-use of our customers?

An underestimated factor in every Lean Six Sigma deployment is the talent we could tap by working together, and that isn't just our staff, as in the 8th waste. We often get so busy with what each of us is doing that we lose sight of how much more powerful we could be if we combined our efforts.

In my opinion, herein lies the secret of Lean Six Sigma: give everyone, including your customers, the chance to take part and share in the experience, for maximum results.

Ask yourself how you can make the most of people's time and effort in improvement, and how it could benefit your business. Your resources, employees and customers are all important assets. How are you using them to become a more agile and cost-effective business?

Always start by asking, "What can I do?" This personal, hands-on approach can truly make a difference in any business.

## Listen to your customers

Learn what not to do from the mistakes, discoveries and insights of others. This matters most of all with your customers. Listen to their complaints. Survey them regularly to find out which of your current products they would like more of, or new versions of, and what products you don't offer yet that they would like to see in your catalogue. They are the people who pay all our wages, and without them the business would not exist.

## Expect some resistance

Be aware that not everyone will share your enthusiasm. Some people may dread what it will do to their workload and their world. Some resistance to any change is normal. Showing the value, or "what's in it for me", is a very important part of the whole effort.
