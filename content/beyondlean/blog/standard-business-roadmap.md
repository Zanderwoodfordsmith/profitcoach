Improvement models come in many forms. The difference with this one is that it is tried, tested and proven to work. Not by academics, or by people who have studied Lean systems at university, but by people who have done it at every level of organisations. It is used by change agents, managers and consultants on the front line.

The acid test for any business improvement system is: "Does it work?"

This system is no different. Results are today's business currency. Without results, no system is worth the paper it's written on.

This system isn't based on business improvement theory or strategic waffle. It's based on hard-won experience, and it was developed by people who have used the tools in it to get measurable results, time after time.

Follow the process step by step, in the order it is laid out, and you will get results.

It can be used anywhere, from a single department or small business right up to a major corporation.

Theories and complex business models may look impressive in boardroom presentations, but only action gets results.

## Five phases

A highly structured five-phase approach for major business improvement. Standard inputs produce standard outputs.

1. **Diagnostic:** get a clear picture of where you are now.
2. **Stability:** get control, consolidate and put in a solid foundation to build from.
3. **Strategy:** get a clear vision of the direction you want to go, and put the plans in place to get there.
4. **Action:** don't worry about getting it right, just get it going.
5. **Evaluation:** measure your success so far and create the next future state.

![The Standard Business Roadmap: diagnostic, stability, strategy, action and evaluation, repeated in cycles](/beyondlean/blog/standard-business-roadmap/roadmap-five-phases.jpg)

Every major department in the company has a role to play as you move through each phase.

**Diagnostic.** In the diagnostic phase, a thorough business evaluation takes place, including business assessments, value stream mapping and cultural assessments, to give you a clear picture of the current state of your business.

**Stability.** Once you have a clear picture of where you stand, the recommendations from the business assessment make any gaps in the basics clearly visible. Fixing these in the recommended way gives you the stability of a solid foundation to drive forward from. [Read more about the Stability phase.](/beyondlean/blog/stability-first)

**Strategy.** Once you have an accurate current state and a stable foundation, you set the vision and goals, along with realistic plans for exactly how you are going to get there. [Read more about the Strategy phase.](/beyondlean/blog/business-strategy)

**Action.** Time for action. Using whichever Lean, or other, improvement tools suit your situation, drive the changes forward. In this phase it is vitally important not to neglect the cultural side of your business. This is where the [Gateway](/beyondlean/blog/gateway-partnership) step comes in. You may also decide to bring in a cultural change expert at this point, which will speed up your change. [Read more about the Action phase.](/beyondlean/blog/kaizen)

**Evaluation.** Finally, measure your success, consolidate your position and carry on to the next stage of the cycle.

## Evaluation: today's improvement is tomorrow's standard

To see clearly the results you've achieved so far, now is the time to repeat the business assessment, the cultural audit and the current state mapping, so you can compare the results with the first diagnostic and measure exactly how much has improved.

Celebrate your success, but don't rest on your laurels.

This is a cycle. Although this is the end of the first cycle, it is also the start of the second.

Any improvement process is a continuous journey. If you stand still, you are effectively moving backwards, because you can guarantee your competitors will be moving forward. The only way to keep succeeding is to improve faster than your competitors.

Celebrate your results, consolidate them and look at how you can improve them further.

Today's improvement is tomorrow's standard, and the day after's regression.

## Each cycle builds on the last

The timescales are meant as a guide only. With each cycle, you will notice the "noise" of running the business growing less.
