This is a business diagnostic of the packing area at a snack food manufacturer. It shows what we found, what we recommended and the size of the prize. The money figures are the diagnostic's estimates, not results.

## Strengths

- A strong focus on delivery, and a good organisational structure.
- A strong desire, right through the organisation, to give the best service.
- Demand forecasting and high-level business planning in place.
- Recent improvement work had already paid off (extra baskets for changeovers).
- Middle managers visible in the operation.

## Key issues

- Packing machine use was extremely low in most packing areas.
- There were no standard ways of working. The packing process varied from line to line.
- There was no sign of operational communication or visual management.
- Data on what the machines could do existed, but not in a usable form.
- Work flow was out of balance across the packing process.
- People had little awareness of how productive they were.
- There was no sign of team or individual performance management.

## Where the time went

When a crisps line was running, it ran faster than its target. Lines 5 and 6 had a target run rate of 75.5 and ran at 80 while running. But their actual run rate across the day was only 46.9, because the machines weren't running for much of the time.

Across a day, the lines were productive for only part of the time available:

- Crisps lines 5 and 6: 62% (893 of 1,440 minutes)
- Crisps lines 1 to 4: 43% (616 of 1,440 minutes)
- Savouries lines 7 to 13: 33% (158.5 of 480 minutes)

![Crisps lines 1 to 4: productive time was only 43% of the time available](/beyondlean/blog/case-study-snack-manufacturer/lines-1-4-time-breakdown.jpg)

The lost time fell into two kinds. Set-up time is a reducible loss: quick changeover methods can cut it, but the opportunity is limited. Idle and waiting time are avoidable losses that can be removed, and a large share of them were planned lunch and break times, when the lines simply stopped.

Target run rates were also set at around 80% of the machine maker's run rate, even though some products already ran faster than the maker's rate.

## Reorganising the work

On the crisps lines, each machine operator ran two machines but wasn't fully used, and the stackers who put boxes onto pallets were under-used too. Having one stacker cover two lines would free up 2 FTE per shift across lines 1 to 4.

On the savouries lines, each operator ran just one machine. Running two machines per operator, using a smaller table so packers could push boxes straight through the tape and label machine, and having stackers cover two machines would free up 7 FTE. The main risk was resistance from staff who had worked there a long time.

The freed-up people would form a "roving team" on each shift to cover all breaks, so the lines keep running. That would lift productive time towards an initial target of 70%, rising to 75% within six months. The crisps area would need two extra staff per shift to make it work.

## The size of the prize

Assuming 210 productive days a year and 5p gross profit per bag (to be confirmed by the client), the diagnostic estimated:

- Crisps area: £846,720 a year, less £80,000 for four extra staff, giving £766,720.
- Savouries area: £717,948 a year, less £60,000 for three extra staff, giving £657,948.
- **Total opportunity across all lines: £1,484,668 a year.**

On lines 5 and 6 alone, each extra 1% of productive time is worth 1,152 bags a day per line.

## What we recommended

**Short term (1 to 3 months):**

1. Develop and put in place operational KPIs at every level: team and supervisor, operations manager, operations director and chief executive. If you can't measure it, you can't manage it. If you can't manage it, you can't improve it.
2. Put in visual management for all production areas, linked to those KPIs. This is the key to making any improvement last.
3. Start a five-minute daily team talk at the start of each shift, linked to the visual management, so everyone knows what is expected of them, what a good day looks like and how we did yesterday.
4. Write standard operating procedures for the packing set-up on each line and product, and train all staff in them. This is essential to move the target run rate from 80% to 100% of the maker's rate.
5. Write standard operating procedures for changeovers on all lines, using quick changeover methods. Every minute saved is an extra 50 to 80 bags per line.
6. Set up roving teams on all shifts, using the freed-up capacity plus some investment in staff, to drive towards the initial target of 70% productive time.

**Medium term (6 to 12 months):**

1. Set clear roles and responsibilities at every level, linked to the operational KPIs, so every staff member and manager knows what is and isn't part of their role, and what they are responsible and accountable for.
2. Train all managers and supervisors in operational management: management routines, team communication, understanding and using the figures, managing and motivating people, managing the day's work flow, and problems and countermeasures.

**The KPIs we recommended for production:**

- Effective run rate against the maker's target run rate
- Line production percentage
- Idle time
- Adherence to plan
- Quality

The diagnostic ended with a dated plan, with an owner for every milestone, covering KPIs, visual management, daily team talks, standard set-ups and changeovers, roving teams, and roles and management training.
