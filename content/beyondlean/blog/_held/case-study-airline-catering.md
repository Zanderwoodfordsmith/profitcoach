This project took place in the Club meals area of an airline catering company. The target was clear: bring the headcount in Club meals down from 57 full-time equivalents (FTE) to 45, an annual saving of £216,000.

## What we found

We started by collecting data and mapping how the work really flowed, from the stock check in the Club fridge and laying up the tables, through making the meals, to taking the finished liners to the right area.

- On average, only 63% of people's time was adding value.
- 25 minutes were lost at every shift start-up or changeover.

A cause-and-effect (fishbone) diagram on "why is the Club area inefficient?" brought out the causes. Among them:

- **Material:** shortages of food, and late food from production.
- **Machine:** damaged or dirty equipment, and damaged plates and bowls.
- **People:** staff capability, discipline, flexibility and new staff.
- **Measurement:** no times given, no targets and a poor rota.
- **Method:** changeover times, flight changes, communication, the movement of liners and flight labels, no organisation and a lack of control.

## Choosing what to fix first

We scored each improvement idea from 1 to 5 for ease, cost and impact, and multiplied the scores together to rank them. The top three were:

1. Improve the porter feed (score 100).
2. Split the 1st and 2nd choice tables (score 80).
3. Move cheese and dessert into the 2nd meal (score 50).

We also ran a failure mode and effects analysis (FMEA) on the process, to tackle the highest risks first. The two biggest were food not arriving on time because of batching in production, stores, the Club area and the bakery, and flight labels printed 24 hours ahead, which missed any later increases and top-ups.

## What changed

![Before: 18 people per shift, 57 FTE in total](/beyondlean/blog/case-study-airline-catering/layout-before.jpg)

- A job description for the porter and feeder, with clear roles and responsibilities, was written and issued.
- Flow was put in place for the main meal, 1st and 2nd choice.
- The chef's salad and Club side salad were moved, and their layout redesigned so the work flows.
- Workstations were set up in the order the meal is made, with a "golden sample" to copy and the specs on display. Cheese and dessert went from batches of 20 bowls to batches of 4, packed straight into the liner, and the chef's salad workstation was set up for batches of 10.
- The packer was removed from cheese and desserts, which became a "make and pack" job.
- A manpower calculator, based on passenger numbers and current run rates, was issued to the production managers for trial. All meals were re-timed, and the calculator updated when the menu changed.
- Table schedules were written for all make tables.
- Performance measures were designed for the Club area.
- Standard work videos were made for every Club "make" job, showing the layout and best practice needed to hit the cycle times.

![After: 13 to 14 people per shift, one-piece flow and freed-up space](/beyondlean/blog/case-study-airline-catering/layout-after.jpg)

## The results

- Non-value-added time on the main meal (1st and 2nd choice) fell from 38% to 16%, thanks to flow and clear porter roles.
- Chef's salad cycle time fell from 97 seconds to 30 seconds.
- Cheese and dessert became a make-and-pack process, saving two shifts a day.
- Floor space used fell from 156 m² to 120 m², a 23% reduction.
- Headcount fell from 57 FTE to 40 FTE, and from 18 people per shift to 13 or 14.

Total annual saving: 17 FTE at £18,000 a year each, which is **£306,000 a year**, against a target of £216,000.
