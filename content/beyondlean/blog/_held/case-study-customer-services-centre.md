This is a business diagnostic we carried out in the customer services centre of a housing association, and what was delivered as a result.

## How we measured it

We used our Operations Excellence Assessment. It is a tool to help you understand the strengths and weaknesses of a service operation, and to judge whether it has the building blocks of an excellent service operation in place. Each area is scored as basic, competitive or leading:

- Customer and supplier awareness
- Capacity, skills management and work organisation
- Operational management and controls
- Team leader role, training and development
- People management and communication
- Process management and improvement
- Technology and workplace environment

At the start, nearly every area scored in the "basic" band.

## What we found

- **No standard ways of working.** Performance varied widely from one adviser to the next.
- **The wrong main measure.** Average handling time (AHT) was the main measure for advisers, and there was no true first-contact resolution measure. AHT drove the wrong behaviours and worked against the customer experience. Its targets were arbitrary rather than accurate. At one point, 63 people were above the AHT target.
- **No team targets.** Teams had no targets, only individual targets, reviewed monthly. Quality checks were seen as a tick-box exercise, the "killer questions" weren't linked to customer outcomes, and the feedback loop was too long.
- **Team leaders with no time to lead.** Team leaders spent very little time actually managing their teams. Their measures focused on admin and resourcing tasks, not customer outcomes. They sat away from their teams, weren't always on the same shifts, and did no call monitoring or coaching. They weren't measured on their team's performance, and there was no structured training to help new team leaders make the move into the role.
- **Briefings nobody read.** Operational briefings went out by email, with no check that people understood them. Roughly 7% were effective. The monthly team meeting was the only face-to-face communication, so an adviser coming back from holiday or sickness, with calls queueing, had no time to catch up on emailed briefings.
- **No visual management at team level.** There was a department-level board, but staff didn't refer to it.
- **Out-of-date processes.** Process documents were out of date and had gaps.
- **Low morale.** Staff turnover of 21% over the last 12 months suggested low morale.
- **Blurred responsibilities.** There was no clear line between capacity planning and the centre's own responsibilities.

Here is what this costs. A new self-serve system went live, but one adviser hadn't had time to read the briefing, so didn't know how to reply to a system-generated message. The email went back round the system instead of to the customer, which would probably have led to a repeat call.

## What was delivered

- Standard ways of working, developed and rolled out across the centre, focused on resolving the customer's query first time.
- Operational KPIs redefined and rolled out, focused on the customer experience and driving the right behaviours.
- A "structured day" for team leaders, with coaching to match. Team leaders now sit with their teams and do the call monitoring and coaching themselves.
- Quality, productivity and delivery measures for every team and individual, linked to customer outcomes.
- Capacity planning and resourcing work moved back from the team leaders to the resourcing team.
- Operational communication and visual management designed and put in place across all teams, at every location.

## Before and after

We repeated the assessment five months later, in August. Most areas had moved up out of the "basic" band and into "competitive".
