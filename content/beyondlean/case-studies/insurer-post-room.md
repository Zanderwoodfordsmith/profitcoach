## At a glance

- **10% to 15%** of post went to the wrong team
- **About 60%** reached teams unopened
- **20.2 seconds** to sort a letter with an unclear address, against 5.8 for a clear one
- **74 hours** a week of savings found, about two people's time

## The problem

Every letter to the insurer passed through its post room. Staff there spent about 33 hours a week just reading letters to work out who they were for. Even so, 10% to 15% reached the wrong team, and about 60% arrived unopened. Every area handled its post its own way, and policy teams each spent 1.5 to 2.5 hours a day sorting and passing it on.

Scanning had problems too. Teams believed it was slow, up to three weeks by their account. Each department prepared documents for it differently, and some held work back until a tray was full.

## What we did

We mapped how post and scanning worked, timed each step and marked the waste. Then we proposed two rounds of fixes.

In the first 6 to 8 weeks: open every letter in the post room before sorting, sort in two stages (floor first, then team), put a standard reference on all outgoing letters, and agree one way to prepare documents for scanning.

Over 3 to 12 months: give each team its own reply envelopes and address, add team addresses to forms and the website, and collect scanning work from teams on a regular round.

## What changed

The review found savings of about 74 hours a week across the post room, the policy teams and scanning. That is about two full-time people.

## What an owner can take from this

Look at where work comes into your business: post, email, phone and web forms. If it lands in one general pile and someone has to read it to send it on, you pay for that every day. Ask customers to quote a reference and give each team its own address or inbox. Then the work arrives in the right place first time.
