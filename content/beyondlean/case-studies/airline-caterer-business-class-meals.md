## At a glance

- **57 to 40** people for the same meals, against a target of 45
- **£306,000** a year saved, against a target of £216,000
- **38% to 16%** wasted effort on the main meal lines
- **23% less** floor space, from 156 m² to 120 m²

## The problem

The caterer made business-class meals for airlines in its own part of the kitchen. That area had 57 people, and the budget allowed 45.

We timed the work. On the main meal lines, 38% of the effort added nothing for the customer: waiting, walking, fetching and sorting. Each shift lost 25 minutes just getting going. Food came late from other parts of the kitchen, the porters who stocked the tables had no clear job, and nobody had set targets or timings.

## What we did

We scored every idea on how easy, cheap and useful it was, and started with the best. Better supply from the porters came top.

- A clear job for the porters, so the tables stopped running short.
- Main meals made one at a time along the table, instead of in big batches.
- New layouts for the salad, cheese and dessert tables, each with a model tray to copy and smaller batches: 4 bowls at a time instead of 20 for cheese and dessert.
- Cheese and dessert made and packed in one go, with no separate packer.
- A simple calculator that set staff numbers from passenger numbers.
- Timings for every meal, schedules for every table, performance measures and short films of the best way to make each item.

## What changed

The same output took 40 people instead of 57. At £18,000 a head, that saved £306,000 a year. Wasted effort on the main meal lines fell from 38% to 16%, and a chef's salad took 30 seconds instead of 97. Cheese and dessert needed two fewer shifts a day, and the work fitted into almost a quarter less floor space.

## What an owner can take from this

Time the work before you ask anyone to work harder. Fix the supply to the people doing the job first, because a person waiting for stock is a person you pay to stand still. Smaller batches and one clear example of good work make the job quicker and easier to teach. Let staffing follow the work, whether that is orders, covers or passengers, and you stop paying for quiet hours.
