## At a glance

- **57 to 40** people in the meal area, against a target of 45
- **£306,000** a year saved, against a target of £216,000
- **38% to 16%** wasted effort on the main meal lines
- **23% less** floor space, from 156 m² to 120 m²

## The problem

Paul was Continuous Improvement Project Manager for the UK and Ireland at a global airline caterer, which made meals for flights. One of its meal areas had 57 people, and the target was 45, in line with the budget.

Timing the work showed that, on average, only 63% of effort added value. On the main meal lines, 38% added nothing. Each shift lost 25 minutes at start-up or changeover. A risk table put food not arriving on time at the top. Other likely causes included no dedicated porters, no targets and no set times for each job.

## What we did

The project scored seven ideas on how easy, cheap and useful each was. Better supply from the porters came top. Then it made these changes:

- A written job description for the porters who keep the tables supplied.
- Main meals made one at a time, in a flow.
- New layouts for the salad, cheese and dessert tables, each with a finished example to copy and smaller batches: 4 bowls at a time instead of 20 for cheese and dessert, and 10 instead of 60 for the chef's salad.
- Cheese and dessert made and packed in one go, with no separate packer.
- A staffing calculator, based on passenger numbers, given to the production managers to trial.
- Timings for every meal, schedules for every table, performance measures and videos of the best way to do each job.

## What changed

Headcount in the area fell from 57 to 40. At £18,000 a head, that saved £306,000 a year. Wasted effort on the main meal lines fell from 38% to 16%, and a chef's salad took 30 seconds instead of 97. Cheese and dessert needed two fewer shifts a day, and the area used almost a quarter less floor space.

## What an owner can take from this

Time the work before you ask anyone to work harder. Fix the supply to the people doing the job first, because a person waiting for stock is a person you pay to stand still. Smaller batches and one clear example of good work make the job quicker and easier to teach. Let staffing follow the work, whether that is orders, covers or passengers, and you stop paying for quiet hours.
