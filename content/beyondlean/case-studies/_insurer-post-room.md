- **74 hours** a week of savings found

## The problem

Post arrived at the insurer's post room, where staff had to read unclear letters to work out who they were for. That took about 33 hours a week. Even so, 10% to 15% of post reached a team it wasn't meant for, and about 60% arrived unopened. Every area handled its post its own way, and policy teams each spent 1.5 to 2.5 hours a day sorting and passing it on.

Scanning had problems too. Teams believed it was slow, up to three weeks by their account. Each department prepared documents for it differently, and teams held work back until a tray was full.

## What we did

The review mapped how post and scanning worked and measured the time spent sorting, opening and re-routing post. It proposed two rounds of changes.

In the first 6 to 8 weeks: open every letter in the post room before sorting, sort in two stages (floor or department first, then team), put a standard reference on all outgoing letters, and agree one way to prepare documents for scanning.

Over 3 to 12 months: give each team its own reply envelopes, put team addresses on forms, and add department addresses to the website.

For scanning, it also proposed a regular round to collect work from teams.

## What changed

The review found savings of about 74 hours a week across the post room, the policy teams and scanning.

## What an owner can take from this

Look at where work comes into your business: post, email, phone and web forms. If it lands in one general pile and someone has to read it to send it on, you pay for that every day. Ask customers to quote a reference and give each team its own address or inbox. Then the work arrives in the right place first time.
