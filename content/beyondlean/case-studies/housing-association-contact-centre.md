## At a glance

- **2 to 12** of 16 areas at competitive level, from March to August
- **63 of 136** advisers over the call-time target before the work
- **21%** of staff had left in the previous 12 months
- **About 7%** effective: the rating for briefings sent by email

## The problem

The centre handled customers' calls and emails. We scored it on 16 areas of good operations, from basic through competitive to leading. In March, 14 of the 16 scored basic.

Advisers were judged mainly on how long their calls took. That drove the wrong behaviour and worked against the customer's experience, and nothing measured whether a problem was fixed first time. Of 136 advisers, 63 were over the call-time target, and the wide spread showed people doing the same job in different ways.

Team leaders spent very little time managing their teams, and their own targets focused on admin and staffing tasks. They sat away from their teams, were not always on the same shifts, and did no call listening or coaching, which a separate coaching team handled. Briefings went out by email with no check that anyone understood them. The only face-to-face team meeting was monthly, and a fifth of staff had left in a year.

## What we did

- One standard way of working, built around fixing the customer's problem first time.
- New measures of quality, output and delivery for every team and person, linked to what customers get.
- A "structured day" for team leaders, with coaching to run it. Leaders moved to sit with their teams and took over call listening and coaching.
- Staff planning went back from the team leaders to the resourcing team.
- Team communication and visual boards set up in every team, at every site.

## What changed

We ran the same assessment again in August. Twelve of the 16 areas now scored competitive, against 2 in March.

## What an owner can take from this

People chase what you measure. Measure only speed and you get quick calls and repeat calls, so measure whether the customer's problem got fixed. Your team leaders, or you, need time with the team every day to listen and coach. If rotas and admin eat that time, hand them to someone else. And brief people face to face, then check they understood. Here, email briefings were rated only about 7% effective.
