## At a glance

- **26 hours** a week of reporting in a team that earned about half its unit's profit
- **3 teams** each building their own version of the same daily report
- **74%** of the steps in one report added nothing
- **£1,022,022** a year found across three changes
- **1 week** for the first change to start adding profit

## The problem

The trading team's job was to make money from trading and hedging, and it earned about half the profit of its whole unit. Yet it spent about 26 hours a week, almost one full-time person, building reports.

Three teams built the same daily position report from the same data, each with its own home-made tools, and then checked them against each other. Three teams also built the daily profit report. One worked from the previous day's closing position and the other two from the opening position, so they met every day to sort out the differences.

A daily and weekly market report had also followed the person who first ran it into the trading team, though it belonged with market analysis. It took the trading team 6 hours a week, and 74% of the steps needed to produce it added nothing.

## What we did

The review mapped how the market report was made, step by step, and marked which steps added value. Then it recommended three moves:

- Put each report with the team whose job it is. The market report would go back to market analysis straight away, and the position and profit reports would go to the one team set up to produce them.
- Agree one version of the truth for the position and profit numbers.
- Simplify and automate what is left. The market report could drop from 6 hours a week to 1.5.

## What changed

These are the savings the review found. Moving the market report was worth £403,768 a year, starting within a week, plus £12,011 once it was automated. One team owning the position report was worth £121,448, and the profit report £484,795. That is £1,022,022 a year, mostly extra trading profit from giving the traders their time back. The team's senior manager put the wider prize, if all its reporting moved out, at £2m more profit, as a cautious estimate.

## What an owner can take from this

Look at who in your business earns the money: the salespeople, the engineers, you. Count the hours they spend on reports, admin and checking numbers. Every hour moved off their desk can go back into earning. And if two people keep the same spreadsheet, pick one and trust it.
