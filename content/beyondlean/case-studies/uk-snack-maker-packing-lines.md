## At a glance

- **33% to 62%** of the day spent actually packing, depending on the line
- **70%** packing time as the first target, then 75% within six months
- **About £1.4m** a year in extra profit found, after paying for extra staff

## The problem

The business made crisps and savoury snacks on 13 packing lines. The machines ran at or near target speed, but they didn't run much. A day's data from the machines showed lines 5 and 6 packing for 62% of the day, lines 1 to 4 for 43%, and the seven savoury lines for just 33%. Much of the idle time was planned breaks, when whole lines stopped.

Each line packed in its own way, nobody could see how a shift was going, and some people were stretched while others waited. Targets sat at about 80% of the speed the machine maker quoted.

## What we did

We mapped who did what on every line. One stacker could serve two lines and one operator could run two machines, which would free 2 people a shift on crisps and 7 on savouries. With a few extra hires, they would form roving teams that cover breaks, so the lines keep running.

We also recommended:

- a few key numbers for every level, shown on boards on the floor
- a five-minute team huddle at the start of every shift
- one written way to set up each line for each product
- faster changeovers, where every minute saved means 50 to 80 more bags per line

We turned it all into a dated plan for January to September 2019, and named the main risk: long-serving staff resisting the change.

## What changed

These are the savings the review found. Lifting the lines to 70% packing time was worth about £767,000 a year on crisps and £658,000 on savouries, after paying for the extra staff. Together, that is about £1.4m a year in extra profit. The sums assume 5p profit per bag, which the business still had to confirm.

## What an owner can take from this

If your machines log running time and stoppages, read that before you buy another machine or add a shift. A line that stops for every break loses money at every break, and a small team covering breaks keeps it earning. And set targets from what the machine can really do, with your most experienced people in the room.
