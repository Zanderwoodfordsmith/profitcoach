## At a glance

- **33% to 62%** of the time available spent packing, depending on the line
- **70%** packing time as the first target, then 75% within six months
- **About £1.4m** a year in extra profit found, after paying for extra staff

## The problem

The business packed crisps and savoury snacks on 13 lines. When the machines ran, they ran at or near target speed, but machine use was very low in most areas. A day's machine data showed lines 5 and 6 packing for 62% of the time available, lines 1 to 4 for 43%, and the seven savoury lines for just 33%. A large part of the lost time was planned lunch and other breaks.

Each line packed in its own way, with no boards or team updates to show how a shift was going. The work was unevenly spread, and stackers and machine operators had spare time. Targets sat at about 80% of the speed the machine maker quoted.

## What we did

We mapped who did what on every line. In the crisps area, one stacker could serve two lines, freeing 2 people a shift. In the savoury area, one operator could run two machines and one stacker could cover two, freeing 7 people. Those people, plus a few extra hires, would form roving teams on each shift to cover breaks, so the lines keep running.

We also recommended:

- key numbers for every level, shown on boards in every production area
- a five-minute team huddle at the start of every shift
- one written way to set up each line for each product
- faster changeovers, where every minute saved means 50 to 80 more bags per line

We turned it all into a dated plan for January to September 2019. For the savoury lines, we noted one risk: staff who had worked there a long time might resist the change.

## What changed

These are the savings the review found. Lifting the lines to 70% packing time was worth about £767,000 a year on crisps and £658,000 on savouries, after paying for the extra staff. Together, that is about £1.4m a year in extra profit. The sums assume 5p profit per bag, which was still to be confirmed.

## What an owner can take from this

If your machines log running time and stoppages, read that before you buy another machine or add a shift. A line that stops for every break loses money at every break, and a small team covering breaks keeps it earning. And set targets from what the machine can really do, with your most experienced people in the room.
