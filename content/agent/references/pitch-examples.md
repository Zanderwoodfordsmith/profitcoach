# Pitch patterns (not scripts to recite)

Use these when the Golden Thread matches. Copy the shape, then rewrite from this prospect's notes. If you could read a paragraph unchanged to a different owner, it is still the example, not their pitch.

## 1. More customers

**Thread:** a predictable way of attracting and winning more of the right customers.

1. Why acquisition is inconsistent: market, offer, message, leads, follow-up or conversion. A starting point, not more marketing and hope.
2. Strategy around the customers they actually want, and the volume needed for the goal.
3. A 90-day plan for the acquisition work, with owners and measures, so it does not stop when they get busy.
4. Acquisition cost, margin and customer value, so they do not grow turnover without profit.
5. The engine: targeting, messaging, leads, sales process, follow-up, conversion, repeat.
6. Delivery, capacity and the customer journey, so extra work does not create chaos.
7. Leads, conversion, cost of acquisition, average value, pipeline, repeat.
8. Review what is working, test, improve the offer, someone actually manages it.
9. Who owns marketing, follow-up, sales, pipeline and retention, so it is not only the owner.

**Outcome:** a more predictable, profitable way of winning customers.

## 2. Off the tools

**Thread:** less doing, more leading. The business runs without them in every job.

1. Where the business still depends on them: tasks, decisions, customer issues, operational fires.
2. What their role should look like. Not random delegation. Redesign the job.
3. A 90-day handover: which work moves, which systems, which people first.
4. Whether the margins and cash can fund the capacity that replaces their time.
5. Freed time goes into better customers and the pipeline, not only a quieter week.
6. Processes, handovers, standards and job ownership, so work does not need their head.
7. Numbers that show jobs and margins are on track, so they manage from information, not by checking.
8. Managers who decide, solve and review, instead of escalating everything.
9. Roles, decisions, accountability and leadership, so the business depends on structure, not one person.

**Outcome:** less time on the tools. More time leading and owning the business.

## 3. Busy but not enough profit

**Thread:** more profit from the effort already going in.

1. Where profit leaks: customers, products, jobs or activities that consume time without return.
2. Strategy around profitable growth, not turnover for its own sake.
3. A 90-day plan on pricing, margin, efficiency, cost and revenue quality.
4. Margins, pricing, cash, costs, overheads, working capital.
5. Better customers, higher-value work, pricing, average sale, repeat.
6. Waste, rework, delays, overtime, poor handovers. Protect the margin on every job.
7. Gross margin, labour efficiency, cost per job, conversion, average sale, productivity. Early, not at year end.
8. Offers, ways of working, cost ideas, management disciplines that keep improving profit.
9. The team sees its impact on margin, waste, productivity and cost. Profit is not only the owner's job.

**Outcome:** more profit from the work, customers and resources they already have.

## 4. Better systems

**Thread:** a business that runs without everything living in people's heads.

1. Where missing systems hurt most: stuck work, mistakes, lost information, the owner dragged in.
2. Systems for the business they are building, not a write-up of today's mess.
3. A 90-day implementation plan with owners, so it is not "we should" forever.
4. Waste, duplication, errors, rework, overtime, missed billing. Weak systems cost money.
5. Repeatable ways to attract, follow up and convert, so new work is not memory and chasing.
6. How work moves, handovers, quality, how customers are served.
7. Measures on whether the processes are working. Documented is not the same as managed.
8. Managers review, fix and update processes as the business changes.
9. People follow and own the systems. Standards and accountability, not a folder nobody opens.

**Outcome:** consistent because the systems are clear, measured and owned.

## 5. The team will not step up

**Thread:** a capable, accountable team that can run more of the business.

1. Why they are falling short: unclear roles, weak standards, communication, training, weak management, or the owner stepping in too fast.
2. The structure the business actually needs as it grows.
3. A 90-day people plan: what transfers, who develops, which expectations tighten first.
4. How poor accountability, mistakes or over-dependence hit profit and cash.
5. Everyone's role in winning and keeping customers, not only the owner's.
6. Clear processes and standards, so people stop asking what to do.
7. Objective measures of good, so conversations are not opinion.
8. Managers who review, coach, solve and improve, so fewer issues reach the owner.
9. Expectations, decisions, leadership and ownership. People take responsibility for results.

**Outcome:** the owner leads. The team owns the work.

## The lesson

Same step, different so-what.

- Profit and Cash might mean "can we afford to get you off the tools?"
- Or "are the customers we are winning actually profitable?"
- Or "where are weak systems leaking margin?"

Do not describe the roadmap. Translate it into the problem they told you.
