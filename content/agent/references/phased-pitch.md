# Phased pitch: what happens when

The second way to pitch. The nine-step pitch shows what we fix. This one shows what happens after they say yes, phase by phase, and what is left for them to do. It comes from the BCA Trusted Advisor pitch (October 2026), turned round for a coach selling to an owner.

Use it when the owner wants to know how it works, what they get and when, has been burned before, or is cautious. It is also the second pitch on a follow-up call, so the coach is not repeating the nine steps.

Same five facts. Same Golden Thread. Every phase is tied to their problem.

## The shape

It follows the BCA Trusted Advisor pitch closely, part for part, at about three quarters of its length: around 1,000 spoken words. The ready-made versions in the app (src/lib/salesCall/script.ts, phasedPitch) are the pattern. A personalised one keeps that shape and length and makes every line theirs.

**Bridge.** "You know how you said [their problem] ..." and what it is costing them. "So here's what we're going to do." The Profit System in their business, and the coach as their trusted advisor: someone in their corner every month who helps them see clearly, decide better and follow through.

**Their part.** "And the part that matters most is this." We do the thinking, the planning and the follow-up together, which leaves three things only the owner can do:

1. Turn up and be honest about what is really going on
2. Make the decisions
3. Do what we agree, or get it done through the team

Then: "My job is to make sure you always know the next move, and that it happens." Say it once in the bridge and once in the recap, never more. Close the bridge with how this takes them from where they are to their result, the three phases in one line (we get clear, we fix what matters, then we make it last), and "Let me take you through each one."

**About you (optional).** Two or three sentences on who they would be working with, only from the coach's real record. Use it when the owner wants to know.

Each phase has the same build: set the scene, say what the phase is and when, tie it to their words ("You know how you said ..."), three or four points (what we do and why it matters to them), their part or the time it takes, then "So what that means for you is ...".

**Phase 1. Get clear (the first month).** When you're running a business every day it's hard to step back and see what's really going on. That's nobody's fault, so never blame the owner. We score the business across all nine areas and find what is really causing their problem (the obvious problem is not always the real one), agree where the business is going, and build a 90-day plan: what, who, when, and how we will know. We go after their problem from the first or second session, so they feel a difference straight away. Built together, around their business. Which means they stop guessing, have a plan, and are already working on their problem.

**Phase 2. Fix what matters (months two and three).** They've already started on their problem. Now we finish the job: fix what causes it so it stays fixed, and work the rest of the plan. Quick wins first, often profit and cash. A weekly scoreboard: a few numbers the team fills in and they read in under a minute. Every session: what got done, what is stuck, what is next. Why it works: a few things done properly, in the right order, and the coach makes sure they happen. Their part and their time. Which means things actually change. If the coach has named when a first result shows, say it here.

**Phase 3. Make it last (from month four).** Open with the result they want and why it matters to them. Every 90 days we score the business again and set the next plan. We work on the numbers, the systems and the team, so the business does not depend on them for everything. The shift: they lead it, the team runs it, the coach advises. A few hours a month of their time. Which means the result sticks.

**Check-in after each phase.** One short question, not two or three. "Does that make sense so far?" "Can you see how that takes [their worry] off you?" "Does all of that make sense?"

**Support.** From day one they are not on their own: how they reach the coach between sessions, and their scores, plan and scoreboard always to hand.

**Recap.** "So, to recap." One line per phase, the coach with them the whole way, their part and the coach's job, then tie it back: from their problem to their result, so they don't have to deal with what it is costing them again.

## Rules

- The coach advises; it is not done for you. We work things out together, and the owner and their team do the work. Keep it calm and easy to take in, never a wall of promises.

- Session rhythm: use the coach's own if they gave one. If not, the BCA format: weekly for the first four to six weeks, then two 90-minute sessions a month.
- Only promise what the coach controls: clarity, a plan, sessions, the scoreboard, accountability. Never promise a revenue figure.
- Proof: at most one real result from the coach's record, after the phase it fits. None if they have none.
- Same three-link test as the nine steps: because you told me, what we will do, which means.
- Could I say this phase to a different owner? Then it is still too generic.
