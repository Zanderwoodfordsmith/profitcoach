# Phased pitch: what happens when

The second way to pitch. The nine-step pitch shows what we fix. This one shows what happens after they say yes, phase by phase, and what is left for them to do. It comes from the BCA Trusted Advisor pitch (Alex, October 2026), turned round for a coach selling to an owner.

Use it when the owner wants to know how it works, what they get and when, has been burned before, or is cautious. It is also the second pitch on a follow-up call, so the coach is not repeating the nine steps.

Same five facts. Same Golden Thread. Every phase is tied to their problem.

## The shape

**Bridge.** Read their problem and its cost back in one or two lines, then say what we are going to do. Name the three phases in one line: we get clear, we fix what matters, then we make it last.

**Their part.** The coach does not run the business for them. Say plainly that three things stay with the owner, because only they can do them:

1. Turn up and be honest about what is really going on
2. Make the decisions
3. Do what we agree, or get it done through the team

Then: "My job is to make sure you always know the next move, and that it happens." Say it once in the bridge and once in the recap, never more.

**Phase 1. Get clear (the first month).** Open with their worry ("You told me ..."). We score the business across every area, find what is really causing their problem (the obvious problem is not always the real one), agree where the business is going, and build a 90-day plan: what, who, when, and how we will know. Which means they stop guessing and know the first move towards their result.

**Phase 2. Fix what matters (months two and three).** Open with their worry. We work the plan, starting with their problem. Quick wins first, often profit and cash. A weekly scoreboard: a few numbers the team fills in and they read in under a minute. Every session: what got done, what is stuck, what is next. Which means things actually change, not another list of ideas. If the coach has named when a first result shows, say it here.

**Phase 3. Make it last (from month four).** Open with what they want long term. Every 90 days we score the business again, see what moved, and set the next plan. We work on the numbers, the roles and the team, so the business does not depend on them for everything. Which means the result sticks, and they get the personal thing they told you about.

**Check-in after each phase.** One short question, not two or three. "Does that make sense so far?" "Can you see how that takes [their worry] off you?" "Want me to show you what happens after that?"

**Recap.** One line per phase, then their part in one line, then tie it back: from their problem to their result, and why it matters to them.

## Rules

- Session rhythm: use the coach's own if they gave one. If not, the BCA format: weekly for the first four to six weeks, then two 90-minute sessions a month.
- Only promise what the coach controls: clarity, a plan, sessions, the scoreboard, accountability. Never promise a revenue figure.
- Proof: at most one real result from the coach's record, after the phase it fits. None if they have none.
- Same three-link test as the nine steps: because you told me, what we will do, which means.
- Could I say this phase to a different owner? Then it is still too generic.
