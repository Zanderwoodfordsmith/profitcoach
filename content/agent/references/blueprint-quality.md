# What good looks like in the Blueprint

The bar Ava holds answers to. Each answer also comes with its own example and "why we ask" from the tools: use those first.

## Results (Story · Results)
- Needs a from, a to and a timeframe: "Cut debtor days from 74 to 31 in 9 months as Finance Director at Harlow & Pike."
- A mechanism (how they did it) makes it stronger, but it is optional.
- Vague ("grew profit a lot", "big turnaround") is not enough. Ask for rough numbers: before and after, or a percentage. Ranges are fine ("about £150k to £900k").
- One strong precise result beats five vague ones. Client results beat career results.
- If they truly cannot give numbers after two asks, save it with precise false and pass it to BCA.

## Superpowers and what makes them different
- Specific and owner-facing: "Finding the cash a business already has but cannot see." Not "I'm a people person" or "strategic thinking".
- Test: would an owner say "I need that"? Would another adviser say the same about themselves? If so, ask what only they would say.

## Problems people ask them to solve
- In the owner's words, one per line: "We are busy but cash is always tight." Not service names ("cash flow consultancy").

## Who they help (Market, Avatar)
- Industries where their results would impress and they know the language, not "SMEs" or "anyone with a business".
- Avatar lines are concrete: age, role, money, where they spend time. Pains and wants in the person's words, strongest first.
- "Why you" is proof the person would trust. Never an invented limit or claim.

## Offer
- A real minimum fee in money per month. Payment terms in plain words. Capacity as a number.
- Guarantee terms in the coach's words; BCA writes the shorter versions.

## Voice
- "Sounds like you" should be something they actually wrote or would send, not a description of their style.
- How they want to be known, and the kind of coach they are (fun, serious, strict, nurturing, kick up the backside, boot camp, hugs, or a mix), should be specific enough that two coaches would not give the same answer.
- Values, likes, dislikes and pet peeves stay in their words. Short lines, not slogans.
- Favourite book, film, hobbies and a story they would share can be anything, including a childhood book. Capture the name, and a line of why if they give one. Never suggest a book or film for them.

## Written copy (sections BCA wrote)
- Follows the writing rules: plain UK English, short sentences, no em dashes, no hype words.
- Uses only facts already in the Blueprint. Messages stay short enough to send, with {first_name} style tokens kept exactly.
