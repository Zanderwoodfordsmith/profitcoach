# LinkedIn marketing

## What this is
The coach's public LinkedIn engine: one newsletter issue, then the messages that send people to it. You write it in their voice, for their avatar, from their real proof. It lives on the LinkedIn Newsletter page of their Blueprint.

## Before you write
1. `blueprint_overview` on `content` (or the page they are on) and `read_section` for `content:newsletter` and anything they name.
2. You need a topic, the avatar and one real story or result. Pull the avatar and proof from the Blueprint. If the topic is missing, ask. If a personal story would make the issue better, ask for one. Do not invent one.
3. Phone and booking details come from the Blueprint. If a calendar link is missing, write `[calendar link]` and say so. Never invent a number or a URL.

## The process
1. Say the plan in a line ("I'll write this week's issue on cash in scaffolding, then the cover, the inbox note and the post").
2. Draft the issue in the chat first when they want to see it. Then `propose_copy` for `content:newsletter` with the whole section in blocks. Nothing lands until they accept the card. This issue only. Do not add a list of later issues.
3. After they accept the issue, offer the pieces that belong to it, one at a time: `content:cover`, `content:inbox`, `content:post`, `content:inmail`, `content:article`. Each is its own section.
4. `content:checklist` is the habit, not an issue. Write or refresh it when they ask how to run the newsletter.
5. The Analogy issue (`content:analogy`) and the KPIs issue (`content:kpis`) only when they ask, and only from a sport, hobby, book or film they already gave us.

## Issue shape
The method file is the contract. In short: 500 to 1500 words, language a 12-year-old would understand, headings they can skim. Five strategies, three things to avoid, a seven-point checklist, then a Discovery call CTA with their name, Business Profit Coach, calendar and mobile.

Write the issue as headings, paragraphs, numbered lists and bullets. Do not put the whole issue in one message block.

## The pieces
- Cover: headline, tagline, what the picture shows.
- Inbox: first line in square brackets (something from one of their posts). Then the newsletter, two strategies, a link placeholder.
- Post: the three things to avoid, and a pointer to the newsletter.
- InMail: the five strategies, why to read the rest, link, book-a-call.
- Article: same topic, points back to the newsletter, ends with how to book.

## What NOT to do
- Do not invent a story, a result, a client or a number.
- Do not write the cover, inbox, post, InMail or article before the issue exists, unless they only want a post and have already given you the three things to avoid.
- Do not sell coaching in the first line. Give the value, then the call.
- No em dashes. No "transform", "unlock", "game-changer".
