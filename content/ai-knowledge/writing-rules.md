# Writing Rules - All Brands

These rules apply to ALL written output across BCA and Profit Coach, in every format: LinkedIn posts, scripts, sales copy, internal docs, community messages, emails.

## Non-Negotiable

1. **Never use the em dash character.** It is the clearest giveaway of AI-generated writing. Humans almost never use it. Use a normal hyphen (-), a comma, a full stop, or restructure the sentence. No exceptions.

2. **Write like a human.** Short sentences. Conversational. No corporate language. No coaching cliches. If it sounds like a LinkedIn influencer wrote it, rewrite it.

3. **One truth per document.** Don't duplicate information across files. Cross-reference instead.

## Craft Principles

These are how to execute "write like a human" in practice. Apply in roughly this order of priority.

### 1. Present tense by default
Use present tense wherever possible. Transform past and future constructions to present unless the time reference is essential.
- Yes: "We close the sale on the call."
- No: "The sale was closed on the call."

### 2. Active voice
Lead with the subject doing the action. Passive voice hides who did what and adds words.
- Yes: "We carried the body."
- No: "The body was carried out of the room."

### 3. Strong verbs, no adverbs
Pick one specific verb instead of a weak verb plus an adverb.
- Yes: "We slammed the door."
- No: "We shut the door really hard."

### 4. Short sentences, one idea each
Break complex sentences into multiple simple ones. One sentence, one thought.
- Yes: "Keep going. People can't see negatives."
- No: "Keep going because people are unable to perceive negative instructions."

### 5. Simple vocabulary
Write at a 3rd to 5th grade vocabulary level. Choose common words over rare ones.
- Yes: "walked"
- No: "ambulated" or "traversed"

### 6. Positive framing
Tell the reader what to do, not what not to do, unless the prohibition is the point.
- Yes: "Keep going."
- No: "Don't stop."

### 7. Cut redundancy
Remove repeated words and repeated ideas ruthlessly. Every word earns its place.
- Yes: "We walked outside."
- No: "We simultaneously walked out of the big door."

### 8. The if-it-makes-sense-without-it, cut-it rule
Read each sentence. Remove any word where removal doesn't change the meaning. If it didn't change meaning, it was filler.

### 9. Consistent tense
Hold one tense inside a piece unless you deliberately shift. Tense-flipping reads as amateur.

### 10. Format to context, not by reflex
- Documents, guides, handbooks: use headings, bullets, and **bold** for scanability.
- Short messages (community posts, DMs, short emails): prose. Avoid bullet lists for anything under 5 sentences. Bullets in a 3-sentence post read as corporate.
- Sales copy: prose with strategic formatting. Never walls of bullets.

### 11. AI tells to cut
The patterns below give away AI-generated writing instantly. Strip them out.

**Forced negation hooks ("not X, it's Y"):**
Don't lead a point with what it isn't. Just say what it is.
- Yes: "Leverage matters more than effort."
- No: "It's not about working harder. It's about leverage."

**Staccato one-liners stacked together:**
Short sentences are good. A wall of one-line sentences in a row reads as machine-written. Merge into normal paragraphs unless the rhythm is intentional.

**Double-negation framing ("we don't X, we Y"):**
A specific case of the negation hook. Cut it. Just describe what you do.
- Yes: "We orient you."
- No: "We don't teach on this one. We orient."

**Generic AI vocabulary:**
Cut on sight: "let's unpack this", "in today's world", "dive deep", "cut through the noise", "navigate the journey", "leverage" as a verb, "robust", "seamless", "crucial", "elevate", "empower", "unlock". Replace with how you'd actually speak.

## Self-edit checklist

After drafting, run these passes:

- Cut every unnecessary word.
- Replace passive with active.
- Swap weak verb plus adverb for one strong verb.
- Flip "don't" constructions to "do" where possible.
- Scan for em dashes. If you find one, rewrite.
- Scan for forced negation hooks, staccato runs, and AI vocabulary. Rewrite each.
- Read the first sentence. If it doesn't earn the second, rewrite it.
