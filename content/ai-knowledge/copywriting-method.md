# Copywriting method: think first, then write

How to write anything an owner will read: a LinkedIn profile, a connection note, a follow-up, a post, a page. Strategy first, words second, checks last. Built from Jon Street, Alex Hormozi, Evaldo Albuquerque, Kyle Milligan, Charlie Morgan, Mark Ford and BCA's own data. Writing rules (writing-rules.md) still apply to every word.

## 1. Before writing: answer five questions

1. **Who exactly is reading?** A named group ("UK manufacturing owners, 11 to 50 staff"), never "business owners". The tighter the group, the more they think "this is for me".
2. **How aware are they?** Our owners know the problem (too much runs through them, profit doesn't match the work) and have usually tried a fix that let them down. So: lead with their pain in their words, never with what is sold. They are serial buyers: they need a reason past fixes failed.
3. **What is the one belief?** Finish this sentence before anything else: "[New way] is the key to [what they want], and the only way to get it is through [our mechanism]." Ours, by default: "Fixing the right few things in the right order is the key to more profit and a business that doesn't need you, and the Profit System shows you which few things." Cut anything that doesn't serve it.
4. **What must they believe to say yes?** Pick the beliefs this piece has to build (section 2). A connection note builds one or two. A profile builds five or six. A sales page builds all of them.
5. **What is the smallest next step?** One action, easy to take: a yes or no reply, the BOSS Score, a newsletter. Never two asks.

## 2. The beliefs that make a yes (Jon Street)

Build them in roughly this order. Each line is what the reader should think.

1. "This is for me." Name their group and a moment they lived this week (the 7am phone call, the quote they had to check, the holiday cut short). Moments, not concepts.
2. "I do that." Pains as behaviours they recognise, in their words.
3. "It wasn't my fault, because..." Let them off the hook and give the reason: the thing that was missing. Examples: "You were trained to make things, not run a business." "Cost-plus pricing quietly caps your profit." "Nobody hands owners a system for the business itself."
4. "I've never thought of it that way." A new angle on the problem: the order matters more than the ideas ("These are all good ideas, just in the wrong order"), or the constraint ("one thing is holding you at this level").
5. "This is new." A named, simple mechanism: the Profit System in three phases (get clear, fix what matters, make it last), the BOSS Score. Owners have heard "I help businesses grow" too many times.
6. "Others like me got there." Proof from people like them, with a number and a timeframe.
7. "I can't figure this out alone." Show what the system is, never the full how.
8. "Anyone can do this." Remove the excuse: "You don't need a board, a big team or an MBA."
9. "Now, not later." The cost of waiting, as a moment or a number, never fake scarcity.

## 3. Angles and hooks

- **Lead with growth or time.** In our own data owners act most on growth plans (65% of those replies interested) and being too busy (45%). Profit squeeze and hard times pull least as the hook. Use profit and pricing in the "wasn't your fault" line and the mechanism instead.
- **Hormozi's strongest truths for owners** (use freely, they're true and most owners haven't heard them):
  - Find the constraint. Each stage has one thing holding the business; fix that, not everything.
  - Most people go wide when they need to go deep. The fastest owners pick the right few things.
  - Cost-plus pricing caps profit. Price on value.
  - The owner's role has to change as the team grows: doer, then manager, then leader. You're not failing, you're at a stage.
  - Fix retention and margin before chasing more leads.
  - Skills like hiring and hard conversations are learnable; owners avoid them, they're not incapable.
  - Value = how big the outcome x how likely it feels / (how long it takes x how much effort). Make it bigger, likelier, faster, easier.
- **Hook formula** (first one or two lines): a dream outcome or a sharp pain, for a character they relate to, with no conditions. Specific beats clever.
- **Question hooks** must be specific to their world ("Still checking every big quote yourself?"), never flat ("Are you struggling with growth?").
- **Short lines any owner nods at** (banners, headlines, post openers). Patterns: blame the order or the system, not the person; a yes or no test they answer in their head; a moment from their week; a stuck feeling named as a stage; an offer to get their number. Lines Zander likes: "Every business has one constraint. Find yours." "Find the one thing holding you back." "The right few fixes, in the right order." "Your job changes as your business grows." More to try: "Good ideas. Wrong order." "Can it run for a fortnight without you?" "Every question still ends up with you." "What would your business score out of 100?" "Stop fixing what isn't holding you back." Narrow lines ("Fix your margin before more leads") suit a post about that one problem, not a banner every owner sees.
- **Zander's ranked hooks** for owners: the knowing-doing gap ("You know what needs doing. You just can't get to it."), the operator trap ("You built it for freedom. Now it owns you."), overwhelm ("You need the one thing to fix first"), the five levels ("Most owners think they're owners. They're actually operators.").

## 4. Proof

- Proof multiplies everything. A real result with a number and a timeframe, from someone like the reader.
- Put proof early: in the connection note (100k requests: 0.31 meetings per 100 with proof against 0.12 without) and in the first two lines of a profile.
- Wrap longer proof in "And, but, therefore": what was happening, what was wrong, what changed.
- Mix proof types: a client result, the coach's own career result, a named credential.
- Never invent or round up. A missing number is a gap with the question to ask the coach.

## 5. Shapes that work

- **Connection note:** group named, one proof, a yes or no question, nothing for sale, no link. 300 characters or under once filled in; the full rules and real examples are in the connection campaign skill (practice-skills/connection-campaign/note.md).
- **Follow-ups:** message 1 carries the proof in Zander's ad style (what they want as 1️⃣ 2️⃣, a result with numbers, "✅ Is this of interest?"); the later ones each give one thing, and the last makes "not now" easy to say. Pam's Starbucks rule for every one: answerable in a few seconds in a queue. The sequence is in practice-skills/connection-campaign/follow-ups.md.
- **Open Profile or longer message:** hook question, two numbered outcomes, two results, one line of credibility, one-word ask.
- **LinkedIn headline:** a simple promise plus proof: who you help, the result, the number. Never a job title alone. Creativity belongs in the About opener.
- **LinkedIn About:** see linkedin-profile/about.md for the parts. Order: hook (the group, what they want, and proof, all visible before "see more", ending on an open loop), pains, "wasn't your fault", who I work with, results, how it works (the three phases), it all starts with the BOSS Score.
- **LinkedIn banner:** one line any owner nods at, plus a picture that makes the same point (the wheel with the constraint lit up, the five levels, the three steps). Readable on a phone. Leave the coach out: the profile photo and name sit right below it.
- **Callout** (Cole Gordon's video mid-roll; also the end of a post, a newsletter P.S. or a webinar ask). Under 120 words, said fast:
  1. Who it's for and a quick "listen up": "If you run a business doing £1M+ and want my help, listen up."
  2. What you'll do yourself: look at the whole business across the ten areas.
  3. What they walk away with: the one thing holding the business back, and the one to three things to do next.
  4. What they get anyway: their BOSS Score report and the plan, theirs to keep.
  5. One action, said twice in the exact words they'll see: "Take the free BOSS Score" or "Book your BOSS Score Review".
  Only say places are limited if they are.
- **Body of any longer piece:** objection, then claim, then three mixed proofs, then the benefit.

## 6. Voice

- Calm authority. Practical, not motivational. The Owner Test: would a busy owner think "finally, something practical" or "another coaching programme selling feelings"?
- Operational words, not therapy words. Talk about quotes, margin, managers, hours, holidays.
- Every piece carries four things (Milligan): new, easy, safe, big. A headline uses at least two of: urgent, unique, useful, very specific.
- One idea per message or post. Mix short and long sentences.
- Line breaks in every message, connection notes included: a blank line between each part (the greeting and question, the proof, the ask), so it reads easily on a phone. Each line break counts as a character in the 300.
- Never: hype ("unlock", "secret", "crack the code", "10x"), coaching clichés ("transform your life", "your journey", "step into your power"), founder-led openers, naming competitors, promising a specific revenue figure.
- Terms: BOSS Score (out of 100), Profit System (the method). Where a coach sells (profile, website, messages, posts that pitch), show how it works in the three phases of the call pitch, not the five levels; the levels belong in the BOSS Score report. Discreet coaches are never called a coach and never say "book a call".

## 7. Check before you hand it over

1. Can I name the one belief and the one next step?
2. Does the first line name their group or a moment they live, not us?
3. Is there a real number with a timeframe, from their record?
4. Is there a "wasn't your fault, because" and a named mechanism (for anything longer than a note)?
5. Could a competitor say this word for word? If yes, it's too generic.
6. Can it be answered in a few seconds (messages)?
7. Writing rules: no em dashes, everyday words, nothing invented.
8. For headlines and hooks: write at least five, score each for specific, curious and "this is for me", and offer the best two.
