# Follow-ups

Five messages after they accept. Every one gives something or asks one easy question; none just "checks in". Pam's Starbucks rule: an owner standing in a queue can answer it in a few seconds, with a yes or no or a choice of two.

Never write "as I mentioned in my connection message": some invites go out blank, and many people never read the note. Each message stands on its own.

## Message 1: the ad shape (5 minutes after they accept)

The longest message, up to about 600 characters. Same shape as BCA's LinkedIn ads and the Classroom's Open InMail: what they want as two numbered points, proof, then the real problem in one line.

> Hi {first_name}, thanks for connecting.
>
> Are you looking to:
> 1️⃣ [the thing they want most, in their words]
> 2️⃣ [the second thing]
>
> I ask because [Company / during my X years as role at Company] used a simple system to [before → after] in [timeframe].
>
> [One line on the real problem: what was wrong, and that most firms like theirs have it.]
>
> ✅ Is this of interest?

- **1️⃣ and 2️⃣ are what they want, not what the coach sells.** Never the BOSS Score, a call or an audit here. Lead with growth or time: in our data owners act most on growth plans and being too busy. Keep the pain for the one line after the proof.
- **The proof:** if the coach has a second result, use it here so the note and message 1 carry different proof. If they have only one, repeat it in fuller words with the before and after.
- **The problem line** lets them off the hook: it wasn't them, it was what was missing ("They were busy firefighting instead of chasing the jobs that pay").
- Two numbered points, three at most.

Example (from the Classroom's scaffolding client):

> Hi {first_name}, thanks for connecting.
>
> Are you looking to:
> 1️⃣ win more of the profitable jobs
> 2️⃣ stop putting out fires all day
>
> I ask because CASS Scaffolding used a simple system to go from £200K to over £1M profit in under 3 years, and turnover from £3M towards £8M.
>
> Their problem wasn't the work. They were busy firefighting instead of chasing the jobs that pay. Most scaffolding firms are the same.
>
> ✅ Is this of interest?

## Message 2: their problem, in their words (1 day)

Under 300 characters. One problem owners like them describe, then ask if it's true for them.

> {first_name}, most scaffolding owners I speak to say the same thing: there's plenty of work, but the profit doesn't show it. Is that true for you, or is it different your end?

## Message 3: one useful insight (2 days)

Under 300 characters. One idea with a number, tied to the proof or the Profit System, and one question.

> {first_name}, a bit of maths I share with owners. Five numbers sit behind profit: leads, conversion, average job, how often clients come back, and margin. Lift each by 10% and profit grows by about 61%, because they multiply. Which of the five has the most room in yours?

Other insights that work: every business has one constraint holding it at its level, and most owners are guessing which one it is; the owner's job changes as the business grows (doer, manager, leader).

## Message 4: the easy next step (4 days)

Under 300 characters. Offer the BOSS Score as a yes or no. Send the link only after they say yes.

> {first_name}, would it help to see where your business is strongest and weakest? The BOSS Score takes 3 minutes and shows the one thing holding it back. Want me to send it over?

## Message 5: the close (2 weeks)

Under 300 characters. Make "not now" easy to say. Then move them into Ongoing nurture.

> I'll leave it there, {first_name}. If the business ever needs you more than you'd like, I'm easy to find. Either way, good luck with the rest of the year.

## Never

- "Free Coaching Discovery Call", or any offer of coaching or a call before they show interest.
- Fake scarcity: "still a few calls left this week".
- "Jaded by all the other coaches", "just checking if you saw my last message", "circling back".
- A link before they ask for one.
- A merge field that can break: write "Hi {first_name}," with the comma and space, never "Hey{first_name}".

## The coach's voice

Match the kind of coach they are in the word choice only: a boot camp coach is more direct than a hugs coach. No hobbies, books or stories in this campaign; there isn't room. Those belong in reactivation and nurture.
