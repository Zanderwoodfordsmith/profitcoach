# Connection note

The coach's most viewed message in their whole business. It has one job: get an owner to say "yes, tell me more". Count {first_name} as 10 characters and {company} as 30; the note must come to 300 or under (290 of your own words with only {first_name}). The notes that got 2% or more interest ran 255 to 265, so use the room for proof, not padding.

## The template (from the Classroom)

Client proof, when the coach has results with clients:

> Hi {first_name}, I see you run a [country] [industry] company. Are you looking to [main desire]? I ask because [Company] used a simple system to [before → after] in under [timeframe] by [the thing they think they need]. Is this of interest?

Career proof, when the results are from their career or past businesses:

> Hi {first_name}, I see you run a [country] [industry] company. Are you looking to [main desire]? I ask because during my [X] years as [role] at [Company] I used a simple system to [before → after] in under [timeframe] by [the thing they think they need]. Is this of interest?

Tribe note, when there is no real number yet, or the list shares a group (a region, a role, a moment):

> Hi {first_name}, I see you run a business in [region]. I'm connecting with owners here who [want what they want, in their words]. Is that you?

## The 10-step checklist

The note needs to be a 10 out of 10.
1. **Length.** 300 or under, counting {first_name} as 10 and {company} as 30.
2. **Greeting.** "Hi {first_name},"
3. **Specific callout.** The kind of business and the country: "I see you run a UK engineering company."
4. **Hook as a question** about the one thing they want: "Are you looking to win bigger contracts?"
5. **"I ask because"** to justify the question.
6. **"A simple system"** for curiosity.
7. **Specific proof** with a before and after and a timeframe.
8. **Mechanism** tied to the hook: the thing they already believe they need.
9. **Their language.** Simple words they would use.
10. **Interest ask:** "Is this of interest?"

## Zander's review rules

From his reviews of 38 coach notes. These are the corrections he made most, most common first.
1. **Before and after, not a bare gain.** "£5.1M to £10.2M in under 4 years" beats "+£5M". "Doubled profit" or "127%" means nothing without the numbers.
2. **Exact, odd figures read as true.** £1.17M, not "+£1M". £10.2M, not £10M.
3. **Write the hook the way they'd say it.** Would they say it to their partner at the kitchen table, or to their 10-year-old? Aim between the two. Is it what they lie awake thinking? "Convert your know-how into sales" and "supercharge your sales people" fail that test.
4. **The mechanism is the thing they think they need.** Not "by integrating methods" or "by getting more from the team". Concrete: "by filling more rooms", "by getting off the tools", "by winning more profitable jobs".
5. **One hook, one proof.** Two hooks or a stack of results confuses. Keep the other proofs for tests.
6. **Shorter timeframes are more believable.** Pick a win in months over one across 7 years, even if the 7-year one is bigger.
7. **Proof at their scale.** A $400bn bank portfolio or a global firm's numbers feel unrelated to an owner. Pick the result closest to their size and world.
8. **Name the role if it adds weight** ("during my 4 years as MD at CACI"), and say if it was in their industry.
9. **Small words.** "Run", not "lead" or "am the MD of": shorter, and it fits everyone on a broad list. "Simple", not "easy" (gimmicky), "approach" or "framework". "VP", not "Executive Partner".
10. **End on "Is this of interest?"** Not "Curious?", "Let's connect", "Would you be open to exploring how this could help?" or the coach's name.

## What the results showed

Interest rates the coaches reported for their notes (22 of 38 had one). Self-reported and on lists of mixed quality, so read them as a guide.
- **Plain hooks won; jargon hooks lost every time.** Every hook in jargon, odd wording or about something other than the owner's main want got under 1%: "improve MRR/ARR", "improve risk management", "supercharging the results of your sales people", "reduce staff turnover", "do you want your team to make more profit for you". The 2% notes asked for one plain thing: profit, revenue, margins, new listings.
- **A pain hook did no harm.** "Are you struggling to improve your margins?" got over 2%. Lead with growth by default and test a pain hook as the variant.
- **Proof needs a result they can picture.** The two notes with no result at all got under 1%. The one note without a figure that still got over 2% had a vivid before and after: a home-kitchen recipe to market leader. Numbers are the safe way to get there.
- **The industry callout beat {company}.** 11 of 16 notes that named the industry got 1% or more, against 2 of 5 that used the company name. Small numbers, but it matches Zander's advice.
- **The hook must point at what the coach sells.** A coach's "Are you looking to get new listings?" to drinks brands got over 2%, but most of the calls thought she ran a sales agency. Point the hook at an outcome coaching gives, and make clear it's coaching by the second message or the call.

## Examples

These show the shape. Copy their order and tone, never their facts, numbers or companies: use only this coach's proof. Never show them to a coach as another coach's message.

**Good, 2% or more interested (client proof, UK construction owners and MDs).** Zander: "It's perfect."
> Hi {first_name}, I see you run a UK Construction Company. Are you looking to grow your profit? I ask because Al of Total Build Ltd used a simple system to go from -£76K to +£121K in 12 months by getting off the tools and streamlining jobs. Is this of interest?

**Good, 2% or more (career proof, IT consultancies).** Big before and after, role at a known firm.
> Hi {first_name}, I see you are leader of an IT Consultancy. Are you aiming to reignite revenue & drive profit growth? I ask because during my time at CACI I used a simple system to grow PS revenue from £5.5M to £18.47M organically over 4 yrs. Is this of interest?

Zander's improvements even so: "run" instead of "leader", add "UK", a simpler hook, the role, and a mechanism after the result.

**Good, 1.5 to 2% (client proof, accountancy practices).**
> Hi {first_name}, I see you are the owner of an accountancy practice. Are you looking to maximise billable hours? At H&E & Co we used a simple system to maximise billable hours by 32% increasing profit margins & growing their bottom line by £64K. Is this of interest?

Better: "I see you run a UK accountancy practice", "I ask because", and lead with the money: "grow profit by £64K by lifting billable hours 32%".

**Classroom example (career proof, HVAC).**
> Hi {first_name}, I see you run a UK HVAC company. Are you looking to grow your profit? I ask because I used a simple system to grow (& sell) A&P Mechanical Services from £400K → £1.5M t/o in under 3yrs by sorting out the lads & issues with other trades. Is this of interest?

The original said "I used a simple system to double profit in under a year by using this technique": no numbers, no mechanism. Owners need to see what you have done before they believe you can help them.

**Good note, wrong list (under 1%, UK electrical contractors).** Zander: "Great message, surprised it is under 1%." Trades barely use LinkedIn (2 to 7% acceptance in our data). His one change: hook "grow your profit", with "spending less time on site" as the mechanism in place of "integrating methods to focus on business growth".
> Hi {first_name}, I see you run a UK Electrical company. Are you looking to spend less time on-site? I ask because, at A&P Mech I used a simple system to increase profit from 17k→317K in 3yrs, by integrating methods to focus on business growth. Is this of interest?

**Bad: not following the template.**
> Hi {first_name}, Are you looking to maximise your profits & reduce customer churn? I ask because Company Name used a simple system to boost customer LTV 127% in just 3 months, by improving engagement & value delivery. Is this of interest?

No callout, so it feels automated. Two hooks. "127%" is abstract: was that £10K to £27K or £1M to £2.7M? "Engagement and value delivery" isn't how they think at 3am.

**Bad: selling straight away.**
> Hi {first_name}, Would be great to connect and see if I can support you boost profits, cut costs and reduce churn? I've helped firms like Company Name achieve just that, streamlining sales and enhancing customer value swiftly. Interested to learn more?

Asks them to decide about coaching in the first line. "I've helped" is about the coach; "I ask because" plus the system is more believable. No result.

## Pick strong words

The hook and the mechanism carry the note. "Are you looking to improve cashflow?" lands harder than "Are you looking to grow your business?" Build it from one word in each column, e.g. "significantly grow cash in the bank":

| Adverb | Verb | Noun |
|---|---|---|
| rapidly, sustainably, significantly, immediately | improve, increase, boost, grow, scale | cashflow, cash in the bank, sales, profit, revenue, margin |

Use their industry's words: customers become contracts, retainers, deals or covers; staff become the lads, associates or the team.

## Cut wasted words

| Original | Shorter |
|---|---|
| I see you're the MD of an HVAC company within the UK construction industry turning over £1-10m. | I see you run a UK HVAC company. |
| We were able to grow the business from under £400,000 to just shy of £1.5 million in less than 2 years. | grew it from £400K → £1.5M in under 2 yrs |
| Would this be of interest? [Coach name] | Is this of interest? |

## Red flags

- Not personalised: no callout of the kind of business.
- Generic: any coach could have written it.
- Abstract results: no before and after, no timeframe.
- Selling coaching or a call, or "how I can support you".
- About the coach's expertise, not the owner's outcome.
- A link of any kind.
