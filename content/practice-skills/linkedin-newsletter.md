# LinkedIn newsletter

A monthly or fortnightly LinkedIn newsletter for owners in this coach's market. It builds trust in public. Each issue gives away the work. The last line asks for a Discovery call.

Name it after the outcome for this market ("Scaffolding Profit Notes", "Law Firm Profit Newsletter"). Say what it is on the tin.

## The 5-3-7 shape

Every issue has the same bones:

1. One topic. One pain the avatar feels this week.
2. Five strategies they can do.
3. Three critical things to avoid.
4. A seven-point action checklist they can run this week.
5. A call to book a Discovery call with this coach, named as a Business Profit Coach, with a calendar link and their mobile.

500 to 1500 words. Language a 12-year-old would understand. Short sentences. Headings and sections so a busy owner can skim. Intriguing, not hype.

Write the issue as the kind of coach they are (boot camp, hugs, a mix). How they want to be known should be readable by the end. Use a personal story they already gave (Your Voice, a career result, a client story) as the opening when it fits. Analogies from a favourite book, film, hobby or sport only when they named it on Your Voice. Never invent a story, book, film or hobby from their life. If they gave none, stay with the avatar's world and their proof, and leave a square-bracket prompt for a story.

Write this issue only. A later issue is its own issue. Do not put a plan of future issues inside this one. Do not describe the newsletter in the issue. No line about it being a short read, or one idea per issue, or who it is for. That sits in Newsletter info. Start with this week's topic. If they wrote closing wording, use that sentence at the end.

## The pieces of this issue

Each one is its own section. Write only the one you were asked for.

**Cover.** Headline, the line under it, and what the picture shows. They drop that onto the header template.

**Inbox (top 100).** Personalise the first line with something from one of their posts, in square brackets for the coach to fill. Then a short template: what the newsletter is, why it helps, two strategies from this issue, a link placeholder. INSIGHT still applies: one useful thing, then one small next step.

**Post.** The three things to avoid from this issue. Point them to the newsletter for the strategies and the checklist.

**InMail.** The five strategies, why the rest of the issue is worth the time, a link, and a book-a-call link.

**Article.** The same topic, longer. Reference the newsletter and end with the coach's name, Business Profit Coach, and how to book.

## Other issues

**Analogy issue.** Pair this avatar's business with a sport, hobby, book or film the coach already cares about (from their voice answers). Same 5-3-7 bones. Specific, not cute.

**KPI article.** The same pairing, but through numbers: what a good week looks like in the hobby, and what a good week looks like in the business.

## How to run it (the 2026 checklist)

Not copy. The habit.

- Publish the issue, then share it the same day.
- Send the VIP inbox note to the top 100. First line personal, rest a template.
- Turn on the LinkedIn bell for everyone on that list.
- Check notifications the same week.
- Like, comment and share on other people's posts, especially the VIP list.
- Next day: one article or post per strategy if they want to stretch the issue.
- A carousel from the five strategies or the seven-point checklist.
- Invite readers to a workshop (never say webinar) when there is one.

Soft CTA most weeks. The Discovery call is the close of the issue, not every comment.
