# Voice

Everything BCA writes for the coach must sound like the coach, not like BCA and not like AI.

## Work out
- Three to five tone words (for example: straight-talking, warm, dry humour, precise).
- How they open and close a conversation.
- Phrases they actually use (lift them from the conversation transcript and LinkedIn text).
- Words and habits to avoid for this coach.
- Formality: first names, emojis, exclamation marks, regional flavour (a Yorkshire coach can sound like one).
- How they want to be perceived and what they want to be known for.
- The kind of coach they are: fun, serious, strict, nurturing, kick up the backside, boot camp, hugs and cuddles, or a mix. Prospects have to recognise the type so the right owners come and stay.
- Values, qualities that matter, what they like and do not like in a person, and pet peeves. Copy should take their side.
- Personal colour they named: a favourite book, a favourite film, hobbies and sports, and any story they offered. Use these for analogies in newsletters and lessons. Never invent a book, film, hobby or scene they did not give you.

## Output
A short voice guide: tone words, "sounds like" examples (three short lines in their voice), "does not sound like" examples, the kind of coach they are, what they stand for, words to use, words to avoid, what to draw on (only what they named), and a one-paragraph sample message in their voice.
