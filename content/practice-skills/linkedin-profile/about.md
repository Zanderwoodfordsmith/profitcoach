# About

First person, about 2,000 characters (never over 2,300: LinkedIn stops at 2,600 and each bold letter counts as two), in this order. It follows the buyer's beliefs: this is for me, I do that, it wasn't my fault, others like me got there, this is new, and one easy next step.
- Hook: name the group and what they want (growth, or time back), and take away what they hate. Then one real result with a number. Both must show before "see more" (about the first two lines). Specific to their world ("Gym owners: want the place to grow without you opening up every morning?"), never a compliment ("You built something worth being proud of"). End on an open loop ("Here's how I help owners like you do the same").
- ▶︎ 𝗦𝗢𝗨𝗡𝗗 𝗙𝗔𝗠𝗜𝗟𝗜𝗔𝗥?: three or four ❗ lines the owner recognises ("I do that"), as behaviours, not concepts. No lead-in line under the header: the hook already named the group.
- "It wasn't your fault, because...": one line with the reason, the thing that was missing.
- ▶︎ 𝗪𝗛𝗢 𝗜 𝗪𝗢𝗥𝗞 𝗪𝗜𝗧𝗛: two ⭐️ lines from this coach's ideal client: what they run, and where they want to go. Their week is already in the pains, so don't repeat it. No turnover band or staff count.
- ▶︎ 𝗥𝗘𝗦𝗨𝗟𝗧𝗦: two or three ✅ lines, each with a number and a timeframe. Then, with no header, one or two lines: the kind of coach they are, from how they want to be known, in their own words ("Honest, even when it stings"), and one credential. Never from how they sound: that describes the tone of their messages, and turned into a claim it reads oddly ("I'll notice what you've built and say so").
- ▶︎ 𝗛𝗢𝗪 𝗜𝗧 𝗪𝗢𝗥𝗞𝗦: a mini version of the three-phase call pitch, one 1️⃣ 2️⃣ 3️⃣ line each, with when, tied to this owner's problem: Get clear (month one: score the business, find what's really holding it back, a 90-day plan), Fix what matters (months two and three: fix that first, a few numbers the team tracks each week), Make it last (month four on: score it again every 90 days and set the next plan, so the business doesn't need them in every decision). Then: "You make the decisions and your team does the work. My job is to make sure you always know the next move, and that it happens." Use Vision, Velocity and Value as the phase names only if the coach already uses them. No five levels in the profile: they belong in the BOSS Score report.
- ▶︎ 𝗜𝗧 𝗔𝗟𝗟 𝗦𝗧𝗔𝗥𝗧𝗦 𝗪𝗜𝗧𝗛 𝗬𝗢𝗨𝗥 𝗕𝗢𝗦𝗦 𝗦𝗖𝗢𝗥𝗘: one 📥 line: what the free BOSS Score gives them (13 quick questions, a score out of 100, the one thing holding the business back), that it's in Featured, or a message to talk it through. Links in the About don't work, so point to Featured. Discreet coaches: the BOSS Score or their newsletter, never "book a call".

Layout: short paragraphs. Bold only short section headers, as "▶︎ 𝗪𝗛𝗢 𝗜 𝗪𝗢𝗥𝗞 𝗪𝗜𝗧𝗛" in Unicode bold capitals (LinkedIn has no bold; screen readers and search skip it). Never markdown: ** and "-" bullets paste into LinkedIn as plain symbols. Each list line starts with its emoji marker. Emojis as markers only (❗ pains, ⭐️ who, ✅ results, 1️⃣ phases, 📥 the ask), never in a discreet coach's profile if their voice avoids them. Write for owner-led firms: a team and managers, not a board. Keep the "gap" argument (little help between start-ups and big firms) for explainers and calls, not the profile.

Why it works: the group, what they want and a real result all show before "see more". The pains are moments from the owner's week. Who it's for comes early, so the right owner keeps reading. The results are named businesses with numbers and timeframes. How it works says what happens and when, without the how, and leads straight into the one ask.

## Bad

Peter, before:

> I work with directors and boards to help them get increased profit, sharper at how they lead, make better decisions, and avoid the mistakes that quietly sink businesses.
> A client gained 4x turnover with 2x profit, another that had almost failed fought back to £2m turnover in 3 years with £500k profit. It can be done.
> I've spent over forty years building and advising across property, manufacturing, retail, services, research, social enterprise, housing — you name it — and a lot of what I teach comes from what I've seen go wrong firsthand, not just what looks good in a textbook.
> I also chair Refugees Rock, which gets people seeking asylum into climbing centres to build community and stay well. So, my world is a mix of boardroom strategy and making sure organisations actually serve people.

Why it fails: opens on the coach ("I work with"), not the owner's problem. "Directors and boards" isn't an owner-led firm. Good results, but no who or when, so they don't land. A list of industries says "anyone", so no owner thinks "this is for me". No system, no ask. 800 characters: too short to carry the proof.
