# The offer

## Sell the outcome, not hours
Owners buy the gap closed. Package the coaching around the result and make an hourly rate hard to work out.

## The destination
The BOSS score ends on Money, Time and Team. The offer leads with one of those. Income, Impact and Freedom stay with the academy.

Write the destination as short lines in the coach's words: what owners get more of, and what they get less of. Strongest first. That is Jon Street, not a picker. Not essays.

## What they help with
The specific work, in their language. Not a list of Profit System areas to tick. Things that are not on the system list are fine. Three to six lines is enough. Imperium's rule: less is more.

## Taki's offer diamond
Five pieces. Promise first. Payment last.
- Promise: specific, a timeframe they can point to, something they want, instantly clear, believable.
- Guarantee: removes risk after the promise is clear. 60-day love it or leave it is the BCA default if they want one. Never guarantee income.
- Bonuses: only if the coach has them. Most do not. Do not invent them.
- Payment: after they can see the vehicle.
- Urgency: only if it is real (capacity, a start date). Do not invent scarcity.

## The recommended format
- Weekly sessions for the first 4 to 6 weeks for quick wins and momentum.
- Then two 90-minute sessions a month, ongoing.
- Stack clients on the same days (for example the 1st and 3rd Tuesday).
- Paint the long-term picture from the first call: "As long as you are in business we are going to be working together."
Coaches can adjust (Ashley Maile does one half day a month plus a short call).

## Pricing
- Monthly: start at £1,450 a month and raise by £500 every 2 to 4 clients. Experienced coaches start higher.
- Six months upfront: £7,800 or £9,600, with 2, 3 or 4 pay options, or 50% upfront then monthly.
- If asked early: "Our top coaching clients invest up to £50,000 a year, but you can start for under £1,000 a month. It depends on your situation. I could do a call on Monday or Wednesday to look at what makes sense for you. Which day works best?"
- £10k a month is six clients (four at £1,500, two at £2,000).

## The written offer
Same skeleton as Imperium's finalised offer. Do not include the price. The words must sound like this coach, not like EasyGrow.

1. Who we help
2. Our promise
3. Our strategy (what they help with, and why it works)
4. Our guarantee
5. If you work with us / Or you can
6. Think of this as (what it is, and what it is not)

Positioning line if you need one: "I help {core client} {achieve outcome} without {the pain}, using {mechanism}." Back it with the best proof they actually have.

## Shape, not copy
Copy the shape. Rewrite from this coach's record. If you could read a paragraph unchanged to a different owner, it is still the example.

### James Baker
Manufacturing and engineering. Career proof from JCB.
- Leads with Money: a weekly number the team hits.
- Who: owners who are still in the work and cannot get the team to hold the plan.
- Promise: the business hits the number because the team is accountable, not because the MD is chasing everyone.
- Strategy: the weekly number, the 90-day plan, roles so the MD is not chasing. "I used this as MD at JCB to take attachments from £150k profit to £9m in four years."
- Think of this as: a simple operating rhythm, not another consultant report.

### John McCarthy
Law firms. Client proof, hours as well as profit.
- Leads with Time, with Money next to it.
- Who: law-firm MDs who have grown the practice and are still the one holding it together.
- Promise: more profit, and the owner's week back. Stephen Ward, Clerksroom: profit £200k to £1m in three years, hours cut in half in one.
- Strategy: cash and margin first, then the team so the hours do not snap back onto the owner.
- Think of this as: getting your life back without the practice going backwards.

### Will Walsh
Founders at a critical juncture. Fundraising and growth.
- Leads with Time at the make-or-break stretch.
- Who: founders with a product or a raise in front of them who need a sounding board who has done it.
- Promise: get through the stretch with a plan, not another pep talk.
- Strategy: the work they already do — raise, grow, invent — with a business underneath so it is not a scramble.
- Think of this as: a partner for the next 90 days, not a course.

## Your offer in three lengths
One idea, said three ways. Each length has a job, so label where it is used.
- Short (6 to 12 words): the headline. "I help building services owners find the cash they cannot see." No method names, no jargon.
- Medium (one or two sentences): the answer to "what do you do?". Who, the gap you close, and how you are different.
- Long (a paragraph): who it is for, the gap, how you close it, and the best proof with a number. Ends on what working together looks like.
All three say the same thing. Never promise income or results. If there is a guarantee, write it short ("Love it or leave it at 60 days") and medium ("Try it for 60 days. If it is not right, stop and pay nothing more."), from the coach's own terms only.
