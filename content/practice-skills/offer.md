# The offer

## Sell the outcome, not hours
Owners buy the gap closed. Package the coaching around the result and make an hourly rate hard to work out.

## The recommended format
- Weekly sessions for the first 4 to 6 weeks for quick wins and momentum.
- Then two 90-minute sessions a month, ongoing.
- Stack clients on the same days (for example the 1st and 3rd Tuesday).
- Paint the long-term picture from the first call: "As long as you are in business we are going to be working together."
Coaches can adjust (Ashley Maile does one half day a month plus a short call).

## Pricing
- Monthly: start at £1,450 a month and raise by £500 every 2 to 4 clients. Experienced coaches start higher.
- Six months upfront: £7,800 or £9,600, with 2, 3 or 4 pay options, or 50% upfront then monthly.
- If asked early: "Our top coaching clients invest up to £50,000 a year, but you can start for under £1,000 a month. It depends on your situation. I could do a call on Monday or Wednesday to look at what makes sense for you. Which day works best?"
- £10k a month is six clients (four at £1,500, two at £2,000).

## Positioning statement
"I help {core client} {achieve outcome} without {the pain}, using {mechanism or method}." Back it with the best proof. Name the offer so it says what it does.

## Output
The positioning line, the gap in this market (current vs ideal), three key messages written about the buyer's problems (not the product), the offer name, what is included, the format, the price options, and a short proof stack.

## Your offer in three lengths
One idea, said three ways. Each length has a job, so label where it is used.
- Short (6 to 12 words): the headline. "I help building services owners find the cash they cannot see." No method names, no jargon.
- Medium (one or two sentences): the answer to "what do you do?". Who, the gap you close, and how you are different.
- Long (a paragraph): who it is for, the gap, how you close it, and the best proof with a number. Ends on what working together looks like.
All three say the same thing. Never promise income or results. If there is a guarantee, write it short ("Love it or leave it at 60 days") and medium ("Try it for 60 days. If it is not right, stop and pay nothing more."), from the coach's own terms only.
