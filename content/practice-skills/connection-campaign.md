# Connection campaign

For new owners from a list, 2nd and 3rd degree. The main way coaches get calls.

This file is what the whole campaign needs: the recommended sequence, who to send it to, and the length rule. Each part has its own file in `connection-campaign/`: `note.md` (the connection request) and `follow-ups.md` (messages 1 to 5). Follow the copywriting method for the thinking, then the file for the part you are writing.

Sources: the Classroom lesson "Write a LinkedIn connection message", 110,769 connection requests from 43 coaches (outreach-research.md), and Zander's reviews of 38 coach notes with their interest rates (Connection Messages list in ClickUp, Nov 2024 to Jul 2025).

## The recommended campaign

BCA's recommendation as of 29 October 2026. It joins what worked in each source; the full sequence has not been measured yet.

| Step | When | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Connection request | Day 0 | Proof note (note.md). Tribe note if the coach has no real number yet. |
| Message 1 | 5 minutes after they accept, while they are still on LinkedIn | The ad shape: what they want as 1️⃣ 2️⃣, proof, one line on the real problem, "✅ Is this of interest?" |
| Message 2 | 1 day | One problem owners like them describe, in their words. Is that true for them? |
| Message 3 | 2 days | One useful insight with a number, and one question. |
| Message 4 | 4 days | Offer the BOSS Score as a yes or no. Send the link only after a yes. |
| Message 5 | 2 weeks | A polite close that leaves the door open. Then move them into Ongoing nurture. |

Why this shape:
- A short note with a real result gets fewer accepts but 2 to 3 times the meeting requests of a blank invite (0.31 against 0.12 per 100). "Fewer accept a note. More of them meet."
- Replies came 20% before any message, 17% after message 1, 14% after message 2, then 8%, 7% and 4%. The later messages still bring about a fifth of the interest, so keep five and make each one give something.
- A link from a stranger is the worst thing we tried. The BOSS Score comes after a conversation starts.

## Which connection request

| Use | When | In our data |
|---|---|---|
| Proof note (default) | The coach has a before and after number from someone like the reader, or from their own career. | 11.9% accept, 0.31 meeting requests per 100 |
| Tribe note | No real number yet, or the list shares a clear group: a region, a role, a moment. | 0.30 to 0.49 meeting requests per 100 |
| Blank invite | Only as the test arm against a proof note on the same kind of people. | 17.8% accept, 0.12 meeting requests per 100 |

Never a link. Never sell coaching or a call.

## Who to send it to

The list matters as much as the note. In Zander's reviews, strong notes still got under 1% from electrical contractors and toy distributors, while the same shape got over 2% from UK construction owners and IT consultancies.

- **Job title.** Founders answered about 4.7 times more than people titled "Owner" (2.46 against 0.52 interested per 100). Founder, Co-founder, CEO and Partner first, then Managing Director, then Owner.
- **Active people.** Open profiles answered about twice as often (26% accept and 2.24 interested per 100, against 14% and 0.93).
- **Sectors on LinkedIn.** Consulting, IT, recruitment, accountancy, property, training and marketing owners live on LinkedIn. Trades, construction, manufacturing, engineering, logistics, hospitality and care mostly don't: expect fewer accepts there. Test 500 before judging. If acceptance stays far under the 17% average, that is the channel, not the copy.
- **One market per campaign.** One industry in one country, so "I see you run a UK [industry] company" is true for every person on the list. Use {company} only on a clean list.
- **Start warm.** Messages to existing connections got about twice the replies of cold ones.

## Message or list?

When a campaign is quiet, look at where it stops.
- Few accept: the list or the channel. Check sector, titles and whether they are active.
- They accept but nobody replies: the message. Check the hook and the proof against note.md.
- A strong note to a sector that barely uses LinkedIn: move that sector to email, phone or events. Don't keep rewriting the copy.

## Length

LinkedIn allows 300 characters in a connection note, once the merge fields are filled in. Count {first_name} as 10 characters and {company} as 30 (the campaign builder counts the same way); the whole note must come to 300 or under. With only {first_name}, that leaves 290 characters for your own words. {company} costs 30, which is another reason to call out the industry instead.

Follow-ups: message 1 can be longer (up to about 600 characters). Messages 2 to 5 stay under 300.

## When they reply

Answer within the hour. Offer two specific times, with the calendar link as a backup (replies.md). 29% of replies in our data never got an answer.

## Tokens

Use {first_name} and {company} exactly. Anything the coach fills in by hand goes in [square brackets].

## Test next (not proven yet)

- Proof note against a blank invite on the same list, pooled across coaches, counting meetings.
- A pain hook against a growth hook in the note.
- A short video or voice note in message 2 or 3.
- Message 1 in the ad shape against a plain one-question message.

One coach's test can't settle these: at about 1 interested reply per 100 requests, a clear answer needs thousands of people per side. BCA runs the big tests.

## Open Profile message

Owners with an Open Profile can be messaged without connecting. Send them Message 1 (the ad shape), very nearly word for word: the same two outcomes, the same proof and the same ask. Only the opening changes, because there is no "thanks for connecting": use the connection request's opening line instead ("Hi {first_name}, I see you run a ..."). It sits under its own heading, not in the sequence; the page shows it as the Open tab on the connection request.

A B version of the connection request is written only when asked for, under its own heading "Connection request B": the same rules, a different angle. Get Clients sends A and B to half the list each.
