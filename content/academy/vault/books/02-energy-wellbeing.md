*By Matthew Walker*

**The big idea:** Sleep is the single most effective thing you can do for your brain and body each day. Cutting it short costs you memory, mood, health and performance.

## Key ideas

**Deep sleep files your memories.** During the day, your brain stores new information in a short-term holding area. Deep sleep moves it into long-term storage. Most deep sleep happens early in the night, so going to bed late cuts it short.

**Light sleep clears space.** Light sleep empties the holding area so you can learn again tomorrow. Most of it comes at the end of the night, so an early alarm after five hours cuts your ability to learn.

**REM sleep sparks creativity.** Dreaming sleep links ideas in new ways and calms the emotions from the day.

**Sleep loss is a performance killer.** After a few nights of six hours, people perform as badly as someone who stayed up all night, and they don't notice.

**Simple ways to sleep better:**
- Go to bed and wake up at the same time every day.
- Keep the bedroom cool, around 18°C.
- Dim lights and avoid screens in the last hour.
- Cut caffeine after midday and limit alcohol.
- A hot shower before bed helps your core temperature drop.

## Use it with clients

- Many owners wear long hours as a badge. Show them that sleep is a performance tool, and that their worst decisions often come on little sleep.
- When an owner reports poor focus or a short temper, ask about sleep before anything else.

## Try it yourself

Pick a fixed bedtime and wake-up time and keep them for seven days, including the weekend. Notice your energy by day five.

*By James Nestor*

**The big idea:** You take about 26,000 breaths a day. Breathing through your nose, slowly and lightly, changes how you feel, think and sleep.

## Key ideas

**Breathe through your nose.** James Nestor plugged his nose for ten days. His stress rose, his blood pressure jumped 13 points and his focus fell. Within hours of nose breathing again, he felt clear and calm. Your nose filters, warms and moistens air and helps you absorb 10 to 15% more oxygen.

**Breathe slower.** Most of us breathe too fast. A calm rhythm of about 5.5 seconds in and 5.5 seconds out balances your nervous system.

**Breathe less.** Overbreathing leaves you tense. Light, quiet breaths help your body use oxygen better.

**Use your breath to switch state.** A long, slow out-breath calms you down. Short, strong breaths wake you up.

## Use it with clients

- Owners under pressure often shallow-breathe through meetings. Teach them a minute of slow nose breathing before a big call or tough conversation.
- Suggest a breathing reset between back-to-back meetings to stop stress building over the day.

## Try it yourself

Several times today, ask "Am I breathing through my nose?" Before your next important call, take one minute of slow breaths: five seconds in, five seconds out.

*By John Ratey*

**The big idea:** Exercise is the best thing you can do for your brain. It speeds up learning, lifts mood and fuels creativity.

## Key ideas

**Exercise feeds your brain.** When you exercise, your body releases a protein called BDNF. Ratey calls it "Miracle-Gro for the brain". It helps brain cells grow and connect, so you learn faster.

**Exercise boosts creativity.** BDNF floods the part of your brain that links new information with old memories. That's where new ideas come from. Many people have their best ideas on a walk or run.

**Exercise beats stress.** A workout burns off stress hormones and leaves you calmer and more resilient.

**Exercise lifts mood.** Regular exercise works on anxiety and low mood. It raises the same brain chemicals many medicines target.

**Time it well.** Exercise before learning or creative work to get the biggest boost.

## Use it with clients

- Owners who say they're too busy to exercise are often the ones who need it most. Frame it as a business tool for sharper thinking and better decisions.
- Suggest a walking meeting or a short walk before strategy sessions.

## Try it yourself

Before your next block of important work, do 20 minutes of exercise that gets your heart rate up. Notice how your focus compares to a normal day.

*By Jim Loehr and Tony Schwartz*

**The big idea:** Manage your energy, not your time. Top performers work in short bursts of intense focus followed by real recovery.

## Key ideas

**Recovery separates the best.** Jim Loehr studied top tennis players. Between points, the best players ran short rituals that dropped their heart rate by up to 20 beats per minute. Lower-ranked players stayed wound up and made more errors late in the match.

**Work in sprints.** Focus hard for 90 minutes or less, then take a real break. Long, unbroken days drain you and lower the quality of your work.

**Energy has four sources.**
- **Physical:** sleep, food, movement.
- **Emotional:** positive feelings and relationships.
- **Mental:** focus and attention.
- **Spiritual:** a sense of purpose.

**Build rituals.** Willpower runs out. Rituals, like a fixed time to exercise or a walk after lunch, protect your energy without effort.

## Use it with clients

- Owners often run all day without a break and wonder why afternoons are poor. Help them plan focus blocks and short recovery breaks.
- Look at energy across the week, not just the diary. Which meetings drain them? Which tasks energise them?

## Try it yourself

Work in 90-minute blocks today with a 10-minute break between each one. Step away from screens during the break.

*By Tom Rath*

**The big idea:** Tom Rath studied 2,600 ideas for better days. Three things separate a fully charged day from an average one: meaning, positive interactions and physical health.

## Key ideas

**Meaning: see who your work helps.** When radiologists saw a photo of the patient with the scan, their accuracy improved by 46%. Keep a reminder of who your work helps where you can see it.

**Interactions: more good than bad.** You need three to five positive moments to outweigh each negative one. Aim for 80% of your conversations to focus on what's going right.

**Health: small choices add up.** Every meal, every hour of sitting and every night of sleep moves your energy up or down. Stand and move often. Eat to fuel the afternoon, not just to fill up.

**Charge your day early.** Start with a small act of meaning, a positive conversation and a healthy choice.

## Use it with clients

- Owners who feel drained often lose sight of the people they help. Ask them to share a recent client win with the team.
- Encourage owners to open meetings with what's going well before what's broken.
- Look at the owner's typical day. Where could they add movement or better food?

## Try it yourself

Put a photo or note of someone your work helps where you'll see it every day. Start three conversations this week with something going right.

*By Hal Elrod*

**The big idea:** Your success rarely outgrows your personal development. Six simple habits each morning, which Elrod calls the SAVERS, help you become the person who gets the results you want.

## Key ideas

**S - Silence.** Start with a few minutes of calm instead of your phone. Meditation or quiet breathing sets a peaceful tone for the day.

**A - Affirmations.** Say what you'll become and why it matters, out loud and with conviction. Repetition builds belief, and belief drives action.

**V - Visualisation.** Picture yourself doing the work well, not just the end result. See yourself making the call or giving the talk.

**E - Exercise.** Even a few minutes of movement wakes up body and mind.

**R - Reading.** Read 10 pages of a useful book. Over a year that's around 18 books.

**S - Scribing.** Write in a journal: what you're grateful for, what you learned, what matters today.

**Short on time?** Do one minute of each and you have a six-minute miracle morning.

## Use it with clients

- Owners who start the day in their inbox start reactive. A short morning routine helps them start on purpose.
- Suggest owners add a few minutes of planning to the scribing step: their top three priorities for the day.

## Try it yourself

Tomorrow, try the six-minute version: one minute each of silence, affirmation, visualisation, exercise, reading and journaling.

*By Robin Sharma*

**The big idea:** The hour from 5 to 6am is your "victory hour". It's quiet and free of distraction, so it's the best time to build yourself before the world wakes up.

## Key ideas

**Use the 20/20/20 formula.**
- **5:00 to 5:20 - Move.** Sweat hard for 20 minutes. Exercise floods your brain with chemicals that sharpen focus and help you learn.
- **5:20 to 5:40 - Reflect.** Plan, journal or meditate. Write down your goals and how you'll make today count.
- **5:40 to 6:00 - Grow.** Read, listen to a podcast or study something that makes you better.

**Make it easy to start.** Sleep in your workout clothes or lay them out. Put your alarm across the room. Remove every excuse the night before.

**Protect your evening.** An early start needs an early night. Switch off screens an hour before bed.

**It takes 66 days.** New routines feel hard, then messy, then automatic. Push through the first few weeks.

## Use it with clients

- Owners often say they have no time to think. An early hour before the phone starts ringing gives them space to plan and work on the business.
- The 5am time isn't the point. The point is a protected first hour. Help owners find theirs.

## Try it yourself

For one week, protect your first hour of the day. Spend 20 minutes moving, 20 reflecting and 20 learning before you open email.

*By Aubrey Marcus*

**The big idea:** To own the day, own the morning. A few simple actions after waking set your energy for everything that follows.

## Key ideas

**Hydrate first.** You lose a lot of water overnight, and even mild dehydration causes headaches, low mood and poor focus. Before coffee, drink a large glass of water with a pinch of sea salt and a squeeze of lemon.

**Get light early.** Morning sunlight sets your body clock. It helps you feel awake now and sleepy tonight. Step outside for a few minutes soon after you wake.

**Move a little.** A short walk, stretch or a few exercises gets blood flowing and wakes up your brain.

**Delay caffeine.** Wait an hour or so after waking. Your body is already producing its own wake-up hormones, and coffee works better once they settle.

**Plan the rest of the day.** Marcus covers food, focus, training and sleep with the same idea: small, smart choices across the day add up to high energy.

## Use it with clients

- Owners who rely on coffee to get going and crash in the afternoon often skip the basics. Water, light and movement first.
- Small energy changes are a quick win early in a working relationship. They build trust for bigger changes.

## Try it yourself

Tomorrow: a big glass of water, five minutes outside and a short stretch before coffee. Notice how mid-morning feels.

*By Kelly McGonigal*

**The big idea:** Stress only harms you when you believe it does. See stress as energy that helps you rise to a challenge and it becomes a strength.

## Key ideas

**Mindsets change outcomes.** A short message about belonging changed students' grades, health and happiness for three years. Beliefs about stress work the same way.

**Belief decides the damage.** In one large study, high stress raised the risk of early death only for people who believed stress was harmful. People with high stress who saw it as helpful did fine.

**Your stress response is trying to help.** A racing heart sends more energy to your brain and body. Breathing faster brings in more oxygen. Read these signs as "my body is getting ready".

**Stress makes you social.** Stress releases oxytocin, which pushes you to connect and seek support. Reaching out to others turns stress into courage.

**Find the meaning.** Stress shows up around things you care about. Linking it to your values makes it easier to carry.

## Use it with clients

- When an owner says "I'm so stressed", help them name what they care about underneath it. That's often the real conversation.
- Before a big pitch or hard meeting, reframe nerves as readiness: "This is your body getting you ready."

## Try it yourself

Next time you feel stress rise, say "My body is helping me rise to this." Then name the thing you care about that's causing it.

*By Dale Carnegie*

**The big idea:** Worry is like a rocking chair. It gives you something to do but gets you nowhere. Clear thinking and action solve problems. Worry just drains your energy.

## Key ideas

**Analyse the worry.** Write down two questions: "What am I worried about?" and "What can I do about it?" List at least three actions. Pick the best one and start. Action makes worry shrink.

**Accept the worst, then improve on it.** Willis Carrier, inventor of air conditioning, faced a failing project. He asked what the worst outcome could be, accepted it, and then worked calmly to make things better than that. Once you accept the worst, you stop panicking and start fixing.

**Live in day-tight compartments.** Close the door on yesterday and tomorrow. Focus on today's work. Most worries are about a future that never arrives.

**Keep busy.** It's hard to worry when you're fully absorbed in useful work.

**Check the odds.** Most of what we fear never happens. Ask how likely it really is.

## Use it with clients

- When an owner is stuck worrying, walk them through the two questions and get one action on paper before the session ends.
- For big fears like losing a key client or a cash crunch, use Carrier's method: name the worst case, accept it, then plan to beat it.

## Try it yourself

Write down your biggest worry right now. Answer the two questions, list three actions and take the first one today.

*By Susan David*

**The big idea:** Bottling emotions up or brooding on them both make them stronger. Emotional agility means noticing your feelings, naming them and choosing actions that match your values.

## Key ideas

**Are you a bottler or a brooder?** Bottlers push feelings away and keep busy. Years later they're often stuck in the same situation. Brooders replay feelings over and over until the feelings take over.

**1. Name it.** Use precise words. "Stressed" might really be disappointed, lonely or nervous. Naming the exact feeling makes it easier to handle.

**2. Step back.** Say "I'm having the thought that I'm failing" instead of "I'm failing." The words create distance between you and the thought.

**3. Find the value.** Emotions point at what you care about. Frustration with a team member may show how much you value quality.

**4. Take a small step.** Make one small change that moves you towards your values. Tiny tweaks add up.

## Use it with clients

- Owners often bottle stress until it spills out at staff or family. Give them space to name what they're really feeling.
- Use the "I'm having the thought that..." line when an owner is stuck in a story like "I'll never get out of the day-to-day."

## Try it yourself

Next time a strong feeling hits, write down three precise words for it. Then ask what it tells you about what you value.

*By Phil Stutz and Barry Michels*

**The big idea:** Two psychotherapists share simple visual tools that help you push through fear, avoidance and anger. They feel odd at first. Try each one a few times before judging.

## Key ideas

**The Reversal of Desire (for avoidance).** When you're putting off a hard task:
1. Picture the pain as a cloud in front of you.
2. Silently shout "Bring it on!" and move into it.
3. Inside the cloud, say "I love pain."
4. Feel it push you out the other side as you say "Pain sets me free."

**Active Love (for anger).** When someone makes you angry, picture sending them pure love until you feel calm. You free yourself from replaying the fight.

**Inner Authority (for insecurity).** Before a nerve-racking moment, picture your insecure self as a figure beside you. Turn and face the audience together. You stop hiding the part of you that feels unsure.

**Grateful Flow (for worry).** When your mind spirals, list small things you're grateful for until the worry loosens.

## Use it with clients

- Many owners avoid the same tasks again and again: chasing payment, hard conversations, sales calls. The Reversal of Desire gives them a quick way in.
- Use Active Love when an owner is consumed by a falling-out with a partner, client or employee.

## Try it yourself

Pick the task you've avoided longest. Use the Reversal of Desire before starting it today.

*By Dan Harris*

**The big idea:** News anchor Dan Harris had a panic attack live on air. He found meditation, and it made him about 10% happier. Meditation is simple exercise for the mind. Do the reps and the results come.

## Key ideas

**You react less.** Mindfulness creates space between what happens and how you respond. One business owner called being the calmest person in heated meetings his "superpower".

**You bounce back faster.** Harsh self-criticism doesn't drive success. Being firm but kind with yourself helps you recover from mistakes and keep going.

**You worry less.** Meditation helps you notice worry as just thoughts. You can still strive while knowing most of what you fear won't happen.

**How to start:**
1. Sit comfortably and close your eyes.
2. Notice your breath, the in and the out.
3. When your mind wanders, gently bring it back. Each return is one rep.
4. Start with five minutes a day.

## Use it with clients

- Owners who snap at their team or make rushed calls under pressure gain a lot from a few minutes of daily practice.
- Frame meditation as a performance tool, not something spiritual. Many sceptical owners respond to that.

## Try it yourself

Meditate for five minutes a day for the next week. Use a timer and count each return to the breath as a rep.

*By Shawn Achor*

**The big idea:** Most people think success brings happiness. It works the other way round. Happy brains perform better, so get happy first.

## Key ideas

**Happiness boosts performance.** Doctors primed with positive feelings made accurate diagnoses 19% faster. Optimistic salespeople outsold others by 56%. Positive emotions make your brain learn and solve problems better.

**Five quick boosts:**
- **Use a strength.** Doing something you're good at gives a burst of positivity.
- **Write three good things.** Each night, write three things that went well. Your brain learns to scan for positives.
- **Help someone.** Small acts of kindness lift your mood as much as theirs.
- **Exercise.** A short workout lifts mood for hours.
- **Meditate.** A few minutes a day builds calm and focus.

**Move the goalposts.** If happiness only comes after the next win, you'll never get there, because the goalposts keep moving.

**Lean on people.** Strong relationships are the best predictor of happiness and help you cope with stress.

## Use it with clients

- Owners who say "I'll relax once we hit target" often stay stressed forever. Help them build small daily boosts now.
- Suggest a "three good things" round at the end of team meetings to shift the culture.

## Try it yourself

Every night this week, write down three things that went well and why. Notice your mood by the end of the week.

*By Marcus Aurelius*

**The big idea:** Marcus Aurelius ran the Roman Empire through war, betrayal and plague. His private journal shows the Stoic practices he used to stay calm, fair and effective.

## Key ideas

**Expect difficulty.** Each morning Marcus reminded himself: "Today I shall meet interference, ingratitude, insolence, disloyalty and selfishness." Picturing trouble in advance meant it didn't knock him off balance. Imagine the hard meeting going badly, then rehearse how you'll handle it.

**Focus on what you control.** You can't control other people or events. You can control your judgements and your actions. Spend your energy there.

**Take the view from above.** Picture yourself from high above, then your city, your country, the planet. Problems shrink when you see how small they are in the bigger picture.

**Remember you will die.** Life is short. Use that to drop trivial worries and do what matters today.

**Do your job well.** Marcus asked himself each day whether he was doing the work of a good person and a good leader.

## Use it with clients

- Before a hard conversation with a team member or client, have the owner picture the worst version and plan their response. They walk in calmer.
- When an owner is consumed by a small setback, use the view from above to restore perspective.

## Try it yourself

Tomorrow morning, spend two minutes picturing the hardest moment of your day going badly. Decide how you'll respond calmly.

*By Ryan Holiday*

**The big idea:** A short dose of Stoic thinking each morning steadies your mind for the day. Five ideas, one for each workday, help manage stress and focus on what matters.

## Key ideas

**Monday - The one thing I control is my mind.** You don't control your body, other people or your day. You control how you reason and respond. That's freeing, because once you've done your best, you can let the result go.

**Tuesday - Focus on the process.** Results depend on many things outside your control. Do the next step well and the results follow.

**Wednesday - Obstacles are fuel.** Each problem is a chance to practise patience, courage or creativity.

**Thursday - Less, but better.** Ask "Is this essential?" before taking on anything new.

**Friday - Review the week.** Like the Stoics, look back each evening or week: What did I do well? Where did I fall short? What will I do differently?

## Use it with clients

- Owners often take on stress about things they can't change, like the economy or a competitor. Monday's idea refocuses them on their choices.
- Build a Friday review into the owner's week. Fifteen minutes on what worked and what didn't beats any course.

## Try it yourself

Each workday this week, read one of the five ideas before you open email. Do the Friday review on Friday afternoon.

*By Ryan Holiday*

**The big idea:** Stillness, a calm and steady mind, sparks new ideas, sharpens judgement and helps you keep going. Build it in three areas: mind, body and spirit.

## Key ideas

**Mind: empty it on paper.** Writer Julia Cameron starts each day with "morning pages": three pages of whatever is in her head, written by hand. It clears the mental clutter so you face the day with clear eyes.

**Mind: limit inputs.** Constant news and notifications stop you thinking. Choose what you let in.

**Body: build routines and walk.** Simple routines remove small decisions. Long walks clear the head and spark ideas. Many great thinkers walked every day.

**Body: rest.** Sleep, hobbies and time off aren't weakness. They're where good thinking happens.

**Spirit: know what matters.** Get clear on your values and say no to things that break them. Look for what's enough instead of chasing more.

## Use it with clients

- Owners rarely get quiet time to think. Encourage a weekly thinking walk or a slot with no phone and no inbox.
- When an owner is overwhelmed, a page of free writing often shows them what's really bothering them.

## Try it yourself

Tomorrow morning, write three pages by hand of whatever comes into your head before you look at your phone.
