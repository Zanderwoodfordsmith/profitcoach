Your phone, desktop and browser are built to pull at your attention. This video shows Zander's whole system for turning that around, so the only things in front of you are your goals and the work that moves you towards them. Take what works for you; the next lessons show each part step by step.

## Start with your calendar

1. **Block sleep first.** At least eight hours, at the same time every day, weekends included.
2. **Add your fixed routines.** Morning focus time, exercise, meals, and an hour and a half to relax before bed.
3. **Plan tomorrow tonight.** List what you want to get done and block time for it, on paper or in a tool like Google Tasks or Sunsama.

This becomes your default diary. You decide once, in advance, so during the day the only question is "am I doing what I planned?"

## Clear the clutter

- **Browser:** a clean bookmarks bar with only what you use each week, and a start page that points you at your goals.
- **Desktop:** no loose files (keep them in Google Drive) and only your daily apps in the dock.
- **Phone:** your goals on the lock screen, a home screen with just your calendar, and every other app out of sight.

## Work in focus blocks

Zander uses a timer for 53 minutes of focused work and a 17-minute break, three times in a row. For the best break, get outside, walk and talk to someone. Focus music, like Brain.fm, helps some people settle into work.

## Block what pulls you away

Use the Freedom app or a browser extension to block distracting sites during work hours. Add extensions that hide the Facebook feed and YouTube's recommended videos, and a speed controller so you can watch training videos faster.

## Use it with clients

Owners who say they have no time often have a phone that interrupts them every few minutes. Walk them through this set-up in a session and help them win back a few hours a week.

Setting up your phone well might be one of the biggest time savers in your business. Every buzz pulls you out of deep work, and it takes around 15 minutes to get back in. This video shows how to make your phone a tool you use on purpose.

## Make your goals the first thing you see

1. Set your **lock screen** to an image of your goals or targets.
2. Keep your **first home screen** empty apart from one app at the bottom, like your calendar or goals notes.

## Hide everything else

1. Delete every app you don't need, including email. Email is ten times faster on a computer.
2. Put the apps you use daily on the second screen. Eight at most.
3. Put everything else in an "Extras" folder on the third screen.

## Turn off the noise

1. **Notifications:** in **Settings > Notifications**, turn them off for every app that isn't essential, even apps you like. Check messages on your break.
2. **Do Not Disturb:** in **Settings > Focus**, schedule Do Not Disturb to come on automatically through your peak work hours and from the evening until morning. Allow calls from Favourites, and repeated calls, if people need to reach you in an emergency.
3. **Screen Time:** check **Settings > Screen Time** each week and aim to bring the number down.

## Keep it out of reach

When you do deep work, put your phone in another room or a drawer. It's far easier to remove the temptation than to resist it.

## Two extras

- Use the same browser on your phone and computer, signed in to the same account, so bookmarks and history match.
- The Freedom app (next lessons) can block social media on your phone during set hours.

The video is from 2020, so some menu names on your phone may differ, but every setting is still there.

Make your computer's desktop calm and clear, so you go straight to the right work each morning. The video shows a Mac, but the same ideas work on Windows.

## Clear your desktop

1. Move every file off your desktop into one folder called "Desktop tidy-up".
2. Sort that folder by file type, like spreadsheets, images and videos.
3. Book time to file them properly, ideally in Google Drive (see the Google Drive lesson).
4. Set your desktop background to an image of your goals, or something calming.

## Keep only daily apps

1. Remove every app from the dock that you don't use daily. Open the rest with a quick search (Spotlight on a Mac, Start on Windows).
2. Hide the dock: **System Settings > Desktop & Dock > Automatically hide and show the Dock**.
3. Hide the menu bar too, if you like: **System Settings > Control Centre > Automatically hide and show the menu bar**.

## Turn off notifications

Go to **System Settings > Notifications** and switch off everything you don't need. Or schedule Do Not Disturb (under **Focus**) to cover your whole working day.

## Useful extras

- **Magnet** (Mac) or the built-in snap layouts (Windows) put two windows side by side in one click.
- A night-light setting, like **Night Shift** on a Mac, cuts blue light in the evening.
- **Shift** pulls several inboxes and chat apps into one window, though one email account is better.

## End each day clean

Empty your Downloads folder and Trash, and file anything left on the desktop. You start the next morning with a clear screen and a clear head.

The video uses older Mac menu names (System Preferences). The settings above use today's names.

Set up your browser so you find what you need fast and the sites built to distract you can't pull you in. The video uses Google Chrome.

## Use one signed-in browser

Sign in to Chrome with the same account on your computer and your phone. Bookmarks, passwords and extensions then follow you everywhere.

## Clean up your bookmarks

1. Delete bookmarks you don't use.
2. Save articles and information in a notes app instead of as bookmarks.
3. Keep the bookmarks bar for things you use every week, grouped into a few folders like "Active work", "Business" and "Personal".

## Set a start page that focuses you

In **Settings > On start-up**, choose **Open a specific page or set of pages** and add the page that keeps you on track, like the Academy's Classroom or your goals.

## Add these extensions

Search the Chrome Web Store for each one and click **Add to Chrome**:

- **News Feed Eradicator** hides the Facebook feed but keeps groups and messages.
- **Unhook** (or a similar extension) hides YouTube's recommended videos and comments.
- **Video Speed Controller** lets you watch any video at up to 3 or 4 times speed. Press S to slow down and D to speed up.
- **A website blocker** for the sites that eat your time, if you don't use Freedom (next lesson).
- **Your password manager's extension** (see the password lesson).

The video also adds the Evernote Web Clipper; use the clipper for whichever notes app you use.

## The idea behind it

It's far easier to remove the temptation than to resist it. Each extension takes a minute to install and saves you hours.

[Freedom](https://freedom.to) blocks distracting websites and apps on your phone and computer on a schedule you set once. You stop relying on willpower, because the distraction simply isn't there.

## Set it up

1. Sign up at freedom.to. There's a free trial, then a yearly plan.
2. Click **Add Device** and install Freedom on your computer and phone.
3. Create a **blocklist**. Start with the built-in distracting websites list, then add your own time-wasters.
4. Add a **recurring session**: pick the blocklist, the devices, the times and the days.

## Ideas for schedules

- **Start with your best four hours** of focus each day and block everything distracting then. Stretch it once that feels normal.
- **Add an evening session** on your phone, say from 8pm until midnight, so you aren't scrolling social media before bed.
- **Make a red list** of sites you never want to visit and block them all the time.

## Make it hard to undo

- Turn on **Locked Mode** so you can't edit blocklists or devices during a session.
- If you need one page, like the Profit Coach community, add it as a **website exception** before the session starts.

Within a week your brain stops reaching for the blocked sites, and the urge fades.

Newsletters and automatic emails you no longer read quietly eat your time. With 40 of them sending two emails a week, at two minutes each, you lose almost a month of work a year. Clearing them takes five minutes.

## A note on Unroll.me

The video uses Unroll.me. It works by reading your inbox, and in 2017 it came out that its owner sold data from users' emails. Today you can do the same job without it:

- **Gmail:** open the menu and choose **Manage subscriptions** to see every sender and unsubscribe in one click.
- **Outlook and Apple Mail:** use the **Unsubscribe** link that shows at the top of most newsletters.

## Clear your inbox

1. List every newsletter or automatic sender you get.
2. Unsubscribe from anything you don't read.
3. Keep only the few you value.
4. From now on, when you sign up to get a free tool or guide, download it and unsubscribe straight away.

## Keep it clear

- Use one email account for the business.
- Check email once a day if you can, late in the afternoon after your deep work.
- If something takes less than two minutes, deal with it there and then.

Put the time you win back into finding clients.

This short video has no sound. It shows [Sunsama](https://www.sunsama.com)'s evening planning routine, which turns tomorrow into a plan you can follow.

## The routine

1. **What do you want to get done tomorrow?** Pull in tasks from your to-do list or project tool.
2. **What can wait?** Move anything that isn't essential to the day after, or later.
3. **Finalise your plan.** Put the tasks in order and drag each one into a time slot on your calendar.

The result is a day blocked out in advance, so you start each morning knowing exactly what to work on.

## No Sunsama?

The same routine works on paper or in Google Calendar. Spend ten minutes each evening choosing tomorrow's tasks, cutting what can wait and blocking time for the rest. The Set Up Your Digital Environment lesson shows a version with Google Tasks.
