Pick how your coaching business is set up legally. Most coaches trade as a limited company in the UK or an LLC in the USA, but it's your call. Talk it through with an accountant.

## When to do this

After your marketing is running. It's a small decision and you can change it later, so leave it for a Friday evening when you have no energy left for anything else.

## Sole trader or limited company

- **Sole trader.** You and the business are the same in law. Tax and admin are simpler, but you're personally liable for all business debts.
- **Limited company (UK) or LLC (USA).** The business is its own legal entity, so your personal assets are protected. Tax is more complex and you file company accounts each year.

## Next steps

1. Read the comparison above and decide what matters most to you: simplicity or protection.
2. Speak to an accountant. Ask in the community if you want a recommendation.
3. Register the business and keep going with your marketing.

This lesson covers the UK and USA and isn't legal or tax advice.

A business bank account keeps business money apart from your own. Your records stay clean, tracking income and costs gets easy, and clients pay your business name, which looks more professional.

## When to do this

Once your marketing is running. A client paying into your personal account at the start is a nice problem to have, so this can wait for a quiet Friday evening.

## Set it up

1. **Compare a few options.** Look at traditional banks and app-based business accounts like Revolut Business or Wise. In the UK, Business Coach Academy uses Revolut. Pick one with good support.
2. **Get your documents ready.** You need photo ID and proof of address. If you've set up a limited company or LLC, you also need your business registration documents.
3. **Apply online.** Most accounts open within a day or two.

An hour of research is plenty. This lesson covers the UK and USA and isn't financial advice.

Insurance gives you a safety net if a client ever claims your advice cost them money. In years of working with hundreds of coaches, we've rarely heard of it happening, but it's worth having in place.

## When to do this

After your marketing is live. Look into professional indemnity insurance properly, but keep it from holding you back.

## The three to know about

- **Professional indemnity insurance** covers claims that your advice or guidance was negligent or wrong. This is the main one for most coaches.
- **Public liability insurance** covers accidents or injuries to the public on your business premises. If you coach online or at clients' offices, you may not need it.
- **Employers' liability insurance** applies once you take on employees. In the UK it's a legal requirement then.

## Next steps

1. Research professional indemnity cover first.
2. Speak to an insurance broker, or ask the community for an introduction.
3. Read what each policy covers, buy what you need and renew it each year.

What you need varies by country. This lesson isn't legal or insurance advice.

Know which taxes apply to your business so you can stay compliant and ask your accountant the right questions. This lesson is a checklist of what to raise with them.

## When to do this

A few weeks after your marketing is up and running. You won't turn on your marketing and sign a client an hour later without noticing, so get going first.

## UK

- **VAT** only applies once you earn more than £90,000 in 12 months (the threshold at the time of recording; check GOV.UK for the current figure). After that, you charge VAT to UK businesses.
- **Corporation tax** on your company's profits, if you trade as a limited company.
- **Salary versus dividends.** How you pay yourself changes the tax you pay, and so does a director's loan.

## USA

- **Federal and state taxes.**
- **Self-employment tax.**
- **S corporation election**, which can lower your tax once profits grow.

## Next steps

1. Write down which of these apply to you.
2. Take the list to an accountant. Ask in the community if you want a recommendation.
3. Put aside money for tax every time a client pays you, and keep your records up to date.

This lesson covers the UK and USA and isn't tax advice.
