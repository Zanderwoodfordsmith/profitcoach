Tech is how one coach runs a full practice. It books calls, sends reminders, follows up with prospects and keeps every detail in one place. The learning curve is real, and it's short. This lesson gives you six ways to get past "I'm bad at tech".

## Why it matters

Picture following up with every interested prospect 11 times by hand, or sending thousands of connection requests one at a time. Automation does it in a few clicks. You only need to learn which clicks.

## Six ways to get over tech trauma

1. **Change your mindset.** Technology is a tool that saves you hours. Swap "I'm bad at tech" for "I can learn new things". You can, at any age.
2. **Take baby steps.** Learn one tool, or one feature, at a time. Go through the training in order and aim for one new feature a week.
3. **Use the guides.** Most steps have a short video and screenshots. Rewatch the one-minute video while you do the task.
4. **Practise often.** Build a small tech task into each day. Practise with dummy data, or send a test message to a teammate, until it feels normal.
5. **Stay positive and patient.** Everyone starts as a beginner. When something seems broken, it's almost always a missed step, so go back through the guide one step at a time.
6. **Use the team and the community.** Join the live calls, ask your coach and post in the community. Someone has already solved the problem you're stuck on.

## Celebrate the wins

Sent your first email from the CRM? Launched your first campaign? Post it in the community. Small wins add up fast.

Some of our most successful coaches started out hating tech. Pam jokes that she can't work a toaster, yet she grew to 47 one-to-one clients by learning just enough of the tools. If she can do it, so can you.

Keep every business file in one place, in a structure you can find your way around in seconds. This video shows how Zander sets up his own Google Drive.

## Set up your top-level folders

Number your folders so they sit in the order you want, not alphabetical order:

1. **Business** for everything you create for the business.
2. **Personal** for your own files.
3. **Admin** for bank, tax and company documents.
4. **Media library** for images, videos, logos and testimonials you reuse.
5. **Resource library** for material from training programmes, Profit Coach included.
6. **Public** for anything you share with others, so you always know what's shared.
7. **Collection box** for anything you need to save in a hurry. Sort it into the right folder later, when your energy is low.

## Go deeper inside each folder

Break Business down by how your business works. For a coach that might be clients first, then marketing, then your offers. Inside each one, break it down again so every file has an obvious home.

## Add shortcuts

Install Google Drive for desktop (the video calls it Google File Stream, its old name). Your Drive then shows up in Finder or File Explorer, and you can add shortcuts to the folders you open every day.

## Start small

You won't need many folders at first. Set up the top level now and add depth as your business grows. The video also mentions Evernote for quick notes; any notes app does the same job.

You share files all the time as a coach: a scorecard with a new client, a template, a session plan. Set the sharing right first time and you skip the "I can't open it" emails and keep client details private.

## Share with one person

1. Open the file in Google Drive and click **Share**.
2. Type the person's email address.
3. Pick their role: Viewer, Commenter or Editor.
4. Add a short note if it helps, then click **Send**.

## Pick the right role

- **Viewer** can read the file. Use it for guides and reference documents.
- **Commenter** can read and suggest changes. Use it for drafts you want feedback on.
- **Editor** can change the file. Use it for working documents you and a client fill in together.

## Share with a link

Under **General access**, choose **Anyone with the link** and anyone who has the link can open the file. Keep **Restricted** for anything with client numbers or personal details, and share those by email address instead.

## Give a client their own copy

For a template, send a copy link. The client gets their own file and your master stays clean. Copy the file's link and change the end of the address from `/edit` (and anything after it) to `/copy`. When the client opens the link, Google asks them to make their own copy.

## Stop sharing

Open **Share**, click the role next to the person's name and choose **Remove access**. To turn off a link, set **General access** back to **Restricted**. Do this when a client finishes working with you.

## Google's own guides

Google keeps these up to date when the screens change:

- [Share files from Google Drive](https://support.google.com/drive/answer/2494822)
- [Stop, limit or change sharing](https://support.google.com/drive/answer/2494893)

Zoom is where most coaching and sales calls happen. This video walks through creating an account, scheduling a call and recording it. It's from 2020, so the screens look different now, but the steps are the same.

## Create your account

Go to zoom.us, click **Sign up** and use your work email. The free plan is fine to start, but it caps meetings at 40 minutes. Upgrade to a paid plan once you run sales calls of 45 minutes or more.

## Schedule a call

1. In Zoom, open **Meetings** and click **Schedule a Meeting**.
2. Name it with the client's name first, for example "Gary and Zander: first conversation". They see it from their side.
3. Set the date, time and length you agreed, in the client's time zone.
4. Choose your settings (below) and click **Save**.
5. Add it to your calendar and invite the client by email.

## Settings that help

- Let the client join before you. Zoom tells you when they arrive.
- Use a new meeting ID for each call, not your personal meeting ID.
- Record every call automatically, and tell the client you're recording. The free plan saves recordings to your computer; paid plans save them to the cloud.

## Listen back

Recordings are how you get better at calls. Listen back, spot what to improve and post a clip in the community for feedback.

## Share one window and keep your notes in view

On a sales call you often want to show something while you follow your notes. Open what you want to show in its own browser window. In Zoom, click **Share**, pick that window rather than your whole screen, and click **Share**. The client sees only that window (it gets a green border), and your notes stay private on the rest of your screen. You can scroll in the shared window. If you click into another window, click straight back.

## Zoom's own guides

Zoom keeps these up to date when the screens change: [Creating and scheduling meetings](https://support.zoom.com/hc/en/category?id=kb_category&kb_category=44a2ba128720391089a37408dabb359f).

A password manager keeps every login, bank detail and card number in one locked vault and fills them in for you. You remember one strong master password instead of dozens of weak ones.

## A note on LastPass

The video uses LastPass. In 2022 hackers stole copies of LastPass users' encrypted vaults, so today we suggest **1Password** or **Bitwarden** instead. If you only use Apple or only use Google devices, the free password manager built into your phone and browser also works. The steps below are the same in all of them.

## Set it up

1. Install the password manager's app and browser extension.
2. Create your account and choose a master password. Write it down and keep it somewhere safe at home. Most password managers can't reset it for you.
3. Log in to a site you use often, like Google. When the password manager offers to save the login, say yes.
4. Next time you visit, click the extension and it fills in your details.
5. Keep going as you log in to each site. Your vault builds up in a week or two.

## Store more than passwords

Save bank accounts, card details, addresses and secure notes in the vault too. Everything sits in one searchable place, and you stop hunting for card numbers or old emails with logins in them.

## Make it safer

Turn on two-step login for the password manager itself and for your email. Then use the password manager to create a new, random password for every site.
