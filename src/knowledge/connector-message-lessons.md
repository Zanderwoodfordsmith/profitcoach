# Connector message lessons

What 38 real coach connection notes and Zander's feedback on them taught us. Summarised from `connector-message-feedback.csv` (the raw data stays there for admins; it is not loaded into prompts).

## The template that works

> Hi {first_name}, I see you run a UK [industry] company. Are you looking to [simple outcome]? I ask because [as ROLE at COMPANY / CLIENT] I used a simple system to [result: before → after] in [time] by [mechanism they already believe in]. Is this of interest?

Five parts, in this order: personalisation line, hook, "I ask because", proof with a mechanism, "Is this of interest?". Notes that dropped the template (no personalisation, no "I ask because", no "simple system", self-focused pitches) all landed under 1% or were sent back to be redone.

## What the best performers had (2%+ interest)

- A plain positive hook: "Are you looking to grow your profit?"
- A start and end state, not a bare gain: "-£76K to +£121K in 12 months".
- A short time frame: months, not many years.
- A mechanism the owner already believes in: "by getting off the tools and streamlining jobs".
- An exact industry callout: "I see you run a UK Construction Company".

## Lessons from the feedback, most common first

1. **Use before and after numbers.** "£5.1M to £10.2M in under 4 years" beats "+£5M". Pick odd, exact figures (£1.17M, not £1M+); they read as true.
2. **Keep the hook simple and positive.** Grow profit, increase sales, fill more rooms. Test a negative hook only as a variant. Avoid jargon or clever phrasing ("convert your know-how into sales", "supercharge your sales people").
3. **Write the hook in the owner's words.** Ask: is this what they lie awake thinking, or tell their partner? If not, simplify.
4. **The mechanism is the thing they think they need.** "Getting more from the existing team" or "balancing product enhancements" is too abstract. "By filling more rooms", "by cutting wasted cost", "by spending less time on site" are concrete.
5. **Say "simple system".** Not "approach", "framework" or "easy system" ("easy" sounds gimmicky).
6. **"I see you run..." beats titles.** "MD", "CEO" or "leader" narrows or misfires when the list is broad, and costs characters. Use an industry callout; company name only on a clean list.
7. **Make the proof relevant to the reader.** Name the role and company only if it shows the coach knows this industry ("as Head of Sales at..."). Big-corporate wins that feel unrelated (bank, global portfolio) put owners off; pick a result at their scale.
8. **Pick one hook and one proof.** Two hooks or a stack of results confuses. Save other proofs for testing.
9. **Make clear it's coaching, not an agency.** Strong revenue hooks can attract people who want done-for-you sales. Position the coaching angle in the second message or before the call.
10. **Short time frames are more believable.** "7 years" undersells even a huge result; choose the shorter win if there is one.
11. **Close with "Is this of interest?".** Not "Let's connect", "Curious?" or a long ask.
12. **Watch the 300-character limit.** Cut redundancy and long titles to make room for the proof.

## Red flags seen in weak notes

- No personalisation line or a vague one ("lead a business in Sustainability").
- A question the owner would never ask themselves.
- Proof without a number, or a result without a time frame.
- Talking about the coach's expertise instead of the owner's outcome.
- Selling a call or an assessment in the first message.
