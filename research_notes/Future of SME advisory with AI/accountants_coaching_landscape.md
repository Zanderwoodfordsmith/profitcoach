# Competitive landscape for SME advisory: accountants, coaches, fractional executives and AI (researched 26 Nov 2026)

Scope note: about 20 search/fetch calls. The ICF 2025 Global Coaching Study was read in full (text pulled from the PDF). Most other figures come from search-result summaries of primary pages, and these are flagged where the primary page could not be opened. Vendor reports (Xero, Intuit, CPA.com, BetterUp, Valence, EOS) carry an obvious interest in an "advisory boom" or "AI coaching" story. Read them as signals, not neutral measures.

## Accountants: the shift to advisory, AI automating compliance, how many firms deliver advisory, pricing, demand, and PE roll-ups

### Takeaway
Vendor and professional-body surveys all say accountants are moving into advisory, with AI pushing them there. Xero's US survey says 85% of practices "offer" client advisory services, up from 41% in 2023. CPA.com puts median CAS (client accounting services) growth at about 17%. But "offer" is a loose word, compliance still takes most of accountants' time (Intuit: 62%), and the UK picture is being reshaped by private equity. PE is consolidating mid-tier firms, and critics say this raises fees and pushes smaller owner-managed clients down the queue. That leaves a gap in the advice market for owner-led businesses.

### Cited Findings
**AI and the profession (ICAEW / Chartered Accountants Worldwide)**
- CAW/Ipsos global survey of chartered accountants (published Apr 2025, via ICAEW): 85% would use AI if given the chance. About 70% use public GenAI chatbots (ChatGPT, Gemini) at least once or twice a month. 52% name too few skills and too little training as the biggest barrier. 65% want professional bodies to lead on training and standards. The report expects AI to "enhance strategic advisory by improving accountants' ability to interpret data and provide actionable, tailored recommendations." — [ICAEW: Accountants embrace AI but need new skills and leadership (Apr 2025)](https://www.icaew.com/insights/viewpoints-on-the-news/2025/apr-2025/accountants-embrace-ai-but-need-new-skills-and-leadership); [CAW/Ipsos report PDF](https://www.ipsos.com/sites/default/files/ct/publication/documents/2025-04/CAW_AI%20in%20Accountancy_Apr2025.pdf)
- ICAEW Evolution of Mid-Tier Accountancy Firms 2025 (published 2 Jul 2025): 72% of mid-tier firms see AI, including GenAI, as a major trend. Only the snippet was seen. — [ICAEW mid-tier report PDF](https://www.icaew.com/-/media/corporate/files/technical/practice-resources/evolution-of-mid-tier-accountancy-firms-2025-report.ashx)
- Edelman DXI survey for CAW (Sep 2025), 1,725 finance decision-makers (director level and above) across 10 markets: accountants rank third globally for trust, behind doctors and engineers. Trust is the accountant's moat. — [ICAEW: Global trust in chartered accountants rises in AI world (Sep 2025)](https://www.icaew.com/insights/viewpoints-on-the-news/2025/sep-2025/global-trust-in-chartered-accountants-rises-in-ai-world)
- No standalone ICAEW survey on how many UK firms deliver advisory turned up in this search.

**Vendor practice reports (vendor bias: these companies sell to accountants)**
- Xero US State of the Industry 2025 (Jun 2025; 250 respondents; fieldwork by Quadrant Strategies, 19 Dec 2024 to 8 Jan 2025): 85% of practices offer client advisory services, up from 41% in Xero's 2023 survey. Nearly three-quarters reported higher profits. Main challenges: economic uncertainty (38%), the need to understand AI (36%), and clients expecting forward-looking strategic advice (35%). — [Xero US media release](https://www.xero.com/us/media-releases/us-state-of-the-industry-report-2025/)
- Xero UK Accounting and Bookkeeping Industry Report 2025 (250 UK respondents): 79% of practices reported higher revenue than the year before. Advisory is "growing in importance" but bookkeeping remains a core earner. One search summary said advisory makes up 32% of UK accountants' revenue, but a second check could not confirm the 32% against the UK report. Treat it as unverified. — [Xero UK industry report page](https://www.xero.com/uk/campaign/partner/industry-report/); [UK report PDF](https://brandfolder.xero.com/NE531UQB/as/r5g98sw38wt9fvb5pn9cxtx/855350-UK_Accounting_and_Bookkeeping_Industry_Report_2025-PDF)
- Xero UK Modern Practice Playbook 2026 (520 UK respondents, May 2026): advisory has the highest profit margin of any service UK firms offer, at 51%. Xero says the top firms are twice as profitable, "with AI adoption at the core." — [Xero media release: Modern Practice Playbook UK](https://www.xero.com/media-releases/modern-practice-playbook-uk/); [Playbook PDF](https://brandfolder.xero.com/NE531UQB/at/tgh6v2cvwr7bq8v6bvs2q57/UK_-_Xero_Modern_Practice_Playbook.pdf)
- Xero Canada 2025: adding new services was a main driver of profit and revenue growth, and the report is headlined "Shift to Advisory Services and Technology Adoption Drives 80% Optimism." — [Xero Canada release](https://www.xero.com/us/media-releases/canada-state-of-the-industry-report-2025/)
- Intuit QuickBooks 2025 Accountant Technology Report: 94% of accountants expect more strategic advisory work to grow firm revenue. Nearly 8 in 10 expect more competition for advisory clients over the next 12 months. Accountants spend 62% of their time on compliance work. Firms use 8 digital tools on average, and 89% say those tools need to work together better. - Xero UK data: only 49% of small businesses use accounting software, and only 40% of clients benefit from connected bank feeds. Many SMEs still lack clean data, which advisory and AI tools depend on. Figures come from a search summary of the Xero UK report. — [Xero UK industry report page](https://www.xero.com/uk/campaign/partner/industry-report/)
- Sage: no Sage practice or advisory report turned up in these searches.

**US CAS benchmarks (CPA.com / AICPA, also vendor-adjacent)**
- CPA.com/AICPA CAS Benchmark Survey (2024 edition, 2023 data): median CAS growth of 17%, and CAS is growing faster than total firm revenue. Median CAS revenue was $1,606,409, up 61% on the 2022 survey. Firms project 99% growth over three years at the median. Firms with significant CFO-level advisory revenue reportedly earned over 30% more monthly recurring revenue. — [CPA.com CAS Benchmark Survey PDF](https://www.cpa.com/sites/cpa/files/2024-12/2024-CAS-Benchmark-Survey.pdf); [CPA.com blog: Cracking the CAS growth code (May 2025)](https://www.cpa.com/blog/2025/05/05/cracking-cas-growth-code)

**Private equity roll-ups (UK and US)**
- UK is the most active PE market for accountancy in Europe, with over 40% of all PE-backed accountancy deals. Accountancy Europe expects PE to keep a major role "especially in markets with fragmented firm structures and a strong SME focus." — [Accountancy Europe: PE investments in accountancy firms](https://accountancyeurope.eu/publications/private-equity-investments-in-accountancy-firms/)
- Accountancy Daily Top 75 Firms Survey 2026: growth has shifted to PE-backed mid-tier firms making acquisitions aggressively. Dains made its sixth deal since IK Partners became its backer in 2025. One 2026 analysis counts 17 PE-backed firms in the Top 75, with combined fee income up nearly 20% year on year against a market average of 5%. Seen via search summary; the page returned 403. — [Accountancy Daily Top 75 2026](https://www.accountancydaily.co/top-75-firms-survey-2026-private-equity-drives-growth-and-ma-surge)
- Named UK deals in 2026: Affinia linked up with three UHY Hacker Young offices (Feb 2026), and RedBird Capital agreed to buy Affinia from Sovereign Capital (Apr 2026). Reported press figures, not confirmed terms: a Grant Thornton UK valuation of about £1.5bn, an Apax deal of about £700m, and Bridgepoint buying Interpath for about $800m. Some article dates in the results were inconsistent. — [Founder Capital: UK accountancy consolidation map](https://www.foundercapital.co.uk/insights/uk-accountancy-consolidation-map); [Accountancy Today acquisitions tag](https://www.accountancytoday.co.uk/tag/acquisition/)
- The PE model, per Founder Capital: buy a platform firm, invest in shared technology, back office and compliance, then bolt on smaller firms whose founders want to exit. There is a "long tail" of thousands of independent firms with £500k to £5m of recurring fees, with ageing founders and no institutional buyer paying attention. — [Founder Capital](https://www.foundercapital.co.uk/insights/uk-accountancy-consolidation-map)
- US: PE-owned CPA platforms are moving into buying advisory firms (Nov 2026). — [CPA Trendlines PE deal tracker (5 Nov 2026)](https://cpatrendlines.com/2026/11/05/pe-deal-tracker-cpa-platforms-accelerate-into-advisory/); PE-backed consolidation is "picking up steam." — [CFO Brew (4 Mar 2026)](https://www.cfobrew.com/stories/2026/03/04/pe-backed-public-accounting-consolidation-picks-up-steam)
- Accounting Today "State of PE in Accounting 2025": over two-thirds of partners in PE-backed firms are somewhat or very satisfied, against 15% of other staff. "It is a dumpster fire," said one director at a large firm. One respondent: "The typical business model is to double or triple the fees and reduce the customer service experience for clients." — [Accounting Today: PE in accounting firms: from 'dumpster fire' to excitement](https://www.accountingtoday.com/news/pe-in-accounting-firms-from-dumpster-fire-to-excitement); [Accounting Today: PE 'cancer' or cure](https://www.accountingtoday.com/news/pe-cancer-or-cure-for-accounting-firms)
- IFAC research (summarised in search) says PE roll-ups could mean higher fees for clients and a shift of investment toward advisory and away from audit and assurance. Raconteur's sources say long-standing mid-tier clients "may find themselves deprioritised, or priced out entirely," and one warns of a crash within 5 to 10 years as staff and clients leave. — [Raconteur: Private equity's accountancy binge risks implosion](https://www.raconteur.net/finance/private-equitys-accountancy-binge-risks-implosion); [AAT Comment on PE](https://www.aatcomment.org.uk/audience/members/what-private-equity-buying-up-accounting-firms-means-for-the-industry-and-employees/)

### Inferences
- Accountants hold the data and the trust (third most trusted profession), and their software vendors are pushing them hard into advisory. That makes them the most direct competitor for "trusted adviser to a £1M+ owner-led business." But "85% offer advisory" almost certainly includes light-touch work such as management accounts commentary and tax planning. With 62% of time still on compliance, few firms run a structured growth or operations programme with owners.
- PE roll-ups take accountancy capacity toward bigger clients, higher fees and standardised service. Owner-led firms at £1M to £10M turnover, served by a local partner who has now sold out, are the clients most likely to feel underserved. This is an opening for non-accountant advisers who sit next to the accountant rather than replace them. Coaches who partner with independent practices (the "long tail") could be a channel.
- AI first takes away the bookkeeping and compliance hours that pay for the accountant's relationship. Some firms will turn that into advisory. Others, especially PE-owned ones, will bank the margin. Either way, financial reporting becomes cheaper and more of a commodity, so value moves to interpretation, accountability and implementation.

### Gaps
- No reliable UK count of firms delivering paid, structured advisory (rather than merely "offering" it), and no UK advisory pricing benchmarks. ICAEW, ACCA, CA ANZ, Karbon and AICPA's own advisory surveys were not reached within the call budget.
- No independent (non-vendor) data on SME demand for advisory from accountants or willingness to pay.
- The Xero UK "32% of revenue from advisory" figure is unconfirmed.
- No AccountingWEB coverage surfaced. UK owner-client reactions to PE-owned firms are anecdotal.

## Fractional executives (fractional CFO/COO/CMO) growth in the UK and US

### Takeaway
Most sources describe strong growth, but nearly all the numbers come from fractional-CFO vendors or market-research marketing pages. There is no authoritative UK headcount. UK fractional CFO day rates of about £600 to £1,200 are the most usable price benchmark.

### Cited Findings
- UK fractional CFO day rates are "between £600 and £1,200 per day," with most engagements at £700 to £900 (vendor page). — [FD Capital: Fractional CFO pricing UK](https://www.fdcapital.co.uk/fractional-cfo-pricing-uk/)
- The CFO Centre has more than 750 fractional CFOs across 18 countries (firm-level figure, via search summary). — [Beyond Elevation: Fractional executive market 2026](https://beyondelevation.com/insights/fractional-executive-trend-2026-data)
- Claimed growth: the UK fractional market is growing 44% a year against 31% in the US (no primary source named). An IIM 2026 survey is cited as finding 25% of UK interim assignments are already fractional. A Deloitte survey is cited as finding 38% of mid-market companies used a fractional executive in the prior 12 months, up from 11% in 2021; this could not be checked against Deloitte. — [Beyond Elevation](https://beyondelevation.com/insights/fractional-executive-trend-2026-data)
- Global "fractional executive marketplace" put at $17.6bn in 2025 (market-research marketing page; low reliability). — [Dataintelo](https://dataintelo.com/report/fractional-executive-marketplace-market)
- ScaleUp Institute 2025 Annual Review: a record 44,595 UK scale-ups (firms growing turnover or headcount by more than 20% a year). This is a proxy for demand, not a fractional count. — via [Beyond Elevation](https://beyondelevation.com/insights/fractional-executive-trend-2026-data)

### Inferences
- Fractional CFOs and COOs compete for the same £1M+ owner budget, at roughly £700 to £900 a day, as a Profit Coach. They lead with functional expertise, while coaches lead with method and accountability. AI tools ("AI CFO" platforms, below) push down the price of the analysis part of fractional CFO work, which may tip fractional CFOs toward a more coach-like, relationship-led role.

### Gaps
- No primary UK or US headcount, revenue or growth data for fractional executives. The IIM survey and any Deloitte report should be checked directly.
- Fractional COO and CMO markets were not covered separately.

## Business coaching industry: size, income, AI use, franchises, implementation systems and peer groups

### Takeaway
ICF's 2025 study (conducted by PwC) shows global coaching growing: $5.34bn revenue, up 17%, and about 123k practitioners. But average coach income is modest ($49,283), Western Europe's coach numbers fell 7%, and 64% of coaches say winning and keeping clients is their top worry. Only 6% use AI-powered coaching tools, and 61% of Western European coaches use no digital coaching platform at all. "Small business" coaching is a tiny niche (about 2% of coaches) with below-average earnings, while coaches whose clients are mainly business owners earn $51,646. Peer and implementation brands (Vistage, EOS, ActionCOACH) are large and say they are growing, but nearly all their numbers are self-reported.

### Cited Findings
**ICF 2025 Global Coaching Study (PwC; final report modified 2 Sep 2025)** — [ICF 2025 Global Coaching Study final report PDF](https://icahq.com/icf/2025-global-coaching-study-final-report.pdf); [ICF executive summary](https://coachingfederation.org/resource/2025-icf-global-coaching-study-executive-summary/)
- Global revenue is $5.340bn, up 17% on the 2023 study. Average annual revenue per active coach is $49,283.
- There are 122,974 coach practitioners. The final report text says this is "a 13% increase" over 2023, while some secondary summaries say 15%; the report text is preferred. 90% have active clients.
- North America has 44,270 coaches (36%), up 29%, and $2.889bn revenue, with an average of $71,719 per coach and $297 an hour. **Western Europe has 28,558 coaches (23%), 7% fewer than in 2023**, $1.175bn revenue, an average of $44,712 per coach and $227 an hour.
- Global averages are $234 per one-hour session, 11.6 coaching hours a week and 12.4 active clients. Most coaches are part-time in practice.
- Experience matters a lot: coaches with 10+ years average $69,721. Baby Boomers average $60,323 against $33,553 for the youngest cohort.
- Income by main area of coaching: Executive $76,539 ($338/hr), Team $54,601, Leadership $51,736, **Small business $36,801 ($176/hr)**, Business/Organisations $39,898, Life Vision $20,879.
- Income by client type: Executive (CEO, CFO etc.) $82,949 ($357/hr). **Business owner or entrepreneur $51,646 ($220/hr, 10.7 clients, 12.6 hrs/week).** Personal client $22,303.
- Coaches with mostly sponsored clients (paid by an organisation, 76 to 100%) average $59,413. Coaches with mostly self-paying clients (76 to 100% "primary" clients) average $33,722.
- Specialism shares: Leadership is the largest area (about 36% in the global column). "Small business" is the main focus for about 2% of coaches across regions.
- Growth outlook: 59% expect revenue to rise in the next 12 months, mainly through more clients (60%) and more sessions (51%). Only 37% expect to raise fees. Western Europe is least optimistic (54% expect revenue growth).
- Biggest internal worry: **winning and keeping clients (64%)**, then reduced revenue (31%). Biggest external worries: corporate budget cuts (64%), inflation (50%), and **reputation of the coaching profession (39% globally; 45% in Western Europe)**.
- Technology and AI: only 19% invested in new technology in the past year, and 27% plan to in the next 1 to 3 years. 47% use digital coaching platforms, but just **6% use "AI-powered coaching and chatbots"** (4% in Western Europe). 53% use no digital platform (61% in Western Europe). 54% say better platforms and technology are needed to meet future client demand.
- Delivery: 87% coach over video, 73% in person. Coaches offer 2.9 extra services on average (training, consulting, mentoring and so on).

**AI policy from the coaching bodies**
- ICF AI Coaching Framework and Standards (v1.01, revised 8 Oct 2024): six domains built on the ICF Core Competencies, covering ethics, transparency, privacy, safety, accessibility and quality, with companion "Examples" and "Questions stakeholders should ask." — [ICF AI framework page](https://coachingfederation.org/resource/icf-artificial-intelligence-ai-coaching-framework-and-standards); [Framework PDF](https://coachingfederation.org/app/uploads/2024/11/ICF-AI-Reports-Framework-Standards.pdf)
- ICF white paper "Coaching + AI: A Partnership to Unleash Potential" (2026) recognises three modalities across coaching and AI and states that general-purpose LLMs used as coaches are "not designed to meet ICF coaching standards and code of ethics." In July 2026 ICF set up an Ethical AI Standards & Governance Initiative working group. Seen via search summary. — [ICF white paper PDF (Oct 2026)](https://coachingfederation.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/icf-ai-coaching-ai-partnership-unleash-potential.pdf)
- EMCC: no AI position statement turned up in search.

**Franchises, implementation systems and peer groups (mostly self-reported)**
- Vistage: about 45,000 members, reportedly grown from about 28,000 in 2022 under CEO Sam Reese (2026 profile, not primary). Its "members grow 2.2x faster" claim rests on 2017 Dun & Bradstreet data. — [Vistage](https://vistage.com/); [YesPress profile of Vistage](https://yespress.io/vistage-worldwide-inc)
- ActionCOACH: 83 countries, over 1,100 franchise partners, about 28,000 businesses coached weekly (2025 profile; sources disagree on scale). Its 98% recommendation rate is self-reported. — [YesPress profile of ActionCOACH](https://yespress.io/actioncoach-global); [Akta company profile](https://akta.pro/company-profiles/003fzso/actioncoach)
- EOS Worldwide: the homepage claims 850+ EOS Implementers and 280k+ companies; a conference page says 800+ and 250k+. Dates are unclear. EOS One, the official software "with AI built in," costs $10 per user per month. EOS's line on AI, from an implementer post: "Why AI makes EOS more important, not less." — [EOS Worldwide home](https://www.eosworldwide.com/home-2025); [EOS One](https://eosone.com/); [EOS implementer post](https://implementer.eosworldwide.com/implementer-post/why-ai-makes-eos-more-important-not-less/)
- Vistage: no dedicated AI coaching product found. AI appears as speaker content. — [YesPress](https://yespress.io/vistage-worldwide-inc)

### Inferences
- The UK and Western European coaching market is shrinking in coach numbers (down 7%) while North America grows. That fits a credibility and client-acquisition squeeze: 45% of Western European coaches worry about the profession's reputation, and 64% of all coaches say winning clients is their main problem. BCA's done-for-you lead generation goes straight at the most-cited pain point.
- Coaches who serve business owners earn about $51.6k on average. That is above the all-coach average but well below executive coaches ($83k), so it does not support a "£100k+ coach" claim without a stronger model behind it. "Small business coaching" as a labelled niche earns less ($36.8k), so placing coaches at £1M+ businesses matters.
- AI uptake among coaches is very low: 6% use AI coaching tools, 19% invest in technology at all. A coach armed with an in-app AI assistant and diagnostic is unusual today. The window may be short as EOS One, BetterUp and others build AI into their methods.
- The implementation-system brands (EOS, Scaling Up) frame AI as making their method "more important." The common defence is method plus human accountability plus community, with AI as an add-on, which is similar to BCA's own position.

### Gaps
- ICF 2023 study details were not pulled separately. The implied 2023 revenue (about $4.56bn) is derived from the 17% growth figure, not quoted.
- No independent data found on The Alternative Board, Shirlaws, FocalPoint, Scaling Up, Pinnacle, EO, YPO or TEC/Vistage UK membership trends, franchisee churn, or coach income spread beyond ICF averages. No median income was published in the parts read; ICF reports averages, which are likely skewed upward.
- No hard data on coach churn or how many coaches leave the profession.
- No UK-specific ICF breakdown; the data covers Western Europe as a whole.

## AI coaching products, AI vs human coach evidence, and "AI business advisor" startups for SMEs

### Takeaway
Enterprise AI coaching is well funded and in use (Valence/Nadia raised $50M in Sep 2025, BetterUp has an AI coach). The academic evidence (Terblanche et al.) suggests AI coaches can match human coaches on narrow goal attainment, but the stronger designs are new and small. For owner-led SMEs specifically, no clearly funded, traction-proven "AI business advisor" turned up. The money is going to AI agents that do back-office tasks (receptionists, AI CFO bookkeeping and forecasting) rather than AI that advises the owner.

### Cited Findings
- Terblanche, Molyn, de Haan and Nilsson (PLOS ONE, 2022): two matched randomised trials over 10 months, one with human coaches and a replication with the AI chatbot coach "Vici" (experimental group n=75, six months). The AI coach was as effective as human coaches at goal attainment by the end of the trials. This is a cross-study comparison, not a head-to-head trial. — [PLOS ONE via PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9212136); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35727801)
- de Haan, Terblanche and Nowack (Human Resource Development International, 2026): the first direct randomised comparison of accredited human coaches and automated AI coaches (N = 114), measuring goal attainment, wellbeing and self-efficacy. Results not seen; DOI 10.1080/13678868.2026.2633990. — reported in search results; [related IJEBCM paper](https://radar.brookes.ac.uk/radar/items/05cbe328-2b58-4a51-8f64-1e70401e28fd/1)
- Valence: $50M Series B led by Bessemer (Sep 2025). Its AI coach Nadia is deployed at Fortune 500 companies including Experian, Delta, Kraft Heinz and General Mills. Usage claims are vendor-reported (90% regular use, NPS 91). — [Built In: Valence Series B](https://builtin.com/articles/valence-raises-series-b-20250926); [Bessemer: Meet Nadia](https://www.bvp.com/news/meet-nadia-valences-ai-coach-for-leadership-development-and-workplace-collaboration)
- BetterUp launched AI coaching alongside its human coaches. Its own research: 34% of employees prefer human coaches, 15% prefer AI, and 51% are open to both (vendor). — [Training Industry: BetterUp launches AI coaching](https://trainingindustry.com/press-release/artificial-intelligence/betterup-launches-ai-coaching-bridging-human-expertise-and-ai-innovation-to-transform-organizational-impact-at-scale/)
- SME AI startups found:
  - Beside raised $32M (Nov 2025) for an AI receptionist for small businesses. — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/11/11/beside-ai-voice-startup-raises-32-million-ai-receptionist-for-small-business/)
  - Blaze (YC S24) pitches itself as "an AI CFO that pays bills, invoices clients, and forecasts cash." — [YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/industry/payments)
  - Datarails raised $70M Series C (Jan 2026) for FP&A software. — [Wikipedia: Datarails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datarails)
  - UK firm Implement AI helps SMEs adopt AI, with a reported £250k seed (2024) and £1.3m (2025). — [Wikipedia: Implement AI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Implement_AI)
- ICF says general-purpose LLMs used as coaches do not meet its standards (2026 white paper, above). — [ICF white paper](https://coachingfederation.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/icf-ai-coaching-ai-partnership-unleash-potential.pdf)

### Inferences
- The evidence points to AI soon being "good enough" for goal-setting and accountability nudges. That threatens low-end coaching, which mostly does reflective questioning and check-ins. It threatens a diagnostic-plus-method-plus-done-for-you model much less.
- Funded SME AI tools automate tasks (calls, bookkeeping, forecasts). None that surfaced pairs an AI adviser with a human who takes responsibility for owner-level decisions, which looks like an open position. The biggest near-term "AI adviser" competitor for owners is probably general ChatGPT/Claude use plus accounting software's built-in AI (Xero, Intuit), not a dedicated startup.

### Gaps
- Outcomes of the 2026 de Haan/Terblanche/Nowack head-to-head RCT were not seen.
- No traction data (users, revenue) for any SME-focused AI business advisor. Xero's and Intuit's AI assistants ("JAX", "Intuit Assist") were not researched in detail within budget.
- No BetterUp funding figure was verified from a primary source.

## What practitioners and leading voices say about the future of advising owners

### Takeaway
The clearest documented voices are Blair Enns and David C. Baker (2Bobs podcast). In their view, AI will kill labour-based pricing in most firms, make productised and outcome-based offers viable, flood the market with knowledge, and concentrate trust in fewer experts with strong reputation and reach. The implementation-system camp (EOS) says AI makes a method more important. Verified 2023–2026 quotes from Harnish, Wickman, Hormozi, Ron Baker, Blumer and Sutherland were not found within the budget.

### Cited Findings
- Blair Enns: AI is "the last great forcing function that will ultimately kill labor-based pricing in 90% of the businesses out there." He points to productised services, subscriptions and outcome-based pricing as the alternatives. — [2Bobs: Is AI going to kill labor-based pricing?](https://2bobs.com/podcast/is-ai-going-to-kill-labor-based-pricing)
- Enns changed his mind on productised services (Jul 2026 episode): "And AI is the reason." — [2Bobs podcast feed](https://rss.libsyn.com/shows/93875/destinations/492492.xml)
- Enns: AI has caused an oversupply of knowledge products and "a signal-to-noise ratio problem." He sees reputation and reach reshaping the knowledge economy. — [2Bobs: Demand is the new supply](https://2bobs.com/podcast/demand-is-the-new-supply)
- Baker and Enns see firms "losing more and more work to competitors moving to off-shore teams and AI centered services." They expect advisers to rethink positioning, and strategy more than positioning. — [2Bobs: Positioning, strategy, and AI](https://2bobs.com/podcast/positioning-strategy-and-ai); [2Bobs: Have we hit peak strategy?](https://2bobs.com/podcast/have-we-hit-peak-strategy)
