# AI-native services: what investors, technologists and economists say, and what stays human for SME advisers

Research date: 26 Nov 2026. Evidence labels used: **[EVIDENCE]** = peer-reviewed or randomised/field data; **[DATA-VENDOR]** = company-reported numbers; **[THESIS]** = investor or commentator argument, not yet demonstrated.

## 1. "Services as software" / AI-enabled services / roll-up theses (Sequoia, GC, a16z, YC, Mayfield)

### Takeaway
The 2024–2026 VC consensus is that the bigger prize is selling *finished work* (outcomes) rather than tools, because services spend dwarfs software spend; the route in is work buyers already outsource, and the model is either AI-native "autopilots" or buying existing service firms and automating them. Every major thesis still leaves *judgement-heavy* work (management consulting, strategy) as the least automatable and an open question, and the margin story is unproven.

### Cited Findings
- **Sequoia, "Services: The New Software" (Julien Bek, 5 Mar 2026)** [THESIS]: "A copilot sells the tool. An autopilot sells the work." Copilots sell to professionals who own the output; autopilots sell the outcome straight to the buyer. — [Sequoia](https://sequoiacap.com/article/services-the-new-software)
- Sequoia splits work into *intelligence* (rule-based) and *judgement* (experience, taste): "Writing code is mostly intelligence. Knowing what to build next is judgement." Autopilots win first where the intelligence share is high; "today's judgement becomes tomorrow's intelligence" as AI collects data on good judgement, so copilots and autopilots converge. — [Sequoia](https://sequoiacap.com/article/services-the-new-software)
- Sequoia numbers: "For every dollar spent on software, six are spent on services"; a firm may pay $10K/yr for QuickBooks but $120K for an accountant to close the books; the next $1T company is a software company "masquerading as a services firm." — [Sequoia](https://sequoiacap.com/article/services-the-new-software)
- Sequoia on the wedge: "The outsourced task is the wedge. The insourced work is the long-term TAM." Replacing an outsourcing contract is "a vendor swap"; replacing headcount is "a reorg." — [Sequoia](https://sequoiacap.com/article/services-the-new-software)
- Sequoia on consulting (most relevant to coaching/advice): management consulting is a $300–400B market but "the work is mostly judgement"; whether it splits into automatable intelligence (data gathering, benchmarking) and human judgement (strategic recommendations) is open and best candidates are "TBD." Tax advisory by contrast is 80–90% intelligence. Coaching is not mentioned; margins are not discussed. — [Sequoia](https://sequoiacap.com/article/services-the-new-software)
- Sequoia's named examples: copilots Harvey (law firms), Rogo (banks), Basis (accountants); autopilots Crosby, Lawhive, WithCoverage, Rillet, Pace, TaxGPT and others. — [Sequoia](https://sequoiacap.com/article/services-the-new-software)
- **General Catalyst** [THESIS + DATA-VENDOR]: $1.5B of its fund put into an AI-enabled roll-up ("creation") strategy across 7 industries, planned up to 20; led by Marc Bhargava. Bhargava: services ≈ $16T global annual revenue vs ≈ $1T software; 30–50% of services work automatable, up to 70% of core call-centre tasks; aim to at least double acquired firms' EBITDA margin. Titan (IT MSP, $74M from GC) claims pilots automate 38% of typical MSP tasks. — [TechCrunch, 28 Sep 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/28/the-ai-services-transformation-may-be-harder-than-vcs-think/)
- GC frames this as "AI transformation and product reinvention," not a PE cost-extraction roll-up; Long Lake (its first expression) has bought 18 businesses; April 2026 GC essay argues conditions for AI roll-ups are especially concentrated in Europe. — [General Catalyst: The future of services](https://www.generalcatalyst.com/stories/the-future-of-services); [GC: Europe's AI transformation in services](https://www.generalcatalyst.com/stories/europes-ai-transformation-in-services)
- **Mayfield** [THESIS]: $100M set aside for "AI teammates"; Navin Chaddha projects 80–90% gross margins where AI does 80% of the work, blended 60–70%, 20–30% net income. Portfolio company Gruve bought a $5M security consultancy and grew it to $15M revenue in six months [DATA-VENDOR]. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/28/the-ai-services-transformation-may-be-harder-than-vcs-think/)
- **a16z** margin benchmark (older guidance, pre-2024): AI companies ~50–60% gross margin, between software (60–80%+) and services (30–50%). Oct 2025 a16z/Mercury report expects "more end-to-end agentic products – and even AI-native services businesses." — [a16z: The new business of AI](https://a16z.com/the-new-business-of-ai-and-how-its-different-from-traditional-software/); [The Register summary](https://www.theregister.com/2025/10/03/startups_binge_on_ai/)
- **Wellington Management** [THESIS]: AI-native services revenue "may initially resemble services revenue, and gross margins may be lower"; the opportunity is where marginal delivery cost keeps falling as more of production moves to software. — [Wellington](https://www.wellington.com/en-de/institutional/insights/ai-native-services)
- **Y Combinator**: 2025 RFS lists pushed "full-stack AI" companies (run the service themselves with AI underneath); Spring 2026 RFS has "AI-Native Agencies" (Aaron Epstein): "Agencies have always been crazy hard to scale. Low margins, slow manual work, and the only way to grow is to add more people." Suggests picking a vertical and building a full-stack workforce. — [YC RFS](https://ycombinator.com/rfs); [Inc.](https://www.inc.com/ben-sherry/y-combinator-just-released-its-latest-startup-wishlist-heres-every-idea-it-has-for-founders/91297137); [VC Cafe on Summer 2025 list](https://www.vccafe.com/2025/05/12/requests-for-startups-2025-part-3/)
- **UK example — Lawhive** (human-lawyers-plus-AI, close analogue to a coach-plus-platform model): AI platform ("Lawrence," described as paralegal/junior-lawyer level) supports a network of human lawyers; bought SRA-regulated Woodstock Legal Services (reported Sept 2025) as the "first-ever" law-firm takeover by an AI-native platform, with lawyers keeping their way of working; raised $60M in Feb 2026. — [Law Gazette](https://www.lawgazette.co.uk/news/ai-platform-buys-existing-firm-as-law-embraces-new-tech/5124460.article); [Lawyers Weekly](https://www.lawyersweekly.com.au/newlaw/42964-ai-platform-makes-history-with-first-law-firm-acquisition); [Fortune, 5 Feb 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/02/05/lawhive-ai-law-firm-startup-series-b-venture-funding/)
- **Counter-argument — "workslop"** [EVIDENCE, survey]: Stanford Social Media Lab + BetterUp Labs survey of 1,150 full-time staff: 40% had taken on extra work due to polished-but-empty AI output, ~2 hours to handle each instance, ~$186/person/month hidden cost. TechCrunch's point: if roll-ups cut staff, fewer people catch AI errors; if they don't, the margin gains don't appear. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/28/the-ai-services-transformation-may-be-harder-than-vcs-think/)
- Commentary on GC's model: the moat is distribution and proprietary data, not the AI; integrating many different acquired businesses and client relationships may be harder than the thesis implies. — [Capital and Clarity](https://capitalandclarity.substack.com/p/the-general-catalyst-behind-15-billion)

### Inferences
- In Sequoia's terms, BCA is a **copilot for coaches** (Ava drafts, coach owns output) that is edging toward **autopilot on lead gen** (done-for-you LinkedIn outreach, possibly paid on results). Sequoia's own logic suggests the autopilot part (outreach, marketing drafts — high "intelligence" share) is where AI-native competitors will appear first; the advisory core (strategy for a £1M+ owner) sits in the "mostly judgement" bucket that Sequoia calls TBD.
- Lawhive is the closest public analogue: AI platform + human professionals + buying existing practices. It suggests investors are willing to fund "human-plus-platform" when the human carries regulation/accountability. Coaching has no regulatory moat, so the human must justify itself on trust, accountability and results.
- The roll-up thesis is a live threat/opportunity: someone could buy UK coaching/consulting practices and automate them. It also hints at an exit or growth path for BCA.

### Gaps
- No primary 2024–2026 a16z or Bessemer essay with numbers on AI-native services margins was retrieved; Bessemer's view was not found in this pass.
- No investor thesis found that addresses *business coaching* specifically.
- Margin claims (Mayfield 80–90%, GC doubling EBITDA) are projections, not audited results.

## 2. Outcome-based / performance-based pricing

### Takeaway
Outcome pricing for AI agents is now proven at scale in customer support (Intercom Fin, Sierra), but the fights are over what counts as an "outcome." For human advisers there is little hard data on performance pricing trends in this pass.

### Cited Findings
- **Intercom Fin** [DATA-VENDOR]: launched 2023 at $1.90 per resolved ticket, later $0.99 per resolution. A resolution counts when the customer confirms it was satisfactory *or exits without asking for more help*; handoffs and disqualifications also $0.99; booking a call or starting a trial billed at $9.99. — [Intercom help centre](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/8205718-fin-ai-agent-pricing); [Stripe case study](https://stripe.com/en-cn/customers/fin-ai)
- Stripe case study claims Intercom added an "eight-figure business line" from Fin's outcome model [DATA-VENDOR]. Market estimate: AI ~$0.99–1.50 per resolution vs ~$10–15 for a human-handled case [secondary estimate]. — [Stripe](https://stripe.com/en-cn/customers/intercom-pricing); [Built In](https://builtin.com/articles/outcome-based-pricing-ai-economy)
- **Sierra** (Bret Taylor): says it gets paid "only when we complete a task for you," with blended consumption pricing for routing/greeter work; rates not public (third-party estimate ~$1.50/resolution, unverified). Announced $100M ARR in Nov 2025, ~21 months after founding; later estimates $150M (Feb 2026) and ~$200M (May 2026, Sacra) — figures conflict. — [Sierra blog](https://sierra.ai/de/blog/outcome-based-pricing-for-ai-agents); [TechCrunch, 21 Nov 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/21/bret-taylors-sierra-reaches-100m-arr-in-under-two-years/); [Sacra](https://www.sacra.com/c/sierra)
- Eudia (GC, legal) sells fixed-fee AI-powered legal services to in-house teams — a step from hours to fixed outcomes. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/28/the-ai-services-transformation-may-be-harder-than-vcs-think/)
- Sequoia's "autopilot sells the work" framing implies pricing per unit of work done, not per seat. — [Sequoia](https://sequoiacap.com/article/services-the-new-software)

### Inferences
- A "share of revenue" lead-gen deal for coaches fits the Fin/Sierra pattern (pay per booked call is literally Intercom's $9.99 "booking a call" outcome). The lesson from Fin/Sierra: define the outcome tightly (booked call that shows up? signed client?) because that definition is where disputes arise.
- Paying per booked meeting is likely to become the market reference price for AI outreach; coaches will compare BCA's share-of-revenue against it.

### Gaps
- No reliable 2024–2026 data found on outcome/performance pricing among human business coaches or consultants (e.g. share of advisory firms using success fees). Needs a separate search.

## 3. Economists and researchers on AI and expert labour (does AI lift novices?)

### Takeaway
The strongest field evidence says AI lifts less-experienced workers most **on well-defined tasks inside its competence** (support agents +34–35%, below-average consultants +43%). But for open-ended business advice to owners, the one RCT (Kenyan entrepreneurs) found **no average effect** and that weaker businesses got *worse* — they asked harder questions and AI's plausible advice wasn't enough. Macro economists (Acemoglu) expect modest aggregate gains.

### Cited Findings
- **Brynjolfsson, Li & Raymond, "Generative AI at Work," QJE 140(2), 2025, 889–942** [EVIDENCE]: ~5,179 support agents at a Fortune 500 software firm; AI assistant raised issues resolved per hour by 13.8% (~14%; earlier drafts 15%); lowest-skill/least-experienced workers +34–35%; zero or small negative effect for the most experienced. Mechanism: the tool spread the behaviour of top agents to newer ones. — [NBER w31161](https://nber.org/papers/w31161); [NBER Digest](https://www.nber.org/index%2ephp/digest/20236/measuring-productivity-impact-generative-ai); [RePEc QJE](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:qjecon:v:140:y:2025:i:2:p:889-942.)
- **Dell'Acqua, Mollick et al., "Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier" (BCG, 758 consultants ≈7% of BCG ICs; later Organization Science)** [EVIDENCE]: inside AI's frontier, +12.2% more tasks, 25.1% faster, higher quality; below-average consultants +43% vs +17% for above-average. *Outside* the frontier, AI users were more likely to be wrong, while producing more persuasive-sounding arguments. — [SSRN PDF via MIT Sloan](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/sites/default/files/2023-10/SSRN-id4573321.pdf); [HBS Working Knowledge](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/humans-vs-machines-untangling-the-tasks-ai-can-and-cant-handle); [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2023/09/29/ai-mistakes-consulting-bcg-study)
- **Mollick, "Centaurs and Cyborgs on the Jagged Frontier"** (One Useful Thing): "centaurs" split tasks between human and AI by strength; "cyborgs" weave AI through the whole task. Both styles beat unaided work on frontier tasks. — [One Useful Thing](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/centaurs-and-cyborgs-on-the-jagged?r=15jjiq)
- **Otis, Clarke, Delecourt, Holtz & Koning, "The Uneven Impact of Generative AI on Entrepreneurial Performance" (Management Science, 2024/25)** [EVIDENCE — most directly relevant to SME advice]: 640 Kenyan entrepreneurs, ~5 months (May–Nov 2023), GPT-4 business mentor via WhatsApp vs ILO training guides. No significant average effect; high performers ~+15% (revenue/profit), low performers ~−8% (press figures). Authors: "low-performing entrepreneurs in our sample asked the AI mentor for assistance with more challenging tasks than high performers." — [Management Science](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2024.06909); [HBS Working Knowledge](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/what-happens-when-business-owners-turn-to-chatbots-for-advice); [Berkeley Haas](https://newsroom.haas.berkeley.edu/research/gen-ai-experiment-shows-mixed-results-in-helping-small-businesses-grow/)
- **Autor, "Applying AI to Rebuild Middle Class Jobs" (NBER w32140, Feb 2024; NOĒMA essay)** [THESIS from top labour economist]: AI as a decision-support tool could let workers with foundational training do higher-stakes expert decision-making previously reserved for elite experts — restoring middle-skill work — *if* designed to support rather than replace workers. Explicitly "not a forecast." — [NBER w32140](https://www.nber.org/papers/w32140); [NOĒMA PDF](https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/2026-04/NOEMA-Autor-Feb-2024.pdf)
- **Acemoglu, "The Simple Macroeconomics of AI" (NBER w32487; Economic Policy 40(121), 2025)** [EVIDENCE-based model]: ≤0.66% TFP gain over 10 years (~0.064%/yr), GDP +0.93–1.16% over a decade; early evidence comes from easy-to-learn tasks, while much future value sits in hard-to-learn tasks, so estimates may be too high. — [NBER w32487](https://nber.org/papers/w32487); [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2024/05/29/ai_gdp_inequality/?td=rt-3a)
- **Agrawal, Gans & Goldfarb, "Prediction, Judgment and Complexity" (NBER w24243)**: AI makes *prediction* cheap; *judgment* (deciding payoffs/what matters) stays human and becomes more valuable; "Prediction and judgment are complements as long as judgment is not too difficult," and not all judgment complements AI. — [NBER w24243](https://www.nber.org/papers/w24243)
- **"How People Use ChatGPT" (Chatterji, Deming et al., NBER w34255, 2025)** [EVIDENCE, OpenAI data]: ~49% of messages are "Asking" (advice/information), ~40% "Doing," ~11% "Expressing"; authors conclude much of ChatGPT's economic value is decision support; non-work share rose from 53% to 70%+. — [NBER w34255](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34255); [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/how-people-are-using-chatgpt/)

### Inferences
- Supports the BCA design: a less-experienced coach with Ava is the Brynjolfsson/BCG pattern (AI spreads top performers' playbook to novices). The staged method and diagnostic score are the kind of codified "best-agent behaviour" that drove those gains.
- The Kenya RCT is the key warning against "owners just ask ChatGPT": unaided AI advice didn't help the average small business and hurt the struggling ones, because they couldn't judge which advice fit their hard problem. That is a gap a human coach fills — choosing the problem and judging fit (Agrawal's "judgment"). Caveat: Kenyan micro-firms ≠ UK £1M+ firms; better-run firms (closer to BCA's market) *did* benefit, which cuts both ways (they may need coaches less).
- BCG "outside the frontier" result: AI makes wrong advice more persuasive. For coaches, the human's job includes catching AI's confident errors.

### Gaps
- No RCT found comparing AI-alone vs human-coach-plus-AI for SME owners.
- Did not retrieve Mollick's *Co-Intelligence* text directly; centaur/cyborg drawn from his blog.

## 4. What people still want humans for: trust, accountability, empathy, follow-through

### Takeaway
People don't uniformly reject AI advice: they accept it when AI is seen as more capable *and* the decision isn't personal; personalised, high-stakes decisions still favour humans. AI-only support can work (Therabot RCT), but human guidance modestly improves completion — the effect is real but small and inconsistent.

### Cited Findings
- **Dietvorst et al. (2015)**: people lose trust in algorithms faster than humans after seeing them err ("algorithm aversion"). **Logg et al. (2019)**: people often prefer algorithmic advice to human advice ("algorithm appreciation"). — [Summary in Böhm et al., Communications Psychology 2023](https://link.springer.com/10.1038/s44271-023-00032-x)
- **Lu et al. meta-analysis, Psychological Bulletin (2025)** [EVIDENCE]: 163 studies, 287 effect sizes, 119,000+ people. Capability–Personalization Framework: AI is preferred only when it's seen as more capable than humans *and* the decision doesn't call for personalisation. — [MIT News, 10 Jun 2025](https://news.mit.edu/2025/how-we-really-judge-ai-0610)
- **Böhm et al. (2023)**, N=3,308 US: people rated generative AI as less competent but were not necessarily less willing to take its advice; preference for AI advice rose after positive past experience. — [Communications Psychology](https://link.springer.com/10.1038/s44271-023-00032-x)
- **Therabot RCT (Heinz, Jacobson et al., NEJM AI, 27 Mar 2025)** [EVIDENCE]: first RCT of a generative AI therapy chatbot; ~6 hours average use over 4 weeks (~8 sessions' worth), users often started conversations themselves (incl. late at night); clinically significant symptom reductions at 8 weeks, which researchers framed as comparable to outpatient CBT (not head-to-head). Authors: no generative AI agent is ready to work fully autonomously in mental health. — [Dartmouth](https://tto.dartmouth.edu/news/2025/03/first-therapy-chatbot-trial-yields-mental-health-benefits); [MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2025/03/28/1114001/the-first-trial-of-generative-ai-therapy-shows-it-might-help-with-depression/amp/)
- **Human guidance and adherence** [EVIDENCE, mixed]: Musiat et al. meta-analysis (22 web mental-health studies): full completion 12% higher with guidance, small effect. Proposed mechanism: accountability from coaches' scheduled contacts. Other reviews find little or non-significant effect of human support; a cardiometabolic eHealth meta-analysis found self-help as effective as human-supported on health outcomes. — [Psychological Medicine](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/psychological-medicine/article/impact-of-guidance-on-intervention-adherence-in-computerised-interventions-for-mental-health-problems-a-metaanalysis/02990B48E8FEB565569640C54BF8E9F1); [PMC review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9244656); [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.716106/pdf)
- Sequoia lists closing candidates and assessing culture fit as human parts of recruitment; strategic recommendations as human in consulting. — [Sequoia](https://sequoiacap.com/article/services-the-new-software)

### Inferences
- An owner's growth plan is a highly *personalised* decision — per Lu et al., the zone where people still prefer a human. Generic marketing drafts and outreach are low-personalisation — the zone where AI is accepted.
- The value of the human coach is more about **accountability and follow-through** than information, but the evidence that human contact raises follow-through is modest (+12% completion), not decisive. BCA should measure it in its own data (do coached owners act more?).
- As owners have good experiences with AI advice (Böhm), willingness to skip the human rises over time — the moat erodes unless the human adds visible value.

### Gaps
- No study found on whether SME *owners* act on AI vs human business advice.
- No AI business-coaching adherence RCT found (only mental health / health coaching analogues).

## 5. Direct-to-SME AI advisers and the "owners just ask ChatGPT" risk

### Takeaway
SME owners are already using AI for advice at scale (about half in UK surveys), and accounting/commerce platforms are shipping agents that act, not just answer. But SMEs still go to their accountant first, and the only RCT on AI business mentoring shows unaided AI advice doesn't reliably help.

### Cited Findings
- Worldpay study (via Startups.co.uk): 53% of UK SME owners regularly use AI tools for business advice; 60% of owners aged 25–34. Original report not retrieved. — [Startups.co.uk](https://startups.co.uk/news/lonely-entrepreneurs-using-ai/)
- Simply Business 2026 SME Insights (1,842 UK small firms): 47% of owners use AI tools, up from 22% in 2025. — [Simply Business](https://www.simplybusiness.co.uk/knowledge/business-news/small-business-ai-usage-doubles/)
- Enterprise Nation survey (1,000 UK SME decision-makers): ChatGPT used by 64%, Gemini and Copilot 42% each, Claude 11%; SMEs go to their accountant (64%) and online search (56%) for advice well before government (22%). — [Enterprise Nation](https://www.enterprisenation.com/press/uk-smes-save-over-half-a-day-a-week-with-ai/)
- UK government AI Adoption Survey 2026 exists (exec summary) — not read in detail. — [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-adoption-survey-2026/ai-adoption-survey-report-2026-executive-summary)
- **Xero JAX** [DATA-VENDOR]: launched Sept 2025 as an "AI financial superagent" orchestrating multiple agents; 73% of customers had used AI on the platform since March 2025; Aug 2026 Xerocon: JAX "doesn't just surface information, but acts on it," with a custom agent builder and month-end agent coming. — [Xero media release](https://www.xero.com/uk/media-releases/xeros-ai-financial-superagent-jax-launches-powerful-new-features/); [Xero blog, Xerocon 2026](https://blog.xero.com/nz/product-updates/xerocon-denver-2026-agentic-workflows-ai-era/)
- **Intuit Assist**: built into QuickBooks/TurboTax; drafts client emails and social posts. — [Fit Small Business](https://fitsmallbusiness.com/intuit-assist-vs-jax/)
- OpenAI usage data: ~49% of ChatGPT messages are asking for advice/information. — [NBER w34255](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34255)
- Otis et al. RCT: AI business mentor had no average effect on small firms; harmed low performers. — [Management Science](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2024.06909)

### Inferences
- "Owners ask ChatGPT" is already happening, not hypothetical. The coach's question is not "will owners use AI" but "what does a coach do that the owner's own AI doesn't" — choosing the right problem, judging fit, accountability, and doing the work (implementation).
- Xero/QuickBooks agents will own the finance-data layer; a coach platform that reads those numbers (rather than competing) could make the diagnostic stronger.

### Gaps
- Shopify Sidekick and Microsoft Copilot for SMB adoption figures not retrieved.
- No data on how many £1M+ UK owners specifically use AI *instead of* paying an adviser.

## 6. "Distribution and trust are the moat" — and the counter-arguments

### Takeaway
Investors increasingly argue the defensible assets in AI services are distribution, client relationships, proprietary outcome data and accountability — not the model. Counter-arguments: AI also cuts the cost of distribution (outreach), trust erodes as people gain good AI experiences, and judgement itself gets automated over time (Sequoia's "today's judgement is tomorrow's intelligence").

### Cited Findings
- GC's roll-ups buy firms "with existing clients and cash flow"; commentary says the moat is distribution and proprietary data rather than AI. — [Capital and Clarity](https://capitalandclarity.substack.com/p/the-general-catalyst-behind-15-billion); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/28/the-ai-services-transformation-may-be-harder-than-vcs-think/)
- Sequoia: copilots sell to professionals "who take responsibility for output" — responsibility/accountability is the human's role; but judgement data accumulates and the frontier shifts. — [Sequoia](https://sequoiacap.com/article/services-the-new-software)
- Sequoia on regulation as moat: tax advisory's CPA licensing protects it despite most work being automatable. — [Sequoia](https://sequoiacap.com/article/services-the-new-software)
- Lawhive keeps human regulated lawyers in front of clients while AI does junior work. — [Lawyers Weekly](https://www.lawyersweekly.com.au/newlaw/42964-ai-platform-makes-history-with-first-law-firm-acquisition)
- Counter: preference for AI advice grows with positive experience (Böhm et al.); AI preferred where seen as more capable and non-personal (Lu et al.). — [Communications Psychology](https://link.springer.com/10.1038/s44271-023-00032-x); [MIT News](https://news.mit.edu/2025/how-we-really-judge-ai-0610)
- Counter: "workslop" and fewer staff to catch errors may stop AI-services margins appearing. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/28/the-ai-services-transformation-may-be-harder-than-vcs-think/)
- Counter (macro): Acemoglu expects modest gains because hard-to-learn tasks are slower to automate — implying expert judgement work changes slower than the hype. — [NBER w32487](https://nber.org/papers/w32487)

### Inferences
- For BCA, the defensible assets are: (1) a network of trusted human coaches with relationships in UK owner-led firms; (2) proprietary outcome data (diagnostic scores, method stages, which actions moved results) — Sequoia's "data about good judgement"; (3) doing the work (done-for-you lead gen) rather than giving advice. Information/advice alone is the part most exposed.
- Coaching has no licence moat, so accountability and results must be shown, not assumed.
- Overall read: the human-plus-AI-platform model is consistent with the copilot/Lawhive pattern and with the evidence (AI lifts novices on defined tasks; unaided AI advice to small firms underperforms; personal decisions favour humans). The risk is less "AI replaces coaches" in the next 2–3 years and more (a) AI-native lead-gen/marketing autopilots undercutting the done-for-you piece on price, and (b) better-run owners — BCA's target — being exactly those who can get value from AI alone.

### Gaps
- No Stratechery or Bessemer piece retrieved on the trust/distribution moat specifically; argument assembled from GC/Sequoia sources and commentary.
- No evidence found on willingness to pay for human advisers in an AI-saturated market (UK SMEs).
