# How AI is changing consulting and professional advisory (big consultancies, HBR, academics), 2023–2026

Notes compiled 26 Nov 2026. Each claim has a date and a source. Labels used: **[evidence]** = peer-reviewed or preregistered experiment, or audited/official figure; **[firm claim]** = a statement by a consulting firm about itself (self-interested, not checked); **[survey]** = commissioned or member survey; **[press]** = journalism; **[vendor]** = marketing by a firm selling AI or software; **[secondary]** = blog or aggregator, weaker.

## 1. What the big firms (McKinsey, BCG, Bain, Deloitte, PwC, Accenture, EY, KPMG, Gartner) have said and done, 2023–2026

### Takeaway
The big firms are cutting the bottom of the pyramid (graduate intake, back office, junior research and analysis), adding AI agents, and moving some pricing to outcomes. At the same time they are growing AI-related revenue and the number of client-facing staff. The message from inside: the work is changing shape more than disappearing. Clients, though, are now openly pushing fees down and taking work in-house (FT, Aug 2026).

### Cited Findings

**McKinsey**
- [firm claim] Nov 2025: about a quarter of McKinsey's global fees come from outcomes-based pricing, said UK managing partner Michael Birshan at a London press briefing ("We're doing more performance-based arrangements with our clients"). Alex Smaje (global leader of technology and AI) gave a lower figure of 20%, growing over "the last few years". Under these deals the fee depends mostly on results, with some fixed costs. Success is measured against a client scorecard: investor targets, revenue or profit goals, operational measures, customer satisfaction. Smaje: "your scorecard with your board is our scorecard"; "We're not a supplier, we're not a vendor, we're a genuine partner"; "many of the fundamentals of the professional services model are coming under challenge." Smaje said outcome pricing came before AI but suits AI transformation work. — [Business Insider via AOL, 17 Nov 2025](https://www.aol.com/articles/ai-reshaping-mckinsey-makes-money-115132273.html)
- [firm claim] Nov 2025: strategy advice is now **less than 20%** of McKinsey's work (Smaje). Clients want "deep implementation expertise" and transformation support lasting several years. — [Business Insider via AOL, 17 Nov 2025](https://www.aol.com/articles/ai-reshaping-mckinsey-makes-money-115132273.html)
- [firm claim] Jan 2026 (CES): CEO Bob Sternfels said McKinsey has about **40,000 people and ~25,000 AI agents**. A McKinsey spokesperson confirmed the agent figure to Business Insider. Agents reportedly saved ~1.5 million hours of research and synthesis work in 2025. **Clients pushing back (the demand side)**
- [press] FT, 31 Aug 2026: large and small clients are demanding lower fees or bringing work in-house. SAP says its changes could cut external consultant costs by up to 50%; its CFO said AI will "massively displace" parts of consulting. Capgemini's CEO: "We don't see 50 per cent." Bristol Myers Squibb pressed advisers to move from hourly billing to fixed or performance fees; its Greg Meyers is "totally fine" with firms making more margin "as long as we get to participate in the downside." Bayer uses 30 AI agents on its SAP programme, aiming for "significantly fewer consultants." SharkNinja's CEO: "We are definitely spending less with consultants." UniCredit's external consulting spend fell 24% in H1 and Société Générale's 9%. Commerzbank plans €600m of AI investment for €500m a year in savings, partly from consulting. — [FT via Irish Times, 31 Aug 2026](https://www.irishtimes.com/business/2026/08/31/consultants-head-for-a-showdown-with-their-own-clients/)

### Inferences
- The pyramid is losing its base (research, analysis, first drafts), while senior, client-facing, relationship and implementation roles are protected or growing. McKinsey's "25 squared" is the clearest firm-stated version.
- Big firms are moving from advice to implementation and shared results (strategy <20% of McKinsey's work; outcome fees 20–25%). Pure "diagnose and recommend" work is the part being squeezed.
- Firms say these things partly to reassure investors. Agent counts and "AI share of revenue" have no agreed definition.

### Gaps
- No primary figures found for Bain AI revenue or any Gartner numerical forecast on consulting.
- Could not verify McKinsey headcount cuts or Deloitte UK figures from primary sources.
- KPMG/Deloitte/PwC statements on outcome-based pricing for mid-market clients: not found.

## 2. The Harvard/BCG "jagged technological frontier" study and later studies

### Takeaway
On tasks inside what AI can do, AI made BCG consultants much faster and better, with the biggest gains for weaker performers. On a task just outside it, consultants with AI were *more* likely to get it wrong, because they trusted it. A 2025 follow-up at P&G found one person with AI matched a two-person team without it, while human judgment still mattered for picking the best ideas.

### Cited Findings
- [evidence] Dell'Acqua, McFowland, Mollick, Lifshitz-Assaf, Kellogg, Rajendran, Krayer, Candelon, Lakhani (HBS Working Paper 24-013, Sept 2023; later revision). 758 BCG consultants (~7% of BCG's individual-contributor consultants), preregistered, using GPT-4. **Inside the frontier** (18 realistic consulting tasks: product ideas, market analysis, persuasive writing): 12.2% more tasks completed, 25.1% faster, 40%+ higher quality. **Outside the frontier** (a business case built from BCG's own interview cases, needing evidence from interviews and spreadsheet data): consultants with AI were **19 percentage points less likely** to reach the correct answer. — [HBS PDF](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/dell-acqua-et-al-2026-navigating-the-jagged-technological-frontier_5c589c8c-fbb5-458f-b285-c944746cd717.pdf); [SSRN working paper PDF](https://s21.q4cdn.com/371534297/files/doc_news/2025/05/SSRN-id4573321.pdf)
- [evidence] The same paper found below-average performers gained more than top performers (well-known result of the study, reported in the paper). It describes two working styles: "Centaurs" (split tasks between human and AI) and "Cyborgs" (weave AI through the task). — [HBS PDF](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/dell-acqua-et-al-2026-navigating-the-jagged-technological-frontier_5c589c8c-fbb5-458f-b285-c944746cd717.pdf)
- [critique] A later reanalysis argues the evidence for a truly "jagged" frontier is weaker than the headline. The two tasks differ in more than difficulty, and the study used one firm, one domain and one model (GPT-4, June 2023). — [Reasoning with AI Substack](https://reasoningwithai.substack.com/p/reconsidering-the-jagged-frontier)
- [evidence] "The Cybernetic Teammate" (Dell'Acqua et al., NBER WP 33641, Mar 2025; published in *Organization Science*, 2026). Field experiment with 776 P&G professionals (791 in the published version). **Individuals with AI matched teams without AI.** AI removed the functional split: R&D and commercial staff produced more balanced proposals. AI users were more likely to produce top-10% ideas; individuals were ~16% faster. Published abstract: "AI primarily enhances the quality of generated ideas … whereas human judgment retains value in evaluative selection." AI users also reported more positive emotions. — [NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w33641); [Organization Science](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/orsc.2025.20702); [HBS Working Knowledge](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/when-ai-joins-the-team-better-ideas-surface)

### Inferences
- For an adviser serving owner-led firms: AI makes a solo adviser work like a small team (P&G result), and most lifts the average or less experienced adviser (BCG result). That is the case for a trained network using a shared AI platform.
- The 19-point drop is the risk: advisers who trust AI on messy, data-plus-interview problems (exactly what diagnosing a real SME is) get worse answers. Method, checks and human judgment on the final call are needed.

### Gaps
- No experiment found on AI and advisers working with small businesses, or on client outcomes (as opposed to adviser output quality).
- Studies used 2023–2024 models; the frontier has moved since.

## 3. What HBR and MIT SMR say (Christensen 2013 to 2025–26 pieces)

### Takeaway
Christensen et al. predicted in 2013 that consulting would break into parts and that knowledge would be packaged into tools ("asset-based consulting"). HBR in 2025 says AI is doing this now: the pyramid becomes an "obelisk", and billing moves from hours to fixed, subscription or outcome fees.

### Cited Findings
- [academic/HBR] Oct 2013, Christensen, Wang & van Bever, "Consulting on the Cusp of Disruption" (HBR). Consulting was long shielded by **opacity** (clients can't judge quality, so they buy brand) and **agility**. Disruption comes as firms move from integrated "solution shops" to **modular providers**. One form is packaging ideas, processes, frameworks and analytics into software ("asset-based consulting", e.g. McKinsey Solutions). Cited figure: classic strategy work fell to ~20% of traditional firms' work from 60–70% thirty years earlier. — [HBR excerpts PDF (BTG)](https://businesstalentgroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/02/PDF_HBR_Consulting_on_the_Cusp_Excerpts.pdf); [course copy PDF](https://is.muni.cz/el/econ/podzim2020/MPH_MZPO/um/37c77613dcf24d852f545fa0a52079fdcb06.pdf). Note: McKinsey's 2025 statement that strategy is <20% of its work fits this.
- [HBR] 10 Sept 2025, Duncan, Anderson & Saviano, "AI Is Changing the Structure of Consulting Firms." AI can now automate junior work (research, modelling, analysis). "Consulting isn't disappearing; it's being fundamentally reshaped." It proposes the **"consulting obelisk"**: fewer layers and smaller teams instead of a wide base. Incumbents rarely disrupt themselves. Its example of an AI-native firm is **Unity Advisory**. Conflict of interest: two authors run Disruptive Edge / Aucctus AI. — [HBR](https://hbr.org/2025/09/ai-is-changing-the-structure-of-consulting-firms); [consulting.us summary](https://www.consulting.us/news/12442/how-ai-is-revolutionizing-consulting-firm-structure)
- [HBR] Oct 2025, "How AI Is Upending How Consulting Firms Hire Talent": fees have mostly been based on hours. In some professional services (e.g. law), fixed fees are now much more common and subscription fees are gaining ground. — [HBR](https://hbr.org/2025/10/how-ai-is-upending-how-consulting-firms-hire-talent)
- [vendor] HBR also carries sponsored content (e.g. Certinia, May 2026); do not cite it as HBR editorial. — [HBR sponsored](https://hbr.org/sponsored/2026/05/why-professional-services-organizations-keep-solving-the-wrong-ai-problem)

### Inferences
- The "obelisk" (a small senior team plus AI, no large junior base) is close to the shape of a solo Profit Coach backed by a platform. HBR is describing the big firms moving towards the kind of model BCA already runs.
- Christensen's "opacity" point matters: as AI makes analysis cheap and visible, clients can judge the analysis themselves, so brand-only advice loses value and visible results and measured outcomes gain value.

### Gaps
- Full text of both 2025 HBR pieces was paywalled. Only abstracts and excerpts were read.
- No MIT SMR article on AI and SME advisory was retrieved in this pass.

## 4. The Susskinds and other academics: breaking professions into parts and commoditising them

### Takeaway
The Susskinds argue professional work gets split into tasks ("decomposition"). Each task goes to the cheapest capable provider (machine, paraprofessional, the client), and clients care about the outcome, not the process. Richard Susskind's 2025 book restates this for generative AI, and also names "not-us thinking": professionals accept AI for other fields but deny it for their own.

### Cited Findings
- [academic] *The Future of the Professions* (Richard & Daniel Susskind, OUP 2015; updated edition with a new preface). Main ideas: decomposition of professional work into parts, some done by machines or non-specialists, which lowers costs and prices even while humans run the process. Commoditisation: clients want "the hole, not the drill." — [OUP](https://global.oup.com/academic/product/the-future-of-the-professions-9780198841890); [SCL review](https://www.scl.org/3469-book-review-the-future-of-the-professions/)
- [academic] Richard Susskind, *How to Think About AI: A Guide for the Perplexed* (OUP, 2025). Splits "process-thinkers" (how AI works) from "outcome-thinkers" (what it delivers). Clients care about the result, not the process. "Not-us thinking" is professionals' belief that their own work can't be done by AI. Reviewers call it darker than his earlier work. — [Legal Cheek review, May 2025](https://www.legalcheek.com/2025/05/book-review-susskinds-how-to-think-about-ai); [OUP chapter](https://academic.oup.com/book/59718/chapter/507110843); [Legal Futures](https://www.legalfutures.co.uk/latest-news/susskind-machines-will-replace-lawyers-if-they-deliver-better-outcomes/print/)
- [academic] Susskind, British Academy Review (essay, ~2018): "our challenge … is to think deeply about our telos and find new ways to deliver long-established outcomes." — [British Academy](https://www.thebritishacademy.ac.uk/publications/british-academy-review/34/ai-work-and-outcome-thinking)

### Inferences
- In Susskind's terms, the SME adviser's lasting value is the *outcome* (more profit, an owner with more freedom), not the hours of analysis. That backs outcome-led offers and positioning.
- "Not-us thinking" is a warning for coaches who assume "owners will always want a human for this."

### Gaps
- The content of the new preface to *The Future of the Professions* (on generative AI) could not be retrieved.
- No academic work found that applies decomposition to SME business advice in particular.

## 5. Which advisory tasks are most and least exposed

### Takeaway
Most exposed: desk research, synthesis, benchmarking, modelling, first drafts of reports and slides, and idea generation. Least exposed: judging and choosing between options, taking accountability, getting things implemented, change management, and the trust and emotional side. Even sources keen on AI cap automation at about half of advisory work.

### Cited Findings
- [evidence] Microsoft Research "Working with AI" (Tomlinson et al., July 2025, 200k Bing Copilot conversations matched to O*NET tasks). **Management analysts** (the US category for consultants): coverage 0.68, completion 0.90, scope 0.54, AI applicability score **0.35**, about 34th of the 40 most-affected jobs (838,140 workers). Business and financial jobs "may delegate tasks to AI and focus more on the parts of their jobs that require a human touch." — [arXiv 2507.07935](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.07935v1); [Inc. coverage](https://www.inc.com/bruce-crumley/microsoft-study-identifies-jobs-most-and-least-affected-by-ai/91221483)
- [firm claim] McKinsey (Jan 2026): agents saved ~1.5m hours of **research and synthesis**; non-client-facing roles down 25%, client-facing up 25%. — [AOL/BI](https://www.aol.com/articles/mckinseys-ceo-breaks-down-ai-100301936.html)
- [HBR] 2025: AI automates junior research, modelling and analysis. — [HBR, Sept 2025](https://hbr.org/2025/09/ai-is-changing-the-structure-of-consulting-firms)
- [evidence] Jagged frontier (2023): AI is strong at idea generation, market analysis and persuasive writing. It hurts accuracy on problems that need you to combine messy qualitative evidence (interviews) with numbers. — [HBS PDF](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/dell-acqua-et-al-2026-navigating-the-jagged-technological-frontier_5c589c8c-fbb5-458f-b285-c944746cd717.pdf)
- [evidence] Cybernetic Teammate (2025/26): AI raises idea quality; "human judgment retains value in evaluative selection." — [Organization Science](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/orsc.2025.20702)
- [survey] Source Global (late 2025 survey of 50+ senior US corporate tax clients): **expertise and accountability cap AI at 50–60% of current tax advisory work**. Time and cost could fall ~30%. More than a quarter think at least half of tax advisory work will be done by AI within five years. — [Source Global, 3 Feb 2026](https://sourceglobalresearch.com/market-update/will-the-efficiency-gains-from-ai-destroy-the-professional-services-industry-probably-not)
- [firm claim] Clients want "deep implementation expertise" and multi-year transformation support (McKinsey, Nov 2025). — [AOL/BI](https://www.aol.com/articles/ai-reshaping-mckinsey-makes-money-115132273.html)
- [press] Accountability risk: Deloitte Australia's partial refund over AI-made-up citations (Oct 2025) shows the client still holds the human firm responsible. — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/10/07/deloitte-ai-australia-government-report-hallucinations-technology-290000-refund/)
- [press] Client view (FT, Aug 2026): managed-services tech support costs "collapsing"; cyber monitoring contracts "going away"; coding and testing given to agents (Bayer). Implementation-heavy IT work is exposed too, not only analysis. — [FT via Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/business/2026/08/31/consultants-head-for-a-showdown-with-their-own-clients/)
- [survey, unverified] A claimed "70% of 3,887 companies distrust consultancy reports produced with AI", credited to Source Global, appears only in an aggregator. Do not use without checking. — [ConsultingDemand (secondary)](https://consultingdemand.com/blog/ai-consulting-statistics/)

### Inferences
- Exposure ranking (synthesis):
  - **High:** desk research, benchmarking, data analysis and modelling, report and slide drafting, generating options, first-pass diagnostics.
  - **Medium:** turning messy interview data into a diagnosis (AI helps, but blind trust lowers accuracy).
  - **Low:** choosing priorities, accountability for advice, holding owners to actions, implementation, change management, emotional support.
- For BCA: the diagnostic score and the AI platform cover the high-exposure layer cheaply. The coach's paid value should sit in the low-exposure layer: judgment, accountability, implementation and the relationship.

### Gaps
- No direct academic evidence was found on emotional support or accountability in business coaching specifically (the "human" claims rest on firm statements, surveys and the selection-judgment finding).
- Anthropic Economic Index data on consulting tasks was not retrieved.

## 6. Demand and pricing data, 2024–2026 (Source Global, MCA UK, Gartner, Kennedy) and what it means for SME advisers

### Takeaway
Demand has not collapsed. UK and US consulting is still forecast to grow, and almost all large clients now buy AI-related advice. But clients expect faster delivery and want to share in the savings, so pricing is moving from hours to fixed and outcome fees. Owner-led SMEs already ask AI before their accountant, and many see their adviser as "compliance only".

### Cited Findings
**Demand**
- [survey] MCA (UK) Member Survey 2025 (Savanta), Jan 2025: sector expected to grow 6.4% in the next 12 months and 8.7% in 2026. **AI** was the top expected growth area (66% put it in their top three), then digital (59%) and cost reduction (46%). — [MCA press release](https://www.mca.org.uk/press-releases/british-consulting-sector-surges-ahead-with-record-exports-and-strong-growth-forecasts)
- [survey] MCA, July 2025: 2025 forecast cut to **3.6%**, 2026 to **7.8%**. — [MCA press release](https://www.mca.org.uk/press-releases/mca-forecasts-growth-and-highlights-continuing-improvements-in-social-mobility-in-the-consulting-sector)
- [survey] MCA, Jan 2026: UK consultants forecast higher growth in 2026 on digital and technology demand; most firms plan to raise their own AI investment. — [PR Newswire, 21 Jan 2026](https://tools.prnewswire.com/en-us/live/20823/release/20260121EN66087)
- [press] Sept 2026: MCA predicts ~6% growth in 2026 and 7% in 2027 (consultancy.uk); a separate blog quotes 5.7%/7.4%. These conflict, so check the MCA. — [consultancy.uk](https://www.consultancy.uk/news/44009/uk-consulting-clients-turning-to-sector-for-ai-transformation-support); [City AM](https://www.cityam.com/uk-consulting-sector-to-grow-for-next-two-years-as-ais-impact-expands/)
- [survey] Source Global: share of clients who paid consultants for AI support in the past 12 months rose from 81% (Q2 2025) to 88% (Q1 2026). A Forbes piece gives a conflicting date for the 81%. — [Source Global blog](https://sourceglobalresearch.com/blog-post/understanding-the-market-for-ai-related-consulting-services)
- [survey/press] Source Global: clients will spend **$420bn on technology consultants in 2026, +8%** ($236bn of it implementation). ### Inferences
- Implications for advisers to £1M+ owner-led firms (synthesis, not sourced claims):
  1. Owners already get free generic analysis from AI. Selling the analysis or the report alone is weak; owners will pay for what AI can't give them: judgment on *their* business, accountability and implementation.
  2. The demand gap is real: owners see their accountant as compliance-only, and 92% say they would pay for broader advice (vendor survey, so treat with care).
  3. Pricing should follow the big firms and Source's data: fixed or subscription fees plus a link to results, rather than time. Big clients now expect AI savings to be shared, and SME owners will too.
  4. AI lets a solo adviser deliver like a team (P&G study) and most lifts average advisers (BCG study). This supports a trained network on a shared AI platform, provided there is a method that sets where human checks happen (the 19-point "outside the frontier" risk; the Deloitte refund).
  5. The firms' own turn to implementation and outcomes (McKinsey strategy <20%) points the same way: a staged growth system with ongoing support beats one-off diagnostic reports.
