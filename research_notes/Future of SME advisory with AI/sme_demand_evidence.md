# SME demand for business advice, the evidence that advice works, and SME AI adoption (UK focus)

Research date: 26 Nov 2026. Vendor-funded surveys are flagged **[vendor]**. Figures from secondary write-ups that I could not check against the primary source are flagged **[secondary, unverified]**.

## (a) Do UK owner-led SMEs want and pay for external business advice?

### Takeaway
Only about a quarter of UK SME employers use any outside advice in a year (27% in 2024). The share rises steeply with size: 44% of medium firms (50 to 249 staff) use it, against 25% of micro firms. Those that do use advice mostly pay for it, and 84% of medium firms pay. Accountants are the biggest single source (37%), but consultants and business advisers are close behind (34%) and lead among small and medium firms (43 to 47%). Use of advice is still below 2015 levels. The demand is real but narrow, and it sits mostly in exactly the 10 to 249 employee band that £1M+ businesses occupy.

### Cited Findings
- **Overall use (LSBS 2024, DBT, SME employers with 1 to 249 staff):** 27% used external information or advice in the last 12 months, up from 26% in 2023 and 25% in 2022. "Seeking advice is still less common than in 2015." — [DBT LSBS 2024 SME employers](https://gov.uk/government/statistics/small-business-survey-2024-businesses-with-employees/longitudinal-small-business-survey-2024-sme-employers-businesses-with-1-to-249-employees)
- **By size (same source):** medium (50 to 249) 44%, unchanged on 2023; small (10 to 49) 35%, +1pp; micro (1 to 9) 25%, +2pp. — [DBT LSBS 2024](https://gov.uk/government/statistics/small-business-survey-2024-businesses-with-employees/longitudinal-small-business-survey-2024-sme-employers-businesses-with-1-to-249-employees)
- **By nation:** England 28%, Scotland 26%, Wales 26%, Northern Ireland 24%. **By sector:** highest in primary (47%) and information and communications (41%); lowest in transport and storage (15%), accommodation and food (20%), and manufacturing and retail and wholesale (24% each). — [DBT LSBS 2024](https://gov.uk/government/statistics/small-business-survey-2024-businesses-with-employees/longitudinal-small-business-survey-2024-sme-employers-businesses-with-1-to-249-employees)
- **What the advice was for (share of advice-seekers):** financial advice for running the business 25%; business growth 23% (25% in 2023); legal 15%; employment law (small firms 16%, medium 17%). — [DBT LSBS 2024](https://gov.uk/government/statistics/small-business-survey-2024-businesses-with-employees/longitudinal-small-business-survey-2024-sme-employers-businesses-with-1-to-249-employees)
- **Where advice came from (share of advice-seekers, 2024):** accountant 37%; consultants or business advisers 34% (36% in 2023); business networks or trade associations 22% (17% in 2022, rising); solicitors 15%; local council 6%; internet or search engine 6% (10% in 2022, falling); GOV.UK 5%; specialist financial adviser 5%. — [DBT LSBS 2024](https://gov.uk/government/statistics/small-business-survey-2024-businesses-with-employees/longitudinal-small-business-survey-2024-sme-employers-businesses-with-1-to-249-employees)
- **Source by size:** accountants are used most by micro firms (39%, against 34% of small and 29% of medium). Consultants and business advisers are used most by larger SMEs (small 43%, medium 47%, micro 31%). So among firms with 10+ staff, consultants or advisers overtake accountants as the main source. — [DBT LSBS 2024](https://gov.uk/government/statistics/small-business-survey-2024-businesses-with-employees/longitudinal-small-business-survey-2024-sme-employers-businesses-with-1-to-249-employees)
- **How advice was delivered:** face-to-face 45% (22% in 2020, 41% in 2022); email 23%; phone 21%. — [DBT LSBS 2024](https://gov.uk/government/statistics/small-business-survey-2024-businesses-with-employees/longitudinal-small-business-survey-2024-sme-employers-businesses-with-1-to-249-employees)
- **Paying for advice (England and Wales):** 65% of advice-users paid (64% in 2022); medium 84%, small 75%, micro 60%. — [DBT LSBS 2024](https://gov.uk/government/statistics/small-business-survey-2024-businesses-with-employees/longitudinal-small-business-survey-2024-sme-employers-businesses-with-1-to-249-employees)
- **Growth intent (LSBS 2024):** 71% aim to grow sales over 3 years (75% in 2023); small 85%, medium 89%. 38% plan to "increase manager leadership capability" (41% in 2023), and 57% say rising costs hit their growth plans. 45% arranged or funded training, against 82% of medium firms. — [DBT LSBS 2024](https://gov.uk/government/statistics/small-business-survey-2024-businesses-with-employees/longitudinal-small-business-survey-2024-sme-employers-businesses-with-1-to-249-employees)
- **Scotland's comparable survey (2024 to 25):** 26% of SME employers used external advice. — [Scottish Government, Small Business Survey Scotland 2024-25](https://www.gov.scot/publications/small-business-survey-scotland-2024-2025/pages/business-practices/)
- **Willingness to pay for structured management training:** Help to Grow: Management completers "commonly valued the programme above the £750 fee, with most willing to pay £1,000 to £2,499". Only a small minority (5%) gave it a lower value. Government covered about 90% of the cost (£223m public funding). The evaluation recommends considering a higher fee. — [Ipsos/IES for DBT, HtGM Phase 1 final evaluation, June 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a297517d95ffddb05d4ae28/help-to-grow-management-phase-1-final-evaluation-report-process-and-impact-evaluation.pdf)
- **Who signs up for subsidised management training:** HtGM enrolees had a median turnover of £970k and a mean of 28 employees. About half had been trading 10+ years. In other words, the programme drew the low end of BCA's £1M+ target band. — [HtGM final evaluation, June 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a297517d95ffddb05d4ae28/help-to-grow-management-phase-1-final-evaluation-report-process-and-impact-evaluation.pdf)
- **Demand for broader advisory [vendor]:** a Censuswide survey of 500 UK SMEs, commissioned by Ravical (an accounting-tech vendor), found that 92% would pay more for a fuller range of advisory services if those services better matched their needs. — [cfotech summary of Ravical](https://cfotech.co.uk/story/uk-smes-turn-to-ai-before-accountants-survey-finds); [Ravical ROI Report 2026](https://ravical.com/resources/ai-impact-on-uk-accounting-clients)

### Inferences
- In BCA's target band (roughly 10 to 249 staff), 35 to 44% use outside advice each year and 75 to 84% of those pay. For firms with 10+ staff, consultants and advisers are already the most-used source, ahead of accountants. So a paid adviser is a normal purchase for this group, not a new category.
- The other ~60% do not use advice at all in a given year. The main competitor is "no adviser", not another coach.
- Advice for growth is a minority purpose (23% of advice-seekers, about 6% of all SME employers). Finance, legal and HR "keep the lights on" advice is at least as common. A Profit Coach offer framed around profit and cash may meet existing demand better than one framed around "growth".
- The rise of business networks (17% to 22%) and in-person delivery (45%) suggests peer formats and face-to-face contact still matter.
- HtGM's willingness-to-pay data (£1,000 to £2,499 for a 12-week course) suggests a price anchor for short programmes. It is far below typical retained coaching fees, which a £1M+ firm would be comparing against.

### Gaps
- **Reasons for not seeking advice:** the 2024 LSBS reasons table did not appear in the part of the gov.uk page I could read. Older LSBS waves and ERC work typically cite "no need", cost and lack of trust, but I did not verify current figures.
- **Revenue band:** I found no split of advice use by revenue (£1M to £50M) as opposed to employee count.
- **Not read:** British Business Bank Small Business Finance Markets, the FSB, Be the Business and ERC "State of Small Business Britain 2024" ([PDF](https://www.enterpriseresearch.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/The-State-of-Small-Business-Britain-2024-Final-1.pdf)). The ERC report is the next source to read.
- **Spend:** no reliable figure found for average annual SME spend on advisers.

## (b) Hard evidence that advice, coaching and management training improve SME performance

### Takeaway
Better management is strongly linked to productivity. The best randomised trials, mostly in emerging markets, show large effects from intensive, hands-on consulting: +17% productivity in the first year in India, and higher total factor productivity and return on assets in Mexico. Monthly peer meetings raised sales by about 8 to 10% in China. UK evidence is weaker and more mixed. The large UK Growth Vouchers trial found only a short-term turnover gain (+8.2%). The Help to Grow: Management evaluation (June 2026) found no significant effect on growth or productivity yet, only big self-reported gains in confidence and a weak survival effect. Intensity and hands-on implementation seem to be what make advice work. Light-touch, voucher-style advice moves little.

### Cited Findings
**Correlation: management practices and productivity**
- ONS MES: management practice scores (0 to 1) are associated with higher productivity and resilience. The 2023 mean is 0.55 after an ONS correction (0.49 in 2020), and the median is 0.60. Larger firms score higher than smaller ones; services score 0.56 and production 0.52. — [ONS, Management practices in the UK 2016 to 2023](https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/economicoutputandproductivity/productivitymeasures/bulletins/managementpracticesintheuk/2016to2023); [ONS dataset](https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/economicoutputandproductivity/productivitymeasures/datasets/managementpracticescoresanddistributionsbyfirmcharacteristicsuk/current)
- A Productivity Institute commentary on MES 2023 says management is on an "upward trend, but work to be done". It reports a small correlation coefficient between productivity and management score, based on figures from before the ONS correction. — [The Productivity Institute](https://www.productivity.ac.uk/news/uk-management-upward-trend-but-work-to-be-done/)
- ONS experimental data on British manufacturers: multinationals, large firms (250+) and non-family-owned firms have higher management scores and outperform domestic, smaller and family-owned firms on productivity. Family-owned but not family-managed firms have structured practices similar to multinationals. — [ONS, management practices of manufacturing businesses in GB](https://www.ons.gov.uk/employmentandlabourmarket/peopleinwork/labourproductivity/articles/experimentaldataonthemanagementpracticesofmanufacturingbusinessesingreatbritain/latest)

**Randomised and quasi-experimental evidence**
- **Bloom, Eifert, Mahajan, McKenzie and Roberts (QJE 2013), Indian textile firms RCT:** free, intensive management consulting raised productivity by 17% in the first year, through better quality and efficiency. The authors attribute non-adoption of good practice mainly to firms not knowing about it, an information barrier. An earlier NBER working paper version reported about 11%. — [QJE PDF via IPA](https://poverty-action.org/sites/default/files/publications/Does-Management-Matter-Evidence-from-India-Bloom-et-al-2013.pdf); [NBER w16658](https://www.nber.org/papers/w16658); [eScholarship](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/0qh4n1gt)
- **Bruhn, Karlan and Schoar (JPE 2018, vol. 126(2)), Mexico (Puebla):** 432 SMEs, about 150 randomly given one year of subsidised management consulting. Positive impact on total factor productivity and return on assets. Working-paper versions report monthly sales up about 80% (noisy, small sample). A five-year follow-up tracked employment (summarised by Kellogg Insight), but I did not obtain the coefficients. — [MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/121090); [World Bank WP](https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/entities/publication/527d2245-f888-53e2-9192-e10c72e4d947); [Kellogg Insight](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/kwo/spr18/insight/difference-year-makes.htm)
- **Cai and Szeidl (QJE 2018, 133(3)), China (Nanchang) peer-meeting RCT:** about 2,800 young firms; 1,480 managers randomly put into monthly meetings of about 10 managers. Sales rose 8.1% at midline and 10.3% at endline (p < .05). There were also significant, lasting effects on profits, employment, fixed assets and inputs. Mechanisms: learning from peers, plus more partnerships and higher trust with firms met monthly. — [NBER w22951](https://www.nber.org/papers/w22951); [J-PAL summary](https://povertyactionlab.org/media/file-research-paper/interfirm-relationships-and-business-performance)
- **UK Growth Vouchers RCT (BIS/Behavioural Insights Team, Jan 2014 to Mar 2015; vouchers up to £2,000 for strategic advice):** 15,207 randomly selected SMEs. Turnover rose by 8.2%, but only in the short term, and possibly partly at the expense of firms that did not get support. Advice "appears to improve firms' capabilities and practices". Propensity-score matching, the usual non-experimental method, gave a sizeable upward bias that grew over time. — [LSE CEP discussion paper / LSE Research Online 2024](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/126834); [gov.uk Growth Vouchers collection](https://www.gov.uk/government/collections/growth-vouchers-programme); [BIS 6-month impact report, Feb 2016](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a7f4b52ed915d74e6229956/BIS-16-30-growth-vouchers-programme-evaluation-cohort-1-impact-at-6-months.pdf)
- **Help to Grow: Management, Phase 1 final evaluation (Ipsos/IES for DBT, June 2026):**
  - The programme is a 12-week course at UK business schools, with £750 paid by the SME and about 90% subsidised. It includes 10 hours of one-to-one mentoring and peer learning. 11,744 leaders enrolled on completed cohorts, and 84% completed. Total public funding was £223m.
  - **No statistically significant evidence of growth or firm-level productivity gains.** Administrative data only runs to March 2023, and the report says such effects "typically emerge at least five years post-intervention".
  - Survival: leaders who dropped out or withdrew were 1.3 percentage points less likely to still be trading than completers (marginal effect −0.013, p = 0.052). Partial completers showed no significant difference. Note that the comparison is with dropouts, not a randomised control group.
  - Self-reported results: 75% say it increased resilience, and 42% say it helped pandemic recovery. Completers showed significantly more confidence than matched non-participants. Mentoring take-up was weaker than planned: 87% were matched with a mentor, and 61% of those completed all 10 hours. Growth Action Plan completion missed target.
  - Source: [HtGM final evaluation, June 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a297517d95ffddb05d4ae28/help-to-grow-management-phase-1-final-evaluation-report-process-and-impact-evaluation.pdf); [Year 3 report](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/679cf4903f28b7444270a35d/help-to-grow-management-end-of-year-three-evaluation-report.pdf)
- An independent academic study of HtGM also finds low engagement with the peer and mentoring parts. — [Journal of Management and Organization](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-management-and-organization/article/does-business-training-make-a-difference-exploring-individual-and-strategic-business-outcomes-of-the-help-to-grow-management-programme/7B6F42A7FF8459D86DAD4B306610BA3D)
- **Goldman Sachs 10,000 Small Businesses UK [programme-funded, not independent]:**
  - The 2025 "15 years" impact report covers 2,500+ alumni. It gives self-reported revenue increases, with 51% attributing growth to the programme, and modelled figures for jobs and revenue.
  - The 2018 report claimed alumni grow revenue 25% faster than similar high-growth firms, and estimated £307m extra revenue and 5,840 jobs. The 2021 report said 48% of alumni increased turnover in the previous 12 months.
  - There is no randomised design, and alumni are selected for high growth.
  - Sources: [GS 15-year report PDF](https://d3cobg6h0snvt3.cloudfront.net/images/community-impact/10000-small-businesses/uk/news-and-programme-information/15-year-impact-report/celebrating-15-years-of-growth.pdf); [GS UK 15 years page](https://www.goldmansachs.com/community-transformation/10000-small-businesses/uk/insights/10ksbuk-15-years-of-growth); [UCL/ERC progress report news](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/news/news-articles/1114/171114_UCL_Goldman_Sachs_10000_small_businesses_progress_report)

**Missing middle and the UK productivity gap: is it linked to poor management in owner-led firms?**
- Bloom and Van Reenen (World Management Survey, medium-sized manufacturers in the US, France, Germany and the UK): weak competition and family firms account for about half of the "long tail" of badly managed firms. Firms run by founders' descendants, especially eldest sons (primogeniture), score worst. British and French firms show more primogeniture. — [CEP abstract](https://cep.lse.ac.uk/_new/publications/abstract.asp?index=2313); [LSE impact case study](https://www.lse.ac.uk/research/research-impact-case-studies/2021/improving-productivity-through-better-management-practices)
- Productivity Institute Working Paper 051 (Cardiff, March 2025): family ownership weakens the positive link between management practices and productivity, especially for target-setting, performance review and bonuses. Bringing in professional management softens this. — [TPI WP051](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/176851/1/WP051-The-Role-of-Management-Practices-in-Productivity-March-2025.pdf)
- The HtGM rationale (DBT) states that UK productivity has not recovered since 2008, that the slowdown is concentrated among a "long tail" of SMEs, and that firm-level productivity can be raised by better leadership and management. It cites Haldane's 2019 "hub, no spokes" speech and the 2018 Commons "Small Business and Productivity" report. — [HtGM final evaluation](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a297517d95ffddb05d4ae28/help-to-grow-management-phase-1-final-evaluation-report-process-and-impact-evaluation.pdf)

### Inferences
- What works best is intensive, hands-on, implementation-focused consulting (India, Mexico) and regular peer groups (China). A 12-week class (HtGM) or a one-off £2,000 voucher (Growth Vouchers) does much less. A staged, ongoing Profit Coach model plus peer groups matches the stronger evidence. A course-only product does not.
- Effect sizes to quote with care:
  - India: +17% productivity, but these were large plants and the consulting was worth about $250k per plant at market rates (from my knowledge of the paper, not verified here).
  - China: +8 to 10% sales from peer meetings, which are cheap to run.
  - UK vouchers: +8.2% turnover, short-lived.
  - HtGM: no measurable growth effect yet.
- Information barriers ("firms don't know they are badly managed", Bloom et al.) are an argument for a diagnostic score as the entry point. That is BCA's BOSS Score model.
- UK claims of large programme effects (10KSB, business-school marketing) mostly rest on self-report and selection. The LSE Growth Vouchers paper shows matching methods overstate effects. BCA marketing should not lean on those numbers as causal.
- Family-managed, owner-led firms are where the management gap is biggest. That is BCA's market, and the gap shrinks when outside, professional management practice is brought in.

### Gaps
- I did not verify these figures this session:
  - Bloom, Sadun and Van Reenen's estimate of the share of cross-country TFP gaps explained by management (often quoted as about 30%).
  - The WMS UK-versus-US management score gap.
  - McKenzie and Woodruff's meta-analysis of business training effect sizes. My recollection is small, often insignificant effects for micro-enterprise training.
- The Bruhn, Karlan and Schoar employment coefficients from the 5-year follow-up were not obtained.
- I found no RCT of paid, private-sector business coaching for UK SMEs of £1M+ turnover. Evidence for "coaching" specifically, as opposed to consulting or training, is thin.
- Not covered: the ScaleUp Institute and Be the Business "Mentor Me" evaluations.
- I did not get the exact effect sizes from the Growth Vouchers paper beyond turnover, such as employment or how long the effect lasted.

## (c) How UK SMEs are adopting AI, and whether that reduces their need for advisers

### Takeaway
UK business use of AI has roughly tripled since 2023: about 35% of firms with 10+ staff in June 2026, up from about 12% in Sept 2023 (ONS BICS). Use is shallow, though. Only 10% use it "extensively", the average adopter uses 1.6 tools, and 41% of firms report no barriers, which suggests many don't see a use case. Owners increasingly ask AI first. A vendor survey says 7 in 10 UK SMEs act on AI advice before speaking to their accountant. But surveys keep finding low confidence, accuracy worries and a wish for human oversight on strategic decisions. AI looks set to replace low-value, look-up advice, while raising demand for help with judgement, implementation and AI adoption itself.

### Cited Findings
**Adoption: official data**
- **ONS BICS (wave 159, June 2026; released 20 July 2026), firms with 10+ staff unless stated:**
  - About 35% use at least one AI technology, up from about 12% in Sept to Oct 2023. By size: 28% of 0 to 9 employee firms and 49% of 250+.
  - Technologies used: large language models 18%, visual content creation 16%, ML data processing 12%, image processing 6%, robotics 2%.
  - Main use: improving business operations (about 60%).
  - Depth: 1.6 AI technologies per adopter (1.4 in 2023). 10% use AI extensively. In 15% of firms more than half of staff use AI daily. Only 11% have trained more than half their workforce.
  - 55% of employees use AI for work, against 35% of businesses, so staff are ahead of firm policy.
  - Sector: information and communication 58%, construction 13%.
  - Barriers: 41% report none. Lack of expertise about 18% (100 to 249 staff); cost 7 to 14%. Earlier analysis named difficulty identifying use cases, cost and lack of expertise as the top barriers.
  - Headcount: about half report no impact; about 6 to 7% report a decrease.
  - Source: [ONS, AI in UK businesses 2023 to 2026](https://www.ons.gov.uk/businessindustryandtrade/business/businessservices/articles/artificialintelligenceinukbusinesses/2023to2026)
- The all-sizes ONS headline was 29% in June 2026, 8pp higher than June 2025. A later reading of about 30% (Sept 2026, wave 165) is reported second-hand. — [The Next Web on ONS](https://thenextweb.com/news/uk-ai-adoption-widening-not-deepening-ons-2026) **[secondary]**; [whito.co.uk](https://whito.co.uk/research/uk-ai-adoption-statistics/) **[secondary, unverified]**
- DSIT AI adoption research is reported as 32% adoption in 2026 (16% in 2025), with limited AI skills a barrier for 60% of businesses. — reported via [whito.co.uk](https://whito.co.uk/research/uk-ai-adoption-statistics/) **[secondary, unverified]**
- **LSBS 2024:** 65% of SME employers use technology or software to manage the business (45% in 2022); 80% use accountancy software. — [DBT LSBS 2024](https://gov.uk/government/statistics/small-business-survey-2024-businesses-with-employees/longitudinal-small-business-survey-2024-sme-employers-businesses-with-1-to-249-employees)

**Adoption: business groups and vendor surveys**
- **BCC with TCS (Nov 2026):** 1,300+ business leaders, 87% SMEs. 72% of firms "have adopted AI" (a broad definition), but deeper use lags. Barriers: security, privacy and compliance 40%; skills or knowledge gaps 33%; cost 17%. — [BCC, Nov 2026](https://www.britishchambers.org.uk/news/2026/11/business-ai-adoption-rises-but-deeper-use-lags)
- **BCC/Intuit (Sept 2025) [vendor co-funded]:** 35% of SMEs actively using AI (25% in 2024); firms with no AI plans fell from 43% to 33%. Barriers listed: disconnected tools, manual processes, scattered data, uncertainty about where to deploy AI, weak security. — [BCC "The Turning Point for SMEs" PDF](https://www.britishchambers.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/The-Turning-Point-for-SMEs-Unlocking-the-next-level-of-AI.pdf)
- **BCC (Dec 2025):** 89% of retail staff had received no AI coaching. BCC launched an AI Academy (with Pair) in Oct 2025, which signals demand for AI upskilling help. — [BCC, Dec 2025](https://www.britishchambers.org.uk/news/2025/12/retail-staff-being-left-behind-on-ai/)
- **Simply Business (2026; 1,842 UK owners) [vendor]:** AI use rose from 22% (2025) to 47%. Only 19% are "very confident" using it day to day. Barriers: security and privacy 44%; doubts about accuracy 36% among hesitant owners. — [Business Matters](https://bmmagazine.co.uk/ai/ai-adoption-small-businesses-doubles/); [ITBrief](https://itbrief.co.uk/story/uk-small-firms-ai-use-more-than-doubles-in-a-year)
- **FSB:** 46% of small firms say they lack the knowledge to use AI. — reported via [Thames Valley Chamber](https://www.thamesvalleychamber.co.uk/only-1-in-10-uk-smes-are-turning-ai-into-real-productivity-gains/) **[secondary, unverified]**. The same article's headline says only 1 in 10 UK SMEs are turning AI into real productivity gains.

**Do owners use AI instead of advisers, and do they trust it?**
- **Ravical / Censuswide (500 UK SMEs) [vendor: accounting-tech]:** 7 in 10 act on AI-generated financial, tax or business advice before consulting their accountant; only 5% rarely or never do. 92% would pay more for broader advisory that fits their needs. — [cfotech](https://cfotech.co.uk/story/uk-smes-turn-to-ai-before-accountants-survey-finds); [Startups.co.uk](https://startups.co.uk/news/ai-financial-advice/); [Ravical](https://ravical.com/resources/ai-impact-on-uk-accounting-clients)
- **American Express "Peer Power" (500 UK owners) [vendor]:** 26% now get guidance from generative AI that they previously sought from fellow business owners. **Worldpay [vendor]:** 53% consult AI platforms such as ChatGPT for business advice. — [AI Magazine](https://aimagazine.com/news/smbs-turn-to-ai-for-advice-while-trusting-tech-partners); [ChannelX](https://channelx.world/2026/03/uk-small-business-owners-turn-to-ai-for-advice-but-human-connection-matters-most/) **[secondary]**
- **Karbon (US/intl, 350 small business owners, Aug 2026) [vendor: practice-management software]:** clients believe AI will not and cannot replace their accountant. They want human oversight of strategic decisions and value relationship qualities such as knowing their history, judgement, emotional support, proactivity and accountability. — [CPA Practice Advisor](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2026/08/05/clients-believe-ai-will-not-and-cannot-replace-their-accountant/187959/)
- **Small Business Britain / Alibaba.com (1,000 UK owners) [vendor co-funded]:** 70% are somewhat or very confident with generative AI and 48% use it regularly. The report calls for "trusted training and clear guidance around responsible use". — via [ChannelX](https://channelx.world/2026/03/uk-small-business-owners-turn-to-ai-for-advice-but-human-connection-matters-most/) **[secondary]**
- **Australia, NAIC SME AI Adoption Tracker (Dec 2025 to Feb 2026):** 65% of non-adopters cite lack of trust in AI decision-making as the biggest barrier. — reported in search results; the primary source was not opened **[unverified]**.
- The internet or search engine as an advice source fell from 10% (2022) to 6% (2024) among SME advice-seekers. LSBS does not yet list AI as a source. — [DBT LSBS 2024](https://gov.uk/government/statistics/small-business-survey-2024-businesses-with-employees/longitudinal-small-business-survey-2024-sme-employers-businesses-with-1-to-249-employees)

### Inferences
- AI is replacing the "search engine" layer of advice: quick tax, legal and finance look-ups. It is not yet replacing paid, relationship-based advisers. In the official data, paid advice use is flat to slightly up (65% paying), and consultant use by larger SMEs is high.
- The gap between adoption (29 to 35% official; 47 to 72% in broad vendor or BCC definitions) and depth (10% extensive use, 19% "very confident", 1 in 10 getting productivity gains) is a service opportunity. Owners repeatedly cite skills, knowledge and use-case gaps (BCC 33%; FSB 46% [unverified]; ONS "identifying use cases"). An adviser who brings AI tools into the firm, as with BCA's platform, answers a stated need.
- Asking AI before the accountant raises the bar for advisers. Owners arrive pre-informed, so value moves to judgement, accountability, implementation and peer contact. Those match the mechanisms in the strongest RCTs: hands-on implementation, and peer trust and learning.
- The trust data is almost all vendor-funded, and the vendors gain from either "AI replaces" or "humans still needed" stories. Treat it as directional only.

### Gaps
- I found no robust, independent UK survey that directly compares SME owners' trust in AI against a human adviser for strategic decisions.
- I found no UK data on whether AI use has reduced SME spending on accountants or consultants.
- The Sage, Xero and Intuit QuickBooks 2025 to 2026 UK SME AI surveys were not found or opened this session.
- The primary DSIT "AI adoption research" and FSB AI figures were not opened; the numbers above come via aggregators.
- ONS BICS figures for share planning to adopt AI were not in the fetched article. A secondary source cites 18% planning to adopt within 3 months, unverified.
