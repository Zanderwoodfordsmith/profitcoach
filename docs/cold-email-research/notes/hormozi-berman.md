# Email notes: Hormozi and Berman

## Video 1: Alex Hormozi, "Learn Email Marketing in 39 Minutes!" (pLhQOYMGa88)

**What it covers: email to your own opted-in list (newsletter, broadcast, nurture) only. There is nothing on cold email.** He says the contact was already "acquired" and email is "just follow up" [00:01:00]. Use it for the nurture emails a coach sends owners after a BOSS Score Review, not for first-touch cold outreach. The deliverability habits (plain text, few links, getting replies) also carry over to cold email.

**1. Core principles**
- An email has one of two jobs: make a purchase more likely later, or get one now. Treat it as content [00:02:01].
- Reward every step. The subject earns the open, the first line rewards the open, the body rewards the read, and the PS rewards finishing [00:09:00–00:12:00].
- Skip an email rather than send a bad one. One useless email teaches people to stop opening [00:13:00].
- Who gets the email matters more than how good it is [00:08:00].

**2. Sequence structure**
- About 3 a week is the ideal pace. More sends mean more sales, as long as every email is worth reading [00:21:01].
- Starting out: send once a month. Write the year's emails in one sitting (he wrote 24 in half a day) [00:22:01].
- Adding email follow-up to Skool (his online community product) gave a 40% lift in conversion [00:23:01].
- Welcome email: build some anticipation, deliver the promised freebie, sell them on staying subscribed, and ask them to reply "yes" to get a bonus [00:29:01–00:31:00].

**3. Copy**
- Keep the same layout every time: subject, quick reward (a quote or tweet), one idea in under 200 words, CTA, then PS [00:15:00–00:16:00].
- One idea per email. Set up a problem that makes the fix make sense, then give a tactic they can use right away [00:11:01].
- Always add a PS. "Beyond the headline it's the most read part of the email" [00:12:00].
- Make the CTA fit the email, not a copied block. One or two CTAs are fine if each is for a different reader, for example "scaling? go here / starting? go here" [00:18:01], [00:37:01].
- Write the preview text yourself. 24% of people read it before opening [00:17:01].
- Example subject: "Mozy Money Minute: 391% increase in sales from one change" [00:15:00].

**4. Targeting and personalisation**
- Segmenting the list (HubSpot data) gave a 791% increase in email ROI. He sends by revenue band: 0–100K, 500K+, 5M+ [00:07:00–00:08:00].

**5. Deliverability**
- Make it look like an everyday email: all text, no images, one or two links at most [00:24:01–00:25:00].
- Take money language out of the text, because it pushes emails into the promotions tab [00:25:00].
- 16.99% of B2B marketing emails never reach the inbox [00:25:00].
- Replies tell the email provider people want your mail, so ask for replies [00:29:01].
- Make unsubscribing easy. People who stay subscribed but stop reading hurt deliverability [00:06:01].
- Links to Google's own services (YouTube, Google Drive) get through more often [00:26:01].

**6. Numbers**
- Email ROI is roughly 35–45 times what you spend [00:00:00].
- His first send lost just over 1% of the list to unsubscribes. Now it is about 0.42% per send [00:08:01].
- Open rate is 35.7% and click rate 8.5%. Track clicks over opens, because Apple's privacy changes make open rates unreliable [00:31:00–00:32:01].
- Best days: B2B does best on Wednesday (about 18% higher open rate). B2C does best on Monday and Tuesday [00:33:01].
- Best times: 10am to noon and 1pm to 3pm [00:34:02].

**7. Mistakes**
- Making unsubscribing hard [00:05:00].
- Sending the freebie on its own without selling them on staying subscribed [00:31:00].
- Copy-pasting the same generic CTA into every email [00:37:01].
- Emails that don't display well on a phone (58–60% of web traffic is mobile) [00:14:01].

**8. Small changes, big difference**
- Write the preview text: about 10 seconds of work [00:17:01].
- Split-test the subject line on a small slice of the list, then send the winner to everyone else [00:27:00].
- Put a teaser in the email and the full piece behind a link, so readers get used to clicking [00:13:00].

**9. For a free review offer**
- After the review, send short one-idea nurture emails that help the owner straight away. The link to book is always there, like a "link in bio" [00:21:01].
- Ask owners to reply, for example "reply yes and I'll send the checklist" [00:29:01].
- Split the list by turnover band [00:08:00].
- Send on Wednesday, 10am to noon or 1pm to 3pm [00:33:01].

## Video 2: Alex Berman, "How To Write Killer Cold Emails That GUARANTEE Responses (45.8% Reply Rate?!)" (881Dr4lMey4)

**What it covers: cold email.** He claims 30–50% reply rates [00:00:00]. The 45.8% figure is only in the title.

**1. Core principles**
- The PC formula relies on "open loops": people remember things left unfinished [00:00:00].
- Show you did the research. Put the personal detail in line 1 and the direct ask in line 2. Going broad fails [00:02:00].
- The formulas are "worthless" without the basics in section 3 [00:07:00].

**2. Sequence structure**
- He gives no number of emails or timing. He only mentions that follow-ups are covered in his paid course [00:06:00].

**3. Copy: the three formulas**
- **PC = Pain point + CTA** [00:01:00]: "Hey Andrew, noticed that Workday still hasn't found a digital sales manager. Bummer. I run a cold email automation tool that can make this role irrelevant and flood you with meetings. Want me to send more info?"
- Second PC example [00:01:00]: "Hey Mike, your website speed is worse than 87.6% of your competitors based on a test our dev team just conducted. We developed an AI software that can drastically improve this… Want to try it out?"
- **PEC = Personal story (their biggest fear or desire) + Evidence (case study) + CTA.** He calls the template "the Biggest Fear" [00:03:00]: "Julia, I know you're worried about missing out on building personal connections and trust with your clients… With that in mind, I created a tool that will help BombBomb qualify leads faster… Just like we did for Mailchimp, this can be a game-changer for your sales team." Name a big client if you have one. Otherwise say "just like we did for another email provider". Don't lie in case studies [00:03:00–00:04:00].
- **PPC = Pain point + Partial solution + CTA to explain the rest** [00:05:01–00:06:00]: "Hey Scott, noticed your team was spending a ton of money to sponsor Clutch. Did you know that by tweaking the wording on your profile every day, you can rank higher organically? The clients I've done this for have seen no reduction in lead flow even after they stopped their sponsorships, saving them thousands each month. If you like that, I've got three more ideas for you to implement to increase your agency lead flow. Let's do a call this week." This is the new version of his "two ideas email".
- A PPC variation: send a Loom video audit of something to improve, then ask them to book a call to hear the full fix [00:06:00].
- Subject line: "Something simple like 'quick question' still works best" [00:07:00].

**4. Targeting and personalisation**
- Find the pain point from public signals: LinkedIn job ads left unfilled, a website speed test, paid listings on directories like Clutch ($4K–12K+) [00:01:00], [00:05:01].
- Spend minutes researching each lead, tailored "in a way that AI can't do". A personal compliment is no longer enough [00:07:00].

**5. Deliverability**
- Not covered. The nearest point: have a website and post now and then, so you aren't "an online ghost" when they Google you [00:08:01].

**6. Numbers**
- PEC-style emails at Galadon (his own product) built $170,000 of pipeline in under 3 weeks [00:04:00].

**7. Mistakes**
- Generic pitches, such as "you run a website, I have a tool that helps websites" [00:02:00].
- Made-up case studies [00:03:00].
- No visible presence online [00:08:01].

**8. Small changes, big difference**
- End the PPC email by offering "three more ideas". The call is pitched as getting the rest of the solution [00:06:00].

**9. For a free review offer**
- PPC fits a BOSS Score Review best. Name one visible leak in their business, give one quick fix, then offer the rest on a 30-minute call.
- The Loom audit version is close to a free review: one finding up front, the full answer on the call [00:06:00].
- For PEC, use a real result from a similar UK business, unnamed if needed ("just like we did for another £3M manufacturer").
