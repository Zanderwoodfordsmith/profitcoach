# Lead Gen Jay, 7-hour cold email course, part 2 of 3 (02:29:00 to 04:54:00)

This part covers the end of deliverability, lead lists, verification, AI qualification, offers, lead magnets, copywriting, sequences, split testing, opt-outs and how to build a campaign in Instantly.

## 1. Core principles

- Cold leads "do not know you, don't trust you, and are not looking for your solution". You are making targeted guesses that they have the pain right now. Most guesses are wrong, and that's fine. [02:41:00]
- The email's job is not to sell. It is to find out whether the lead has a problem you can solve and get them to "raise their hand". "The goal is not to get them to buy from your first cold email." [03:50:01] [04:07:00]
- It should read like an email to a friend, not a sales pitch. [04:08:01]
- When results are poor, the lead list is the most overlooked cause, and the one everybody thinks they have right. [02:40:01]
- Emailing the wrong person with the wrong details is the quickest way to get spam reports. A good list is worth paying for. [03:21:00] [03:22:00]
- The offer comes first: know who you serve and what they actually want. "If you don't know, ask." Keep the offer clear and simple. [03:49:01]

## 2. Campaign structure

- Default is **3 emails**. Use 2 to 3. As few as 1 works if you have a huge market. [04:29:01] [04:30:01]
  - Email 1: the "triple tap" (preview text, then body, then CTA). Heavily A/B tested.
  - Email 2: a simple nudge in the same thread. Example: "Just touching base. If you had a chance to review my last email, let me know, or can I provide some additional information?" [04:31:01]
  - Email 3: same thread or a new one (test both). This is where you "dump" the information: explainer video, features, case study links. [04:30:01]
- Leave **2 to 5 days** between emails. In his live build: step 2 after 1 to 3 days ("Did you catch my email yesterday?" needs a 1-day gap), step 3 after 5 days. [04:30:01] [04:52:00]
- Email 1 almost always performs best. Every extra email raises the risk of spam complaints, which is why they now stop at 3. [04:27:00]
- Short sequences reach more people. Use 4 to 7 emails only when the market is small, and only if each email brings new social proof or a new lead magnet and pulls a different lever (money, speed, prestige, story, value stacking, scarcity). Stop if spam rises and replies fall. [04:31:01] [04:32:00]
- **Subsequences** are automatic follow-ups triggered by reply keywords. Example: "reply 'video' and I'll send a custom strategy"; a reply containing "video" (catch misspellings too) triggers an instant follow-up with 0 days' wait, in the same thread. [04:54:01]

## 3. Copy

- **Triple tap:** (1) the open, which depends only on the subject line plus first sentence (the preview text); (2) the read, which is the body; (3) the action, which is the CTA. Write, review and test each part separately. [04:08:01] [04:09:00]
- **The preview text must not signal a sale.** It should look like it could be from a client or supplier, and it must relate to the body: no bait and switch. [04:11:00]
- **Questions get opens.** The old classic was "Question, {first name}?", which is now tired but still worth testing against. [04:12:02]
  - Subject "Sending cold emails?", first line "Not sure if you're sending these or not. {Name}, mind checking?" [04:12:02] [04:13:00]
  - Subject "Is this you on Google?", first line "Just came across this on Google. Can you confirm this is you or not?" [04:13:00]
  - From the live build: subject "Question {firstName}", first line "Hey {firstName}, what's going on? Had a question about your email campaigns. Do you have time to chat?" [04:50:01]
- **The body does two things:** name their problem and how you fix it (the mechanism), and build trust without bragging. Two or three sentences can do it. [04:14:00]
  - Example: "I'm actually running the outbound marketing for Lead Gen Jay. After seeing their pretty crazy success, I was hoping I could do the same for you. Here's an article that USA Today wrote about my methods." [04:15:02]
  - Example take-away line: "If you already have more qualified calls than you can handle, ignore me. But if you're interested in building an outbound machine, I'd love to show you the strategy." [04:15:02]
- **Make the social proof personal:** name clients the reader knows, from their own industry, with specific results. A big name in their own world beats a random name from another sector. [04:16:01]
- **The CTA should be answered with one word ("yes", "sure", "send it").** No links, no clicks, nothing to think about. The test: can they reply one-handed in under a minute? Example: "Would it be cool if I send over the strategy?" [04:17:01]
- **Rules:** six sentences at most, written for a 12-year-old (sixth-grade level). Use Claude to simplify. Match the tone to the reader's profile (age, sex, sector): for example, no jokes with a car dealer in his 50s or 60s. [04:17:01] [04:18:00]
- **No case studies yet?** Use your social following, a YouTube video, press coverage, or do free work to earn one "anchor" case study. [04:18:00]
- **Don'ts:** over-promising ("100 leads in 30 days or your money back" with no "how"), being vague about how you do it, confusing the reader, bait and switch, salesy or promotional words. [04:20:02] [04:21:01]
- **Personalisation:** always use the first name. Company names (with "LLC" or "Ltd", or in capitals) and locations give away that it's a cold email and are often wrong. Done badly, AI personalisation hurts about 95% of the people using it. [04:21:01] [04:22:01]
- **Spintax is "mandatory".** Use `{{random|Hi|Hello|Hey}}`. Preview it, and ask an AI to list every possible version, because some combinations read badly. Then run a spam-word check. [04:22:01] to [04:25:01]

## 4. Targeting and lists

- **Who replies most:** people who get fewer marketing emails ("blue oceans"), decision makers rather than mid-level staff, younger businesses, people new in their role, and ignored sectors such as manufacturing and farming. [02:44:01] to [02:48:00]
- **Who replies least:** big companies (with 500+ staff, expect about 1 positive reply in 1,000), heavily targeted groups (agency owners, doctors, lawyers, anyone who "has money"), and overused signals such as "recently funded". [02:49:01] [02:50:01]
- **The money and replies trade-off:** the more money a lead has, the less likely they are to reply. Look for people who have money but are ignored. [02:51:01]
- **If you have customers:** export them from your CRM, with job title, sector and size, and use AI to profile them. If not, describe your offer in detail and ask AI to suggest segments. Test **5 to 10 segments** at launch. [02:42:00] [02:43:00]
- **Filters to always use:** job title, location (company headquarters), company size (headcount range, for example 3 to 100), company keywords (more accurate than "industry"), and email status = verified. [03:04:01] [03:23:00] to [03:25:00]
- **Filters to treat with care:**
  - Industry overlaps badly (for example, "automotive" pulls in suppliers and agencies). Use keywords, and use exclusion keywords to trim.
  - Technologies and job postings are useful but out of date.
  - Apollo's signals, buying-intent data and revenue figures don't work. Private companies' revenue is a guess, so use headcount as a stand-in. [03:05:00] [03:09:00] [03:12:01]
- **Always check the list by eye.** Go through the results, find wrong matches and exclude those keywords, until about 9 in 10 fit. [03:26:00] to [03:29:01]
- **Where to find leads:** Apollo (filter, then scrape the search URL through TrustedLeads, about $50 per 10,000). If there are no company LinkedIn pages, scrape Google Maps instead. Pull one region at a time to avoid duplicates. [02:56:00] to [03:01:01]
- **AI qualification:** ask an AI to answer "Yes/No: good fit?" about each lead, using the company description, sector, job title and ideally its website. Expect it to remove 30 to 50% of the list. Scrape twice the data you need. Prompt: "You're an expert sales assistant... decide if they're a good fit... Here's my offer: I help X with Y... Output yes or no." [03:44:01] to [03:47:01]
- **Signals work best:** the Inc 5000 winners campaign got a 5% reply rate. Build your own signals with Apify and n8n. [03:13:00] to [03:16:01]

## 5. Deliverability and infrastructure

- **Instantly inbox placement test** ($97/month): an automated daily test that uses your real campaign copy.
  - If placement falls below 80%: pause campaign sending from that inbox for 30 days and tag it "spam warm-up".
  - When it recovers above 80%: turn on a "slow ramp" back up.
  - Don't automate on blacklists, because some don't matter. [02:29:00] to [02:32:00]
- **Inboxes still bad after 60 days of fixes:** replace them. [02:29:00]
- **Warming your main domain:** use a separate unmonitored mailbox on the same domain. Inbox providers discount warm-up emails that you filter or archive. You can also warm a GoHighLevel sending domain through SMTP into Warmy. [02:33:00] to [02:35:01]
- **Google Postmaster Tools:** the only place to see spam-complaint rates for Gmail users. Add your main domains and sending subdomains. He once spiked to 1.5% complaints. [02:36:00] to [02:38:01]
- **Fault-finding order:**
  1. Check DNS (EasyDMARC or MXToolbox; DMARC p=reject is ideal).
  2. Check blacklists.
  3. Run a GlockApps placement test with your real copy. [02:38:01] [02:39:00]
- **Bounces kill your sender reputation as fast as spam reports do.** Send only to addresses marked "good". [03:31:00] [03:34:00]
- **Verification steps:**
  1. Run the list through MillionVerifier.
  2. Re-check the "risky" ones (catch-all or unknown) with FindyMail. About 50% of risky addresses turn out to be deliverable, which matters most when your market is small.
  3. Clay can chain both steps. [03:36:01] to [03:42:01]
- **Instantly settings:** turn open tracking off. Send email 1 as plain text only, with no links. [04:09:00] [04:42:01]
- **Links after email 1:** only links people trust (YouTube, Calendly, Loom), not your new domain's booking page, and never hyperlinked text. [04:43:00]
- **Replying:** once the lead replies it's a conversation, not a cold email. Reply from the same inbox; don't forward the thread to another one. [04:45:01]
- **Spam-word checkers:** Instantly's built-in checker, Mailmeteor, or EmailGuard. [04:39:00] [04:40:00]

## 6. Numbers and benchmarks

- A typical Apollo or TrustedLeads export is only 70 to 80% valid, even with the "verified" filter on. One sample was 75% good, 22% risky and 3% bad. [03:36:01] [03:38:01]
- AI qualification gives "2 to 3x" the reply rate and cuts spam complaints. [03:45:00]
- Jay's own campaigns get at least 2%, and many 5 to 20%. [04:07:00]
- His "your email landed in my spam" signal campaign gets a 20% reply rate, 84.5% of them positive. [03:15:00]
- Good lead magnets "can unlock 10% reply rates". Reverse lead magnets got him to 2 to 3% replies on the hardest offer to sell. [03:59:00] [04:04:01]
- A rough baseline is 1% positive replies, or about 0.1% for companies with 500+ staff. [02:50:01]
- Lead costs: TrustedLeads about $50 per 10,000; Instantly SuperSearch $50 per month for 1,500; ZoomInfo about $15,000 a year; MillionVerifier $2,500 for 10 million checks. [02:59:00] [03:03:00] [03:35:00]

## 7. Mistakes to watch for

- Emailing mid-level managers instead of decision makers. [02:45:00]
- Cheap lifetime-deal databases and bought lists. [02:54:00] [03:21:00]
- Trusting your filters without reading the results. [03:27:01]
- Shocking subject lines that bait and switch. [04:10:00]
- Wild guarantees with no proof or "how". [03:50:01]
- Complicated offers. [03:51:01]
- Sending links, images, unsubscribe links or opt-out wording in email 1. [04:41:01]
- Instantly's AI sequence writer: "never gives good results". [04:26:00]
- Not split-testing emails 2 and 3. [04:37:01]

## 8. Small changes that matter

- **Split tests:**
  - Use separate campaigns for separate audiences, with the same copy in each.
  - Use A/B variants inside a campaign to test copy and offers.
  - Change one part at a time.
  - Test whole offers or lead magnets against each other first, keeping the same subject line.
  - Pick winners by **opportunity rate**, not just reply rate. [04:33:01] to [04:38:00]
- **Prompt for test variants:** "Test one element at a time... never change the offer... output 30 variations, 10 per element." [04:39:00]
- **Getting in when the offer is dull:**
  - A front-end offer (the one thing they most want) or a loss leader.
  - A "Trojan horse": an approach that isn't a pitch, such as a partnership, or "a journalist would love to write about your story".
  - Lead magnets. [03:52:01] to [03:56:02]
- **Reverse lead magnet:** offer to make them something personal, for example "I'd like to spend some time researching and putting this together for you. Is that okay?" The more effort they think you put in, the more it's worth to them. The more effort it costs them to use, the less it's worth. [03:59:00] [04:04:01]
- **Ask before sending a video** ("Okay if I send a short video?"), so the only reply needed is "yes". [04:45:01]
- **Gentle opt-out for email 2 onwards:** "If I'm barking up the wrong tree, just say 'wrong tree' and I'll remove you." Never use the word "unsubscribe". [04:42:01] [04:52:00]
- **Pull the signature in with the signature variable,** set once per inbox. [04:51:00]
- **Name campaigns "segment + offer".** [04:48:01]

## 10. Relevance to UK Profit Coaches and a free BOSS Score Review

- **Legal:**
  - Jay's legal advice is US-only (CAN-SPAM: a way to opt out, a postal address, remove people who ask). He also claims he has "never heard of a violation", and he drops opt-outs from email 1. [04:40:00] to [04:47:00]
  - **Do not copy this for the UK.** PECR and GDPR (legitimate interest, telling people who you are, an easy opt-out) apply. Keep a clear, plain-worded opt-out and a suppression list.
  - His "wrong tree" line could be the gentle wording for that opt-out, but check this with a UK compliance view.
- **Revenue filters:** he says revenue filters are guesses for private firms and to use headcount instead. For £1M to £10M UK owner-led firms, that probably means about 10 to 50 staff, set by sector.
- **Free call or audit:** a free review fits his "front-end offer" or loss leader: the thing they want, low risk, to get in the door.
  - The CTA should ask for a one-word yes, not a booking link in email 1. For example, "Worth me sending over how the BOSS Score works?" and then follow up.
  - "Would it be okay if I spent some time looking at {company} first?" (his reverse lead magnet framing) suits a diagnostic.
  - Lead magnets work better on smaller businesses than big ones. [04:00:01]
- **Decision makers:** target founders, owners and managing directors, not managers. Owner-led £1M to £10M firms in "boring" sectors (manufacturing, trades) are his blue oceans.
- **Social proof:** prefer named UK clients in the reader's own sector with specific results. A coach's own media coverage or videos can stand in early on.
- **Small market:** a single coach's local or sector market may be small, so the catch-all re-check is worthwhile, and a slightly longer sequence is justified only if each email adds new value.
