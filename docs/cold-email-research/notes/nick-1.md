# Nick Saraev, Cold Email Copywriting & Outreach Full Course 2026, part 1 of 2 (00:00 to ~01:55)

Scope note: part 1 covers psychology, campaign goals, his 4-step copy formula, the offer formula and offer examples, and the first two live email rewrites. It says almost nothing about sequences, follow-ups, subject lines in depth, list building or deliverability (he says those come later in the course, so they should be in part 2). Gaps are marked below.

## 1. Core principles
- The job is to get a stranger with zero trust to act. Unlike normal copywriting, there is no opt-in to lean on [00:00:00].
- Seven reasons people say yes to strangers (from Cialdini's *Influence*): give first, micro commitments, social proof, authority, rapport, scarcity, shared identity. He rates shared identity as the most important [00:04:00-00:05:00].
- **Give first:** every cold message should give something (an insight, a fix, a free asset) so the reader feels they got value for their attention. Example: "I noticed X is misconfigured on your landing page... I think you're losing $10-20k a month. Let me show you how to fix this" [00:07:00].
- **Micro commitments:** climb from a small yes to bigger ones (watch 1 minute, then a longer video, then a call, then the close). Never open with the big ask [00:08:00].
- **Social proof:** use specific numbers and names, and pick proof that matches the reader's own kind of business ("an IT business in Tennessee... a 15-minute drive from you"). Mention it in passing, as a throwaway line [00:10:01-00:12:02].
- **Authority:** credentials that matter to this audience (a Google Partner badge worked door to door, a neuroscience degree didn't). Write with confidence and no hedging ("I could absolutely help you") [00:13:00-00:14:01].
- **Rapport:** shared context, plus copying the reader's tone, message length and punctuation [00:14:01-00:15:00].
- **Scarcity:** only real limits, such as your own capacity or schedule, or a proposal that expires at the end of the week. Fake deadlines get spotted. He calls this the least used principle [00:16:00].
- **One message, one job:** each email should be able to do the whole job on its own, even with the rest of the sequence stripped away [00:22:02].
- **Frame = one-to-one:** write like a person, not a company. The reader must believe you wrote it just for them [00:27:00-00:30:00].
- **Iterate like a scientist:** test the market, not your gut. Copy he expected to flop has hit 15% replies [00:30:00-00:33:01].
- **A system beats a template:** templates wear out as the market gets used to them. A formula keeps producing new ones [01:32:01-01:34:00].

## 2. Campaign structure
- **Not covered in part 1.** There's nothing on the number of emails, the days between them, follow-up logic or when to stop. He says a later "follow-ups, iteration" module covers it [00:02:01].
- Pick one goal per campaign: a reply, a watch or click on an asset, a booked call, or (rarely) a purchase. Most of his campaigns aim for a reply, an asset view or a booked call. He doesn't put prices in cold emails [00:23:01-00:24:00].
- If you can't state the campaign goal in one sentence, you aren't ready to write it [00:26:01].
- Testing loop: send 500-1,000 per variant, measure, cut the losers fast, and write new variants based on the winner [00:31:02-00:33:01].

## 3. Copy
**The 4-step formula [00:35:00]: personalisation, then who am I, then the offer, then the call to action.**
- **Personalisation (first line):**
  - The opening is the highest-return part of the email because reader drop-off is steep (about 50% are gone by the fifth word).
  - Shape: a greeting, then an observation or something in common, then a bridge.
  - It must not look like selling. Keep it to 2 sentences at most, ideally 1 [00:35:00-00:37:00].
- **Cold reading:** a general line that feels personal but fits about 80% of readers. Template: "Hey Nick, love your channel, man. Very no BS and has helped me get started in management consulting. Think I can help you with something and maybe return a bit of the favor you've unwittingly done me" [00:40:00]. The aim is to make them think "wait, do I know this person?"
- **Voluntary disclosure:** share a small personal detail to build trust ("helped me get started in management consulting"; "My GF says I listen to you more than I listen to her. Lol") [00:43:00, 01:51:00].
- **Who am I (1-2 sentences):**
  - Use proof from their own world, with an exact number. Template: "I currently work with [client / industry client in location] to help them [thing]. We've done [specific number]..."
  - Write "we" to place yourself inside their group [00:46:00].
  - Example: "I just partnered with [X], a retail company around the same size as you, and generated 85k rev in 12 weeks" [01:02:01].
  - "I'm a thumbnail designer" is weak. Who you are means who you've worked with and the results [01:51:00].
- **Offer = observation + near "too good to be true" offer with risk reversal:**
  - Template: "I will do X in Y time, or Z risk mitigation."
  - It must have a number and a deadline. No ranges.
  - Examples: "I'll generate you $10,000 in 60 days or I'll keep working for free until I do"; "I'll book you 20 meetings in 60 days or you don't pay" [00:48:00-00:51:00, 01:09:00-01:10:01].
- **Call to action:**
  - Make a specific ask with a specific time. Avoid "would you be interested?" and "let me know your thoughts."
  - Template: "Would you be open to a 15-minute chat? If so, I can give you a ring at 3:30pm today or before 12pm tomorrow" [00:57:00].
  - Fuller version: "How does 3:30pm today sound? I can give you a ring at [number] or send you a one-click Google Meet invite" [01:16:02].
- **Lower the effort to start:**
  - "It'll take just 15 minutes of your time over a brief call once at the beginning, and we won't have to talk again until all 20 meetings are delivered" [01:15:00-01:18:00].
- **Subject lines:**
  - Barely covered. He leaves "quick question" alone as fine [01:40:00].
  - What counts is the sender name, the subject and roughly the first 100 characters of preview text [01:35:00-01:36:01].
- **Sign-off:** casual ("Thanks, man", or just "Thanks, [Name]"). Use "I" rather than "we help" [00:29:00].

## 4. Targeting and lists
- **Mostly not covered in part 1.**
- Match proof and tone to the segment. He suggests filtering by gender so you can use "man" or "hey girl" style language [01:41:00, 01:46:00].
- AI step-up: scrape one fact (such as their college) and slot it into a cold-read template [00:42:02].
- Scraping errors wreck trust. "Hi, Nick's Drive Daily Updates" came from scraping a channel name instead of a first name, plus a stray comma [01:48:02].
- Size the offer to the prospect's revenue. $100k extra for a business doing $5-10M is only about a 2% improvement, so it sounds believable [00:54:00-00:56:00].

## 6. Numbers and benchmarks
- A first draft typically gets about 3.5% replies. Testing takes it to 4.5%, 5%, 8% and then 10%+. One-shot wins are rare [00:20:00-00:21:01].
- Some campaigns he expected to fail hit 15% replies [00:33:01].
- Test 500-1,000 sends per variant [00:31:02].
- Each extra back-and-forth before booking loses about 5% of leads. Across several steps that adds up to about 25% of the pipeline [00:58:01].
- **His conversion formula:** conversion rate = ROI x trust you'll deliver ÷ friction to start [00:52:00, 01:06:02].
- A guarantee roughly triples the top of the funnel, at a cost of about 10% from refunds, for a net of about 2.7x [01:22:00].
- **Dental example:** offer 10 new patients in 30 days when you've proven 109 in a week. Promising well under what you've proven makes it believable [01:13:00].
- "Just say yes" offers made him $70k+ in one month, but he no longer recommends them because people are tired of them [01:25:01].

## 7. Mistakes to avoid
- AI-sounding openers ("love how passionate you are about process optimization...") are the top giveaway [00:37:00].
- Corporate signals: "hope this finds you well", big signature blocks, "we help..." [00:29:00].
- Asking for 15 minutes without giving anything. A call is taking from them, not giving [01:38:00].
- Making the reader work out the benefit for themselves. State the money or time saved [01:39:00].
- Vague results ("some meetings", ranges) or no deadline, which makes the guarantee meaningless [01:09:00-01:10:01].
- Too much effort to start (workshops, long onboarding) even when the offer is free [01:15:00].
- Repeating your name in both the body and the sender line, and padded sentences [01:49:01].
- Typos near the top. Some readers write you off right away [01:04:00].

## 8. Small changes that make a big difference
- Put two time options plus a "ring you at this number" in the CTA, so a single "yes" books the call [00:57:00, 01:16:02].
- Add one deliberate typo near the end, after trust is built, so it reads as hand-typed. He uses "GVE me a shout" [01:04:00].
- Use "Sent from my iPhone" style signals [00:29:00].
- Set the guarantee well below what you've already proven [01:13:00].
- Copy the reader's own jargon (for YouTubers: "CTR", "subs", "thumbs") to show you're one of them [01:55:01].
- Use a real capacity limit for scarcity ("juggling a few client projects") [00:16:00].
- Use the "text message test": would a friend think this was a personal message? [00:28:01].

## 10. UK / GDPR / PECR, free call/audit, service businesses
- **Compliance:** he says he'd never include an opt-out and treats LinkedIn-sourced data as "legitimate B2B comms" [01:48:02]. He also flags his later "grey hat" section as dependent on local outbound laws [00:02:01]. *My note, not in transcript:* for UK sends, under PECR and UK GDPR legitimate interest, B2B emails to corporate addresses still need the sender identified and a simple opt-out. Don't copy his no-opt-out stance.
- **A free audit as the give:** offers such as "I'll run a full SEO audit... show you exactly which pages are losing you traffic, completely free... in your inbox within 24 hours" [01:28:00]. Others are "Send me your last three email campaigns, I'll rewrite them for free" [01:31:00] and "I'll redesign your homepage... mockup within 72 hours, only pay if you love it" [01:31:00]. These map directly onto a free BOSS Score Review.
- **For a free 30-minute review call, apply his rules:**
  - State exactly what they get (for example, a score plus the top leaks) and the timeframe.
  - Make starting nearly effortless ("15 minutes", "I'll ring you at 3:30 today or before 12 tomorrow").
  - Offer proof from a business their size and sector, with a number.
  - Use a real capacity limit.
- **Selling services:** booking a call is the normal goal for service sellers. He thinks the guarantee-led offer ("X result in Y time or you don't pay") is what gets high-ticket service sellers past a stranger's scepticism, and he recommends offers that aren't purely money-based [00:24:00, 00:52:00, 00:57:00].
