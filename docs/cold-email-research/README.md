All four cold email teachers are US-based. Where they clash with UK rules, the UK rule wins (see "UK rules" below).

## The big six they all agree on

1. **Who you email and what you offer matter more than the words.** Jay calls the list "the most overlooked" cause of bad results. Berman says test the offer and the list, not the copy. Hormozi: who gets the email matters more than how good it is.
2. **Email 1 doesn't sell. It gets a hand raised.** The goal is a one-word reply ("yes", "sure", "send it"), not a booked call or a sale. No price, no link.
3. **Plain, short and human.** Plain text, no links, images or tracking in email 1. Six sentences at most, written for a 12-year-old. "I" not "we". First name only. It should pass the text-message test: would a friend think this was a personal message?
4. **Give something first, ideally a finding about their own business.** Nick's "give first", Berman's PPC (pain point, part of the fix, then "I've got three more ideas"), Jay's reverse lead magnet ("Would it be okay if I spent some time looking at this for you?"). Jay thinks cold email is heading towards showing the prospect a specific problem before you ever ask for anything.
5. **Never send from your main domain.** Separate lookalike domains, Google mailboxes, at least 30 days old and warmed up, 20 to 25 emails a day per mailbox, every address verified. Keep warm-up on for good.
6. **Measure positive replies, test properly.** Judge on opportunities, not opens or raw replies. 500 to 1,000 sends per version before picking a winner. Change one thing at a time and always keep a control.

## Campaign structure that works

- **Email 1 (day 0).** Subject plus preview text that don't look like a sale. Body: their problem and how you fix it, one line of proof from someone like them, then a one-word call to action.
- **Email 2 (day 2 or 3, same thread).** A short human nudge: "Hey Pete, let me know if this got lost in your inbox." Nick rewrites the opening line and tries a different subject each time.
- **Email 3 (day 5 to 7).** Test a new thread with a new subject ("Yes or no?") against the same thread. A different angle, or the fuller information (a short video or case study). Add the soft opt-out.
- **Stop there.** Nick starts with 2 and only adds a step once a campaign is winning (2 emails 4.8% replies, 3 emails 6.1%, 4 emails 6.8%). Jay defaults to 3. Longer untested sequences earn spam clicks that damage the mailboxes.
- **Automatic branches.** "Reply 'video' and I'll send it" triggers an instant follow-up. "Not now" goes to a 30-day wait. People who never replied can be tried again after 3 to 6 months, never sooner, with new proof.
- **When they reply,** it's no longer cold: links, video and booking links are fine. Answer their question first, then give a next step with set times. If they go quiet, nudge after a week. Booking often takes 2 to 4 replies.

## Copy formulas worth using

- **Subject line:** its job is to get the email opened, not to sell. It works when the reader can't tell if you're a seller, a friend or a customer: "Nick", "Question, Nick?", "quick question". Loss beats gain ("nick, you're wasting 2,300 a month"). Avoid pitches, "Quick collab?" and blank subjects.
- **Preview text:** about 150 characters show before anyone opens. It must look like it could be from a client or supplier and must match the body.
- **Berman's three formulas:**
  - **PC:** pain point, then the ask.
  - **PEC:** their biggest fear or desire, evidence from a similar client, then the ask.
  - **PPC:** pain point, part of the fix, then "I've got three more ideas, worth a call?" PPC fits a BOSS Score Review best.
- **Nick's four steps:** a personal opening line, who you are (who you've helped and the number), the offer (a number and a deadline, risk on you), then the ask.
- **Proof:** one similar business, with a number, said in passing. Promise less than you've proven. Size the claim to their turnover so it's believable (Nick: about a 20% lift reads as realistic).
- **Name the doubt:** "I know this is the first you're hearing of me…"
- **The ask:** "Would it be okay if I sent over…?" (one-word yes), or two set times plus "I'll ring you". Never "Would you be interested?" or "Let me know your thoughts".
- **Take-away line:** "If you already have more calls than you can handle, ignore me."

## Numbers to know

- **Results:** good and scalable is 1 to 2% replies and about 1% positive. Above 5% is excellent (usually narrow lists built on a signal). Under 1% replies usually means you're landing in spam, not that the copy is bad.
- **First drafts:** about 3.5% replies. Weekly testing lifts that to 5 to 10% (Nick).
- **Reply speed:** answering within 30 minutes gives a 60% better chance of a call. Aim for 5 to 10 minutes.
- **Lost leads:** every extra message before booking loses about 5% of leads.
- **Data quality:** bought lists are only 70 to 80% valid, even when marked "verified". AI qualification removes 30 to 50% of a list and doubles or triples replies.
- **Company size:** big companies (500+ staff) reply about 10 times less.
- **Lifespan:** sending domains last 3 to 6 months.
- **Plan backwards from emails per booked call.** At 200 emails per call, 10,000 emails is 50 calls.

## Mistakes they all warn about

- Sending from your main business domain.
- Links, images, open tracking or a tracking footer in email 1.
- Openers that sound like AI ("I love how passionate you are about…") and corporate tells ("I hope this finds you well", "we help…", big signatures).
- Scraped fields used raw: "Hi Nick Automates", "PCG Holdings Ltd". Use first names and casual company names.
- Vague offers, ranges, wild guarantees with no "how", or asking for 15 minutes while giving nothing.
- Long sequences before the copy is proven.
- Picking winners on 50 to 100 sends.
- Mid-level managers instead of owners. Heavily emailed groups. Cheap lists. Skipping verification.
- Slow or canned replies, too much information, no next step, replying from a different mailbox, more than one person sharing an inbox.

## Small things that make a big difference

- **Ask before you send:** "Would it be okay if I spent some time looking at {company} first?" They feel the effort you're putting in, and the only reply needed is yes.
- **Two set times plus "I'll ring you on this number"**, so one yes books the call.
- **One AI-written line at most,** from research that isn't on LinkedIn (news, ads, partners, clients). Read 100 outputs and you need 9 out of 10 good. Otherwise skip it.
- **"Wrong tree" opt-out** from email 2: "If I'm barking up the wrong tree, just say so and I won't email again."
- **One test day a week** (Nick: Sunday, 20 to 30 minutes), logged like an experiment. Big changes early, small ones later.
- **Choose winners on positive replies.** Keep a control running.
- **Tag the booking link** (`?utm_source=coldemail`) so calls and clients from cold email show up in the CRM.

## Where they clash with UK rules (and with us)

- **Opt-out:** Jay and Nick leave it out of email 1, or out altogether. **The UK requires it.** Business emails to company addresses (Ltd, LLP, plc) can be sent without consent under PECR, but every email must say who it's from and give an easy way to stop. Sole traders and some partnerships need consent first. UK GDPR also applies to named people (legitimate interest, and honour every objection). A plain line like "Reply 'no' and I won't email again" in every email meets both rules. This is our reading, not legal advice.
- **Grey hat tactics:** Nick suggests made-up sender names on bought mailboxes, case studies "shaped" from past experience, and "pretend it's your secretary". Don't. It breaks the honesty our brand runs on, and trust is the whole product.
- **Guarantees:** Nick likes "X in Y days or you don't pay", while Jay warns against wild promises with no "how". For a free review, the honest version is the Clarity Guarantee (you leave with your score, your constraint and your first step).

## What this means for Profit Coaches (my take)

- **The BOSS Score Review is already the right offer.** Everyone recommends a free "front end" that gives something real. The email just has to make the review feel specific to their business.
- **Email 1 should be a PPC in plain words.** One leak common in their sector (e.g. prices behind costs), one quick fix, then "I've got a couple more for {company}. Would it be okay if I sent over how the review works?"
- **The hard part is the infrastructure, not the words.** Coaches won't set up domains, mailboxes, warm-up and verification themselves. That makes it a strong done-for-you piece for BCA to set up per coach (job 85). A coach only needs one or two domains and about 100 emails a day.
- **Targeting:** owners, founders and MDs at UK firms with about 10 to 50 staff (headcount stands in for £1M to £10M turnover), one sector at a time. "Boring" sectors like manufacturing and trades get fewer cold emails.
- **Hormozi's video is about emailing your own list.** Use it for the monthly nurture emails after a review: one idea each, under 200 words, a PS, and ask for replies.
