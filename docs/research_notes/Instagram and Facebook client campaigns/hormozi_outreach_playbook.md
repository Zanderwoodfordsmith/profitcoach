## 1. Warm outreach: the exact steps, scripts, volumes and tries

### Takeaway
Warm outreach is his "first five customers" method: list every contact you have on every platform, start on the platform with the most contacts, send 100 personalised check-ins a day (up to three tries each), warm replies up with Acknowledge-Compliment-Ask, and after three or four exchanges ask "do you know anybody who…" while offering five free case-study spots. Charge once people start referring, raising price every five clients. His benchmark is 100 reach-outs, 20 replies, 4 take the free offer, 1 pays.

### Cited Findings

**Definition and the Core Four (general)**
- "Warm reachouts are when you make one-to-one contact with your warm audience, aka the people who know you. It's the cheapest and easiest way to find people interested in the stuff [you sell]… And most businesses don't do it." Warm reach-outs "usually come in the form of calls, texts, emails, direct messages, voicemails, etc." and you advertise either "your lead magnet, something free and valuable, or… your core offer." — [$100M Leads audiobook, Part 3: Warm Outreach (podscripts transcript)](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-3-warm-outreach-100m-leads-book)
- The Core Four, in his words: "One to one to a warm audience, warm outreach. One to many to a warm audience. Posting content. One to one to a cold audience, cold outreach, one to many to a cold audience, paid ads. These are the only four things you can do to let other people know about the stuff you sell." Warm audiences are "people who gave you permission to contact them… friends, family, followers, current customers, previous customers, contacts". — [Part 3](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-3-warm-outreach-100m-leads-book)
- "So if you aren't getting as many leads as you want, you're not doing the core 4 with enough skill or with enough volume." — [Part 3](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-3-warm-outreach-100m-leads-book)

**His own first campaign (includes Facebook messages)**
- "I called, I texted, and I sent Facebook messages to a bunch of people I knew. Hey, do you know anyone who's trying to get in shape. I'm training people for free for 12 weeks. On top of that, I'll make them a custom nutrition and grocery list. All they have to do is donate to a charity of their choice and let me use their testimonial. Only six people said yes. Six. Two high school friends, one college friend and three people they referred." After 12 weeks he asked them to pay; "Once they got results, I asked them to send their friends over… I got another five or six clients from their referrals." It made "about $4,000 per month". — [Part 3](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-3-warm-outreach-100m-leads-book)

**The 10 steps (verbatim headings)**
- "Step one, get your list. Step two, pick a platform. Step three, personalize your message. Step four, reach out. Step five, warm them up. Step six, invite their friends. Step seven, make them the easiest offer in the world. Step eight, start at the top. Step nine, start charging. Step 10, keep your list warm." — [Part 3](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-3-warm-outreach-100m-leads-book); same 10 steps on video: [Alex Hormozi, "Watch this to get your first 5 customers" (2 Aug 2023)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w7g08dVTwaE)

**Step 1, list everyone**
- "Pull up all the email accounts you've ever used over the years. Pull your contacts and address list from each… Now, go to your social media profiles. See your followers, subscribers, friends, connections… Add up all your contacts from all the platforms… For many of you, this will be your first thousand leads." — [Part 3](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-3-warm-outreach-100m-leads-book)
- Video version, three lists: email contacts; "every single social media profile that you have… where you can direct message people so if you have followers that you can DM I want you to write down every single one of those people"; phone contacts, exported. — [YouTube w7g08dVTwaE](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w7g08dVTwaE)

**Step 2, platform order**
- "Pick the platform you have the most contacts on. Phone, email, social media, mail, carrier pigeon, etc. It doesn't matter… You'll hit them all eventually anyways." Video: "if you have the most Instagram people or you have the most email contacts or you have more people in your phone than any of those start there". — [Part 3](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-3-warm-outreach-100m-leads-book); [YouTube w7g08dVTwaE](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w7g08dVTwaE)
- Step 8: "After reaching out to all the leads on one platform, switch to the platform you have the second most leads on… then the third… If between all platforms, you have a thousand leads, that gives you 10 solid days of work, a month of work including follow-ups." — [Part 3](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-3-warm-outreach-100m-leads-book)

**Step 3, the opener (no ask)**
- "Use something you know about the contact as your actual reason to reach out. If you don't have much personal info, you can check their social media profiles, etc. to learn a bit about them first. Don't be a weirdo. Pay your social dues. Remember, you haven't asked for anything. You're just checking in and providing value." Example: "Saw you just had a baby. Congrats. How's the baby doing? How are you?" — [Part 3](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-3-warm-outreach-100m-leads-book)
- Video: "you take 30 seconds you look at someone's profile… hey saw you switch jobs hey saw you move… all you need to show is that you took the few seconds to actually not just blast some sort of message". — [YouTube w7g08dVTwaE](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w7g08dVTwaE)

**Step 4, volume and tries**
- "Reach out to 100 of them per day with your personalized messages. You'll call, text, email, message, send a postcard, etc. And you will reach out to them up to three times, once per day for three days or until they respond, whichever comes first, once per week with physical mail." — [Part 3](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-3-warm-outreach-100m-leads-book)
- "The first reach out is always the hardest and takes the longest. Your second reach out will take minutes. Your third, seconds." — [Part 3](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-3-warm-outreach-100m-leads-book)

**Step 5, ACA (verbatim)**
- "A, acknowledge what they said. Restate in your own words. This shows active listening. Example. Two kids. And you're an accountant… C, compliment them on whatever they just told you. Tied to a positive character trait if you can. Example, wow, super mom, so hardworking, managing a full-time career and two kids… A, ask another question. Lead the conversation to whatever direction you want. In this case, a topic closer to your offer." Examples: "If I were selling therapy or life coaching, do you get time for yourself? If I were selling fitness or weight loss, do you have time to get your workouts in? If I were selling cleaning services, do you have anyone who helps you keep the house tidy." — [Part 3](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-3-warm-outreach-100m-leads-book)
- On video he adds: "we're gonna tie our ask to the compliment we just gave them… if I was selling weight loss I'd be like hey so you're [Supermom; transcript says "Superman"] how do you have time to eat right and work out… this sounds like selling it's not talking humans". — [YouTube w7g08dVTwaE](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w7g08dVTwaE)
- Email differs: "On email, you'll be more up front… you will have a personalized opener to show you actually took the time to research them in some way. Think two to three sentences. Then you'll transition directly to your offer [or] lead magnet… You sort of do it all at once with email or voicemails." — [Part 3](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-3-warm-outreach-100m-leads-book)

**Step 6, when to make the offer, and the referral ask (verbatim template)**
- "Get through a, quote, normal amount of conversation. Think three to four exchanges if on the phone or messaging and three to four minutes if in person. Then you'll make them an offer to see if they're interested." He builds the offer from the value equation (dream outcome, perceived likelihood, time delay, effort and sacrifice). — [Part 3](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-3-warm-outreach-100m-leads-book)
- The script: "By the way, do you know anybody who is [describe their struggle] looking to [dream outcome] in [time delay]? I'm taking on five case studies for free because that's all I can handle. I just want to get some testimonials for my product or service. I help them [dream outcome] without [effort and sacrifice]. It works. I even guarantee people [dream outcome] or I work with them until they do. I just had a girl named [X] work with me, [dream outcome], even though she [described the same struggle that contact has]. I also had a guy, [dream outcome], and it was his first time. I just like more testimonials to show it works across different scenarios. Does anyone you like come to mind?" Then: "Pause if on the phone. If they say no, say, ha ha, well, does anyone you hate come to mind? Ha, this breaks any awkwardness." — [Part 3](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-3-warm-outreach-100m-leads-book)
- Why it works: "We're not asking them to buy anything. We're asking if they know anyone. And of the people who say yes, most say they are interested… Since you didn't ask them to buy anything, you don't come off as pushy. Some people will show interest in your stuff. Some will refer you to those who might. Some will do both. In all three outcomes, you win." The testimonial you pick should be "as close to their situation as possible", which implies likelihood without claiming it. — [Part 3](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-3-warm-outreach-100m-leads-book)
- Short version when there's no room: "I help [ideal customer] get [dream outcome] in [time period] without [effort and sacrifice] and [increase perceived likelihood of achievement]… these work well for emails, text, direct messages, calls, and in-person." — [Part 3](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-3-warm-outreach-100m-leads-book)
- Video phrasing of the friend ask: "oh yeah by the way do you know anyone who's looking for X Y and Z because I'm opening up a few slots to help people do exactly that… you're asking them for a favor you're not asking them to buy from you". An "engaged lead is someone who you can contact, who has shown interest in the thing that you sell". — [YouTube w7g08dVTwaE](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w7g08dVTwaE)
- 11 ways to raise perceived likelihood include your own story, "people just like them", volume of reviews, certifications, stats, experts, a new mechanism, celebrities, a guarantee, "How well you describe them or the current pain… They should think, he really gets me", and "demonstrate the outcome live or show a recording of it". His B2B example: "Advertising agency plays a recording of a call that a gym owner has to make to a lead… Could you handle making a call like this to a lead if we got them for you?… People don't want leads, they want customers." — [Part 3](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-3-warm-outreach-100m-leads-book)

**Step 7, first five free**
- Terms script: "Since I'm only taking on five people, I can give you all the attention you need to get bragworthy results, and I'll give it to you all for free so long as you promise to one, use it, two, give me feedback, and three, leave a killer review if you think it deserves one. Does that sound fair?" — [Part 3](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-3-warm-outreach-100m-leads-book)
- "When you launch a new product or service, make the first five free. The exact number matters less than knowing why you benefit from it." Reasons: reps, "you probably suck. For now", learning, and free customers make money three ways: "they convert into paying customers… they send you paying customers via referrals… their testimonials bring in paying customers." — [Part 3](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-3-warm-outreach-100m-leads-book)
- If people refuse even free: "your free stuff is too expensive" (hidden costs of time, effort, sacrifice). Ask: "What would I have to do to make it worth it for you to continue?" "Yeses give me opportunity. Noes give me feedback. Either way, I win." — [Part 3](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-3-warm-outreach-100m-leads-book)
- Referral hinge: "If you ask for a referral, get a three-way introduction… grab the customer's phone, take a picture of the two of us, then text that picture to the referral and your own number. If I'm virtual, screenshot a video call and do the same thing." — [Part 3](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-3-warm-outreach-100m-leads-book)

**Step 9, start charging**
- "Once people start referring, start charging. When that happens, swap out free in the script above to 80% off for the next five, then 60% off for the next five, then 40% off for the next five… keep raising it by 20% every five until you find your sweet spot." — [Part 3](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-3-warm-outreach-100m-leads-book)
- Moving a free client to paid, video: "I am accepting somebody who's paying full [price] in order to do this if you would like me to keep doing this for you you would have to start paying to match them are you comfortable with that". — [YouTube w7g08dVTwaE](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w7g08dVTwaE)
- Payment line: "would you rather pay less today or get all your money back? Paying less today equals a payment plan… Get all your money back equals prepayment and get a guarantee". Example: "$2,000 a month for three months… with no guarantee. Or… $6,000 upfront with a guarantee… the majority of people take the upfront cash option". — [Part 3](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-3-warm-outreach-100m-leads-book)

**Step 10, keep the list warm and re-engage**
- Give regular value, then use "Dean Jackson's timeless nine-word email template. Are you still looking to [four-word desire]? No images, no frills, no [links; transcript says "lengths"], just a question… These replies should be your top priority for warm reachouts." Example fill-ins include "get more sales leads". — [Part 3](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-3-warm-outreach-100m-leads-book)

**Daily checklist and time (verbatim)**
- "Who? Yourself. What? Your first five for free. Where? Phone, email, physical mail, SMS, etc. To whom? Your contacts. When? First four hours of your day. Why? You want to get customers or introductions. How? Personalized messages using ACA. How much? A hundred attempts per day. How many follow up? Two to three more times after the first. How long? Until you get customers." — [Part 3](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-3-warm-outreach-100m-leads-book)
- "When you're starting out, getting new customers should take the majority of your time. Think four hours per day in minimum. It should be the first thing you do when you get up." Limits: "time" and "the number of people who know you. You'll eventually run out." — [Part 3](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-3-warm-outreach-100m-leads-book)
- "Also, don't do anything sketchy. There are lots of people who pride themselves on pushing the legal envelope. Don't be that person." — [Part 3](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-3-warm-outreach-100m-leads-book)

### Inferences
- For a BCA coach, the warm list is phone contacts, email, Facebook friends, Instagram followers and LinkedIn connections, worked platform by platform, largest first. Facebook is likely the biggest warm list for an older coach with a long career.
- The referral ask suits coaches well because most of their contacts are not £1M+ owners themselves but know some. "Do you know any business owner who…" avoids pitching friends directly.
- "First five free" for a coach selling to £1M+ owners is best read as five free case studies of a narrow piece of the work (for example a BOSS Score review plus a 30-day fix), not the full programme. Hormozi's point is reps, proof and referrals, and "the exact number matters less".

### Gaps
- He gives no platform-specific warm script for Instagram or Facebook beyond "message" and "DM". The same steps apply on every platform.
- The audio has no "free goodwill" or "first five" script for B2B buyers. His examples are consumer (fitness, therapy, cleaning) plus the agency-to-gym-owner demo.
- In the audiobook money maths ("1% of your list buys a $400 offer… 500 reachouts per week equals five customers per week… $2,000 per week times 52 weeks") the transcript says "$10,000". The arithmetic gives about $104,000, which fits his next line ("two times the median household income"). It is a transcription slip.

## 2. Cold outreach: lists, personalisation, big fast value, follow-up, volume, setters, and what he says about Instagram/Facebook DMs, voice notes and video

### Takeaway
Cold outreach is warm outreach to strangers, with three extra problems: no way to contact them (so build a list by software, broker or by hand, and hand-built lists from interest groups are freshest), they ignore you (so personalise with one to three real details and lead with "big fast value", something people normally pay for), and too few chances (so automate delivery and distribution, follow up "more times, more ways", and restart the list every 3–6 months). Volume is 100 a day for 100 days. For DMs specifically, his benchmark is a personal video or voice memo to 100 people, saying their name plus one personal line, which gets about 20% replies, then ACA to qualify for a call. Setters are hired in groups, trained by "document, demonstrate, duplicate", given a fixed batch of leads with an absolute appointment quota, and cut at three weeks if they don't hit it.

### Cited Findings

**Framing (general)**
- "Cold outreach sits atop the foundation of warm outreach. So think of this as a more advanced cousin of warm outreach, no longer limited by your warm audience. If you can figure out a way to contact somebody one-to-one, you can use it for cold outreach… you can send 100 direct messages, you can send 100 voicemails." "Cold outreach has one key difference from warm outreach. Trust. Strangers don't trust you." Three problems: "you don't have a way to contact them… even if you can contact them, they ignore you… even if they give you their attention, they're not interested." — [$100M Leads audiobook, Part 5: Cold Outreach](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-5-cold-outreach-100m-leads-book)
- Multi-step selling for high ticket: "I sell expensive stuff better when I do it in multiple steps rather than on the first contact. So my priority is to get the prospect to show interest in the stuff I sell. When they show interest, I schedule a time to sell them. If my lead magnet requires a second exchange to deliver it, I do that then. If my lead magnet provides value on its own, then the next call is to talk about the value they received." — [Part 5](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-5-cold-outreach-100m-leads-book)
- His proof story: a gym-software company selling to gym owners was "100% cold outreach… about 30 guys… about 10 million a month". His own cold team then went from zero sales in September to 30 sales ($480,000) in May; "Cold outreach veterans told me it would take a year to scale. I figured we could do it in 12 weeks. I was wrong. It took almost a year." The team "asked me to pull the plug" twice. — [Part 5](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-5-cold-outreach-100m-leads-book); retold in [MoreMozi, "You Need to Start Doing Outbound" (24 Feb 2026)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SZ9yqgnz7mw)

**Building the list**
- "There are three different ways I get my targeted lead lists. First, I use software to scrape a list of names. Second, I pay brokers to assemble me a list of targeted leads. And if neither of those work, I manually scrape a list of names myself." Test "a representative sample, say a few hundred from each software". Manual: "I join groups and communities that I think have my audience. When I find people that meet my qualifications, I check to see if they have ways to contact them in the group's directory, like links to their social media profiles… If they don't, I can reach out to them within the platform hosting the group. I prefer to find contact information outside the group so I don't come off as solely trying to milk the group for business, but I will if I have to." — [Part 5](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-5-cold-outreach-100m-leads-book)
- "If you can search the database, so can everyone else. But if you assemble a list of names yourself, it's less likely that person has already seen many cold reachouts… So they're the freshest. Downside, it takes the most time." "Put your first thousand names together. If you have more time than money, you might want to start at step three since it only costs time." — [Part 5](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-5-cold-outreach-100m-leads-book)
- "Interest groups are the warmest cold audiences you can get… concentrated pools of people looking for a solution. Give them one… They're one of my favorite places to fish." — [Part 5](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-5-cold-outreach-100m-leads-book)
- 2025–26 workshop version: "find where the people hang out… B2B oftentimes can be LinkedIn. Local might be something like Google Maps… you can buy leads, you can scrape data or you can manually hunt. All three of these work… if you are poorer, then manually hunting works fine too. A great manual hunting method is to go into Skool communities where those people are and see what they're about… Talk to the group owner. See if you can collab on stuff. Don't be scammy and weird… focus on one source first. So just get good at it, then expand." — [Workshop Session 4 Marketing, 03:04:29–03:05:31](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1CnZZ-WwnhxP3xVJHrAvOadsbayrXfZBK/view)

**Personalisation**
- "We want our cold reach out to look like a warm reach out… Basically, one to three pieces of information we can find that a friend might know about the prospect. Then we want to compliment them on it. And ideally, show them how it benefited us. People like people who like them… This comes in handy for personal subject lines on emails, the first few messages in chat, or the first few sentences someone hears." "Do a little research on each lead before you send a message. We can do this ourselves, pay people to do it, or use software. Batch this work." — [Part 5](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-5-cold-outreach-100m-leads-book)
- Workshop formula: "the whole goal is to make it not look automated, even though it is automated… find something you can reference… a recent LinkedIn post or their favorite sports team… Cold calling might be something from their website or social… simple formula that works everywhere. Specific observation plus relevant compliment plus transition to business. Ta-da… And then you do so much of it that you want to throw up and you keep doing it." — [Session 4 Marketing, 03:05:31–03:06:56](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1CnZZ-WwnhxP3xVJHrAvOadsbayrXfZBK/view)
- Reading level: "I took our cold outreach template and rewrote it below a third grade reading level. The results, 50% more leads responded." — [Part 5](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-5-cold-outreach-100m-leads-book)
- How few leads changes it: "The fewer leads you have, the less automation you should consider using… if there are only a thousand hedge fund managers who meet your criteria, you're going to want to personalize every one of them." — [Part 5](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-5-cold-outreach-100m-leads-book)

**His B2B cold call script (verbatim; the only full cold script in the audio)**
- "[Their name], then pausing… it's Alex, then pauses, I watched a few of your videos and read that recent blog post you wrote on dog training. It was killer. Really helped me out with my Doberman… Oh yeah, sorry, I got ahead of myself. I work for a company that helps dog trainers fill up their books. We like to partner with the best in the area, so I'm always on the lookout. We work with someone about a half hour north of you. Johnny's dog care, you heard of them?… We ended up getting 100 appointments in 30 [days; transcript says "years"] using a combination of text and some ads. Do you offer similar services to them?… Oh, that's perfect. Then we'd be able to use the same campaign in your market… If you got a boatload of high-paying new dog training customers, you wouldn't be upset with me, would you?… Well, tell you what, I can walk you through the entire thing soup to nuts later today. Will you be around at four?" "If that person had started the call with, hey, man, want to buy some marketing services, you probably would have hung up." — [Part 5](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-5-cold-outreach-100m-leads-book)
- He says the book's DM, email and call scripts were cut for length: "I had to cut scripts to make this book a manageable length. If you want to model your scripts off of them, go to acquisition.com forward slash training, forward slash leads." Scripts are short: "phone and chat scripts are never more than a page or two and cold emails are rarely more than half a page… There are no rewards for prettiest script. Get your first hundred conversations or 10,000 emails out of the way before tweaking it." — [Part 5](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-5-cold-outreach-100m-leads-book)

**Big fast value**
- "Strangers give you far less time to prove your worth… So make your life easier by giving away the farm. We're not trying to tickle their interest. We're trying to blow their minds in under 30 seconds… If it's not [big, fast, value] or it's mediocre, you'll blend in with the ocean of people trying to get their attention." — [Part 5](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-5-cold-outreach-100m-leads-book)
- The lead-magnet swap: "The first four months of our cold outreach felt like torture. We offered a game planning session as our lead magnet… most didn't. ## 3. Lead magnets inside outreach (free audit, tool, scorecard) and how to offer them in a DM

### Takeaway
His definition of a lead magnet is "a complete solution to a narrow problem" that reveals the next problem your core offer solves. Three types: reveal a problem (assessments and audits, his favourite), free trial, and one step of many. Deliver as a tool, information, a service or a physical product. In outreach the lead magnet must be "big fast value": something people normally pay for, which blows them away in 30–60 seconds. In warm and DM outreach you offer it after ACA and a few exchanges. In email and voicemail you lead with a two-to-three-sentence personal opener and go straight to it. His own main lead magnet (Scaling Roadmap) is an assessment that tells owners which stage they're at, which is the same shape as a BOSS Score.

### Cited Findings
- Definition: "offer something kind of like a mini offer that's a complete solution to a narrow problem… lower cost or free just to see who's interested and raises their hand… once that little mini offer solves it, it reveals another problem that's solved by your core offer… leads interested in lower cost or free offers now are more likely to buy a related higher cost offer later." — [Alex Hormozi, "Watch This To Generate 1000s of Leads (In Any Niche)" (8 Nov 2025)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Mst4hreQYl0)
- Type 1, reveal a problem: "I personally love these type of lead magnets… It literally creates deprivation… hey, free website speed test and someone does the test and they realize that their website speed is slow, in which case your core offer is how to fix it." Best "for problems that get worse while waiting… Financial audits, hey, your… cash flow is bad. It's only going to get worse… that builds in urgency." One of his highest-converting ads for ALAN (software sold to agencies) was "four reasons why you'll never have a million-dollar agency". Revealing problems "allows you to market more compliantly, but also set more realistic expectations. If I can perfectly nail all the problems in your life… and I just said, I can help you with that. You'd probably be like, yeah, I believe you." — [YouTube Mst4hreQYl0](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Mst4hreQYl0)
- Types 2 and 3: free trial ("the classic try before you buy", then remove it to create deprivation) and one step of many (for example "give the first two videos away in a comprehensive course"). — [YouTube Mst4hreQYl0](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Mst4hreQYl0)
- Delivery: "software or tools… spreadsheets that calculate things, assessment tools, templates… if you have ways that you can say, hey, you're going to be in one of these four categories once you answer these… information… services… physical products". His example: "my scaling road map… you go through the tool and then it gives you the assessment which will then be information… if you would like to figure out what stage of scaling you're currently at, the problems you're dealing with right now, and exactly how to solve them". — [YouTube Mst4hreQYl0](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Mst4hreQYl0)
- Free services only for qualified people: "free audits with some level of implementation, same day service delivery… done for you component", but "What's not a good idea is giving it to somebody who's broke, who can't make a decision, doesn't really need it, and is kind of like not sure if they want to do it now… use them for the right prospects." Name it compellingly ("just ask your audience what they would find interesting") and "embed CTAs within your lead magnet and before and after". — [YouTube Mst4hreQYl0](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Mst4hreQYl0)
- Pick what others charge for: "step one look at what other people are charging for step two look at the hard costs of what it would cost for you to deliver it step three give that thing away for free step four see how many leads you get step five see how many you convert". — [Alex Hormozi, "How To Get Customers So Fast It Feels ILLEGAL" (16 Dec 2024)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FMzKk73iUhw)
- In cold outreach, "you can directly make your offer or offer a lead magnet or both. It gives the person a strong reason to respond. I specifically call out big fast value rather than your lead magnet as a reminder that it needs to be big, fast, value." — [Part 5](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-5-cold-outreach-100m-leads-book)
- Sequencing: "If my lead magnet requires a second exchange to deliver it, I do that then. If my lead magnet provides value on its own, then the next call is to talk about the value they received." — [Part 5](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-5-cold-outreach-100m-leads-book)
- Lead magnet vs a direct offer, for Acquisition.com itself: "Scaling Roadmap for us is a lead magnet. A come to a workshop is a direct offer… Scaling Roadmap has higher ROAS on a longer timeline. The workshop has a higher ROAS on a shorter timeline… it makes more sense for us to get more people on the Scaling Roadmap". 78% of buyers "consumed at least two long form pieces of content" before buying, so he forces that consumption on cold people: "if you have two or three hours with someone, you can usually sell a very expensive thing." — [Session 4 Marketing, 03:26:00–03:28:14](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1CnZZ-WwnhxP3xVJHrAvOadsbayrXfZBK/view)
- Qualifying through the lead magnet: "Don't be afraid of adding friction to get the information you need because it rarely drops sales. So you may get more expensive leads, but your cost to acquire customers will typically go down." He asked registrants whether they were business owners as a "leading indicator on my ad quality" (65% said yes). Price-check on applications: "hey, our ticket's five thousand dollars. You OK with that?" — [Session 4 Marketing, 03:07:31–03:08:34 and 03:29:17–03:30:28](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1CnZZ-WwnhxP3xVJHrAvOadsbayrXfZBK/view)
- "Survey close" after a talk, which works as a lead magnet delivery: "hey, were these slides great?… here's the link so that you can get all my slides and my free thingy… answer a couple questions… would you like to find out more about this thing or were you only here for the free slides?… I want to find out more, which then you can book directly to a phone call". — [Session 4 Marketing, 03:19:01–03:19:44](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1CnZZ-WwnhxP3xVJHrAvOadsbayrXfZBK/view)
- Attraction offer vs lead magnet: "lead magnets are typically free… or a very low cost thing… an attraction offer… you are wrapping something that is already kind of fully formed with a different incentive… buy X, get Y free, a giveaway, win your money back." — [Session 4 Marketing, 02:57:10–02:58:12](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1CnZZ-WwnhxP3xVJHrAvOadsbayrXfZBK/view)

### Inferences
- The BOSS Score is a type-1 "reveal a problem" lead magnet delivered as a tool, exactly like his Scaling Roadmap. For outreach it is probably not "big fast value" on its own, because it's free to anyone and owners don't usually pay for a quiz. The DM offer that matches his rule is the coach doing something owners would pay a consultant for: going through the owner's BOSS Score with them and sending back a short written or recorded "three biggest profit leaks and the first fix" review.
- In a DM it should be offered the way he sequences warm outreach: open with a real observation and a question, use ACA for 3–4 exchanges, then offer the free review (or the referral ask). A text-only first message can carry the review offer directly for cold prospects (his email rule: "sort of do it all at once").
- His "only for the right prospects" rule plus BANT means the coach should check fit (owner-led, roughly £1M+, decision-maker) before giving away review time.

### Gaps
- He gives no wording for offering a lead magnet inside a DM thread, only the warm-outreach offer script and the general sequencing.
- No numbers on lead magnet take rates in DMs. The only take-rate number is "3xed" after swapping the sales-call lead magnet for free service.

## 4. The numbers he gives (reply rates, conversion, rule of 100, show and close, setter targets)

### Takeaway
Warm: 100 reach-outs, about 20 replies, 4 take the free offer, 1 becomes a paying customer. Cold: phone 100 calls, 20% pickup, 25% of those take the lead magnet (4 engaged); email 100, 30% open, 10% reply (3%); DMs with personal voice or video 100, 20% reply. Aim for 3% of a cold list becoming engaged leads and at least 3:1 lifetime gross profit to acquisition cost. The rule of 100 is 100 primary actions a day per person for 100 days (90 in the 2021 video). Setter quotas are absolute (for example 70 leads in, 7 appointments out), and phone follow-up of about 11 touches over 7 days gets about 80% of possible sales.

### Cited Findings
- Warm benchmarks: "Warm reachouts should get you about one in five contacts to engage. So 100 warm reachouts should get you about 20 replies. Of the 20 [who] reply, another one in five ish will take you up on your free offer. So four people. Of the four… You should be able to convert one into some sort of paid offer later… one customer per 100 warm reachouts." — [Part 3](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-3-warm-outreach-100m-leads-book)
- Video version: "if you reach out to 3,000 people with a free thing you'll get five people who will take you up on it I promise you". — [YouTube w7g08dVTwaE](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w7g08dVTwaE)
- Cold phone: "100 cold calls per day… 20% pickup rate… 25% of people… take my lead magnet. That means I got four engaged leads. If it took me four hours… one engaged lead per hour." Cold email: "100 personalized emails per day… 30% open… 10% reply showing interest… three engaged leads… shoot for 3% of your list turning into engaged leads." A niche high-ticket portfolio company: "4% lead-to-engagement rate, and presumably, a third of them convert into sales… one new customer per 100 reach-out attempts." DMs: personal video or voice memo to 100, "20% of people reply". "You know you do well when the cost of doing cold outreach is less than three times what you make in profit from a customer… the portfolio company above gets over 30 to 1." — [Part 5](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-5-cold-outreach-100m-leads-book)
- Scale example: "One virtual assistant sends 2,000 emails per day using multiple pieces of software. This generates the business 40 engaged leads per day… the response rate dropped because we took out so much personalization… 10% of engaged leads sold… four new customers per day… The business makes $20,000 per customer." ### Inferences
- Planning numbers for a coach doing warm outreach by hand: 100 warm messages should give about 20 conversations and about 1 client (Hormozi). A coach with 1,000 contacts has about 10 days of first messages and about a month with follow-ups.
- For cold DMs, his 20% figure assumes personal voice or video. The earlier notes found text-only cold Instagram DMs to business owners at about 2–6% positive replies in practice (non-Hormozi sources). Expect cold text DMs well below his number until a voice or video step is added after a reply.
- His ≥$10K ticket rule supports cold outreach for BCA coaches, whose programmes for £1M+ owners are high ticket.

### Gaps
- He gives no show-rate benchmark for outreach-booked calls in the sources I read (only speed-to-lead claims that speed "increases both show up rates and close rates").
- No per-day DM target for a setter is stated. The 100 a day is a floor for any one person, and the phone rep example is 200 leads a day.

## 5. Personal vs business accounts, content plus outreach, and outreach to business owners (B2B)

### Takeaway
He doesn't discuss personal vs business Instagram or Facebook accounts directly. What he does say is that the account you send from must look real and alive (followers, verification, posts), because prospects click your profile before replying. Content's job alongside outreach is "lead nurture": a minimum of 1–3 posts a week on the channel you DM from, which over time starts producing its own hot leads. For selling to business owners he adds three B2B moves: use outreach to get affiliates who already have your buyers (his example is accountants), qualify with BANT before or during outreach (company size, decision-maker, stated pain, hiring), and use conferences (waive a speaking fee for the attendee list or one email to it).

### Cited Findings

**Account credibility (closest he gets to personal vs business)**
- Cold outreach from a profile with zero followers fails: "you have zero followers… this honestly looks to me like a bot account… invite people who actually know you like in the real world to just follow you… on Facebook and Instagram it's like invite all your friends… and verify yourself and then all of a sudden it'll look a little bit better." — [Skool Live Q&A (2 Apr 2025)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6cWahKKGkR4)
- "If you do outreach from a channel that has literally no posts, people be like, 'This is a scam.' And rightfully so… if you post three times a week and you have something that's relevant to your avatar… they will click over to your profile before responding to your email and they'll be like, 'What is this guy about?'… They'll watch two or three things and they'll come in." "Whatever you link to is what they're going to click. So, I would just link to the one thing that you're active on." — [MoreMozi, "Why Most Cold Outreach Fails" (23 Jan 2026)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KIs-egpg1bg)
- Warm outreach is explicitly from your own personal contacts and profiles: "go to every single social media profile that you have" — [YouTube w7g08dVTwaE](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w7g08dVTwaE); "you do have a warm audience, even if you don't know it. Everybody knows somebody. So your personal contacts are the easiest place to start." — [Part 3](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-3-warm-outreach-100m-leads-book)
- "You never want to risk your profile by doing anything sketchy." — [Session 4 Marketing, 03:14:49](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1CnZZ-WwnhxP3xVJHrAvOadsbayrXfZBK/view)

**Content plus outreach**
- "Brand is the tide that will lift all boats… If you do outreach, brand will act as nurturer. So the content that you make will improve the effectiveness of your outreach… two steps… the just barely alive to show you have a pulse. All we want someone to say is like, okay, they're legit… What will happen though is that over time… you'll start getting leads from it. And they'll be super hot and they'll close really fast… a third of our sales are coming from these tiny posts". — [Session 4 Marketing, 03:55:44–03:56:59](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1CnZZ-WwnhxP3xVJHrAvOadsbayrXfZBK/view)
- Organic has "two levers… MVP level… which satisfies a different need than using organic as my primary method of acquisition… if I use it as like I have to show that I'm alive and that I can provide value, then the purpose of the organic is lead nurture." Content that "does hit and then does create inquiries… is the stuff you give to the sales team as the greatest hits… a searchable database" for "will this work for my type of business" and standard objections. — [MoreMozi KIs-egpg1bg](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KIs-egpg1bg)

### Inferences
- For a BCA coach the profile they DM from (most likely their personal Instagram and Facebook, since owners buy the coach) must pass the "is this person real and relevant" click: a clear bio saying who they help, recent posts for UK owners, real followers, ideally verified. Without it Hormozi's own diagnosis is that low replies are a profile problem, not a script problem.
- His accountant example maps straight onto coaching: accountants, bookkeepers, bank relationship managers and business-group owners already have £1M+ owner clients. Outreach to them for a referral partnership is his "lower leverage path to a higher leverage person".
- Facebook Groups and Skool communities of owners are his "manual hunting" and "interest groups" sources. His etiquette: talk to the group owner, collaborate, prefer contacting people outside the group, "don't be scammy and weird".

### Gaps
- No Hormozi statement on Instagram business vs creator vs personal accounts, Facebook Page vs profile, or Meta Verified as such. "Verify yourself" is the nearest.
- No B2B example specific to Instagram or Facebook DMs. His B2B examples are cold calls (dog trainers), cold email, LinkedIn, conferences and Facebook followers of a B2B firm.

## 6. A hand-run Instagram and Facebook campaign for a BCA coach, built only from the Hormozi rules above

### Takeaway
A 100-day, one-person campaign: a short setup week to make the coach's profile look alive and to build a review offer worth paying for; then each morning a block of 100 primary actions (warm messages first, then hand-built cold lists from Facebook Groups and Instagram); ACA in every thread; the referral ask or a free review as the offer; multi-channel follow-up; and a weekly numbers review with one test. Each step is tagged with the Hormozi rule it comes from. The UK-specific and Meta-limit points come from the earlier research, not from Hormozi.

### Cited Findings (the rules the plan rests on, each sourced above)
- 100 primary actions a day for 100 days, first hours of the day; warm first, then content, then cold — [Part 3](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-3-warm-outreach-100m-leads-book), [Part 5](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-5-cold-outreach-100m-leads-book), [Part 7](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-7-core-four-on-steroids-100m-leads-book)
- ACA; offer after 3–4 exchanges; "do you know anybody who…"; five free case studies; price up every five — [Part 3](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-3-warm-outreach-100m-leads-book)
- Cold: 1–3 personal details plus compliment; big fast value people pay for; follow up more ways; restart in 3–6 months; stop when asked — [Part 5](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-5-cold-outreach-100m-leads-book)
- Voice or video DM, name plus one personal line, about 20% reply — [Part 5](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-5-cold-outreach-100m-leads-book)
- Minimum 1–3 posts a week; Instagram as front door; comment keyword plus ManyChat; "drop" with limited spots to followers — [Session 4 Marketing, 03:56:16](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1CnZZ-WwnhxP3xVJHrAvOadsbayrXfZBK/view), [Session 1, 03:13:29–03:15:12 and 03:55:04](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1RbOspfzghF25i3YLGsWgHce8lpoFvnwr/view)
- Profile must look real (followers, verification, active) — [Skool Q&A](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6cWahKKGkR4)
- Weekly test of one thing per platform; track every stat — [Part 7](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-7-core-four-on-steroids-100m-leads-book), [Part 5](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-5-cold-outreach-100m-leads-book)
- Non-Hormozi constraints carried from the earlier notes: Instagram message requests from non-followers are one text-only message until accepted ([TechCrunch, Aug 2023](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2578436)); the ICO counts social-media private messages as electronic mail under PECR, with sole traders and partnerships treated like consumers ([ICO B2B marketing](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/)).

### Inferences (the campaign)

**Setup week (Hormozi: "have a pulse", "verify yourself", big fast value)**
1. Profile: the coach's personal Instagram and Facebook. Bio says who they help in plain words (UK owners of £1M+ businesses) and links to one place (the BOSS Score). Post 1–3 times a week about owner problems, using owners' own words. Make sure real-life contacts follow the account. Consider verification.
2. The free thing (big fast value): "Free BOSS Score review: I go through your score with you and send back your three biggest profit leaks and the first fix, in 20 minutes." Only for owner-led firms that look about the right size (his "only for the right prospects" rule plus BANT).
3. Proof: one short case per common owner type (his "testimonial as close to their situation as possible"), kept as a "greatest hits" list to send before calls.
4. Tracker (his "know every stat"): one row per person with platform, date of each touch, reply yes/no, engaged yes/no, review taken, call booked, showed, client.

**Daily block, days 1–100 (rule of 100, in the first hours of the day)**
- Days 1–10ish, warm (100 a day): work the largest platform first (often Facebook friends, then phone, email, Instagram, LinkedIn). Opener with no ask, built on something you know: "Saw you've moved the business into the new unit, congrats! How's it going?" Up to 3 tries, once a day.
- On a reply, ACA, for example: Acknowledge "So you've doubled the team in a year." Compliment "That takes real grit while still running the place." Ask "Are you still the one everything comes back to?" After 3–4 exchanges, the referral ask (his template, coach version): "By the way, do you know any business owner who's working all hours and wants the business to run without them in the next 12 months? I'm taking on five free case studies, because that's all I can handle. I want more stories to show it works in different kinds of business. I just helped [name], who had the same problem, [result]. Does anyone come to mind?" If no: "Ha, anyone you don't like come to mind?"
- Then, cold (once warm contacts run low; Hormozi's order): build 1,000 names by hand from UK owner Facebook Groups and Instagram (interest groups are "the warmest cold audiences"). Note 1–3 details per person. Text-only opener (because of Instagram's request rule), with "specific observation plus relevant compliment plus transition to business": "[Name], saw your post about hiring your first ops manager. Brave move, and the way you wrote the job ad was spot on. I help owners like you get the business running without them. Happy to do a free BOSS Score review for you, your three biggest profit leaks in 20 minutes. Want me to send it?" Keep it at a very easy reading level (his "below a third grade reading level, 50% more responded").
- Once they reply or accept: a 20–40 second voice note or short video saying their name and one personal line, then the offer (his 20% DM benchmark).
- Follow-up "more times, more ways": if no reply on Instagram or Facebook, email from their website ("following up on my message on Instagram"), then a call to the business, then a LinkedIn message. Stop at once if they say no. Park non-repliers and restart the list in 3–6 months.
- Facebook or Instagram followers who already know the coach (his 20K-followers consult): a "drop" post, "I'm opening five free BOSS Score reviews this month, comment SCORE", with the comment replied to by hand or by an official tool such as ManyChat, then a DM sprint to everyone who engaged.

**Moving to paid (his step 9)**
- Once free case studies start sending referrals, swap "free" for a discount and raise the price every five clients. Ask every new client "who else do you know who might also want this?" (his top rep's line, not a yes/no question) and do three-way introductions.

**Weekly review (his testing rule)**
- Count reach-outs, replies, engaged, reviews taken, calls, shows, clients. Compare with his benchmarks: warm 20% reply and about 1 client per 100; cold aim for 3% engaged. Change one thing per platform per week (opener line, offer name, voice vs text). Don't tweak the script before the first 100 conversations.

**When a setter or BCA staff run it for coaches (his setter rules)**
- Coach records the checklist (document), shows the setter (demonstrate), watches them do it (duplicate). Give a fixed weekly batch (for example 70 people) with an appointment quota. Review at three weeks. Setters sending as the coach must follow his "make manual obvious" rule, and never use password-sharing automation ("never risk your profile").

**UK and platform cautions (not Hormozi)**
- Cold marketing DMs to sole traders or partnerships may need consent under PECR. Limited companies are lower risk. £1M+ firms are mostly limited companies, but check before training coaches. Meta publishes no DM caps, so keep volumes and pacing human. I'm not giving legal advice.

### Gaps
- This campaign is my assembly of his rules. Hormozi never laid out an Instagram/Facebook-specific B2B campaign, and no source tested this exact sequence.
- The coach-version scripts are drafts in Hormozi's structure, not his words, and they haven't been checked against BCA's writing rules or tested with owners.
- How his 20% voice/video reply rate holds on Instagram and Facebook for UK owners, and how the PECR rules apply to a single hand-written DM, are both unverified.
