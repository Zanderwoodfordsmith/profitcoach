# Instagram cold outbound to business owners: how practitioners run it by hand

Researched 23 Nov 2026. This goes deeper than `docs/research_notes/Coach outreach beyond LinkedIn/instagram_facebook_outreach.md` and tries not to repeat it. That file already covers Hormozi's scripts, the Laura Cohen Skool funnel (1,100 DMs, 6 calls), Meta's limits, the 2023 message-request rule and UK PECR.

How to read these notes:
- Most material comes from YouTube auto-captions, downloaded with yt-dlp and read in full. Captions have no punctuation and sometimes mishear words. Quotes are verbatim from the captions, with obvious mishearings fixed only where the meaning is plain (for example "chbt" means ChatGPT). Emperor Dino's captions read as if machine-translated, so his wording is loose.
- Almost every YouTuber here sells a course, a community or a call. That is noted against each one.
- Reddit could not be read: both the search tool and direct requests to reddit.com were blocked. Skool posts were readable only where public, and comments were hidden.
- Pre-August 2023 advice is flagged. Since 3 August 2023, someone who doesn't follow you can send you only one text message request (no images, video or voice) until you accept it (TechCrunch, cited in the earlier notes).

## 1. Practitioners, their end-to-end campaigns, and how far to trust them

### Takeaway
No practitioner I found sells business coaching to £1M+ owner-led firms through Instagram. The closest are agency owners and freelancers DMing local businesses, coaches and creators. The most credible evidence is small live tests: Charlie Barber booked 2 calls from 39 DMs, Alicia Joseph booked 1 call from 170, Ocean Break Media got 4 replies from 100 and Megan Weeks got 2 replies and no work from 6. Alongside those sits one large claimed track record, Charlie Morgan's roughly 5,000 appointments in 3 years across platforms, with his Calendly analytics shown on screen. Every big-number channel is selling a course.

### Cited Findings

**Tier 1: shows real results (screen-shared DMs, analytics or live tests)**

  3. A "native VSL": a selfie video under 1:59, sent inside the app, not a Loom link or funnel.

## 2. Finding accounts and qualifying them

### Takeaway
Practitioners find owners in six ways:
- search the niche on Instagram;
- scrape Google Maps, then look each business up on Instagram;
- Google "site:instagram.com" plus a niche keyword;
- open the follower or following list of a mid-sized account that serves the niche (a guru, supplier, software brand or "micro-influencer");
- take the likers of that account's best posts;
- follow one good prospect and work through "Suggested for you".

They qualify by eye: posting recently, a real business, a website that is weak or missing, and the owner's own face or personal account. Most keep a Google Sheet. Time per day is rarely stated; the stated figures run from about 2 minutes a profile to 50 DMs in 30 minutes.

### Cited Findings
- **Follower list of a niche guru.** "Find a guru who helps my ICP of a fitness coach… you can obviously go through their engage likes, but also their followers… this gives us the ability just to go through here and really easily send out 50 DMs in a matter of 30 minutes." He used the coach's *following* list, which was full of fitness coaches. He also notes that "when you do follow someone, you get a suggested of other ones as well". He skipped accounts not in English. — [Charlie Barber, 28 Sep 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IdAfHxufsOU)
- **Micro-influencers in the niche.** Find "medium-sized accounts in your niche that your ideal client… are following", for example an account that helps construction firms with preconstruction software (6,800 followers). Open its followers list, search the list for "construction" to filter, and scrape it with a browser extension ("instant data scraper") into Google Sheets. Smaller accounts are used because big accounts' follower lists can't be browsed fully. — [Patrick Spiegel, 3 Sep 2024](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4e--urNopKk)
- **Google search for Instagram profiles.** A Google query on site instagram.com plus the niche ("roofing") plus "gmail.com", optionally plus a location ("New York, Texas, maybe England"). Copy the results into ChatGPT to turn them into a table of profile link, company name, website, phone and email, then paste that into Google Sheets. He gets "around 50" per run by hand, or 200 in about 30 seconds with the scraper. — [Patrick Spiegel](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4e--urNopKk)
- **"Suggested for you".** Search the niche, follow one real prospect, then work through the "Suggested for you" accounts that appear, checking each one by hand "to make sure that these are actually good leads". — [Patrick Spiegel](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4e--urNopKk)
- **Google Maps first, then Instagram.** He searched "Home Improvement in North Carolina" on Google Maps, scraped 100 results (about 20 minutes, PhantomBuster free trial), dropped national chains ("not do the ones that are popular like Lowe's or Home Depot"), then "look up all these companies on Instagram" by hand. — [Ocean Break Media, 7 Feb 2024](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=asVRb34EX5g)
- **Find the owner, not the brand.** "You need to be reaching out to the business owner directly." Google "Instagram [first name] [last name]" to find the owner's personal profile, and DM that rather than the company page. Before contacting, he checked the brand's Facebook page and saw they were running no ads. That gap became his reason for reaching out. — [Jordan Platten, 3 Apr 2020](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qa6U3soYe2g)
- **Likers of adjacent influencers.** Pick 5–10 influencers *adjacent* to the niche (not in it), with 10k–300k followers, and DM everyone who liked their best-performing posts. Bigger accounts bring "audiences from lower-tier countries". — [Pedro & Zach, 4 Apr 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ub6hcCXbYzo)
- **Competitors' comment sections.** Look at the comments on competitors' posts for people asking questions you can answer: "that person is actually looking for answers". — [Emperor Dino, 11 Nov 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=txidjZ-yr0U)
- **Qualify by the problem you fix.** For a web designer: does the business have a website, does it work, does it have recognisable branding? "I just looked up three and none of them have like a legitimate looking website." She also checked that their content was strong: "she has really really great content". — [Megan Weeks, 25 Jan 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ma9vXpJLRzk)
- **Avoid over-pitched targets.** "Local business owners. How many people do you think are reaching out to local businesses for ads, for an AI receptionist, for content? So many people." He also says accounts with very large followings (400k) rarely reply. — [Adam Consult, 18 Jan 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BZvcNrfWLiY)
- **Checklist qualifying.** Add a prospect only if they meet 3 of 4: industry, company size, relevant posts, decision-making role. Work in batches of about 20 at about 2 minutes per profile. Start with existing clients and people who engage with your content, then hashtags and competitors' followers and commenters. Self-published consultancy blog. — [Chrysales, 21 Jun 2026](https://www.chrysales.com/post/instagram-dm-strategy-sales-calls)
- **Spreadsheet.** Lists live in Google Sheets for Patrick Spiegel and in Pipedrive or a Google Sheet for Jordan Platten (about 600 leads, built by his team). aiDr uses Instagram's own labels and flags to track who needs a follow-up: "I forget to follow up… that made me lose a lot of clients". — [Patrick Spiegel](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4e--urNopKk); [Jordan Platten](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qa6U3soYe2g); [aiDr, 17 Jul 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3-jKacJ1ygY)
- **Follower ranges.** Charlie Barber and Patrick Spiegel give no follower range for the prospects themselves, only for seed accounts (Pedro & Zach 10k–300k; Patrick Spiegel "medium-sized", e.g. 6,800). The earlier notes cite Autogram's 5k–50k seeds. An X/Twitter DM dataset summary puts 10k–100k followers as the "sweet spot" for *recipients*, with under 10k "often inactive", but that is X, not Instagram, and second-hand. — [Matthew Metros Substack, 15 Jul 2026](https://matthewmetros.substack.com/p/what-437-million-dms-reveal-about)

### Inferences
- For a UK coach, the closest match to these methods is: pick a seed account the target owners follow, such as a regional business awards account, a trade body, a sector supplier or software brand (for example a gym-software or builders'-merchant account), or a well-known UK business-growth personality. Then browse its follower list, filtered by a niche word, and open each profile by hand.
- "Google Maps first, then Instagram" fits owner-led firms better than Instagram search, because Maps gives size signals (review count, several sites) that Instagram doesn't.
- The qualifier a coach can use, in place of "bad website", is a visible growth sign plus an owner on show: hiring posts, a new site or van, team photos, "we're expanding". That points to a £1M+ owner-led firm with a growth problem.

### Gaps
- No practitioner gave a daily time budget for finding leads alone, apart from "50 DMs in 30 minutes" (Charlie Barber) and about 2 minutes per profile (Chrysales).
- None targeted firms by revenue or headcount. Nobody showed a way to tell a £1M+ business from a sole trader on Instagram beyond judging by eye.
- Location-tag and hashtag-by-Recent methods appear only in the earlier vendor notes. No practitioner in this batch showed them live.

## 3. Warm-up and getting past message requests (the 2023 rule)

### Takeaway
Every post-2023 practitioner treats the first job as getting out of "Requests" and into the main inbox. They do it in four ways:
- touch the profile first: follow, like 1–2 posts, leave a real comment, reply to a story;
- send a first text with no pitch, so a reply moves the thread out of Requests;
- ask permission, then send voice or video only once the person has replied or followed back;
- go around the DM entirely, for example by tagging the prospect in your own story.

The warm-up is usually the same day or the day before. Nobody tested a longer gap.

### Cited Findings
- **The goal of the opener is a reply.** "I would always follow, maybe like one of their posts, we can comment something as well and just go in with a story popup. So the goal is to purely just get a response… because once you get a response, you're then in the actual inbox. So when you present your offer, the person will actually see it… We don't want to get stuck in the request… it's kind of like a Trojan horse." — [Charlie Barber, 28 Sep 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IdAfHxufsOU)
- **Get past "Requests" before you sell.** "The most important part is getting past the requested section… get them to respond to either a question that you've asked them about their business or about their client acquisition… and then you slowly get into the sale." — [Patrick Spiegel, 3 Sep 2024](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4e--urNopKk)
- **Comment the day before.** "Let's say you write two or three comments the day before. So they'll say, okay, I remember Dino from my comments… So you should always at least interact with them, like through a few of their posts… you can even respond on their Instagram story, but just do something so [they know your] face before I send a message." — [Emperor Dino, 11 Nov 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=txidjZ-yr0U)
- **Connect before any voice or video.** "We need some level of connection… so we have permission to message them… if you try and message someone on Facebook without being their friend or on LinkedIn without being connected to them they won't actually receive it." His permission message and pitch are voice or video. On Instagram those can't reach a non-follower until a text request is accepted (2023 rule), so the connect step has to come first. — [Charlie Morgan, 20 Feb 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3OR6sMC1dEY)
- **What the rule looks like in practice.** Her DM with an image attached showed that the recipient "doesn't accept messages from everyone". Later: "One of the doctors just started following me… with her following me back, I have a really good feeling that that probably pushed that message." — [Megan Weeks, 25 Jan 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ma9vXpJLRzk)
- **Official: follow status decides Inbox or Requests (for business-account private replies).** "This reply appears in the person's Inbox, if the person follows the Instagram professional account", otherwise the Request folder. Only one message can be sent, and further messages only after they respond. This is Meta's developer documentation for comment replies through the API, not person-to-person DMs, but it confirms the routing rule. — [Meta for Developers, Instagram Private Replies](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/private-replies/)
- **Bypass the DM with a story tag.** Tagging the prospect in your own story means "you get to avoid the spam box because now you're not going to their DMs. You're going to their notifications." Even with no reply, they usually visit your profile: "It's like a 100% open rate basically", and if your bio fits they follow you, which opens the inbox. — [Klabunde Media Co., 24 Apr 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DSY3w-hPWf4)
- **Pre-2023 version.** Follow, then engage with a couple of posts so "even if she doesn't notice my message request right away she's gonna see a notification from me". Optional unfollow "after a day or two if they don't actually respond". — [Jordan Platten, 3 Apr 2020](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qa6U3soYe2g)
- **Older pre-rule advice.** Watch their stories, read and comment on posts, then message. — [Mel Judson, 4 Nov 2019](https://meljudson.com/instagram-dms-cold-outreach-that-actually-works/)
- **Profile as the landing page.** Every practitioner says the prospect checks your profile before replying. They want a face photo and personal name (Emperor Dino); a bio of who you help, proof and a call to action (Pedro & Zach, Henry Fortunatow); 3–4 highlights with client stories (Emperor Dino); 9+ posts (Henry Fortunatow); posting 3–5 times a week (Pedro & Zach); and Meta Verified (Pedro & Zach, Megan Weeks). Klabunde calls this the "profile funnel" and credits it to "Nick Setting" (a setter-training channel). — [Emperor Dino](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=txidjZ-yr0U); [Pedro & Zach](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ub6hcCXbYzo); [Henry Fortunatow](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZoOBHfY7kEE); [Klabunde](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DSY3w-hPWf4)
- **Following first, X data.** Accounts that follow before messaging see "4x the reply rate". This comes from a second-hand summary of 43.7M DMs on X (Twitter), with no method given. — [Matthew Metros Substack, 15 Jul 2026](https://matthewmetros.substack.com/p/what-437-million-dms-reveal-about)

### Inferences
- A hand-run warm-up that matches these practitioners:
  1. Day 0: follow the owner's personal account and the business account, like 2 recent posts, and leave one specific comment.
  2. Day 1: reply to a story with a real reaction, or send the text opener.
  3. Only after a reply or follow-back: voice note or video.
- Story replies are a natural first message because they arrive tied to something the owner just posted. Whether a story reply from a non-follower lands in Requests or the main inbox could not be confirmed (see Gaps).
- The story-tag trick suits a coach's top 5–10 "dream" local firms, not volume. At about 2 hours per video it's a once-a-day tactic at most.

### Gaps
- No source confirms whether a story reply or comment from a non-follower lands in the recipient's main inbox or in Requests. Instagram's help pages didn't load as text, and Meta's API docs only cover business private replies.
- Nobody tested warm-up length (same day vs 2–3 days) against reply rate.
- Instagram's Hidden Requests folder (spam-filtered requests) is mentioned in the earlier notes. One Skool poster says old outreach accounts drop to about 4 replies from 40 while "new accounts get a great reply rate" and asks whether messages go to spam. No answer was visible. — [Skool, Agency World, Jakob Johnston, Mar 2024](https://www.skool.com/agencyworld/ig-dms-getting-sent-to-spam)

## 4. Openers: verbatim, why they work, and what not to say

### Takeaway
Post-2023 openers that practitioners say work are short questions anyone can answer, with no pitch and no link:
- "just a quick one… are you getting enough work?";
- "have you thought about [the thing you offer]?";
- a permission ask ("can I send you a video I made for you?").

Long personalised paragraphs, compliment-only openers and copy-paste offers did badly in the live tests. Several popular openers pretend the sender might be a customer. That's a deception a coaching brand should avoid.

### Cited Findings
**Question openers (recommended)**
- Alicia Joseph didn't personalise: "hi [name], just a quick one", then a question about whether they're getting enough work: "are you getting work are you getting enough or are you not getting enough". Her reasoning: trades are "very no BS people… they care about them way more than anyone else". When "I get a lot of these personalized DMs… I don't even bother reading the rest because it's just too much paragraph". She suggests one line sent to 40 a day rather than 10 long ones. — [Alicia Joseph, 19 Jul 2024](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9aY92C0HYTo)
- Charlie Barber's best opener after a day of testing: "just asking a question if they've tried kind of what you're going to be offering… if you're offering AI, you could say something like, 'yo Cliff, have you thought about getting an AI setter'". Once they reply: a compliment, then "Would you be opposed to getting a free [thumbnail]?" His compliment line: "I checked out your channel and it's better than I expected." — [Charlie Barber, 28 Sep 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IdAfHxufsOU)
- Emperor Dino's comment-to-DM opener (machine-translated captions, so wording is approximate): "Hello, [Name], I hope you are doing well. I just saw your comment and wanted to respond to it. Tell me, how long have you actually been struggling with this problem?" After "two or three lines", a soft ask: "if you want, we can quickly call… and maybe I'll help you completely free of charge. If not, no problem." — [Emperor Dino, 9 Jun 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=utocuMXtXzA)
- Chrysales's shape is observation, then a light question: "Saw your post about scaling a sales team without burning out." / "Curious, are you handling outreach in-house or outsourcing it?" — [Chrysales](https://www.chrysales.com/post/instagram-dm-strategy-sales-calls)

**Permission openers**
- Adam Consult's direct-value permission DM (text): "hey can I send you a 4-minute video I made specifically for you on how you can launch your own online educational program to help others with your own videography/content skills and how you can make five figures per month revenue from it. The video explains everything in detail… I already made it for you and it's 4 minutes long." The reply was a thumbs up. Why it works, in his words: "People know when you're DMing them you are trying to sell to them because they check your profile… when you're direct with them, they appreciate it." — [Adam Consult, 18 Jan 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BZvcNrfWLiY)
- Charlie Morgan's "Trojan Horse" outline (not his paid wording): "hey John thanks for accepting my request… I know you get pitched here all the time. I want to pitch you something but in order for me to do that I would like to ask for your permission. If that's okay then just let me know." He tells them how to answer: "please just reply one word okay" or "send me a thumbs up". He reuses the same answer word at every later step for "commitment consistency". — [Charlie Morgan, 20 Feb 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3OR6sMC1dEY)

**Offer-first and compliment openers (weaker results)**
- Ocean Break Media sent one line to 100 businesses: "would you be interested in 10 leads for your home improvement business in [town]?" Result: 4 replies in 24 hours (section 7). — [Ocean Break Media, 7 Feb 2024](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=asVRb34EX5g)
- Megan Weeks's draft: "Hey [name], I absolutely love what you're doing and your content. I'm a graphic designer that creates brand websites for wellness professionals like yourself. I wanted to reach out because I love your style…" She avoided naming the problem directly: "I don't want it to be like offensive like I noticed your website sucks". She tried variants with a portfolio link or an example image. Result: 2 polite replies from 6, no work. — [Megan Weeks, 25 Jan 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ma9vXpJLRzk)
- Pre-2023, Jordan Platten used a four-part structure: greeting with first name and who you are; a compliment on something specific on their profile; why you're reaching out (for example, "I notice you're not running any adverts at the minute"); and what you want ("15 to 20 minutes… over the next couple of days"). He sent it as a video. — [Jordan Platten, 3 Apr 2020](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qa6U3soYe2g)

**Pretend-customer openers (flag: deceptive)**
- "Hey I'm just checking out gyms nearby. Do you guys do personal training or just open memberships?" The poster said its response rate was below 30% and asked how to improve it. — [Skool, Agency World, Taj Cuatro, May 2024](https://www.skool.com/agencyworld/dm-response-rate-30)
- Henry Fortunatow's "opener and qualifier": "do you work in XYZ industry? do you run a coaching business? do you do Facebook ads or TikTok ads?" In his words: "The reason this works well is because people think you're a potential client, so the response rate is super super high… like 20%." The next message is the "direct offer": "are you looking to add another channel for your store at the moment?", then "we can help you add on an extra 10K per month using an organic channel for your Ecom store, want to see how it works?" — [Henry Fortunatow, 6 Feb 2023](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZoOBHfY7kEE)
- Adam Consult's failed 2023 opener to local businesses: "hey is this a good place for questions to the owner decision maker?" Now: "forget about it… People know when you're DMing them, you are trying to sell to them." — [Adam Consult](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BZvcNrfWLiY)
- Patrick Spiegel calls the question-then-sell method "a bit sleazy but it works". — [Patrick Spiegel](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4e--urNopKk)

**What not to say**
- Long paragraphs and a pitch in message one (Alicia Joseph). Copy-paste blasts: "I was copying the same bland and vague templates to around 50 to 60 people… none of them actually responded" (aiDr). Over-scripted wording that "felt robotic or like ChatGPT" (aiDr). Generic compliments (earlier notes). Sending at 2am local time: "wondering why I was getting cussed out"; he now DMs 10am–4pm in the prospect's time zone (aiDr). — [aiDr, 17 Jul 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3-jKacJ1ygY); [Alicia Joseph](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9aY92C0HYTo)

### Inferences
- The opener that fits a coach and stays honest is a one-line question tied to something real on their profile, which the owner can answer in a word. For example: "Saw the new van livery. Are you taking on more work this year, or keeping it steady?" Or a permission ask for big fast value: "I did a quick read of your business from the outside. Can I send you a 60-second voice note on the one thing I'd look at?"
- Avoid "pretend customer" and "is this the owner?" openers. They do get replies, but the first thing the owner learns is that the coach misled them. For a trust-based coaching sale that's a bad start. A UK owner who gets 20+ agency DMs will also recognise the trick.

### Gaps
- No A/B test anywhere compares personalised and unpersonalised openers on Instagram for owner-led firms. The two smallest live tests conflict: Alicia Joseph's unpersonalised question did fine, while aiDr's unpersonalised template got nothing.
- Charlie Morgan's and Adam Consult's exact paid scripts were withheld.

## 5. Follow-ups, voice notes, video, and moving to a call

### Takeaway
Two cadences recur. The "slow" one: 2 days, 2 weeks, 2 months, then stop (Emperor Dino), or 3–4 days then a few days more, then stop at three messages (Chrysales). The "relentless" one is Charlie Morgan's: once someone has said yes to a pitch, up to 9 bumps on the video and 9 on the calendar link, using memes and GIFs, with most bookings after bump 6–9. After a reply, practitioners switch from text to a short voice note or a selfie video. That's 30–60 seconds for a permission ask, under 2 minutes for a pitch, or a 3–5 minute personal Loom. They then *ask* whether to send a calendar link, rather than dropping it unasked, and give people a reason to confirm once they've booked.

### Cited Findings
- **Slow cadence.** "2 days after sending the first initial message, you send them the first follow-up message. Then, 2 weeks after… a second message. And 2 months after the first message… a third follow-up message. After that, there's honestly no need." — [Emperor Dino, 11 Nov 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=txidjZ-yr0U). His follow-up wording (approximate, machine-translated):
  1. At 2 days: "Hi [Name], just wanted to see how you're doing. Do you still want to continue the conversation we had that day?"
  2. About 2 weeks later: "Hi, I just posted a free tutorial that might help you… I wanted to write to see if you'd like to get it for free."
  3. About 2 months later: "Hi [Name], remember we chatted a while ago? I just wanted to know if you were still interested as I am opening new [spots] but most of them will probably fill up quickly."

  His reason: "Most meetings don't actually come after your first message. Most of them appear after the second or even third reminder." The two videos differ on whether the 2-week and 2-month gaps count from the first message or from the previous follow-up. — [Emperor Dino, 9 Jun 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=utocuMXtXzA)
- **Relentless cadence.**
  - On the permission message: "follow up twice… 'hey what did you think of the voice note'… just make them curious to click it."
  - On the pitch video: "follow up one two three four five six seven eight sometimes even nine times… we send them GIFs, we send them completely out of pocket memes… Pablo Escobar… captioned 'following up waiting for your reply'."
  - His rule: "once someone's given you permission to pitch they've also given you permission to follow up relentlessly."
  - Calendar links: "80% of your calendar links will be sent after follow-up 7". He then runs another 9-step chase to get the booking, and repeats the same follow-ups on email, phone and Facebook if he can find them. — [Charlie Morgan, 20 Feb 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3OR6sMC1dEY)
- **Same-day bumps (pre-2023, multi-account).** Initial DMs in the morning, follow up at midday and again in the evening, using bumps "like a question mark or like 'thoughts?'". — [Henry Fortunatow, 6 Feb 2023](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZoOBHfY7kEE)
- **Three-message cap.** First follow-up 3–4 days later "with something new", at most two follow-ups. The case study followed up at day 4 and day 7. Only offer a call once they name a problem: "Happy to hop on a quick 15 minute call and walk through what we'd look at first." — [Chrysales](https://www.chrysales.com/post/instagram-dm-strategy-sales-calls)
- **Native selfie video under 1:59, not a Loom.** "We don't send them a Loom, we don't send them a long message, we don't send them a PDF… a native VSL… one minute 59… record it as a selfie video… just your iPhone." The script opens "I'm here to sell you something, I've got your permission… at any moment you can click off". Then the offer, then social proof (a Loom clip of testimonials cut in, or a Miro or Figma workflow diagram if you have none), then "if I have your permission I would love to send over a calendar link". It's one evergreen video reused for everyone. — [Charlie Morgan](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3OR6sMC1dEY)
- **Personal 4-minute video after a thumbs up.** The structure: why you made it, a short personal intro, the opportunity, their custom offer, the partnership, the next step. Then he sends a Calendly link, then "an asset to watch before the call", then a check 30 minutes before: "Everything's still okay for our call in 30 minutes?" — [Adam Consult, 18 Jan 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BZvcNrfWLiY)
- **Permission video, then value video.** "You send them a 10 to 30 second video asking if you can send them some value or an offer video… the first video is around 10 to 15 seconds just asking permission and then you send them the actual value-based video and that is where the sale then happens." He credits this to Charlie Morgan. — [Patrick Spiegel, 3 Sep 2024](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4e--urNopKk)
- **Personal Loom (3–5 minutes) structure.**
  1. Hook in the first 20–40 seconds: face, a wave, "Hey John", one specific thing you noticed, and a bit of humour ("Don't worry, I'm not stalking your profile").
  2. One quick fix shown on their website or profile.
  3. Lead-in: "I actually work with people in the property and finance sector, helping them generate three to five high-quality meetings on a weekly basis".
  4. Proof from the same industry.
  5. The core pain.
  6. Three mistakes, each with its fix and outcome.
  7. A low-friction call to action: "just reply and I'll send you a free quick marketing audit", a thumbs-up reaction, or a Loom booking button. "Most people don't have the intuition to just click that button."

  He says personal Looms beat evergreen tools ("Replic"). — [Ben Greening-James, 11 May 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_ep45ceQEhE)
- **Voice notes raise opens.** "I started sending voice messages and people had to actually open the message and hear it. So that counted as more open rates and more replies." "When I actually started writing like how I talk and actually sending voice messages, the replies increased drastically." — [aiDr, 17 Jul 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3-jKacJ1ygY)
- **Free sample as the bridge.** "I sent one ad creative as a free sample and that led to me landing a client within a week. Free value builds trust. It's not charity, it's strategy." (aiDr). Charlie Barber's bridge was a free YouTube thumbnail. He then tried to move the prospect to WhatsApp ("it's just better for conversions"), but the prospect preferred to stay on Instagram. — [aiDr](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3-jKacJ1ygY); [Charlie Barber](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IdAfHxufsOU)
- **Explainer, then booking ask, then a reason to confirm.** "Okay cool, here's how it works: one we do XYZ, two we do ABC. Best for me to show you how this works over screen share, want me to send a booking link?" Then: "here's the booking link… let me know once you've booked in so I can send you some extra resources". About a third of people who get the explainer agree to a call. — [Henry Fortunatow, 6 Feb 2023](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZoOBHfY7kEE)
- **Ask for a time.** "Are you available at 12:00 p.m. EST?" "Yes." "Awesome. We just need your email and your phone number." — [Pedro & Zach, 4 Apr 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ub6hcCXbYzo)
- **Stay in the chat, then shoot your shot.** With a sceptical prospect, Alicia Joseph went about 10 messages back and forth asking "what are you currently doing for marketing", then: "if you're open to having a call sometime about the problem and how we can fix it". He replied "9:00 a.m. is when I'm free". Her warning: "you could end up losing them if you talk to them too long". — [Alicia Joseph, 19 Jul 2024](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9aY92C0HYTo)
- **Older advice.** Send a calendar link or "offer two times within the next week", then ask them to tell you once they've booked. — [Mel Judson, 2019](https://meljudson.com/instagram-dms-cold-outreach-that-actually-works/)
- **Timing of replies (X data).** 42% of replies arrive within 24 hours, 20% in 1–3 days, 15% in 3–7 days and 22.6% in 7–30 days. Waiting 72 hours before the first follow-up "nearly doubles" response compared with early follow-up (no baseline given). Second-hand, X not Instagram. — [Matthew Metros Substack](https://matthewmetros.substack.com/p/what-437-million-dms-reveal-about)

### Inferences
- Under the 2023 rule, a non-follower can't receive any follow-up until they accept. So all the follow-up cadences above really apply only to people who replied, accepted or followed back. For silent non-followers, the only "follow-up" is more profile touches (likes, story replies) or another channel (email, phone, LinkedIn), as Charlie Morgan does.
- For a coach, a natural middle path between "slow" and "relentless":
  - Before any reply: one opener, then touches only.
  - After a yes: a 60–90 second voice note or selfie video giving one useful observation about their business, then "want me to send a couple of times for a 20-minute call?"
  - Then 3–5 light nudges over two weeks.
  - Memes and Pablo Escobar GIFs are off-brand for BCA's owner audience.

### Gaps
- No source measured voice note vs text vs video reply rates on Instagram. The claims are anecdotal (aiDr) or from other platforms.
- Nobody reported whether a calendar link or "two times" books more calls on Instagram.
- Charlie Morgan's 9+9 follow-up numbers are claimed across platforms with no split for Instagram.

## 6. Volume, daily routine, accounts and blocks

### Takeaway
By hand from one account, practitioners send 20–100 DMs a day. Charlie Barber paces at 20 an hour at most, Adam Consult says 25–50 "quality" DMs a day, and aiDr has dropped to about 5 well-chosen ones. The higher figures (200–500 a day, or about 100 per account) rely on many accounts and VAs. That's against the earlier notes' account-safety evidence and against aiDr's finding that one strong profile beat 70 extra ones. A realistic hand routine is 1–2 hours a day: find and warm 20–30 profiles, open new conversations, then answer replies.

### Cited Findings
- **Pace.** "You can usually get away with like 20 max per hour if you send it in a short period of time. So I don't really want to go over that. So I'm going to take a break." He sent in bursts, then went for a run and came back to replies. — [Charlie Barber, 28 Sep 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IdAfHxufsOU)
- **Account age.** He checked a blog that said newer accounts can send 20–50 DMs in 24 hours and older ones 100–150, then sent 100 from a 5-month-old account. No block was reported. — [Ocean Break Media, 7 Feb 2024](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=asVRb34EX5g)
- **About 100 per account, then multiple accounts.** "Your main account can send approximately like 100 cold messages per day before Instagram stops you." The block sign: "it says the message has failed and you cannot retry… you just have to wait 24 hours". Their fix is extra accounts that "all lead back to your main account". — [Pedro & Zach, 4 Apr 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ub6hcCXbYzo)
- **50 per account, six accounts, VA.** "You can only do a Max of 50 DMs per account per day", so six accounts give 300. A VA sends 200 a day and does the follow-ups, about 4 hours a day. Pre-2023. — [Henry Fortunatow, 6 Feb 2023](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZoOBHfY7kEE)
- **One account beat 70.** "I ended up creating a lot of different pages hoping to outsmart the system… just to break that barrier of sending like 30 DMs an hour… In the end of the day I ended up staying with one profile. I just made it strong and optimized… it's outperforming all the 70 other accounts." "Five DMs a day has been bringing people better results." — [aiDr, 17 Jul 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3-jKacJ1ygY)
- **25–50 quality DMs a day**, plus "follow up every day with prospects". — [Adam Consult, 18 Jan 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BZvcNrfWLiY)
- **Throughput.** Jordan Platten: 20–30 companies in about 2 hours by video DM, from a list his team had built. Charlie Barber: 50 DMs in about 30 minutes when working through one follower list. Ben Greening-James: 3–5 minutes per personal Loom ("to send five videos, it's going to take me 25 minutes"). Klabunde: 2 hours per outbound-story video. — [Jordan Platten](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qa6U3soYe2g); [Charlie Barber](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IdAfHxufsOU); [Ben Greening-James](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_ep45ceQEhE); [Klabunde](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DSY3w-hPWf4)
- **Personal accounts vs many accounts (X data).** Personal accounts reportedly get 8–15% reply rates, against 0.5–2% for operators running 11+ accounts. Second-hand, X not Instagram, no method. — [Matthew Metros Substack](https://matthewmetros.substack.com/p/what-437-million-dms-reveal-about)
- **Old accounts fading.** One Skool poster who sent "10s of thousands of DMs" found his oldest outreach account got about 4 replies from 40 while newly bought accounts did better, and suspected spam filtering. — [Skool, Agency World, Mar 2024](https://www.skool.com/agencyworld/ig-dms-getting-sent-to-spam)
- **Send times.** 10am–4pm in the prospect's time zone (aiDr). — [aiDr](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3-jKacJ1ygY)
- **Smallest routine described.** A 5–10 minute timer, 5–10 helpful comments on posts, then 5–7 new conversations a day with people met that way. He claims 5–10 calls a week, with no proof. — [Emperor Dino, 9 Jun 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=utocuMXtXzA)

### Inferences
- For a coach on their own personal account, the sources support 20–40 new hand-written openers a day, sent in short bursts (under about 20 an hour) between normal scrolling, plus warm-up touches and replies. In Charlie Barber's and Jordan Platten's throughput, that's about 60–90 minutes a day.
- Multi-account farming and bought accounts suit agencies selling volume offers. They don't suit a coach whose personal profile *is* the trust signal, and they carry the block risk described in the earlier notes.

### Gaps
- No practitioner reported a real block or ban rate at a given volume. All limits quoted (20, 50, 100, 150 a day) are rules of thumb that conflict with each other.
- No one separated time spent finding leads from time spent messaging.

## 7. Results: reply rates, calls and clients

### Takeaway
Hand-run results in live or self-reported tests:
- First-reply rates: about 4% (copy-paste offer to trades), 7% (one-line question to trades), 15% (warmed-up online coaches).

- No Reddit evidence (blocked).
