## 1. Gym and sports-venue owners: how gym-owner coaching and marketing firms, and suppliers, find and approach owners on Instagram/Facebook; scripts, groups, content, free offers, what converts

### Takeaway
The firms that built big businesses selling to gym owners did not do it with cold Instagram DMs. Two-Brain (Canada/US) grew on a decade of daily free content, a vetted owners-only Facebook group and a free call with a gym-owner mentor; Gym Launch (US) grew on a results offer it could prove in its own gyms, plus a phone outreach team. Gym owners are flooded with agency pitches promising "30 leads in 30 days", so a lead-count opener now reads as spam; the firms that win pitch sales process and profit, and lead with owner-to-owner proof. Hand-run cold DMs to gyms show tiny hit rates (1 reply in about 150) but can still close with stubborn follow-up.

### Inferences
- The proven pattern for gym owners is "owners' room first, DM second": free content in the owners' own room (podcast, group, webinars), then a free call with someone who has run a gym. DMs work as the follow-up to that, not as the opener.
- Gym owners treat "X leads/members in Y days" as the agency tell. A business coach should lead with what Two-Brain leads with: most owners already have enough leads and lose money in sales, pricing, retention and staff. For a £1M–£5M venue that is margin, staff cost and utilisation, which suits a coach better than an ads agency.
- Gym Launch's early proof was in-person ("fly out, set up a table"), and its first big sales were to warm owners already in the pipeline. A coach entering a new venue niche gets the same lift from one or two named, local case studies (Blackman's "free shoot at the biggest club" is a crude version).
- £1M–£5M padel, climbing and multi-sport venues are a short, public list in the UK (hundreds of venues, not thousands), with clear "new venue" signals (planning applications, LTA court funding, Savills/press on operator roll-outs). That suits hand-run, account-by-account outreach: find the operator, find the owner's name, warm up on Instagram/LinkedIn, then message.
- Utilisation maths is the padel hook that suppliers already use (off-peak court hours, revenue per available court hour). A coach could offer the owner a short "empty court hours" check with a £ figure, which is concrete and not a lead promise.

### Gaps
- No UK gym or padel coach published a campaign with numbers (DMs sent, replies, calls, clients). Justin Devonshire's funnel is inferred from podcast notes.
- No climbing-wall-specific coach, consultant or supplier campaign found; trade bodies for climbing walls were not checked.
- No primary source on how gym equipment or software suppliers (e.g. Escape Fitness, TeamUp, Glofox, PushPress) prospect owners on social beyond podcasts and groups.
- The Gym Launch cold-outreach scripts and close rates are not in any source I could read (book behind paywall; only an AI summary).
- Reddit (r/gymowners) threads were not surfaced by search.

## 2. Food, drink and consumer-brand founders: how agencies, consultants, brokers, investors and coaches find and approach founders (Instagram founder vs brand accounts, LinkedIn, founder communities); how they reach the founder rather than a social media manager

### Takeaway
I found no documented, hand-run Instagram DM campaign to UK food or drink founders with numbers. What the evidence does show is where UK founders gather and get named in public: Bread & Jam (12,000+ member free FoodHUB Facebook group, a festival, an investor contest), accelerator cohorts (Brand Growth Heroes, Tesco, Co-op, Waitrose, Booths), trade press (The Grocer runs a fundraising feed), crowdfunding pages and trade-fair exhibitor lists. Founders of brands at £1M+ are often active on LinkedIn, which is the most direct route to the person rather than the social media manager. Ecommerce agencies prospect founders by spotting a checkable gap (a missing abandoned-cart flow, a stale Amazon listing, a seasonal sale with no plan) and sending a short audit or plan.

### Inferences
- For a coach targeting £1M–£10M UK food, drink and consumer brands, the list-building starts from public, named lists, not Instagram search: accelerator cohorts, Bread & Jam exhibitors and Bread Winner entrants, Grocer fundraising stories, Crowdcube/Seedrs pages, trade-fair exhibitor directories, Great Taste-style award winners, and supermarket "new listing" press. Each gives a dated reason to write ("congrats on the Booths listing").
- At £1M–£10M the brand Instagram is usually run by a marketer or agency, so DMing the brand account reaches the wrong person and often lands in "requests". The founder is easier to reach on LinkedIn (UK founders post listings and raises there) or on a personal Instagram linked from the brand bio or "our story" page. Companies House confirms who the directors are.
- The hooks that work for agencies are checkable gaps and timely plans (missing cart flow, Black Friday plan). A business coach's equivalents are founder problems that are visible from outside: a new supermarket listing (cash flow and working capital squeeze), a raise (board reporting, hiring), a price rise or delisting in the trade press, or a seasonal peak (Christmas gifting, Dry January for drinks, Veganuary).
- Founder communities (Bread & Jam, accelerator programmes) let suppliers in through partnership, talks and mentoring. A coach is more likely to win trust by mentoring at Taste Academy-type programmes or speaking at a Startup Summit than by DMing FoodHUB members.

### Gaps
- No verbatim Instagram DM scripts with results aimed at UK food or drink founders were found.
- No evidence that investors, brokers or retail buyers approach founders by Instagram DM; Piper and similar firms don't publish sourcing methods.

## 3. The free offer or hook that gets replies in each niche, and verbatim openers

### Takeaway
Across niches the replies come from a free thing that is specific to the owner's numbers or calendar: a free call with a peer who has done it (Two-Brain), a free content shoot (padel agency), utilisation maths (padel supplier), a flow audit or seasonal plan (ecommerce). Generic "I can get you X members" offers are the ones gym owners now ignore. Verbatim openers found are few and mostly from agencies; the strongest-documented general pattern is permission first ("can I pitch you?"), then a short native video, then many follow-ups.

### Cited Findings

**Gyms and venues**
- [OWN NUMBERS] Free call with a gym-owner mentor, price published up front, "you leave with action steps" (Two-Brain, per search summary). — [Two-Brain: Book your free call](https://twobrainbusiness.com/book-a-c)
- [OWN NUMBERS] Gym Launch's first lead magnet, a "game planning session": "Some gyms took us up on it, but most didn't." — [Deciphr summary of $100M Leads](https://www.deciphr.ai/podcast/100m-leads-audiobook-part-5--cold-outreach-ep-590)
- [OWN NUMBERS] Cold Facebook DM to gyms, verbatim excerpt: "Excited to share the great news with you! We have launched a new tool for gyms, studio owners, and fitness and wellness professionals where we send you 10+ new members in 7 days ... No catch, we just want to prove ourselves and earn the chance to work together..." (1 reply in about 150; closed after a week of chasing). — [Skool: GoHighLevel community](https://www.skool.com/gohighlevel/dr-client-using-robbs-closing-script)
- [PITCH / plan] Padel agency opener in person or by phone: "Hey, look, I'm a videographer. I've got a marketing agency. I want to come and shoot for you for a couple of hours for free." — [YouTube Gu93AJFvkoA](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gu93AJFvkoA)
- [ADVICE, supplier content] Padel supplier hook: off-peak court-hour maths ("For a four court club, £33,000 a year"), CTA "Planning a paddle club? Tell us about your site." — [YouTube 7LC0H-MEkvM](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7LC0H-MEkvM)
- [ADVICE, PT-to-consumer, included for the call ask only] Fitness DM call ask: "hey based on what you've told me i believe i can help would you be down to hop on a quick call so i can offer some free advice". The same coach warns that "hey i'm zach coburn i'm a personal trainer i have an online program i'd love to see if you'd be a good fit" "comes across as super duper salesy". — [YouTube j91TYMEHIAA](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j91TYMEHIAA)

**Food, drink and consumer brands**
- [OWN NUMBERS] Seasonal plan as the opener (warm list): "Hey, I don't know if you have any Black Friday sales strategy plans coming up, but we've actually created our own and I've recorded a video run through on how it works. Here you go..." — [YouTube OxHAxezrsgo](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OxHAxezrsgo)
- [ADVICE] Audit hook: open on a checkable gap (no abandoned-cart flow live), backed by Klaviyo's $3.65 vs $0.11 per-recipient benchmark (per search summary). — [Klaviyo flow audit checklist](https://klaviyo.com/blog/the-complete-checklist-audit-your-flows)
- [ADVICE] "Mention you use the product" when messaging a founder on LinkedIn. — [Clarity.fm answer](https://clarity.fm/questions/7356/answers/24841/share)

**Cross-niche permission pattern (US/UK agency teacher)**
- [PITCH] Charlie Morgan (Feb 2025) describes a "Trojan horse soft pitch", sent as a voice note or video after connecting ("no text messages because you need to break the pattern"): "hey John uh thanks for accepting my request uh I know you get pitched here all the time I want to pitch you something but in order for me to do that I would like to ask for your permission if that's okay then just let me know". He says this is "not actually the verbatim" (the exact copy is in his paid course). Then a "native VSL": a selfie video under 1 minute 59 sent inside the app, opening "hey I'm here to sell you something I've got your permission to do so", stating offer, proof and a high-level method, ending with "I would love your permission to send over a calendar link". Follow-up: twice after the permission message ("what did you think of the voice note"), up to nine times after the pitch (memes, GIFs), then up to nine more after the calendar link; "80% of your... calendly links will be sent after followup... six or seven eight and nine". — [YouTube 3OR6sMC1dEY](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3OR6sMC1dEY)

### Inferences
- For gym and venue owners, the free offer most likely to land for a business coach is a numbers check tied to their venue: court or class utilisation, revenue per member, staff cost as a share of revenue, or a BOSS-style score, delivered by someone who has run a site. It avoids the "leads" language gym owners now distrust.
- For food founders, the equivalent is a timely, checkable observation: a new listing, a raise, a seasonal peak, or a visible cash-flow risk, with a short plan or one-page check attached.
- The permission-first opener ("I know you get pitched here all the time... can I pitch you?") suits owner niches flooded with pitches (gyms especially), but Morgan's nine-follow-up cadence risks spam reports on Instagram and conflicts with UK PECR caution for sole-trader gyms (see `instagram_facebook_outreach.md`).

### Gaps
- No A/B evidence on which free offer converts best in any of these niches.
- No verbatim agency-to-gym-owner Instagram DM with measured results beyond the single GoHighLevel post.
- Morgan's real script is behind a paywall; the quoted text is his on-video paraphrase.

The firmest numbers are business outcomes the founders report about themselves (Gym Launch's revenue and sale; Two-Brain's client counts). ### Gaps
- No controlled or third-party-verified reply, call or close rates for Instagram/Facebook outreach to gym, padel, climbing, food or drink owners.
- No UK campaign data in either target niche.
- YouTube search yield was poor: queries like "how to get gym owner clients instagram dm" return mostly personal trainers selling to consumers, not firms selling to gym owners; "food and beverage brand outreach agency" returned general social media marketing videos.
