## 1. Which signals setters work, in what order, and where they find them in the app

### Takeaway
Practitioners rank signals by how deliberately the person raised a hand. First: people who commented a keyword, replied to a story, voted in a poll or DMed. Then people who engaged (commented, liked several posts, new followers who also engaged). Then plain new followers and story viewers. Old leads that went quiet come last. A single like or story view is treated as weak. Some practitioners say it isn't a real signal at all. The setter works from notifications, post comments, the story viewer list and the inbox. Teams use a shared inbox (Meta Business Suite or GoHighLevel) so history isn't lost.

### Cited Findings
**The order a working setter follows (Grade A, opened)** — [SFL DM Setter SOP](https://sfl-operations.pages.dev/setter-sop)
- Verbatim rule: "Work these in priority order every day. Hottest first, coldest last." … "Never skip Priority 1 to go straight to outreach. A raised hand is always worth more than a cold follow."
- **Priority 1, Raised Hands:** "People who commented a keyword on a post and received a free guide"; "People who replied to a poll or story vote showing interest"; "People who DM'd asking about the program, coaching, or how May works"; "New inbound applications"; "People who replied to a 2-step post or direct offer post".
- **Priority 2, Content Engagers:** "New followers who liked or commented on recent posts"; "People who commented something genuine on a reel or carousel"; "New Facebook page DMs"; "New Facebook page followers who engage with content"; "People who liked multiple posts in a row (signal of real interest)"; "New Facebook group joins (backup, not daily priority)".
- **Priority 3, Proactive Outreach (when volume is low):** "New Instagram followers who have not been messaged yet"; "People who watched a reel or story but did not comment or DM"; "New TikTok followers — comment engagement only, not DM".
- **Priority 4, Re-engagement (check every few days):** "Anyone in Follow Up / Nurturing (WARM) stage not touched in 2 to 4 weeks"; "Guide recipients who received the guide but never replied"; stalled conversations; people who wanted help but were never offered a call.
- **Where the setter looks:** "Use Meta Business Suite to look at the comments on May's Instagram and Facebook posts. That is how you find who raised their hand and on which post … different posts deliver different guides … The comment tells you their entry point." Conversations run in GoHighLevel's Team Inbox "because it pulls all Instagram and Facebook conversations into one place, keeps the full history even when Instagram hides old chats". The coach "actively deletes low-value conversations from Instagram such as emoji story replies and one-word responses", so the setter checks GoHighLevel history before every reply.
- **Automation handles the first touch to new followers:** "ManyChat automatically sends new followers a welcome message with a free guide offer. The setter does NOT need to do that part. The setter picks up AFTER ManyChat." If the automation "did not fire at all (it happens)", the setter sends the welcome by hand.

**How to find each signal in the Instagram app**
- **Story viewers:** only the account owner can see who viewed a story; on the app you tap the profile pictures at the bottom left of your story (Instagram Help Centre). The view count includes replays, so it can be higher than the number of usernames. — [Instagram Help Centre, story views](https://help.instagram.com/instagram-lite-app/202055156863605). How long the viewer list stays available is unclear: some guides say 48 hours (24 live plus 24 in the archive), others say it disappears when the story expires. — [iDownloadBlog](https://www.idownloadblog.com/2021/03/02/see-who-viewed-instagram-story-stories/); [Online Tech Tips](https://www.online-tech-tips.com/fun-stuff/can-you-see-who-viewed-your-instagram-story/) (per search summary).
- **Poll voters:** Instagram sends no notification for a vote, and outside tools can't see who voted. You see voters in the story's own viewer or results list. One vendor's workaround is a text line under the poll, such as "Reply SPA for the price list", so the reply starts a DM. (Per search summary.) — [Chatfuel, Instagram polls guide](https://chatfuel.com/blog/instagram-polls-guide)
- **New followers:** notifications show new followers. Your own Followers list is usually newest first but isn't guaranteed to be in date order. Only your Following list has a "Date followed: Latest" sort. (Per search summary; how-to sites, not Instagram.) — [Alphr](https://www.alphr.com/view-new-followers-on-instagram/); [Luxury Travel Magazine](https://www.luxurytravelmagazine.com/news-articles/is-instagrams-following-list-in-chronological-order-how-to-track-new-follows)
- **Which inbox your DM lands in:** messages from accounts the recipient follows land in their Chats. Messages from accounts they don't follow go to Message Requests and must be accepted first. — [Instagram Help Centre, message requests](https://help.instagram.com/iphone-app/585369912141614); [Bustle](https://www.bustle.com/articles/160643-instagram-has-a-secret-inbox-for-message-requests-heres-how-to-find-it) (per search summary)
- **Automating the new-follower DM is now allowed:** ManyChat launched "Follow to DM" on 22 Oct 2025 as a "Meta-approved feature that lets you automatically send a DM to anyone who follows your Instagram account", in beta, with a settable delay. (Opened.) — [ManyChat Community, product update, 22 Oct 2025](https://community.manychat.com/product-updates/turn-every-follow-into-a-conversation-introducing-follow-to-dm-7628). Eligibility is set by Meta. It fires only if the follower isn't already a ManyChat contact, and some Pro accounts were told it "isn't available for your Instagram account". (Per search summary.) — [ManyChat Help](https://help.manychat.com/hc/articles/23096654243740); [ManyChat Community FAQ](https://community.manychat.com/general-q-a-43/follow-to-dm-faq-everything-you-need-to-know-9214)

### Inferences
- Warm outbound gets around the 2023 one-message rule. Someone who follows the coach gets the coach's DM in their main inbox, can receive voice notes, and can be followed up. A story viewer or liker who doesn't follow the coach still lands in Message Requests (one text message, no media). So "new follower" and "engaged follower" are the signals worth a manual DM. A non-follower's like is close to cold.
- The new-follower welcome is now done by automation (ManyChat Follow to DM) in the best-run setup found. The human setter's time goes on replies, comment-keyword leads and follow-ups, not on typing "thanks for the follow". Alex Cattoni's experience suggests saying openly that the welcome is automated ("This is an automated message from Alex"), with a human answering every reply, gets better replies than pretending it's personal.

### Gaps
- No source gives a measured comparison of reply or booking rates by signal type (new follower vs liker vs story viewer vs commenter). The priority order is practitioner judgment.
- Instagram's own Help Centre doesn't confirm how long story-viewer lists last. Check in the app.

## 2. Opening messages for each signal, and what stops them feeling like a pitch

### Takeaway
Every opener found has the same shape: name the signal ("saw you voted", "thanks for the comment on my post about X"), then ask one open, curious question about them ("what brought you to my page?", "what about it resonated?"). No link, price or offer. One practitioner (Vegard Mikalsen, Aug 2026) now says "thanks for the follow" reads as a template and should be replaced with a personal, indirect question. The best defence against sounding canned is to rotate phrasing and refer to something real on their profile.

### Cited Findings
**Verbatim openers from the setter SOP (Grade A, opened)** — [SFL DM Setter SOP](https://sfl-operations.pages.dev/setter-sop)
- New follower (manual): "Hey [Name]! Thanks for the follow, really appreciate it! Out of curiosity, what brought you to my page?"
- New follower, content angle: "Hey [Name]! Thanks for following! Just so you know I post a lot about [weight and health / hormones / fat loss] so hopefully you find the content helpful! Is there anything specific you're working on right now?"
- New follower when the automation didn't fire: "Hey [Name]! Welcome and thanks for the follow 😊 I have free guides on fat loss, blood sugar, testosterone, sleep, cholesterol, and healthy shopping. No email needed. Want me to send you one?"
- Commented on a post: "Hey [Name]! Thanks for the comment on my post about [XYZ], really appreciate it! Out of curiosity, what about it resonated with you?"
- Liked a post: "Hey [Name]! Just saw you liked my post, thanks for engaging! Out of curiosity, what made that stand out to you?"
- Liked several posts: "Hey [Name]! I noticed you've been liking a few of my posts, really appreciate the support! Out of curiosity, is there anything health or fitness related you're currently working on?"
- Name keeps appearing: "Hey [Name]! I keep seeing your name pop up, thought it was time to reach out 😊 How's life?"
- Poll voter: "Hey [Name]! I saw you voted on my poll, so glad you did! Curious, what made you vote? Is this something you're actively working on right now?"
- Story vote, interested: "Hey [Name]! Saw you raised your hand on my story, so glad you did! Would you be against me asking a couple quick questions to see if I can actually help? No worries either way 😊"
- Comment-keyword (guide) lead: "Hey [Name]! Here's that free guide you asked about [paste link]. Out of curiosity, what made you reach out for this specific one? Is there something in particular you're working on right now?"
- Reply to a "2-step" (comment if you want help) post: "Hey [Name]! Thanks for commenting, really appreciate it! Before I tell you how I work, mind if I ask a couple quick questions so I can actually help you out? What's the main thing you're trying to fix right now?"
- Someone who DMs asking how it works: "Hey [Name]! Awesome that you want to know more, I'd love to help! Can I ask real quick what's the main thing you're hoping to get help with? Just want to make sure I point you in the right direction 😊"
- Facebook group, after commenting publicly on their post first: "Hey [Name]! I just commented on your post in [group name]. I work with a lot of people dealing with exactly what you described and thought I'd reach out personally. Would you be against me sharing a free resource that might help? No strings attached."
- Moving from small talk to the topic: "Out of curiosity, is there anything health or fitness related you're currently working on or struggling with?" and "So I do this fitness stuff all day and actually work with a ton of people in your exact shoes. I definitely have some suggestions that can help if you're open to it!"
- Why these don't feel like a pitch, in the SOP's words: "You went to them (proactive outreach, new follower, content engager): You initiated. Do not push. Give value, stay warm, ask one question, let them lead." Voice rules: "Short messages. One or two sentences"; "One question per message. Never stack two questions"; "Lowercase is fine … A typo here and there is okay, it feels human"; rotate permission phrases ("out of curiosity…", "quick question…", "would you be against me asking a couple quick questions?") and "Never use the same one twice in a row".

### Inferences
- The best openers tie the message to the exact piece of content the person engaged with ("that reel", "my post about X"). That tells you their problem before they say it. The SOP does this on purpose: each post gives away a different guide, so the comment shows the setter the person's issue.
- For a coach selling to owners, the reel-specific opener moves across almost word for word: "Most owners who found me from that video about [pricing / team / cash] are dealing with [problem]. Is that you, or something else?"

### Gaps
- No source tested openers against each other with measured reply rates (for example "thanks for the follow" vs an indirect question).

## 3. The conversation framework: stages, questions, voice notes, the call invite, and "not now"

### Takeaway
The working pattern has three phases: connect (small talk about them), find the problem (permission, then one question at a time: challenge, what they tried, how long, the goal, why it matters now, ability to pay), then invite them to a call once they want help. Setters never coach in the DMs. They say what the person needs, not how. When the person asks "how?", that's the moment to offer the call. Good setters book the call themselves (two specific times, or a call in five minutes for hot leads) rather than sending a link and hoping. Practitioners disagree on price: the SOP deflects it, while one template states the price range before inviting. "Not now" is never dropped. It goes into a nurture stage with a light touch every one to four weeks.

### Cited Findings
**The SOP's framework (Grade A, opened)** — [SFL DM Setter SOP](https://sfl-operations.pages.dev/setter-sop)
- Three phases, "in order. Never skip ahead": "1. Connection. Build a real, friendly connection first … Thank them for the follow or comment, ask something light, get them talking. This is also where you naturally learn where they are from and what they do. 2. Struggles. … Ask permission first, then dig into the challenge, what they tried, how long, their goal, and the why behind it. 3. Call. Once you know you can help them and they want help, pitch the quick call."
- **RAQ in every message:** "R — Repeat … A — Acknowledge … Q — Question: ask ONE question". Example: "that's awesome you want to lose 20 pounds! I love how clear that goal is. out of curiosity, what's been the biggest thing getting in the way so far?"
- **Ten gates to fill before offering a call:** "Challenge (specific); What they tried; How long it has been a problem; Goal (a number); The why / the moment behind it; Job; Location; Income signal; Urgency (is it a priority now); Willing to invest."
- **Question bank (verbatim):** "What's been the biggest challenge in terms of your health and fitness?"; "What have you tried so far to [solve their problem], if anything?"; "How long has this been going on for you?"; "So if we could fix [main problem], what would your ideal results look like in a perfect world?"; "So I think I've got an answer to this, but I've always gotta ask… is handling this a priority or more of a 'get to it eventually' sorta thing?"; "On a scale of 1-10, how important is fixing this to you?"; "How would your life change if you were actually able to get this handled?"; "Out of curiosity, what do you do for work? Just helps me understand your schedule and lifestyle a bit better."
- **Don't coach in the DMs:** "You can tell them WHAT they need (like 'you'd want to track your protein'), but never explain HOW to do it. When they ask 'how do I do that?' that is your perfect moment to pitch the call. 'honestly that's exactly the kind of thing easier to walk through on a quick call than type out here.'"
- **Free resources as part of the conversation:** "Free guides are not a last resort. Give them throughout the conversation … After you send a guide, ALWAYS follow with one question. Never send a guide and go silent."
- **Voice notes:** the coach "recorded two short voice notes in her real voice. One for brand new followers, and one for people who just grabbed a free guide. Send the matching voice note early in the conversation. This is what makes the person feel like they are actually talking to May and not a bot."
- **Don't pitch new followers too early:** "Most new followers take 8 to 16 weeks to be ready to buy. Pitching a call to someone who followed you 3 days ago almost never closes." The exception: when someone is clearly in pain and eager ("Eagerness overrides newness"). Example given: "my blood sugar levels came back at 7.2, my doctor wants to put me on metformin and I need help now", against "I want to lose weight", which is not a reason to pitch. For the rest: "One warm touchpoint every 1 to 2 weeks is enough."
- **The call invite (verbatim):** "Honestly, would you be against a quick 5-10 min phone call? Definitely have some insight that would be helpful, but it'll be so much easier to talk on the phone rather than being stuck in the DMs lol." / "Honestly, this is definitely worth a quick convo. Would you be against hopping on a quick 10-15 min call? Nothing being pitched, just way easier to actually help you than going back and forth here lol." / If they hesitate: "Totally fair, nothing being sold, just way easier to actually help you than typing it all out here lol. If you don't mind me asking, what's holding you back?"
- **Booking: the setter books it, with two times:** "Offer two specific times. Get their best phone number." "Book it for them directly inside GoHighLevel. Do not just send them a link and hope they book themselves." If hot: "Are you free now? I can give you a quick call in about 5 minutes." If warm: book "the NEXT DAY … The next-day gap acts as a commitment filter." "Never leave the booking loose or tell them to schedule themselves later."
- **Two calls:** the setter runs a 10–15 minute "rapport call" (recorded, eight scripted steps, ending with a commitment check), then books the coach's 30–45 minute Zoom sales call. "The 48 hour rule … Book the sales call Zoom within 48 hours of the rapport call … Anything past 48 hours and the show rate drops by about 15 percent." (The 15% is the SOP's own claim; no data shown.)
- **Price in the DMs is deflected:** "Sure, I totally get why you ask! I actually don't go over pricing until we've had a quick call first. I need to make sure you'd be a good fit and that I can actually help you. If we get to that point, I'll extend you an invitation and we'll talk about what that looks like financially. Sound fair to you?" Also "Think of it like getting your kitchen remodeled. You couldn't get an exact quote over the phone."
- **"Not now" and soft exits:** "Got it, sounds like you mostly just need [whatever applies]. With what you know you probably have what it takes, just keep going. If you ever hit a wall and want professional coaching, let me know." / "Totally understand. Keep using the free resources, and let me know if you ever hit a wall where self-directed isn't cutting it anymore. That's usually when it makes sense to bring someone in."
- **Quality over quantity:** "A good setter books fewer calls that actually close, not more calls that waste everyone's time."
- **Ethics flag in the source:** the setter writes and even speaks as the coach: "The prospect never knows it is not her. No exceptions." On the rapport call, if asked: "I help May with intake, but you'll be talking to her personally on the Zoom!"

**Vegard Mikalsen's version (Grade B, captions opened)** — [Vegard Mikalsen](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lBCNPgxAWOU)
- Outbound (you messaged first): build rapport first, then goals, challenges, how long, "the drive behind it, the reason it's important to them", then "you offer them to help. Basically, are you open for help? … And if they are, pitch them. Pitch them the call." Inbound (they messaged first, for example by sending the keyword): "you can skip this step … You immediately move into the goals."
- "If you can sense that they answer with very long messages, you can move on to the next step immediately." Disqualify people who give "very short" answers.
- When a conversation stalls or pushing would feel pushy: "You send them a long form asset", meaning a longer video or piece that shows expertise, then follow up within 24 hours asking "if they've seen it yet. What they thought about it. What was the biggest takeaway?"
- "You're not here to be agenda-driven. You're not here to like follow an exact script all the way. People are going to know that."

**Giulia Guerrieri, a B2B offer to experts and coaches (Grade B/D, captions opened)** — [Giulia Guerrieri](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=J7XXib-_biU)
- Five steps: the ask (get them to DM a keyword), break the ice, current situation, desired situation, then book the next step.
- Current situation, business questions: "What do you currently sell? Or what's your offer?" and "How do you typically sell, or what does your sales process look like?" Wrong-fit answers ("I want passive income", "low-ticket digital products") get "I just bless them and move on".
- Three buying signals: "they can't do this on their own", "they just want to collapse time", and "they're ready for a proven system".
- Desired situation: "So, Sarah, you know, where would you like to be in the next 6 months if you could wave a magic wand. Tell me revenue level, number of clients, and things like that." Then "That's amazing. And what would that change for you personally?"
- The key question before the invite: "What is holding you back from getting there on your own?" She says the answer is one of three things: faster, a proven road map, or accountability and mentorship. "They've self admitted they have a problem."
- Why she takes them to a call rather than closing in the DMs: price drops by text lead to "the zone of doubt … they start sitting on links, they ghost you". With good DMs, "my team sales calls should literally be like 15 to 20 minutes going over their situation and the pitch".
- If they're not ready, "Just say, 'Hey, this is great. Keep watching my free stuff and I'm going to send you a message in a couple months and check back in.'"

**Ste McGrath, UK (Grade C/D, captions opened)** — [Ste McGrath](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Urrq1K9--yo)
- Four steps (intro, goals, problems, offer help), then offer the call. Acknowledge between every message.
- Goal plus time frame: "Hey, what's the biggest goal that you're working towards with your health and fitness right now?" → "Okay, cool. And what's the time frame to lose that 20 lb?"
- Problem: "In your opinion, what's the biggest thing that's stopping you from achieving those goals right now?" Probe surface answers: "What specifically about your diet do you think is a struggle?"
- Offer help: "Look, based on the goals that you've got and based on the problems that you just told me that you have … this training PDF guide … might help a little bit, but it's not really going to be the thing that helps you lose 20 lb in the next 3 months. However, my coaching program could do that. Have you ever had a coach before?" or "Are you open to getting help?" If no: "Cool. No worries. There's the guide." Then move on.
- Offer the call: "Next step from here is obviously we can book in a proper chat. Dig into your goals a little bit more … so we can come up with a really customized plan … If not, no problems. At least you'll leave with a plan." He claims "usually around 60% of the people will just go, 'Yep. I'm going to need to book a call.'" Then send the link and say: "once you book that, just let me know. I'll double check it on my end and I can confirm it for you."
- Voice notes: "you can either voice note this or, you know, if you want to write it, just keep it a little bit more succinct."

**Mark Coles, UK, on how to word the invite (Grade B, captions opened)** — [Mark Coles](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=inVbcg5ahRs)
- Flow: "opener response question question conversation", using open questions such as "do you have something specific that you're working towards right now".
- Don't propose a sales call; propose help. Verbatim: "I have a suggestion for you rather than you and I going backwards and forwards here why don't we jump on a call I will take a deeper look at exactly what you're doing right now and I will make some suggestions for you". He warns against "why don't we jump on a call and I can tell you what I do and explain more about my program".
- He also says questions like "have you had a coach before … have you followed a program before" signal "that you're about to sell something", so people agree to the call and then don't book. This contradicts Ste McGrath, who uses "Have you ever had a coach before?" as his bridge to the offer.
- Discovery in the DMs should be "what are they currently doing right now where where do they want to get to what have they tried to do so far what do they feel are their current problems right now", not small talk about "their Sport and their kids and their dogs".

**Zidan Akel, UK, on how each message is built (Grade D, captions opened)** — [$30k/mo DM setting framework for online fitness coaches, Zidan Akel, 14 Aug 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rCQ1WIVRwlU)
- Every reply follows "ACA … acknowledge, compliment, ask" or "ARA … acknowledge, relate and ask". This is the same shape as the SOP's RAQ and Hormozi's ACA. Good example: "Nice man. Some solid goals. Always improving. I have similar objectives. Training high intensity twice a week for that. Do you know what could possibly be holding you back from reaching those targets right now?" Bad example: "asking two questions in one message. Not putting the questions as the last thing that the person's going to read."
- Order of the conversation: general goal, specific goal ("What does being healthy look like to you? Do you have a weight goal in mind?"), current situation and what they tried, urgency ("how important is it that you achieve this transformation right now"), then the bridge: "So if we could help you achieve X goal in X time frame to remove X pain points or current situation, would you be down to hear what that would look like?"

**Template version with the price stated up front (Grade C, opened)** — [Coachway](https://coachway.io/downloads/instagram-dm-scripts.txt)
- Three-question qualifier: "Before I suggest anything: what do you want to be different in three months, in one line?" → "Got it. What have you tried in the last 12 months, and what happened?" → "Last one. Coaching with me is [PRICE BAND] a month. Is that a range you are ready to invest in, or should I point you to the free stuff instead? Either answer is fine and I will not chase."
- Invite: "Then a 20-minute call is the right next step. I will tell you how I work and whether we fit; no pressure either way. Here is the link: [BOOKING LINK]. Which day suits?" Hesitant: "No need to book anything now. If you want, I will send you two times for a 20-minute call and you pick one, or neither. Want that?"
- Not now: "Understood, and thanks for telling me straight. I will not chase. If it changes, message me the word BACK and we pick up where we left off."
- Voice notes: "Voice notes to strangers" is on its do-not-send list. "Voice is for people who already replied twice."

### Inferences
- Message count: from the scripts, a warm conversation takes about 6–12 short messages before the invite (opener, 1–3 rapport messages, permission, 4–6 qualifying questions). An inbound keyword lead can be invited after about 3–5. No source gives a measured average.
- Every practitioner uses the same shape for each message: acknowledge what they said (plus a compliment or a short related story), then one question, placed last. The SOP calls it RAQ, Zidan Akel calls it ACA/ARA, and Hormozi's warm-outreach chapter calls it ACA.
- The invite is framed as more help ("I'll take a deeper look and make some suggestions", "you'll leave with a plan"), never as "a call about my programme".
- Two camps on voice notes. The SOP sends a pre-recorded voice note early. Coachway says not until they've replied twice. Both agree voice goes only to people who follow you or have replied, which matches Instagram's rule that message requests are text-only.
- "Book it for them with two specific times" (SOP) and "send the link, which day suits?" (Coachway) are the two invite styles. The SOP's view is that a link left with the prospect loses bookings. Neither gives numbers.

### Gaps
- No source gave measured show or close rates for the SOP's two-call (rapport call, then sales Zoom) structure compared with a single call.

## 4. Follow-up rules for people who go quiet

### Takeaway
Everyone found follows up, but cadences differ: from twice and stop (day 2, day 7) to four quick nudges (days 1, 2, 3, 7) then monthly for ever. The SOP adds non-text nudges (heart their last message, like a post, send a GIF) before writing again. Re-engagement messages usually name the person's own goal. One vendor dataset claims a single follow-up roughly doubles booked calls.

### Cited Findings
- **SOP cadence and verbatim nudges (Grade A, opened)** — [SFL DM Setter SOP](https://sfl-operations.pages.dev/setter-sop):
  - "24 hrs [Heart their last message to send a notification]" → "48 hrs bump: Just bumping this up! Hope all is well. 😊" → "48 hrs pattern interrupt [Send a meme or GIF as a pattern interrupt]" → "Ghost nudge: Hey [Name], you never really struck me as the type to ghost lol is everything alright? Let me know." → "Re-engage: Hey [Name], I take it you're no longer trying to [their goal]?"
  - Same-day ghost recovery: "60 min no reply [Go back and heart/like one of their messages in the chat]"; "120 min no reply [Go to their profile, like a recent post and leave a genuine comment]"; "180 min no reply: Hey, are you still there?"; next day, heart their last message again and send "a simple meme or GIF with no text".
  - Other nudges: "Hey [Name]! I am always losing messages in my inbox, did you still want that info I was going to send over?"; after a guide: "Hey [Name]! Just checking in, did you get a chance to look at that guide? Hoping it was helpful! What's the main thing you're working on right now?"
  - Long-term (2+ weeks): "I actually just came up with a unique solution for your situation around [their challenge]. Would you be open to me sharing it? After hearing it, if you don't think it would be helpful you can always say no! 😊" / "Hey [Name]! Wanted to check in and see how things are going. Any progress on [their goal]?"
  - Rhythm: "space them out (a bump, then a gentle nudge, then a re-engage). Be friendly, never pushy." Warm-nurture leads get re-touched if "not touched in 2 to 4 weeks".
- **Vegard Mikalsen (Grade B, captions opened):** "We do within 1 day, within 2 days, within 3 days, within 7 days, and then we stop. And after that, every month … We always follow up until we disqualify." He follows up monthly with old clients, all past inbound leads, calls that didn't close and people who never booked: "ask them how things are going." He passes on, second-hand, that "a Danish coach … made around 70 to 80% of his revenue from only follow-ups" (hearsay, unverified). — [Vegard Mikalsen](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lBCNPgxAWOU)
- **Coachway (Grade C, opened):** day 2, "No rush on my side. One thing that might help while you think: [ONE SPECIFIC TIP]. Did that make sense for your week?"; day 7, "Last message from me, promise. If the timing is wrong, say so and I will leave it there. If it is right, the link is still here: [BOOKING LINK]. Which is it?" Then stop. Reactivation after 60+ days, once, no link: "It has been a couple of months since we talked about [THEIR GOAL]. No pitch: did you find something that worked, or is it still on the list?" — [Coachway](https://coachway.io/downloads/instagram-dm-scripts.txt)
- **Giulia Guerrieri (Grade B/D):** "value-driven follow-up" tied to what she's running next: "Okay, Fanny, no stress at all. I totally understand now is not the right timing. I'm going to do a webinar next week. Should I send you that link over so you could sign up? It's totally free". After the webinar: "Fanny, you know, what did you think? What's one thing you learned after this webinar?" After a freebie: "Okay, so when are you going to read this? When can I follow up with you?" Leads are tagged in ManyChat, but "you could literally just write people's names down in your notes app". She asserts "People close after the seventh to eighth attempt, not on the first or the second" (no source given). — [Giulia Guerrieri](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=J7XXib-_biU)
- **Alex Cattoni, a one-line follow-up to a mastermind applicant who was accepted but hadn't joined (Grade B):** "Hey name, just checking in. You're ready to join us?" She says that led to "a $10,000 pay in full decision". — [Alex Cattoni, 8 Jul 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a4QDpAYEh1Q)
- **Alex Cattoni (Grade B):** follows up only with people who commented the keyword, opened the DM and clicked the link but didn't buy. She reports a "6.25% conversion rate from all the people that we followed up with and had a conversation with" for one launch. — [Alex Cattoni](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SiDbtfx_n5E)
- **Vendor data (Grade C):** an AI-setter vendor's analysis of "828K" Instagram and WhatsApp DM conversations claims a single follow-up more than doubled booked calls (+106% among engaged leads). It also claims 53% of conversations never get past 2 messages, and conversations with 21+ messages have a 29% booked-call rate. (Per search summary; self-published.) — [SetSmart](https://setsmart.io/ebook); [SetSmart, Instagram sales funnel](https://setsmart.io/blog/instagram-sales-funnel)

### Inferences
- A sensible default that fits the sources: nudge at about 24–48 hours, again at day 3–7, then move to nurture with a monthly check-in that names their goal. The SOP's emoji, GIF and "never struck me as the type to ghost" lines suit consumer fitness. For business owners, the plainer Coachway style ("Last message from me … which is it?") is a safer tone.

### Gaps
- No independent data on the best number or spacing of nudges on Instagram.

## 5. How content is planned to create DM conversations, and how big a following you need

### Takeaway
Content is built to make people raise a hand in a way that can be followed up: "comment WORD for the guide" posts (each post tied to a different guide, so the keyword tells you the problem), story question boxes and polls ("Is X a now or later thing for you?"), and "2-step" posts that ask people to comment if they want help. Practitioners report this working at 1,500–5,000 followers. Their point is that a small, engaged following plus a follow-up system beats a big following with none. No source gives a minimum follower count.

### Cited Findings
- **Story question box, Maria Wendt (Grade D, course pitch, Sept 2023; captions opened):** post a story with a question box: "is getting clients a now or later thing for you", with text underneath saying "type now". People who answer "now" are "white hot leads … you're gonna reply to them you're gonna tell them about what it looks like to work with you how much you charge". She claims "typically about one in five is going to turn into a client" and that "Instagram shows your stories to more followers when you use a poll or question box" (both unverified; the video sells a $24 course bundle). — [Use THIS Instagram DM Script to Land a Client This Week!, Maria Wendt](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8PbaghW-8eI)
- **Comment-a-keyword, Alex Cattoni (Grade B, ManyChat-sponsored; captions opened):** "we used ManyChat to get people to comment season 6 if they were interested in learning more about this 8week live coaching program … we got hundreds of people raising their hands", then ManyChat "instantly deliver[ed] the link to the enrollment page in their DMs". Her point: "here's the mistake that 99% of people and marketers make. This is where they stop." The human follow-up is the step that sells: "Automation gets attention. Personalization gets the sale." People who click the link have consented to more messages, which lets her follow up within Meta's rules. She says a mastermind member "with just over 2,000 Instagram followers … just made $100,000 in a few days with the same strategy" (unverified). — [Alex Cattoni](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SiDbtfx_n5E)
- **Keywords everywhere, Giulia Guerrieri (Grade B/D):** a DM keyword in the bio ("DM me ready if you're ready to start your health journey"); "a call to action in all of your captions … This is free real estate"; keywords kept short ("the best calls to action are only three letters … just comment the number seven"; "Do not be putting random emojis"). A story example: "if you want to earn cash by Christmas … and you want to scale to 15 to 20,000 dollars in the next couple months per month, comment the word cash or reply the keyword cash". She says a keyword comment doesn't mean someone is ready to pay ("they think all of a sudden they want to pay you 5,000 dollars, which is not true"). She claims her Instagram gets "upwards of 7,000 leads into my DMs on a monthly basis" (unverified). — [Giulia Guerrieri](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=J7XXib-_biU)
- **Comment-a-keyword to test an offer and build a waitlist, Alex Cattoni (Grade B, sponsored):** a carousel saying "I'm thinking about hosting a masterclass next month … Does this interest you? Comment retainer below" got "almost 200 comments" and "over 80 email addresses", collected inside the DM with no landing page. The last automated message asks "When it comes to retainer work, what is your biggest doubt or question? Your response will be sent to Alex and the team." For her $10,000 mastermind, the year-round caption call to action reads: "Rain is my mastermind for experienced marketers and business owners. Applications open twice a year. Comment Rain below to get on the waitlist." She says this works "even if you have an audience of, say, under 200 followers" (unverified). — [Alex Cattoni, 8 Jul 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a4QDpAYEh1Q)
- **The SOP's content inputs (Grade A):** keyword-to-guide posts, each giving away a different guide; polls and story votes; "2-step post or direct offer post"; a free Facebook group; and the "REPS" routine of adding 5–10 ideal-client Facebook friends a day and removing 5–10 who aren't. It claims "roughly 1,800 to 3,600 new potential leads per year". In Facebook groups the setter comments publicly first, then DMs ("The public comment makes the DM feel less cold"), and limits it to "5 to 10 people per day from groups". — [SFL DM Setter SOP](https://sfl-operations.pages.dev/setter-sop)
- **Small following, Vegard Mikalsen (Grade B):** before he had a system: "1,500 followers, but got 500 to 1,000 views per story. I average five story replies per day, and got 100 likes on all my videos, but it was the wrong engagement … So, I got zero clients." Later: "I had 5,000 followers when I hit more than 60 clients." Daily target: "start at least 50 conversations a day. If you have very few followers right now, that's okay. Start 25." — [Vegard Mikalsen](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lBCNPgxAWOU)
- **Polls mechanics (Grade C):** voters trigger no notification. Add a "Reply X" text line so a reply opens the DM. (Per search summary.) — [Chatfuel](https://chatfuel.com/blog/instagram-polls-guide)
- **Vendor illustrative model, labelled as not measured (Grade C):** a reel-to-call model assumes 1% of viewers comment, 75% of booked calls show and 20% close. The page says these are "an illustrative model … not measured or guaranteed outcomes". (Per search summary.) — [DM Champ, reel-to-booked-call funnel](https://dmchamp.com/resources/reel-to-booked-call-funnel/)

### Inferences
- Content and DMs are one system. Each post exists to create a reason to DM (a keyword, a vote, a "type now"). The DM then refers back to that post. A coach doesn't need a big following, but needs a steady flow of hand-raises. 1,500–5,000 engaged followers in a tight niche is the range practitioners describe.
- For BCA: "comment SCORE and I'll send you the BOSS Score" fits this pattern exactly. The keyword tells the coach the owner's interest, and the score result gives the setter a natural first question.

### Gaps
- No source measured whether story polls or question boxes really get wider reach (Maria Wendt's claim).
- No source gives a minimum follower count. The examples are self-reported.

## 6. Daily routine, volume and setter pay

### Takeaway
The clearest routine found: three focused blocks a day spread across the prospects' waking hours, 25–35 "touches" per block, hottest leads first, then a written end-of-day report. Coaches doing it themselves aim to start 25–50 new conversations a day. Setters are paid a small base plus about 3–5% of cash collected, or a bonus per closed call ($50 in the SOP).

### Cited Findings
- **SOP routine (Grade A, opened)** — [SFL DM Setter SOP](https://sfl-operations.pages.dev/setter-sop):
  - "In the beginning you work around 3 to 4 hours a day … Aim for 25 to 35 touches per block." Three blocks set to the prospects' time zone (US): about 10–11am, 2–3pm and 6pm Pacific. The setter is in the Philippines. "The US evening block is the most important one because that is when the most prospects are online after work. Never skip the evening block."
  - Morning: reply to comments, delete and block trolls, clear unread DMs, follow up guide opt-ins, revive stalled chats. Midday: replies, new comments, new opt-ins and new followers, book qualified people. End of day: final pass, run rapport calls, log bookings, send the EOD report.
  - EOD report fields (sent on WhatsApp daily): "Minutes Worked; Conversations Started; Rapport Calls; Rapport Calls Pitched; Sales Calls Booked From Rapport Call; Sales Calls Booked Without Rapport Call; Closed Sets", plus self-rating questions ("Am I going to hit projections this week? How do I know? If not, what do I need?"). Weekly totals on Mondays: "Conversations / Follow-ups / Calls pitched / Calls booked / Closes".
  - Pay: setters "earn a bonus of $50 per qualified call that closes" (no base pay stated on the page).
- **Coach doing it alone (Grade B):** Vegard Mikalsen: 50 new conversations a day (25 with a small following), plus daily follow-ups. — [Vegard Mikalsen](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lBCNPgxAWOU)
- **Mark Coles's daily ratios (Grade B):** "from level one to level two I would expect a 30% reply rate so if you send 100 messages you get 30 people replying … you would expect 2 to 3% that's two to three booked calls from 100 messages". Daily target: "the three to four proposals on a day-to-day basis to get one to two book calls", or "100 DMS … four to five proposals every day to one to two calls booked every single day". "How many DMS you send is Vanity how many proposals and book calls you make is sanity." "If you're proposing five or six calls … and you're getting no book calls it basically means you're proposing to the wrong people." — [Mark Coles](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=inVbcg5ahRs)
- **Gym test (Grade A, own test):** Two-Brain's teams "initiated 40 chats per day with qualified leads". (Opened.) — [Chris Cooper / Two-Brain Business, "Sell by Chat for Gyms: Does It Work?", 21 Jul 2021](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/21538260-sell-by-chat-for-gyms-does-it-work)
- **Setter pay from job posts (market samples, per search summary):** "$650 USD per month. Commission: 3-5% of cash collected from every client you book who enrolls"; "Base: 200–300 USD/month … Commission: 3–5% of cash collected"; "$2.4k base + 5-7% cash collected on a $6-8k offer"; an outlier at 10%; one at "$15/hour + 1–2% commission … after KPI targets are met". One company pays setter commission on net cash after financing fees, plus a "$100 weekly bonus" if 80%+ of booked calls attend. — [OnlineJobs.ph, high-ticket DM setter](https://tp.onlinejobs.ph/jobseekers/job/High-Ticket-Coaching-Dm-Setter-1649832); [OnlineJobs.ph, men's coaching brand](https://mail.onlinejobs.ph/jobseekers/job/A-Player-DM-Setter-For-Mens-Coaching-Brand-Performance-Based-Long-Term-1672201); [Skool, Hiring phone setters](https://www.skool.com/dmsales/hiring-phone-setters-two-of-the-biggest-personal-brands-in-coaching); [Coda, Speakers Institute commission policy](https://coda.io/@checkmateclosers/si-sales-guide/speakers-institute-sales-commission-bonus-policy-sheet-51)
- **Setter KPIs in job posts (per search summary):** "2-6 calls booked a day" with "50–100 DMs/day"; OTE (expected total pay) "1,500$ - 3,000$ per month". — [Skool, Hiring A++ DM setters](https://www.skool.com/sales-community-for-closers-1459/hiring-a-dm-setters-ote-3k-per-month?p=dd695b53); [Skool, Hiring high-ticket DM setter, fitness coaching](https://www.skool.com/sales-community-for-closers-1459/hiring-high-ticket-dm-setter-fitness-coaching)
- **A team plan (unattributed Google Doc, per search summary):** "500 New Convos/wk, 1,000 Follow-Up Convos/wk, 30 Sets/wk". — [Google Doc](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1vLkKC_JL3e8ZUcFxv0Mi28lAhgJ6Xibz0d9zwR1_wt4/export?format=pdf)
- **Vendor planning figures (Grade C, per search summary):** 30–60 outreach DMs a day, 8–15% reply rate, 20–40% of replies book, so roughly 2–6% of outreach becomes a booked call; 20–40% of booked calls no-show. — [DM Tracker, managing appointment setters](https://dmtracker.ai/how-to-manage-appointment-setters)

### Inferences
- A part-time setter (3–4 hours, about 75–100 touches a day) is the realistic starting unit. "Touches" include replies and follow-ups, not just new openers.
- Pay on closed cash (3–5%) or per closed call lines the setter up with the SOP's "fewer calls that actually close" rule. Pay per booked call would push setters to book unqualified calls.

### Gaps
- I found no independent survey of setter pay. Figures are from job ads.

## 7. Results: what is evidence and what is a course claim

### Takeaway
There are few real numbers. The only measured test found (Two-Brain, gyms) got about a 2% close rate from chats started: 6 clients in 3 months at 40 chats a day. Self-reported coach figures are better: Vegard Mikalsen's 6–8 calls booked and 3–4 clients a week at $300/month, and Mark Coles's expected 30% reply rate and 2–3 booked calls per 100 messages. Most other figures (one in five become clients, 60% agree to a call, 44.7% of qualified leads book) are course or vendor claims.

### Cited Findings
**Measured or own-test (Grade A)**
- Two-Brain's sell-by-chat test: "Six new clients over three months (two per month) for a close rate of around 2 percent" at 40 chats a day. They don't show where the cross-industry figures come from. Recommendation: "We only recommend sell by chat for gym owners who have extra time to spend on social media"; "No" for founder-stage gyms. - Agency client results: "almost a million dollars in client revenue" in about 8 months, and named clients going from "2K a month to 15K a month", "0 to 12K a month", and "John Scott with just 1,300 followers went from 0K a month to 9K a month in just 30 days". This is a sales pitch for the agency. — [Zidan Akel](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rCQ1WIVRwlU)
- Vendor claims include a 2.4x rise in discovery calls and show-up rate going from 65% to 82% ([InstantDM](https://instantdm.com/instagram-dm-automation/coaches-consultants)), "44.7% of qualified leads" booking ([SetSmart](https://setsmart.io/ebook)), and an AI setter lifting show-up from "50 to 55%" to "60 to 70%" with three reminders ([DM Champ fitness coach page](https://dmchamp.com/resources/fitness-coach-funnel/)). All are self-published and unverified (per search summary).
- The SOP's own claim: booking the sales call more than 48 hours after the rapport call cuts show rate "by about 15 percent" (no data shown). — [SFL DM Setter SOP](https://sfl-operations.pages.dev/setter-sop)

### Inferences
- The figures disagree by a factor of five. Mark Coles expects 2–3 booked calls per 100 messages. Two-Brain says 12–15% of chats book a consultation when that's the goal. They may count different things: all messages sent vs conversations where the person replied. Warm and inbound conversations convert far better than proactive messages to new followers.
- Planning numbers, taking the conservative end: about 2–3 booked calls per 100 proactive warm messages (Mark Coles), rising to about 10–15 per 100 when the person raised a hand first (Two-Brain's consult figure). About 2–5% of chats started become clients (Two-Brain). A coach sending 25 warm messages a day (about 500 a month) might expect roughly 10–25 calls a month, and fewer if the audience is small and niche. This is my blend of the sources, not a measured benchmark.

### Gaps
- No public, independently checked funnel (conversations, calls, shows, closes) for warm Instagram outbound by a coach.
- Reddit was not reachable from this environment (both the search tool and direct fetches were blocked), so Reddit practitioner threads were not checked.

## 8. Does it transfer to selling to business owners?

### Takeaway
The method transfers. The volume and tone do not. I found no public example with numbers of a coach selling to owners of traditional businesses (gym owners, food brand founders) through Instagram warm outbound. The nearest are coaches who sell to other coaches and experts, who are small business owners: Giulia Guerrieri, Mark Coles, Ste McGrath, and Alex Cattoni's mastermind "for experienced marketers and business owners". They run the same playbook with business qualifying questions. One of them says B2B is easier to open, because the profile already tells you whether someone is a prospect. What does carry across: content that makes owners raise a hand, DMs that refer to the exact post, one question at a time, and a two-step call. What changes: a niche B2B audience produces far fewer signals a day; qualifying questions are about the business, not the body; consumer-style texting (lowercase, "lol", GIFs, "never struck me as the type to ghost") would hurt a coach's standing with a £1M+ owner; and owners are more likely to notice, and object to, a setter posing as the coach.

### Cited Findings
- No example in traditional sectors found: searches for coaches selling to gym owners or to food-brand founders through Instagram DMs returned only consumer fitness material and vendor pages. (My searches; see the Gaps for this section.)
- **B2B is easier to open, per Ste McGrath (UK, sells to personal trainers):** "for me, I coach fitness coaches, which means that I can just go on someone's profile, and if it says they're a fitness coach, chances are they're going to be responsive to my message. As a personal trainer, it's really [expletive] weird for you to just like randomly message people saying, 'Hey, you looking to lose weight?'" (Captions opened.) — [Ste McGrath](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Urrq1K9--yo)
- **A B2B version of the same conversation, Giulia Guerrieri (sells to experts and coaches):** the same stages, but the questions are about the business: "What do you currently sell? Or what's your offer?"; "what does your sales process look like?"; "where would you like to be in the next 6 months … Tell me revenue level, number of clients"; then "What is holding you back from getting there on your own?" Her "authority opener" is based on following the prospect's own content: "I've been following your content for a bit and I love what you're doing with animals. Quick question, how long have you been a veterinarian for and do you sell any sort of online courses?" On B2B buyers: "I have a B2B offer, so of course these people are a little bit more business-oriented, but every niche is like this." A lead said to her: "How did you even remember me? I love this follow-up process." (Captions opened.) — [Giulia Guerrieri](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=J7XXib-_biU)
- **Comment-a-keyword for a high-ticket offer to business owners, Alex Cattoni:** "Rain is my mastermind for experienced marketers and business owners … Comment Rain below to get on the waitlist", followed by personal one-line DMs. (Captions opened.) — [Alex Cattoni, 8 Jul 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a4QDpAYEh1Q)
- **Mark Coles (UK, sells to online coaches):** the invite is framed as a review of what they're doing: "I will take a deeper look at exactly what you're doing right now and I will make some suggestions for you". His sales calls aim to help first; he says "90% of the people that come on a call with our team would thank our team for how valuable they've been on the call" (his claim). — [Mark Coles](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=inVbcg5ahRs)
- Gym owners do run DMs themselves (to sell memberships), so they are used to doing business in Instagram DMs. Two-Brain: almost 15% of gym owners don't contact their leads at all, and some avoid DMs because "they're scared of sounding like a salesperson". (Per search summary.) — [Two-Brain Business, gym DM sales](https://twobrainbusiness.com/gym-dm-sales/)
- Two-Brain only recommends chat selling to gym owners who have time and lead flow: "Tinker phase (over $100,000/year, gym runs itself): Yes," and only with a lead system producing "at least 30 leads per day". (Opened.) — [Two-Brain Business](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/21538260-sell-by-chat-for-gyms-does-it-work)
- The SOP's Facebook group method lists "Busy professional groups (nurses, teachers, managers, business owners)" as hunting grounds and says to avoid obvious niche groups that are "too saturated with coaches and people get spammed". — [SFL DM Setter SOP](https://sfl-operations.pages.dev/setter-sop)
- A B2B sales blog suggests a much smaller weekly volume for warm Instagram B2B: identify "5 to 15 high-signal followers a week" and run three messages (opener, value-only, soft call invite). Its claimed "40 to 70%" reply rate for warm followers is unsourced. (Per search summary; vendor.) — [Chrysales, Instagram outreach sequence for B2B](https://www.chrysales.com/post/instagram-outreach-sequence-converts-clients)
- UK flag (from the earlier notes): the ICO treats private social media messages as electronic mail under PECR. Sole traders and partnerships count as individual subscribers, so cold marketing DMs to them need consent; limited companies are treated differently. — [ICO, business-to-business marketing](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/)

### Inferences
- **The opener gets easier and the content gets harder.** With owners, the profile tells you whether they're a prospect (Ste McGrath's point). That makes Giulia's "authority opener" possible: a specific comment on their business content, then a question about their business. The hard part is getting owners to follow and engage in the first place. Content has to speak to owners' problems (pricing, team, cash, owner hours), not general motivation.
- **The invite maps onto BCA's offer.** Mark Coles's "I'll take a deeper look at exactly what you're doing and make some suggestions" is the same promise as a BOSS Score review or Constraint Call.
- **Volume:** a coach posting for UK gym owners or food founders might get a handful of relevant new followers and commenters a week, not the dozens a day consumer coaches describe. Expect "5–15 warm owner conversations a week" (Chrysales' range), not "50 a day" (Vegard). Each conversation is worth far more.
- **Signals keep their order, and matter more.** An owner who comments a keyword on a post about pricing or hiring has told you their constraint. That's the B2B version of the SOP's "different posts deliver different guides". A BOSS Score keyword (comment SCORE) is the obvious BCA fit.
- **Qualifying gates for owners:** the SOP's ten gates map onto challenge (constraint), what they tried, how long, goal (a number, such as profit or hours), why now, role (owner or not), size (revenue, team), location (UK), urgency, and willingness to invest. "Job / income signal" becomes "revenue and team size". Owners will answer these in DMs if asked one at a time with permission, but likely in fewer, longer messages.
- **Tone:** keep the structure (name the signal, one question, permission, RAQ) and drop the consumer style. Plain sentences, no emoji or GIF nudges, no "lol". The Coachway-style closing nudge ("Last message from me … which is it?") fits owners better than "you never struck me as the type to ghost".
- **Setter posing as the coach:** the SOP has the setter pose as the coach, including on a phone call. For BCA coaches selling £-thousands engagements to owners, that's a trust and reputation risk if found out. A safer pattern is a named assistant ("I help [coach] with intake"), or the coach sending the call invite personally. I have not checked whether the practice raises consumer-protection issues in the UK.
- **Where it fits best:** owners of consumer-facing businesses who run their own Instagram (gyms, studios, food and drink brands, salons) are the B2B segment where this transfers best. For £1M+ owners in less visual sectors, LinkedIn is likely the better primary channel, with Instagram as a secondary warm channel. (Judgment, not sourced.)

### Gaps
- No case study found of a coach or consultant selling to UK owners of traditional businesses (gyms, food brands, trades, £1M+ firms) through Instagram warm DMs with reported numbers. The coach-to-coach examples above have no funnel numbers specific to B2B.
- No source on how owners (as opposed to consumers) respond to new-follower DMs or story polls.
