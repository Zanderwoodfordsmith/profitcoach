# Non-LinkedIn prospecting channels and data sources for UK business coaches (UK law and platform rules), as of 21 Nov 2026

Scope: how UK coaches can build lists of owner-led businesses (mostly £1M+ turnover, some small local firms such as gyms, salons and trades) and contact them without LinkedIn Sales Navigator or Instagram/Facebook DMs. Marker **[2025-26 change]** flags anything that changed in 2025 or 2026. "Vendor-reported" means the number comes from a company selling the tool or service.

## 1. List building and data sources

### Takeaway
Companies House (free) is the best base list for £1M+ firms: filter by SIC code, status, age and place through the API, then drop micro-entity and dormant filers as a rough "under £1M" cut. It can't filter by turnover, because most small companies still file accounts without a profit and loss account, and the 2028 plan to make them file one was paused in November 2026. Google Maps scraping (Apify, Outscraper) is the cheap, fast route for local businesses (gyms, salons, trades), at roughly $1.50 to $14 per 1,000 records. Owner emails and mobile numbers then come from enrichment tools (Clay waterfalls, Prospeo, Findymail) or paid UK databases (Cognism, Lusha, Apollo, Market Location). Their accuracy figures are almost all vendor-reported.

### Cited Findings

**Companies House (free API and bulk data)**
- The advanced search endpoint (`GET /advanced-search/companies`) filters by company name (includes/excludes), company_status, company_type, company_subtype, incorporated_from/to, dissolved_from/to, location and sic_codes. It returns up to 5,000 results per request (`size` 1 to 5000) and pages with `start_index`. It has **no filter for accounts type, turnover or employee count**. — [Companies House API spec: Advanced company search](https://developer-specs.company-information.service.gov.uk/companies-house-public-data-api/reference/search/advanced-company-search)
- Default rate limit: 600 requests per 5 minutes. Going over returns HTTP 429 until the window resets, and higher limits are available on request. — [Companies House developer guidelines](https://developer.company-information.service.gov.uk/developer-guidelines). One developer reported their limit doubled to 1,200 per 5 minutes after asking (anecdotal). — [Companies House forum](https://forum.companieshouse.gov.uk/t/companies-house-api-limits/8626)
- The company profile's last-accounts `type` takes these values: full, small, medium, group, dormant, interim, initial, total-exemption-full, total-exemption-small, partial-exemption, audit-exemption-subsidiary, filing-exemption-subsidiary, **micro-entity**, no-accounts-type-available, audited-abridged, unaudited-abridged. — [Companies House api-enumerations, constants.yml](https://github.com/companieshouse/api-enumerations/blob/master/constants.yml)
- Free bulk CSVs ("free company data product") hold basic company data for the whole register. — [GOV.UK: Companies House data products](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/companies-house-data-products). Known quirk: forum users report that `Accounts.AccountCategory` is **blank for micro-entity filers** in the monthly CSV, so "blank" does not mean "unknown". — [Companies House forum](https://forum.companieshouse.gov.uk/t/companies-with-micro-accounts-have-empty-accountcategory-field-in-monthly-csv-dump/1308)
- Companies House doesn't classify companies by size. Companies classify themselves and file the matching accounts. — [Companies House forum](https://forum.companieshouse.gov.uk/t/bulk-file-missing-account-types/1262)
- **[2025-26 change] Size thresholds rose for financial years starting on or after 6 April 2025.** Micro-entity: turnover up to £1M (was £632k), balance sheet up to £500k, 10 or fewer staff. Small: turnover up to £15M (was £10.2M), balance sheet up to £7.5M, 50 or fewer staff. A company is in a category if it meets 2 of the 3 tests. About 113,000 companies move from small to micro. — [ICAEW: thresholds to increase from April 2025](https://www.icaew.com/insights/viewpoints-on-the-news/2024/dec-2024/uk-company-size-thresholds-to-increase-from-april-2025); [ICAEW: size of a company](https://www.icaew.com/technical/financial-reporting/uk-regulation-for-company-accounts/size-of-a-company)
- **[2025-26 change] Turnover stays mostly invisible.** The plan for small and micro companies to file a profit and loss account moved from April 2027 to April 2028 in June 2026. Then in November 2026 the government **paused** P&L filing while keeping software-only filing. — [GOV.UK: accounts filing changes from April 2028](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/companies-house-to-bring-in-changes-to-accounts-filing-from-april-2028); [ICAEW, Nov 2026: government pauses P&L filing](https://www.icaew.com/insights/viewpoints-on-the-news/2026/nov-2026/uk-government-pauses-profit-and-loss-filing-but-sticks-with-software-only-filing). ACCA reads the pause as meaning companies can keep filing abridged and filleted accounts (no P&L) from April 2028. The sources disagree on whether abridged accounts are still being removed. — [ACCA In Practice, Oct 2026](https://www.accaglobal.com/gb/en/technical-activities/uk-tech/in-practice-ezine-archive/In-Practice-archive-2026/October/Companies-House-update-to-accounts-filing.html)
- Many paid scrapers on Apify wrap Companies House data (new companies by SIC and date, officers, accounts and balance-sheet figures parsed from iXBRL). — e.g. [Apify: UK Company Accounts (balance sheet data)](https://apify.com/j0401/uk-company-accounts.md); [Apify: Companies House scraper with directors and accounts](https://apify.com/clearpath/companies-house-scraper.md)

### Inferences
- **Practical £1M+ filter on Companies House:** pull by SIC code, active status and incorporation date (for example 5+ years old) through the API, then fetch each profile's last accounts type and drop `micro-entity`, `dormant` and companies with no accounts. A micro-entity filer usually turns over under £1M (under £632k for periods before April 2025). This is only a filter: a firm turning over £1.2M with 10 or fewer staff and a small balance sheet can still file as micro (2 of 3 tests). Any other type (small, total-exemption, unaudited-abridged) could be anywhere from £0 to £15M. To narrow the range, add other signals: headcount (from vendors or job ads), `full`/`medium`/`group` accounts, balance-sheet size from iXBRL, or number of Google reviews and locations.
- Directors' names from Companies House give the owner's name for free, which is the field Google Maps lacks. A useful pipeline: Companies House (company, SIC, directors) → find the website → enrich the email/mobile (Clay/Prospeo/Findymail) → verify. For local businesses, go the other way: Google Maps (name, phone, website, reviews) → Companies House match (Ltd or not, directors) → enrich.
- Matching a Google Maps record to Companies House also tells you whether the business is a **company (corporate subscriber)** or a **sole trader/partnership (individual subscriber)**, which decides whether you may cold email, text or WhatsApp them at all (see section 4). Many gyms, salons and trades are sole traders.
- At roughly $2 to $14 per 1,000 enriched local records (vendor list prices), list data isn't the cost that limits a coach. Verification, compliance screening and the channel itself are.

### Gaps
- Endole, Kaspr, Seamless and Data Axle UK pricing and UK coverage: I found no primary or independent source in this pass.
- Treatwell's, ClassPass's and trade associations' terms on scraping or reusing listings: not found.
- Google Maps/Google Business Profile terms on scraping: not checked against Google's own terms (Apify only notes that they "still apply").
- No independent UK accuracy test of any data vendor (all accuracy figures are vendor-reported).
- What share of UK £1M+ SMEs file each accounts type: not found.

## 2. Channels: how each works, cost, response rates, best fit

### Takeaway
Cold email is still the cheapest way to reach many prospects (about 1 to 3.4% replies on average, top senders 5 to 10%+), but since Nov 2025 Gmail rejects mail that fails authentication, and Outlook.com has done the same since May 2025. Cold calling connects on about 5 to 10% of dials in vendor data (Cognism: 8% UK conversation rate), and it legally requires TPS and CTPS screening. Cold WhatsApp breaks WhatsApp's own policy (it needs the number given to you plus opt-in) even where UK law would allow it. Direct mail is outside PECR: UK cold mail averages about 0.9 to 1.0% response (JICMAIL) at about £0.91 to £1.80 postage per letter. Referral partners (accountants), networking (BNI about £700 to £800/yr) and lead ads (UK Facebook CPL roughly £20 to £40) work alongside these but have thin hard data.

### Cited Findings

**Earlier repo research (still relevant, summarised from `/Users/zander/Coding/profit-coach-app/docs/cold-email-research/README.md`)**
- From the repo's 22 Oct 2026 synthesis of Lead Gen Jay, Nick Saraev, Alex Berman and Hormozi videos: separate lookalike sending domains (never the main domain), mailboxes 30+ days old and warmed, 20 to 25 emails/day per mailbox, plain-text email 1 with no links or tracking, 2 to 3 emails per sequence, judge on positive replies after 500 to 1,000 sends per variant. Benchmarks: 1 to 2% replies and about 1% positive is "good and scalable", over 5% excellent, under 1% usually means spam placement. Bought lists are 70 to 80% valid. Domains last 3 to 6 months. The UK requires sender ID and an opt-out in every email ("Reply 'no' and I won't email again"). Sole traders need consent. — [Repo: docs/cold-email-research/README.md](/Users/zander/Coding/profit-coach-app/docs/cold-email-research/README.md)

**Cold email: deliverability rules [2025-26 change]**
- Google: a "bulk sender" sends close to 5,000+ messages to personal Gmail accounts in 24 hours. Mail from one primary domain (including subdomains) counts together, and bulk status never expires. — [Google: Email sender guidelines FAQ](https://support.google.com/a/answer/14229414?hl=en-GB)
- Bulk senders need SPF and DKIM, DMARC alignment, TLS, valid forward/reverse DNS, a user-reported spam rate under 0.1% (never 0.3%+), and RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe headers on marketing mail (a link in the body or a mailto doesn't count). Unsubscribes should be honoured within 48 hours. — [Google: Email sender guidelines FAQ](https://support.google.com/a/answer/14229414?hl=en-GB)
- "Starting in November 2025, Gmail is ramping up its enforcement on non-compliant traffic." Non-compliant mail can get temporary or permanent rejections, or go to spam. The guidelines **don't apply to messages sent to Google Workspace accounts**, only to personal Gmail. — [Google: Email sender guidelines FAQ](https://support.google.com/a/answer/14229414?hl=en-GB)
- Microsoft: from 5 May 2025, domains sending over 5,000 emails/day to consumer Outlook.com/Hotmail/Live addresses must pass SPF, DKIM and DMARC. Non-compliant mail goes to junk, then is rejected with `550 5.7.515`. Safe-sender lists are not honoured for this. — [Microsoft Tech Community: Outlook's new requirements for high-volume senders](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/microsoftdefenderforoffice365blog/strengthening-email-ecosystem-outlook%E2%80%99s-new-requirements-for-high%E2%80%90volume-senders/4399730); [Microsoft: fix NDR 550 5.7.515](https://support.microsoft.com/en-gb/outlook/fix-ndr-error-550-5-7-515-in-outlook-com). Vendor blogs disagree on when hard rejections began (Nov 2025 vs Apr 2026). — [Red Sift: bulk sender requirements](https://redsift.com/guides/bulk-email-sender-requirements)

**Cold email: reply rates**

**Cold calling**
- Belkins (vendor, 175,000+ dials in 2025): 9.9% connect per dial, 58% of connects became conversations, 4.6% of conversations booked a meeting (about 1 meeting per 370 dials). — [Belkins: B2B cold calling statistics 2026](https://belkins.io/blog/cold-calling-benchmarks)
- Cognism's 2025 State of Cold Calling (vendor, 204,000+ calls): 8% "success" (conversation) rate in the UK, against 6% in Europe and the US. — [Cognism: Cold Calling Report 2025](https://www.cognism.com/cold-calling-report-2025?no_head=1)
- Other vendor targets: 8 to 15% connect on verified direct dials, about 40 to 60 dials per booked meeting. — [Tomba: B2B cold calling conversion rate 2026](https://tomba.io/blog/b2b-cold-calling-conversion-rate); dial-to-meeting 2 to 3% for pure cold calling. — [Apollo: average conversion rate for cold prospecting](https://www.apollo.io/insights/whats-the-average-conversion-rate-for-cold-prospecting). These conflict with Belkins's 1 in 370.

**WhatsApp**

### Inferences
- **For £1M+ owner-led firms:** cold email to Ltd companies (legal under PECR with ID and opt-out) plus phone calls to TPS/CTPS-screened numbers plus a letter is the strongest stack that is also low-risk. Letters stand out because few competitors send them and PECR doesn't cover post. At about £1 to £3 a letter (postage, print or robot handwriting), a 1% cold response means about £100 to £300 per response.
- **For small local businesses (gyms, salons, trades):** many are sole traders or partnerships, so cold email, SMS and WhatsApp generally need consent. The clean channels are post, screened phone calls (sole traders sit on the TPS, not the CTPS), in-person visits, local networking and lead ads. Check legal form on Companies House before emailing.
- **WhatsApp:** cold first-touch WhatsApp is against WhatsApp's own policy even where PECR would allow it (a company-owned number). That risks the number being banned more than an ICO fine. Use it only after the prospect has given their number and agreed (for example "Can I WhatsApp you the summary?" on a call).
- Most UK SMB owners use Google Workspace or Microsoft 365, which aren't covered by the Gmail/Outlook.com consumer bulk rules. But many trades and salons use personal @gmail/@hotmail addresses, where those rules apply. Coaches sending well under 5,000/day aren't "bulk senders", but authentication (SPF/DKIM/DMARC) is now table stakes either way.
- Reply-rate figures vary about 5x between vendors (Smartlead median 0.74% vs Instantly average 3.43%). Plan with the earlier repo's "1 to 2% replies, about 1% positive" and test.

## 3. Multichannel sequences and orchestration tools

### Takeaway
Modern outbound tools run email + LinkedIn + call (+ sometimes SMS/WhatsApp) steps in one sequence, with conditional branches and one shared inbox. lemlist (Multichannel Expert plan) and Reply.io have WhatsApp steps. Reply.io also has SMS. La Growth Machine covers LinkedIn, email, X, voice and calls. Instantly and Smartlead are email-first and add other channels through integrations. None of the tools found offers native Instagram DM or Facebook Messenger steps.

### Cited Findings
- **lemlist:** WhatsApp steps can be added "just like an email, LinkedIn, or call" step, after connecting a WhatsApp account in Sending Settings. Replies across email, LinkedIn, WhatsApp and calls sit in one inbox. — [lemlist help: use WhatsApp in lemlist campaigns](https://help.lemlist.com/en/articles/11623498-use-whatsapp-in-lemlist-campaigns); [lemlist: WhatsApp prospecting](https://www.lemlist.com/whatsapp-prospecting)

### Inferences
- For UK coaches, a legally clean "beyond LinkedIn" sequence could look like this. Day 0: letter or handwritten note (no PECR). Day 3: email to a Ltd-company address with ID and opt-out. Day 5: call to a TPS/CTPS-screened number. Day 8: second email. WhatsApp only after a reply or call where they agree to it. lemlist or Reply.io can run the email/call/task steps, and the letter can go in as a manual task or through a mail API.
- Because WhatsApp's policy requires opt-in, automated cold WhatsApp steps in these tools are a policy risk for the coach's number whatever the tool allows.

## 4. UK law and platform rules

### Takeaway
UK B2B outreach depends on who the **subscriber** is. Under PECR, companies, LLPs and Scottish partnerships (corporate subscribers) can get cold email, texts and DMs as long as the sender is identified and an opt-out is offered. Sole traders and other partnerships count as individuals and need consent (or the soft opt-in). Live calls need screening against both the TPS and the CTPS. Post is outside PECR. UK GDPR always applies to named people (legitimate interests plus an absolute right to object). **[2025-26 change]** The Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 raised PECR fines from £500k to up to £17.5M or 4% of turnover (in force 5 Feb 2026), counts unconnected calls and messages as breaches, and names direct marketing as an example of a legitimate interest. WhatsApp's own policy is stricter than UK law (opt-in for everyone).

### Cited Findings

**PECR: corporate vs individual subscribers (email, SMS, DMs)**
- "The PECR rule on direct marketing by electronic mail does not apply to corporate subscribers" (companies, LLPs, Scottish partnerships, some government bodies). Sole traders and some partnerships are individual subscribers and need consent or the soft opt-in (details from a sale or negotiation, similar products, opt-out offered at collection and in every message). If unsure, treat the recipient as an individual subscriber. — [ICO: B2B marketing (Welsh-site mirror; the English page returned 403)](https://cy.ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/); [ICO: B2B marketing](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/)
- Even to corporate subscribers you must not "disguise or conceal your identity" and must give "a valid address for business to opt-out or unsubscribe". The ICO recommends a "do not email or text" list. — [ICO: B2B marketing](https://cy.ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/)
- "Electronic mail" includes email, SMS, picture/video messages, voicemail, in-app messages and "direct messaging on social media (ie private messages)". Public feed adverts are excluded. The rules apply even to generic addresses. — [ICO: key concepts for electronic mail](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guidance-on-direct-marketing-using-electronic-mail/key-concepts-for-direct-marketing-using-electronic-mail/)
- The ICO's B2B page now carries a banner: "Due to changes made by the Data (Use and Access) Act, this guidance is under review and may be subject to change." — [ICO: B2B marketing](https://cy.ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/)

**UK GDPR for named business contacts**
- A named employee's work email is a corporate-subscriber matter under PECR, but UK GDPR applies: you need a lawful basis (usually legitimate interests with the three-part test), must give privacy information at collection or within one month when data comes from other sources, and must stop on objection. The right to object to direct marketing is absolute. — [ICO: B2B marketing](https://cy.ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/)
- **[2025-26 change]** The DUAA adds Article 6(11) to UK GDPR, listing direct marketing (with intra-group transfers and network security) as an example of processing that **may** be a legitimate interest. The balancing test still applies. It is separate from the new "recognised legitimate interests" basis (Art 6(1)(ea), Annex 1). — [legislation.gov.uk: DUAA 2025 s.70](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2025/18/section/70); [Mayer Brown: DUAA reform of the GDPR](https://www.mayerbrown.com/zh-hans/insights/publications/2025/06/the-data-use-and-access-act-reform-of-the-gdpr-in-the-uk). The ICO says legitimate interests can cover direct marketing only where PECR doesn't require consent. — [ICO: legitimate interests](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/a-guide-to-lawful-basis/legitimate-interests/)

**Data (Use and Access) Act 2025: PECR changes and timing [2025-26 change]**
- Royal Assent 19 June 2025. Most remaining data-protection provisions, including the higher PECR fines, came into force on **5 February 2026** (Commencement No. 6 Regulations, SI 2026/82). — [ICO: statement on commencement of the DUAA](https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/media-centre/news-and-blogs/2026/02/statement-on-the-commencement-of-the-data-use-and-access-act-duaa); [DLA Piper: commencement of DUAA provisions](https://privacymatters.dlapiper.com/2026/02/uk-commencement-of-the-data-protection-provisions-in-the-data-use-and-access-act/); [RPC: DUAA commencement update](https://www.rpclegal.com/snapshots/data-protection/spring-2026/the-data-use-and-access-act-2025-commencement-update/)
- Maximum PECR fine raised from £500,000 to £17.5M or 4% of worldwide annual turnover, in line with UK GDPR. — [DLA Piper](https://privacymatters.dlapiper.com/2026/02/uk-commencement-of-the-data-protection-provisions-in-the-data-use-and-access-act/); [Clifford Chance: DUAA enforcement](https://www.cliffordchance.com/insights/resources/blogs/talking-tech/en/articles/2025/07/what-you-need-to-know-about-the-data-use-and-access-act-enforcement-and-regulatory-engagement.html)
- "Call" and "communication" now cover "all calls made and all communications transmitted, irrespective of whether they reach their intended recipient". Senders of nuisance marketing "can be fined, even if the communications do not connect". — [GOV.UK: DUAA factsheet, PEC Regulations](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/data-use-and-access-act-2025-factsheets/data-use-and-access-act-factsheet-pec-regulations)
- Other PECR changes: a charitable-purpose soft opt-in (in force 5 Feb 2026, ICO guidance updated 28 Apr 2026, not relevant to coaches), new cookie exceptions, and trade bodies can submit PECR codes of conduct to the ICO. — [GOV.UK factsheet](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/data-use-and-access-act-2025-factsheets/data-use-and-access-act-factsheet-pec-regulations); [ICO: charities given new flexibility](https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/media-centre/news-and-blogs/2026/04/charities-given-new-flexibility-to-contact-supporters-under-data-law-change/)

**Calls: TPS and CTPS**
- Sole traders and some partnerships register with the TPS. Companies, other partnerships and government bodies register with the CTPS. For B2B calls you must screen against **both** plus your own do-not-call list. Registration can take 28 days to become active, and a third party's screening older than 28 days may miss new registrations. Calling a registered number needs the subscriber to have specifically said they want your calls. — [ICO: how to comply with rules on live marketing calls](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guidance-on-direct-marketing-using-live-calls/how-do-we-comply-with-the-rules-on-live-marketing-calls/); [ICO: B2B marketing](https://cy.ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/)
- Callers must identify themselves and show their number (or an alternative), and give contact details or a Freephone number on request. Automated (recorded) marketing calls need specific consent even to businesses. — [ICO: B2B marketing](https://cy.ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/)

- WhatsApp: the number must be given by the recipient plus opt-in, for both the Business App and the API. Business-started API chats must use approved templates. Blocks and reports lead to limits and bans. — [WhatsApp Business Messaging Policy](https://whatsappbusiness.com/en-gb/policy/); [Meta: Get opt-in for WhatsApp](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/getting-opt-in)

### Inferences
- Every 2025-26 fine found is under the old £500k cap, which suggests the conduct predates 5 Feb 2026. The new £17.5M/4% ceiling hasn't been used yet in what I found. All the fines target high-volume consumer calls and texts, not low-volume B2B email to companies. The realistic risk for a coach emailing Ltd firms is complaints and spam-folder placement rather than fines, but buying unscreened phone lists is the exact pattern the ICO fines.
- The DUAA "unconnected calls count" change matters for auto-diallers: dropped or unanswered calls to TPS/CTPS numbers can now be breaches.
- Mobiles: whether a director's mobile counts as a "corporate" or "individual" subscriber depends on who holds the contract. The ICO's "if unsure, treat as individual" rule means texting or WhatsApping a director's mobile without consent is risky.
- The ICO's B2B guidance is "under review" after the DUAA, so expect updated guidance. Check before building a coach-facing playbook.
