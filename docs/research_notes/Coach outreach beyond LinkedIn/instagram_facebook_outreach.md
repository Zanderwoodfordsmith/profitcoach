# Instagram and Facebook outreach for B2B services (coaching sold to owner-led businesses)

Researched 21 Nov 2026. How to read these notes: most material on this topic comes from tool vendors, course sellers and Skool or Substack posts. Hard, independent numbers are rare. Each finding says what kind of source it is. "Opened" means I read the page; "per search summary" means I saw only a search digest of the page, so the wording may be loose. The Hormozi material comes from chapter notes on a third-party notes site, not from the book's own text, so treat the exact wording as approximate.

## 1. Finding people: how prospects are found and qualified on Instagram and Facebook

### Takeaway
Instagram prospecting starts from "buckets": followers and commenters of niche accounts, hashtags sorted by Recent, and location tags. Scraping tools then export profiles. On Facebook, Groups are the main hunting ground, and posts where owners ask for help are a useful sign they need you now. It works like Sales Navigator list-building but with weaker filters. You qualify by eye (activity, followers, website, reviews), not by job title or company size. Scraping contact details raises Meta terms-of-service and UK privacy questions.

### Cited Findings
**Instagram sources of prospects**
- Five lead sources, in a DM-outreach course run by a tool vendor: (1) followers of competitor or niche accounts; (2) niche hashtags; (3) location searches ("scrape recent posts tagged there, and combine with hashtags or account types"); (4) followers and commenters of influencers who speak to your audience; (5) followers of directory or curator accounts that feature businesses in a niche, plus the businesses featured in their posts. Verbatim tips: "Sort by **Recent**, not Top—this helps avoid dead/inactive accounts"; "commenters tend to be even more engaged"; "Scrape **buckets of relevance**"; "Don't just scrape random people." (Opened.) — [Autogram DM Masterclass, "Where to find leads"](https://autogram.gitbook.io/autogram-dm-masterclass/guides/4.-where-to-find-leads)
- The same guide suggests seed accounts with 5k–50k followers as the competitor accounts to mine. (Opened.) — [Autogram](https://autogram.gitbook.io/autogram-dm-masterclass/guides/4.-where-to-find-leads)
- Instagram's own search has "Tags" and "Places" tabs. These let you find users you don't follow, for example through a conference hashtag to reach people attending or speaking at an industry event. (Per search summary; older article.) — [Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/article/295203)
- Scraping tools (Apify "actors") search by keyword, niche or location and export name, followers, phone, email, website and bio. Tips include using specific keywords ("roofers Miami", not "roofers"). These are vendor listings. (Per search summary.) — [Apify Instagram B2B Lead Scraper](https://apify.com/scrapio/instagram-b2b-lead-scraper); [Apify local business discovery](https://apify.com/use-cases/local-businesses-discovery)
- For gyms: people who follow or comment on a gym's posts are treated as warm leads ("DM new followers promptly. Consider them like hot leads."). This is gyms selling memberships, not agencies selling to gyms, but it shows how gym owners themselves use DMs. (Per search summary.) — [Kilo (gym software vendor)](https://usekilo.com/how-to-boost-gym-growth-on-instagram-5-outbound-marketing-tips/); [Two-Brain Business](https://twobrainbusiness.com/exact-dm-scripts/)

**Facebook sources of prospects**
- Hormozi's three ways to build a cold list are: software scraping (fastest, most crowded: "if you can search a database, so can your competitors"); list brokers; and "manual scraping", which means joining groups and communities to find contact details. Manual scraping is the slowest but gives the freshest leads. (Opened; chapter notes.) — [Margin Notes, $100M Leads ch. 9](https://margin-notes-600.pages.dev/books/100m-leads/ch-9)
- Pick Facebook Groups of 500–10,000 members ("big enough to be worth your time, but not so big that you get lost in the shuffle") and avoid groups run by direct competitors. This is old advice (about 2016) and predates Meta's current spam rules. (Per search summary.) — [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/3065985/how-i-use-facebook-groups-to-grow-my-business)
- Signals to watch in Facebook Groups: owners asking for help, such as asking for a web designer or how to show up on Google. Other signals are low Google reviews that mention booking problems, job ads (for example, a marketing coordinator role) and out-of-date websites. The post says to reach out within 48 hours of a public signal; citing "Clyde", it claims this closes at 3–5x the rate of pure cold outreach (unverified). It suggests a 30-minute daily routine to find 3–5 such prospects. (Opened; Substack, 8 May 2026, written for web designers.) — [BotsGoneWild, "Stop Cold DMing. Start Reading Signals"](https://botsgonewildd.substack.com/p/stop-cold-dming-start-reading-signals)
- One vendor FAQ advises DMing only people who react to or comment on your own posts in groups, claiming warm entry converts far better than cold pitching of group members. Its reply-rate figures are for LinkedIn, so they don't carry over. (Per search summary.) — [InVideo FAQ](https://invideo.io/faq/linkedin-dms-vs-facebook-groups-for-finding-ai-video/)

**Qualifying**
- Qualifying signs in the Instagram guide: engagement (likes and comments), recent posting, relevance to niche and location. (Opened.) — [Autogram](https://autogram.gitbook.io/autogram-dm-masterclass/guides/4.-where-to-find-leads)
- Owners of small and mid-sized businesses often run their own Instagram, so a DM can reach the decision-maker directly. (Per search summary; practitioner opinion.) — [Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/how-to-send-a-direct-message-on-instagram-professionally/)

### Inferences
- For UK gyms and sports clubs, Instagram's location tags (town plus "gym", "CrossFit", "PT studio"), followers of equipment suppliers and gym-software brands, and gym-owner curator accounts are the closest thing to a Sales Navigator search. Facebook Groups for gym owners and local business groups are the second source.
- The big difference from LinkedIn: you can't filter by revenue, headcount or job title. You find the business account, then check by eye whether it is owner-led and big enough (team photos, number of sites, Google reviews, website). Expect much more manual qualifying per lead.
- A "£1M+ owner-led business" filter is hard to apply on Instagram. Instagram suits gyms, studios and local service firms, where the owner is the face of the account. It suits £1M+ B2B firms less well.

### Gaps
- I found no UK-specific write-up on prospecting through UK Facebook business groups, such as local "[town] Business Network" groups, or on their no-self-promotion rules. Coaches would need to check each group's rules.
- No source covered Facebook Marketplace pages, Facebook page search filters, or Reels-audience mining as prospecting methods in any depth.
- I didn't verify whether scraping Instagram followers breaks Meta's terms. One search digest flagged it as a possible breach of Instagram's terms and of privacy law, but I did not read Meta's terms.

## 2. Warm-up and first contact

### Takeaway
On Instagram the first contact has to be text. Since August 2023, someone who doesn't follow you can send only one text-only message request until it's accepted. So practitioners warm up first: follow, like, comment, reply to a story, or start from someone who has engaged with you. Then they send a short, specific opener that asks a question. It's like a LinkedIn connection note in that you get one short shot. The difference is that a story reply or comment can make you a known name first.

### Cited Findings
- **Instagram's 2023 limit on message requests (primary news report, opened):** "you will now only be able to send them one message"; "DM invites are now limited to text only" (no images, videos or voice notes until the recipient accepts); "You will only be able to continue to send them DMs once they accept your request to chat." Announced Thursday 3 August 2023. — [TechCrunch, 3 Aug 2023](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2578436)
- The same update added "Hidden Words", which moves requests with offensive terms to a hidden folder, and "Limits", which protects accounts from sudden spikes in DM requests. (Opened.) — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2578436)
- Meta lists "cold outreach at volume, especially right after following someone" among behaviours that trigger restrictions. It also lists "actions without normal browsing behavior such as profile views, scrolling, or dwell time". So a follow-then-instant-DM pattern is a risk. (Opened; vendor blog, 30 Aug 2026.) — [ChatPlace](https://blog.chatplace.io/en/instagram-limits/)
- Entry points that practitioners treat as separate script types: story replies, comment-to-DM keyword triggers, and DMs from ad clicks, alongside pure cold DMs. (Per search summary; vendor.) — [SetSmart](https://setsmart.io/blog/instagram-dm-scripts)
- One suggested warm-up is to leave a personable comment on their post asking them to check their DMs, since they may never look at message requests. (Per search summary; attribution uncertain.) — [Bleeding Edge Substack](https://bleedingedge.substack.com/p/how-to-unlock-opportunities-by-dming)
- Hormozi's aim for cold outreach: "make cold outreach feel like warm outreach". Research each prospect, mention 1–3 specific details, "compliment the detail and show how it benefited you". (Opened; chapter notes.) — [Margin Notes ch. 9](https://margin-notes-600.pages.dev/books/100m-leads/ch-9)
- Gym DM habit from Two-Brain Business: "Start three conversations a day. Three bubbles: their name, what they did and a question that gets a response." The example is someone commenting "So inspiring!" on a member-result post. (Per search summary.) — [Two-Brain Business](https://twobrainbusiness.com/exact-dm-scripts/)
- Generic compliments ("Love the stuff you're doing") read as insincere. (Per search summary; opinion from a seller of a paid DM guide.) — [Gumroad cold DM guide](https://herwhybylaurafama.gumroad.com/l/colddm)

### Inferences
- A workable Instagram first touch for a coach: follow the business, react to or reply to one story with a real comment, then send one short text opener a day or two later. I didn't confirm whether a story reply from a non-follower lands in the main inbox or in message requests. Voice notes and Looms come only after they accept or reply (see section 4).
- On Facebook, the natural warm-up is being helpful in a group thread, then DMing the person who posted. A friend request first can move messages out of "message requests", but I found no source confirming how Messenger filters non-friend messages in 2025–26 (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- No source gave a tested warm-up sequence (for example, "like 3 posts, then reply to a story, then DM on day 3") with results.
- I found no current source on how Facebook Messenger filters messages from non-friends (message requests, spam folder) for personal profiles.
- The 2023 Instagram change is the most recent rule change on DMs I could confirm. I found no 2025–26 Meta announcement changing message-request rules.

## 3. Messaging structures, templates and follow-up sequences

### Takeaway
Three patterns recur. (1) Hormozi: warm outreach with "Do you know anybody who…" and Acknowledge-Compliment-Ask; cold outreach with personalise plus "big fast value", meaning something people normally pay for. (2) Setter frameworks: open, qualify, invite, book, in short messages with one question each. (3) Icebreaker, context, easy call to action. Follow-ups are short "bump" messages, usually 24 hours to a few days apart. Hormozi says up to three attempts per person, then restart the list every 3–6 months.

### Cited Findings
**Hormozi, warm outreach ($100M Leads, ch. 5; chapter notes, opened)** — [Margin Notes ch. 5](https://margin-notes-600.pages.dev/books/100m-leads/ch-5)
- Volume: "Reach out to one hundred people every day", up to three attempts per person. Start on the platform where you have the most contacts (phone, email, Facebook messages or DMs), then move through all platforms and restart.
- ACA: **Acknowledge** what they said, **Compliment** a character trait, **Ask** a question that steers toward the offer.
- Offer script: "Do you know anybody who is [struggle] looking to [dream outcome] in [time]?" The full version offers to take on five free case studies, then asks "Does anyone come to mind?"
- Mindset line: "We're not asking them to buy anything. We're asking if they know anyone."
- First five free, then charge at 80% off, then 60% off, then 40% off, raising the price 20% every five customers.
- Re-engagement (Dean Jackson's 9-word email): "Are you still looking to [4-word desire]?"
- Benchmark math in the notes: 100 warm reach-outs, about 20 replies, about 4 take the free offer, about 1 paying customer.
- "Yeses give me opportunity. Nos give me feedback. Either way, I win."

**Hormozi, cold outreach ($100M Leads, ch. 9; chapter notes, opened)** — [Margin Notes ch. 9](https://margin-notes-600.pages.dev/books/100m-leads/ch-9)
- Problems and fixes: no contact info (build a list); they ignore you (personalise and give big fast value); not enough volume (automate, follow up across channels).
- Big fast value means "something people normally pay for, not something that's merely worth paying for." When Hormozi swapped disguised "game planning sessions" (sales calls) for genuinely free service delivery, take rates tripled.
- Follow-up sequence over 5–7 days across channels: email, then phone call, then voicemail, then DM, then text. Each touch refers back to the last ("follow-up to the email"). Restart the full list every 3–6 months: "Everything may be right except the timing."
- "There are no awards for prettiest script." Send the first 100 conversations or 10,000 emails before tweaking.
- "Quantity has a quality all of its own." Commit to 100 attempts a day and track contacts, responses, engaged leads and sales.

**Setter-style DM frameworks (vendors, per search summary)**
- Keep each message to 2–3 lines and ask one question per message. Don't send several messages without a reply. Structure: opener, qualify, invite, book. — [SetSmart](https://setsmart.io/blog/instagram-dm-scripts)
- A 5-stage automated version: trigger, qualify (one multiple-choice question), book (send a Calendly link), confirm, follow up. — [CreatorFlow](https://creatorflow.so/blog/instagram-appointment-setter-automation/)

**Template examples (Autogram DM Masterclass, opened; vendor, undated)** — [Autogram, DM script templates](https://autogram.gitbook.io/autogram-dm-masterclass/guides/8.-dm-script-templates)
- Framework "ICE": Icebreaker, then Context, then an Easy call to action. Its claim: "Even 1 line of context can 2x your reply rate" (unverified).
- Coaches and consultants opener (paraphrased on the page): notes their positioning with 6–7 figure business owners, then asks whether they use cold DMs or rely only on ads and referrals. The follow-up says DMs have booked calls that ads missed.
- Real estate opener, verbatim question: "Curious if you're doing anything with IG to get more buyer/seller leads?" Follow-up after 24 hours with no reply: it's fine if the timing is wrong.
- Nudges, verbatim: "Hey, just wanted to bump this in case it got buried." / "Still open to chatting? Happy to send a quick breakdown if you want to peek under the hood."

**Signal-based opener (Substack, opened, 8 May 2026; written for web designers but the shape carries over)** — [BotsGoneWild](https://botsgonewildd.substack.com/p/stop-cold-dming-start-reading-signals)
- Four lines, verbatim: "I saw your Google listing mentions customers have trouble booking online" / "I build booking-optimized sites for [niche] businesses" / "I recorded a 2-minute video showing three quick fixes for your current site" / "Worth a look?"
- If there's no reply to the written message after 3 days, phone them and frame it as a follow-up.

**Gym-specific evidence of follow-up mattering**
- A GoHighLevel community member cold-DMed about 150 gyms on Facebook and got one reply, which became a sale after about a week of follow-up. (Per search summary; anecdote.) — [Skool, GoHighLevel community](https://www.skool.com/gohighlevel/dr-client-using-robbs-closing-script)
- Two-Brain advises asking a follow-up question, then reflecting back what the person said, so DMs don't read as canned. (Per search summary.) — [Two-Brain Business](https://twobrainbusiness.com/gym-dm-sales/)

### Inferences
- For a BCA coach, Hormozi's "big fast value" maps naturally onto a free BOSS Score result or a short recorded review of the owner's business. That's something owners would normally pay a consultant for. The "Do you know anybody…" script fits the coach's warm network on Facebook.
- Because of Instagram's one-message limit on requests, a non-follower can only ever get the opener. Follow-up bumps work only once they accept, or if they already follow the coach. So an Instagram "sequence" is really one opener followed by touches on other channels (Hormozi's email, phone, voicemail, DM, text), not a five-step DM chain.
- Stop rule: three attempts per person (Hormozi), then park the lead and re-contact in 3–6 months.

### Gaps
- I found no verbatim Hormozi DM-setting script from his YouTube or podcast. Only the book chapter notes were available, and they don't give the full scripts.
- No source gave a full multi-day Instagram DM sequence with timings and measured results.
- I didn't find named appointment-setter programmes' scripts in a reliable public form. They appear as Scribd and Google Doc uploads and paywalled courses.

## 4. Voice notes and video DMs

### Takeaway
Voice notes and short videos are widely recommended, but almost every number behind them is a vendor claim or comes from LinkedIn. On Instagram they can't be the cold opener to a non-follower, because message requests are text-only until accepted. Use them as the second touch, once the person replies, accepts or follows. Hormozi's benchmark for DMs carrying a personal video or voice note is 20 replies per 100.

### Cited Findings
- Instagram message requests from non-followers are text only. Voice notes, images and videos are blocked until the request is accepted. (Opened; primary news, 3 Aug 2023.) — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2578436)
- Hormozi's channel benchmarks in the chapter notes: DMs with personal video or voice, 100 sent, 20 replies (20%). For comparison, phone gets 20% pickups and 4 engaged leads per 100; email gets 30% opens and 3 replies per 100. "Automate delivery" means using pre-recorded messages instead of delivering live each time. (Opened; chapter notes.) — [Margin Notes ch. 9](https://margin-notes-600.pages.dev/books/100m-leads/ch-9)
- Length: "Fifteen to thirty seconds is the 'sweet spot.'" "A two minute voice note in a DM gets skipped. A 25 second one gets played." About 75 words reads in about 30 seconds. Use voice for welcome messages, delivering a lead magnet (with a text link), following up after a thread goes quiet, event invites and objection handling. Skip it for routine FAQs. It gives no reply-rate data, and the product clones your voice from text. (Opened; vendor blog, 5 June 2026.) — [BooSend](https://boosend.ai/blog/voice-notes-inside-instagram-automations-where-they-earn-their-keep)
- Inrō's voice-note template: send a text line first, "I recorded a quick voice note for you. Have a listen." Then send the audio and wait up to 20 hours for a reply. "A long note asks a lot of a listener." It makes no performance claims. Audio is recorded in the vendor's browser tool, not in Instagram. Meta's API accepts aac, m4a, wav and mp4 up to 25MB (checked 23 Oct 2026). (Opened; vendor.) — [Inrō voice note campaign](https://www.inro.social/templates/voice-note-campaign)
- Cold prospects can't skim a voice note the way they skim text. (Per search summary; LinkedIn-focused.) — [Cognism](https://www.cognism.com/blog/linkedin-voice-notes?hs_amp=true)
- Lift figures of "30–40%" from adding voice, and LinkedIn cases such as 50 replies from 125 voice messages, point to unnamed benchmark reports and are about LinkedIn, not Instagram. (Per search summary.) — [Reply.io](https://reply.io/blog/linkedin-voice-messages/)
- A video-audit claim: video audits convert at 2–3x a plain text email (the post cites "Gem"; unverified). (Opened.) — [BotsGoneWild](https://botsgonewildd.substack.com/p/stop-cold-dming-start-reading-signals)
- Desktop vs mobile: Instagram added voice messages to Direct in December 2018 as a mobile feature (press-and-hold "walkie-talkie"). — [TechCrunch, Dec 2018](https://techcrunch.com/?p=1756647). A 2024 guide says you can record on desktop Instagram (on a Mac, with a key command). — [Sked Social](https://skedsocial.com/blog/how-to-send-voice-message-instagram). Other how-to guides in the same search said desktop lacks voice recording. I couldn't confirm either from Instagram's help centre.

### Inferences
- Practical order on Instagram: (1) text opener; (2) once they reply or accept, a 20–40 second voice note or a short screen-recorded video that gives the "big fast value", for example a quick read of their BOSS Score or one specific fix; (3) a call invite. On Facebook Messenger the same order applies. I found no evidence either way on whether Messenger blocks media in message requests.
- The often-repeated "voice gets 2–4x replies" claim should be treated as unproven. A coach can test it cheaply by sending text-only follow-ups to half the warm replies and voice to the other half.

### Gaps
- No independent study gives voice or video vs text reply rates for Instagram or Facebook. The numbers I found are vendor claims, unsourced, or LinkedIn-based.
- Whether voice notes can currently be recorded on Instagram web or desktop, and on Facebook Messenger desktop, is unconfirmed (sources conflict).
- Whether voice notes can be sent through Meta's API to people who haven't messaged first: official API tools can't message strangers at all (see section 5), so the API is only for inbound.

## 5. Volume, account safety, automation, and UK rules

### Takeaway
Meta publishes no daily DM caps. The figures that circulate (about 20–50 DMs a day for new Instagram accounts, more for aged ones) are vendor guesses that conflict. What triggers blocks is behaviour: even timing, identical text, cold DMs straight after a follow, no normal browsing, and spam reports. Official automation tools (ManyChat-style) can't message strangers, so cold outbound means either manual sending (setters) or grey-hat tools that log in with your password. In the UK, the ICO counts social media DMs as "electronic mail" under PECR. That matters for gyms and clubs run by sole traders or partnerships.

### Cited Findings
**Limits and triggers**
- Meta "has never published a table of per-action caps". Commonly quoted numbers (for example 200 follows a day) are unverified community claims. Limits are per account, and account age matters: "An established account with steady engagement can absorb a burst that would restrict a two-week-old account." (Opened; vendor blog, 30 Aug 2026.) — [ChatPlace](https://blog.chatplace.io/en/instagram-limits/)
- Block triggers listed: even intervals between actions (for example, one every 12 seconds), identical text sent to many people, cold outreach at volume (especially right after following), actions without normal browsing behaviour, and high activity on a new account. An action block stops one action type for hours to several days, and repeat blocks last longer. That's a pattern users report, not a documented rule. Warning signs: rising "Try again later" errors, or a sudden drop in delivered messages. Advice: keep activity irregular, at a pace you can keep up for a month, and pause for a day when warnings appear. (Opened; vendor.) — [ChatPlace](https://blog.chatplace.io/en/instagram-limits/)
- Vendor blogs quote figures such as "New accounts: 20–50 DMs per day" and "20 to 40 daily for a new personal account", with higher numbers for aged accounts. They conflict and none cites Meta. (Per search summary; I didn't open each page to confirm which vendor gives which number.) — [Inro](https://inro.social/blog/how-many-direct-messages-can-you-send-on-instagram); [SuperProfile](https://superprofile.bio/guides/instagram-dm-limits); [InstantDM](https://instantdm.com/blog/instagram-dm-limits-rules-2026-the-ultimate-account-safety-guide)
- Meta's developer changelog raised the send limit to 300 calls per second per Instagram professional account for certain message types. That applies only to API integrations, not manual DMs. (Per search summary.) — [Meta for Developers, Instagram Messaging changelog](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/instagram-messaging/changelog/)
- Facebook Messenger help says you can be "temporarily restricted" from sending messages if they break Community Standards, and may also be "temporarily blocked from sending friend requests". No figures are given. (Per search summary; official help page.) — [Messenger Help Centre](https://www.messenger.com/help/1723537124537415)
- For Instagram professional accounts, Meta's marketing-messages rules cap opt-in requests at 10 per user over 7 days, with up to 5 in a day. That covers business messaging tools, not person-to-person DMs. (Per search summary.) — [Meta for Developers, Marketing Messages API reference](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/messenger-platform/marketing-messages-on-messenger/api-reference)

**Meta enforcement climate (2025–26)**
- April 2025: Facebook began lowering the reach of accounts that share spammy content and making them ineligible for monetisation, targeting networks of accounts that share the same content. — [TechCrunch, 24 Apr 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/24/facebook-cracks-down-on-spammy-content-by-cutting-reach-and-monetization)
- July 2025: Meta acted against about 500,000 accounts "engaged in spammy behavior or fake engagement". It said it removed about 10 million impersonator profiles in the first half of 2025. — [TechCrunch, 14 Jul 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/14/following-youtube-meta-announces-crackdown-on-unoriginal-facebook-content); [NBC DFW / CNBC](https://www.nbcdfw.com/news/business/money-report/meta-removes-10-million-facebook-profiles-in-effort-to-combat-spam/3884912/)
- Some legitimate users said AI systems wrongly removed their accounts as inauthentic. — [Yahoo News](https://www.yahoo.com/news/facebook-erases-10m-fake-accounts-155047003.html)
- Meta reports removing 1.1 billion fake Facebook accounts in Q4 2025, and says it uses machine learning against fake accounts and bulk messaging. (Per search summary.) — [Meta Scam Protection Center](https://www.meta.com/safety/scam-protection-center/safety-tips/policy-enforcement/)
- These 2025 actions were about content and fake accounts. I found no 2025–26 rule that changes person-to-person DM limits. (My reading of the sources above.)

**Automation vs manual setters**
- Two kinds of tool: **grey-hat tools** that "log in with your username and password and imitate app taps", which produce exactly what detection looks for and break when detection changes; and **official integrations** through Meta's authorisation screen, which "cannot message strangers who have not contacted the account". (Opened; vendor that sells an official tool.) — [ChatPlace](https://blog.chatplace.io/en/instagram-limits/)
- The 24-hour window: a business can reply freely for 24 hours after a person's last message. A comment doesn't open that window. Meta allows one private reply to a commenter, so tools send a card with a button, because a tap counts as the person's message. (Opened.) — [ChatPlace](https://blog.chatplace.io/en/instagram-limits/)

**UK law (ICO / PECR), relevant to every UK coach DMing owners**
- The ICO's definition of electronic mail covers "direct messaging on social media (ie private messages)". News-feed ads are outside that definition, though other PECR rules may apply. (Per search summary of ICO pages.) — [ICO, key concepts for direct marketing using electronic mail](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guidance-on-direct-marketing-using-electronic-mail/key-concepts-for-direct-marketing-using-electronic-mail/); [ICO, What is electronic mail marketing?](https://cy.ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guidance-on-direct-marketing-using-electronic-mail/what-is-electronic-mail-marketing)
- "Sole traders and other types of partnerships" are "individual subscribers", whom PECR treats like consumers. You need their specific consent, or the soft opt-in (a past customer for a similar product who didn't opt out), before sending them electronic marketing. The electronic-mail marketing rule "does not apply to corporate subscribers" (for example, limited companies), though the ICO advises keeping a "do not email or text" list of corporate contacts who object. (Per search summary.) — [ICO, Business-to-business marketing](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/)
- The Data (Use and Access) Act added the Data Protection Act's definition of direct marketing into PECR from 20 August 2025. (Per search summary.) — [ICO, Business-to-business marketing](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/)

### Inferences
- Safe operating pattern from the sources: one person's real, aged account; manual sending; each opener written for that person; irregular pace; normal scrolling between sends; stop for a day at the first "Try again later". Volumes in the low dozens a day for a newer account fit every vendor's range, but there is no official figure.
- For BCA, any "done-for-you" Instagram or Facebook outbound run centrally would have to be manual. Doing it through password-sharing automation risks coaches' personal accounts. The legitimate automation (ManyChat-type) only handles people who comment, message or click first.
- UK legal flag: many gyms, PT studios and some sports businesses are sole traders or partnerships. A cold marketing DM to them may need consent under PECR. Limited companies are much lower risk. This is a strong reason to favour inbound (comment-to-DM, ads, content) and warm or signal-led outreach over mass cold DMs to small UK gyms. A UK-qualified adviser should confirm this before BCA builds it into training. I'm not giving legal advice.

### Gaps
- No official Meta daily or weekly DM limits exist for Instagram or Facebook personal or professional accounts. Every number is a community guess.

## 7. Results: reported reply rates, booked-call rates and case studies

### Takeaway
Credible cold Instagram DM numbers for selling to business owners are low: about 2–6% positive replies. Vendor claims of 20–40% aren't backed by data. Warm outreach and DMs carrying personal video or voice do much better: Hormozi's notes give about 20% replies. The best public funnel I found: 1,100 cold DMs, 57 positive replies, 11 booking links sent, 6 calls booked, 1 no-show. That's about 0.5% from DM to call.

### Inferences
- Planning numbers for a UK coach doing manual cold Instagram DMs to owners: expect about 5% positive replies and about 0.5% booked calls per DM sent (Cohen's funnel). That means roughly 200 DMs per booked call. A coach doing 20 hand-written DMs a day would get about 2–3 calls a month from cold DMs alone.
- Warm and signal-led outreach (people who engaged, posted for help, or are in the coach's network) plus a big-fast-value asset (score, short video review) is where the higher 20%+ reply rates come from. That is where coaches should spend their DM time.
- For gyms and sports clubs, small-sample anecdotes (1 in 150 on Facebook) suggest cold DMs alone are weak. Follow-up and a concrete, gym-specific hook matter more than the opener.
