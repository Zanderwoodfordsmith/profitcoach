# Industry channel fit: where UK owner-led business owners can be reached beyond LinkedIn

Research date: 21 Nov 2026. UK focus. Evidence tags used throughout:
- **[HARD DATA]** = regulator, national panel or a survey with a stated method (Ofcom, YouGov Profiles, Censuswide).
- **[VENDOR SURVEY]** = commissioned by a company with something to sell; method often thin.
- **[PRACTITIONER CLAIM]** = what a coach, community or group says about itself (member counts are self-reported and unverified).
- **[US/NA]** = US or Canadian evidence, not UK.

Big caveat up front: I found **no UK source that splits LinkedIn vs Instagram vs Facebook vs WhatsApp use by business owners by sector**. The sector map below is built from (a) general SME platform data, (b) trade-specific surveys (trades, salons), and (c) where owner communities actually live (Facebook groups, trade bodies, podcasts). It is a reasoned map, not a measured one.

---

## 1. Platform usage: what data exists on UK SME owners' use of LinkedIn vs Instagram vs Facebook vs WhatsApp, by sector and size? Which sectors have weak LinkedIn presence?

### Takeaway
UK SMEs as a whole use Facebook (69%), Instagram (67%) and WhatsApp (58%) for business more than LinkedIn (52%), and in trades the Facebook lead is wider (73% Facebook; LinkedIn not even reported). LinkedIn reaches only about a quarter of British adults, skews to employees in B2B firms, and most members rarely post, so it is a thin channel for owners of consumer-facing, trade and local service businesses. No source gives a clean sector-by-platform split.

### Cited Findings
**General UK population (the owner as a person)**
- [HARD DATA] Facebook + Messenger reached 93% of UK online adults and WhatsApp 90% in May 2025, making them the two most-used Meta services. — [Ofcom, Online Nation 2025 summary](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/media-use-and-attitudes/online-habits/from-apps-to-ai-search-how-the-uk-goes-online-in-2025)
- [HARD DATA] Ofcom's Online Nation 2025 report puts Facebook/Messenger time at about 42 minutes a day per online adult (one secondary source says 43) and WhatsApp at 17 minutes a day in 2025. — [Online Nation 2025 report PDF (mirror)](https://www.ot.gr/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/online-nations-report-2025.pdf); [Ofcom summary](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/media-use-and-attitudes/online-habits/from-apps-to-ai-search-how-the-uk-goes-online-in-2025)
- [HARD DATA] 25% of Britons are LinkedIn members (fifth most popular platform), vs 70% for Facebook and 46% for Instagram (YouGov Profiles, published Aug 2023). — [YouGov](https://yougov.com/en-gb/articles/47039-who-are-the-brits-who-use-linkedin)
- [HARD DATA] Of LinkedIn members in Britain: 62% work full-time; 50% work in business-to-business companies; 21% in micro businesses and 14% in small businesses; by industry, management 14%, IT 10%, and manufacturing, procurement and distribution only 2% each. — [YouGov](https://yougov.com/en-gb/articles/47039-who-are-the-brits-who-use-linkedin)
- [HARD DATA] LinkedIn engagement is shallow: 40% of British members never post, 26% post less than once a month, only 16% open it more than once a day; the top use is job applications (54%), then building a network (48%). — [YouGov](https://yougov.com/en-gb/articles/47039-who-are-the-brits-who-use-linkedin)
- [VENDOR/TRACKER] LinkedIn account counts for the UK are much higher (about 47.5m "users" in Nov 2025 per NapoleonCat; 44.6m cited by Sprout Social). These are accounts, not active people, and conflict with each other; the YouGov 25% figure is the better guide to real reach. — [NapoleonCat](https://stats.napoleoncat.com/linkedin-users-in-united_kingdom/2025/11/); [Sprout Social](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/social-media-statistics-uk/)

**UK SMEs (the business)**
- [VENDOR SURVEY] iwoca (SME lender), April 2025: 97% of SMEs use social media for business. Platforms used: Facebook 69%, Instagram 67%, YouTube 63%, WhatsApp 58%, LinkedIn 52%, X 50%, TikTok 49%, Snapchat 23%, Pinterest 20%, Reddit 16%, Nextdoor 7%. No sample size, sector or region split published. — [iwoca](https://www.iwoca.co.uk/news/meta-channels-lead-97-smes-use-social-media); [Financial IT table](https://financialit.net/news/infrastructure/meta-channels-lead-pack-97-smes-now-use-social-media-their-business)
- [VENDOR SURVEY] Same iwoca survey, by size: 11% of businesses with 2-10 staff don't use social media vs 2% of those with 51-249 staff. By owner age: TikTok used by 60% of owners aged 18-34 vs 17% of owners over 55. — [iwoca](https://www.iwoca.co.uk/news/meta-channels-lead-97-smes-use-social-media)
- Note: X at 50% looks high against other UK data, which suggests the iwoca sample may lean towards digitally active owners. Treat the ranking as more reliable than the exact numbers.

**Trades (the one sector with platform data)**
- [HARD DATA-ish, insurer-commissioned, Censuswide] Markel Direct, survey of 501 UK tradespeople and small business owners who use social media to market their business (published July 2026): Facebook used by 73% (75% of over-55s); Instagram 63% (77% of 18-24s, 40% of over-55s); TikTok 73% of 18-24s. LinkedIn, WhatsApp and YouTube not reported. 84% say they win some business through social media; 60% can point to specific work won from a post; 77% run their own social media; 65% have never had training. — [Markel UK](https://www.markeluk.com/knowledge-centre/trades-and-social-media-uk)
- [VENDOR SURVEY, older] IronmongeryDirect survey of 500 tradespeople (2020, re-reported 2023): 93% use social media to attract customers; Facebook best for leads (58%), then Instagram (39%), YouTube (33%), TikTok (31%); 85% say they don't know how to use it well. (Figures from trade-press search snippets; not fetched in full.) — [HVP Magazine](https://www.hvpmag.co.uk/Tradespeople-secure-almost-half-their-jobs-through-social-media-study-finds/17027); [BDC Magazine](https://www.bdcmagazine.com/2020/12/85-of-tradespeople-arent-sure-how-to-use-social-media-effectively/)
- [VENDOR SURVEY] Consumer side: 71% of homeowners would check a trade company or worker on Facebook first (MyJobQuote survey). — [PHPI Online](https://phpionline.co.uk/news/social-media-crucial-homeowners-sourcing-tradespeople/)

**Hair and beauty (indicative only)**
- [PRACTITIONER / trade body, informal] NHBF snap poll of its followers: owners use Instagram to show their business's personality and brand; NHBF guidance calls Instagram "perfect" for salons and barbershops because the work is visual. — [NHBF](https://nhbf.co.uk/news-and-blogs/blog/how-to-use-marketing-effectively); [NHBF social media guide](https://nhbf.co.uk/advice-and-resources/blog/public-relations/social-media)
- [SECONDARY] For most UK salons "Instagram first" and Instagram is "the strongest platform for converting followers into bookings". — [Whito salon industry report](https://whito.co.uk/research/beauty-salon-industry-data-report/)
- Conflicting: one report says YouTube is the most popular marketing channel for hair and beauty SMEs, then Facebook and Instagram (underlying data not checkable). — [Marketing Beat](https://www.marketing-beat.co.uk/?p=46031)

**Weak sources to avoid**
- Rheos "UK sector social media 2026" pages and Gitnux compilations give sector percentages (e.g. "81% of UK retailers use Instagram") with no traceable primary survey. Do not cite. — [Rheos](https://rheos.app/research/uk-sector-social-media-2026); [Gitnux](https://gitnux.org/small-business-social-media-statistics/)

### Inferences
- Sectors with **weak LinkedIn presence among owners** (best supported → least): small trades (no LinkedIn in either trades survey; Facebook dominant), hair/beauty/aesthetics (Instagram-first), gyms and studios, single-site hospitality, nurseries. Supported by the YouGov profile (LinkedIn = B2B employees, mostly passive) plus sector surveys.
- Sectors where LinkedIn is **probably strong**: professional services (accountants, solicitors, IT/MSPs, agencies), B2B tech, and the head offices of larger groups. Supported by YouGov (50% of LinkedIn members work in B2B, IT 10%, management 14%).
- **Manufacturing is a surprise risk**: YouGov shows manufacturing at only 2% of employed LinkedIn members. That does not prove manufacturing MDs are absent, but it means coaches should not assume LinkedIn is enough for engineering/manufacturing owners.
- WhatsApp is near-universal for UK adults (90%) and used by 58% of SMEs for business, so it is the best **follow-up** channel in almost every consumer-facing and trade niche, but cold WhatsApp messages carry PECR/consent and spam-report risk (not researched here).

### Gaps
- No Ofcom figure for LinkedIn reach was found in the Online Nation 2025 materials I could access.
- The DBT Longitudinal Small Business Survey 2025 exists ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/small-business-survey-2025-businesses-with-employees/longitudinal-small-business-survey-2025-sme-employers-businesses-with-1-to-249-employees)) but I did not find a social-platform-by-sector question in it; not checked in full.
- No FSB, Meta-branded or LinkedIn-branded UK SMB report with a sector-by-platform split was found. Statista has UK LinkedIn age/gender splits but nothing by owner sector.
- No sample size for the iwoca survey.

## 2. Sector by sector: where owners show their business, which communities/trade bodies/events exist, and which channel a coach should lead with

### Takeaway
In most consumer-facing and trade niches, owners **market** on Instagram/Facebook, but they **gather as peers** in Facebook groups, trade bodies, trade press, podcasts and local collectives. That peer layer is where a coach gets in. LinkedIn stays the lead channel for professional services and is a sensible lead for manufacturing, care groups and multi-site operators, backed by email, phone and events.

### Inferences
Recommended lead channel per sector (my synthesis; "lead" = first touch, then move to phone/WhatsApp/in-person):

| Sector | Where owners show their business | Where owners gather as peers | Coach should lead with | Then | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Small trades (sole trader to ~£2M) | Facebook, Instagram, Checkatrade-type directories | Big Facebook groups (Elite Trades 170k claim, Trades Freedom Club), FMB, trade podcasts | Facebook group content + Messenger; trade-body partnership (FMB-style programmes) | Phone, WhatsApp | **Medium-high** (two trades surveys + large groups) |
| Larger construction (£5M+, contractors, specialist subbies) | LinkedIn, website, tenders | Trade bodies, regional construction events | LinkedIn + phone/email + events | In person | **Low** (inference only) |
| Gyms, studios, boxes | Instagram, Facebook | Facebook groups (Gym Owners United, Ashbourne UK/IE group), podcasts, Skool | Podcast/YouTube content + Facebook group presence; Instagram DMs to owners | Zoom call | **Medium** (big North American playbook, smaller UK evidence) |
| Leisure centres, sports clubs, trusts | Website, Facebook | Trade body (ukactive, not researched), sector events | LinkedIn + email to managers/CEOs; events | In person | **Low** |
| Single-site hospitality | Instagram, Google | Local collectives, Restaurant Collective, AIR, local events | In person + local collectives; Instagram DM | WhatsApp | **Low-medium** |
| Multi-site hospitality / hotel groups (£1M+) | Instagram (brand), LinkedIn (leaders) | UKHospitality, industry events | LinkedIn + events + warm intros | Phone/email | **Low-medium** (inference) |

| Care homes / home care | Website, directories | Owners clubs (Caring Times), invite-only events, Care England | LinkedIn + email + owner-club events | Phone, in person | **Low-medium** |

- Pattern: in nearly every non-LinkedIn niche, a **niche Facebook group, podcast or trade-press column** is the coach's way in, not cold DMs.
- Trade bodies and suppliers are distribution partners: FMB sells coaching to builders; Ashbourne (gym billing) and Consulting Room (aesthetics) host owner groups; Brabners (law firm) hosts care owners. Coaches could partner with the supplier or trade body that already owns the room.

## 3. Consumer-facing vs B2B: does "consumer-facing = Instagram/Facebook, B2B = LinkedIn" hold? Where does it break?

### Takeaway
Roughly yes for **where the business markets itself**, but it is the wrong question for a coach. What matters is where the **owner gathers with other owners**, and that breaks the rule both ways: B2B niches like MSPs and accountants have active Facebook groups, while consumer businesses at £1M+ with head offices (dental groups, multi-site hospitality, care groups) have leaders on LinkedIn.

### Cited Findings
- LinkedIn members are mostly B2B employees (50% B2B, IT 10%, management 14%) who rarely post, which supports the B2B half of the rule. — [YouGov](https://yougov.com/en-gb/articles/47039-who-are-the-brits-who-use-linkedin)
- Consumer and trade businesses lean on Facebook and Instagram (trades: Facebook 73%, Instagram 63%; salons: Instagram-first). — [Markel UK](https://www.markeluk.com/knowledge-centre/trades-and-social-media-uk); [NHBF](https://nhbf.co.uk/news-and-blogs/blog/how-to-use-marketing-effectively)
- Break 1 (B2B on Facebook): UK MSP marketing coach Paul Green runs his community as a Facebook group alongside his podcast; Engager's Facebook group supports 1,500+ accounting firms. — [Paul Green's MSP Marketing Podcast](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/paul-greens-msp-marketing-podcast/id1485101351); [AccountingWEB](https://www.accountingweb.co.uk/node/225003)
- Break 2 (consumer business, owner reached by trade press/peers, not consumer channels): dentists run large Facebook groups (up to 20,000) and read dental trade press; salon coaches launch through Scratch/HJi/Professional Beauty. — [Dentistry.co.uk](https://dentistry.co.uk/2025/03/10/why-facebook-groups-are-essential-in-dentistry/); [Scratch](https://www.scratchmagazine.co.uk/news/liz-mckeon-launches-international-coaching-programme-to-support-salon-success)
- Break 3 (WhatsApp as the peer room): aesthetics practitioners (UK-IAP) and some salon coaches use WhatsApp groups rather than Facebook. — [UK-IAP](https://sites.google.com/uk-iap.co.uk/iaphub); [Adele Catherine](https://stan.store/AdeleCatherine/p/beyond-the-chair-12-week-group-coaching-program)
- Break 4 (B2B but weak LinkedIn): manufacturing is only 2% of employed LinkedIn members in Britain. — [YouGov](https://yougov.com/en-gb/articles/47039-who-are-the-brits-who-use-linkedin)
- Break 5 (owner age): Instagram use among trades drops from 77% (18-24) to 40% (over-55s), while Facebook stays high (75% of over-55s). Older owners of £1M+ businesses are more likely Facebook than Instagram. — [Markel UK](https://www.markeluk.com/knowledge-centre/trades-and-social-media-uk)

### Inferences
- A better rule for coaches: **"Find the room where owners talk to other owners."** That is a Facebook group in trades, gyms, dental, salons and nurseries; trade press and podcasts in dental, salons, estate agents and MSPs; LinkedIn in professional services; and trade bodies and events in care, manufacturing and multi-site hospitality.
- The owner's age matters as much as the sector: older owners (most £1M+ owners) favour Facebook over Instagram.
- "B2B = LinkedIn" is reliable for professional services and tech, unreliable for manufacturing/engineering and trades-to-trades suppliers.

### Gaps
- No direct survey of where UK owners look for business advice or coaches, by sector.

## 4. Case studies: niche coaches/consultants/agencies that grew by reaching a non-LinkedIn niche, and the channels they used

### Takeaway
The niche coaching businesses that scaled did so with **free value in the owners' own room**: a podcast or book plus a vetted Facebook group (Two-Brain, Elite Trades, salon coaches, Paul Green); paid Facebook ads plus heavy cold calling (Gym Launch, US); trade-press columns, books and speaking (Chris Barrow in dental); and trade-body partnerships (FMB). None of the evidence shows LinkedIn as the main source of clients in these niches.

### Cited Findings
- **Two-Brain Business (gyms) [US/NA, Canada-based]:** launched 13 Feb 2016 with 52 gym owners; says it is "the largest gym mentorship company in the world"; Chris Cooper's book "Two-Brain Business" sold 26,000 copies (company claim). Channels: long-running podcast "Run a Profitable Gym" (600+ episodes), daily blog, book, and the free vetted Facebook group Gym Owners United (10,000+ claimed) sitting next to "Book a call". — [Two-Brain: Chris Cooper](https://twobrainbusiness.com/about/chris-cooper/); [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/run-a-profitable-gym/id1050350101); [Gym Owners United](https://twobrainbusiness.com/gym-owners-united/)

## 5. Size fit: at what size does the owner move off Instagram/Facebook onto LinkedIn? Do £1M+ groups behave differently from single-site operators?

### Takeaway
No source measures this directly. The evidence points one way: micro firms use social media less, LinkedIn skews to staff in larger B2B firms, and trade bodies split into "independents" vs "multi-site groups". Likely shift point: when the business gets a management layer or head office (often multi-site, roughly £3-5M+), its leaders appear on LinkedIn; single-site owners, even at £1M+, stay mostly on Facebook/Instagram/WhatsApp. Treat this as inference.

### Cited Findings
- Micro firms (2-10 staff) are five times more likely than larger SMEs (51-249) to skip social media altogether (11% vs 2%). — [iwoca](https://www.iwoca.co.uk/news/meta-channels-lead-97-smes-use-social-media)
- Only 21% of British LinkedIn members work in micro businesses and 14% in small businesses; half work in B2B firms. — [YouGov](https://yougov.com/en-gb/articles/47039-who-are-the-brits-who-use-linkedin)
- Hospitality bodies split by size: Restaurant Collective is aimed at single-site venues and small groups; UKHospitality covers independents through to large multi-site groups. — [The Caterer](https://thecaterer.com/news/restaurant-collective-launches-support-independent-restaurant-sector); [UKHospitality](https://ukhospitality.org.uk/join-us-operators/)
- Older owners stay on Facebook (75% of over-55 trades owners) while Instagram drops (40%). — [Markel UK](https://www.markeluk.com/knowledge-centre/trades-and-social-media-uk)
- [US/NA] There are dedicated shows for "multi-unit operators ready to scale" in restaurants, which suggests multi-site operators form a separate audience. — [Restaurant Leadership Podcast](https://www.buzzsprout.com/2220802/episodes/16270459)

### Inferences
- **Single-site, owner-on-the-floor businesses** (salon, gym, café, small trades firm), even at £1M: owner lives in Instagram/Facebook/WhatsApp. Lead there.
- **Multi-site groups and £3-5M+ businesses with a management team** (multi-site dental, hospitality groups, gym chains, care groups, larger contractors): the owner or MD is more likely to have a working LinkedIn profile; LinkedIn plus email, events and warm intros become viable. Their ops directors and FDs are on LinkedIn even if the founder is not.
- Owner age often matters more than revenue: many £1M+ owners are over 45, so Facebook (not Instagram or TikTok) is the safer social channel for them.
- Gyms and salons often sit under £1M; for these niches, a coach should expect smaller deal sizes and lean on group programmes reached through Facebook groups and Instagram.

### Gaps
- No UK data on LinkedIn use by owner turnover band or number of sites.
- No data on whether multi-site hospitality/gym/salon owners use LinkedIn more than single-site owners; this is inference from trade-body structure and the LinkedIn member profile.
