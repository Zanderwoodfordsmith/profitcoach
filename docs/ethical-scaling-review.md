# Ethical Scaling: what they gave us (2024) and what we still use

Read 21 Nov 2026 from the Drive folder "Zander Woodford-Smith - Business Coach Academy - RFF" (28 files). The work stopped after their first stage: the Deep Dive was done, but the journey, team, QC and upgrade stages were never finished. Roadmap jobs that came from this doc say "From docs/ethical-scaling-review.md".

Their materials talk about "clients" and "CSMs". For us, clients = coaches and CSMs = BCA staff.

## 1. Client archetypes

First split: does the person lead with logic or with feelings? Then:

| Type | Leads with | What they're like | How to serve them | Answer the |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Doer** | Logic | Rare ("top 1%"). Works hard, takes risks, gives testimonials. Turns into a Controller if they feel unclear or uncared for. | Detail, data, recognition, a steady feedback loop. Keep them moving. | How |
| **Controller** | Logic | Wants it all now. Trusts themselves, not you or the process. Hits the first plateau, overworks, burns out, quits. Lots of questions = unclear on the outcome. | Direct, brief, firm limits. Agree with their facts, not with them. Help them zoom out. | What |
| **Worrier** | Feelings | Loves new things, then the excitement fades and they chase the next one. "Will this work for me?" Easily overwhelmed. | Friendly, let them talk, turn ideas into actions. Stories and testimonials. Answer questions before they ask. Ask before giving feedback. | Who |
| **Follower** | Feelings | Nice, nods along, does little. Hates tracking numbers. "Prefers the certainty of misery to the misery of uncertainty." | Patient, simple, steady. Say exactly what you want. Ask "how" questions. "Bring me the numbers so I can help" (doctor frame). | Why |

People move between types. The best client "believes in themselves, trusts you, and is willing to experiment".

The two scales (Zander's memory of their model, which fits the deck): **belief in themselves** and **belief in the system** (us and the process). Each scored 1–5.

| | Low belief in the system | High belief in the system |
|---|---|---|
| **High belief in themselves** | Controller: trusts themselves, not you | Doer: trusts both |
| **Low belief in themselves** | Worrier: "will this work for me?" | Follower: leans on you, does little |

The aim is to move everyone towards Doer: build their self-belief with early wins, and their trust in the system with proof and keeping promises.

## 2. Buy-in

- Buy-in is a process, not a moment. People slide between buyer's remorse and buy-in in both directions.
- Aim for **high sense of responsibility, low sense of risk**. Never raise their sense of risk. People who feel low responsibility and high risk ghost in the first couple of weeks.
- "What looks like a people problem is often a situation problem. Laziness is often exhaustion. Resistance is often a lack of clarity."
- Elephant and Rider: give the Rider clear steps, give the Elephant a reason to care, and clear the path.
- **Expectation = effort × time.** Agree the hours a week. If the goal doesn't fit, the person chooses: more hours, more time, or a smaller goal. A longer timeline sets up the next offer.
- Engineer a real early win to create hope. Warn them about the dip before results tip over (the ice cube melts at 0°C, not before).
- "They're the hero, we're the guide."

## 3. Change, scarcity and the drama triangle

- **Stages of change:** not thinking about it → thinking about it → preparing → doing → keeping it up → slipping back. Expect the slip and restart from preparing or doing.
- **Six forces:** wanting to, being able to, friends, coach or mentor, rewards, surroundings. Score each +1 or −1 and fix the negatives.
- **Scarcity trap:** "I need this to work yesterday" narrows thinking into short-term choices. Fix: add slack, and take things off their plate. Weak links that cause slips: time, money, physical effort, mental effort, routine. Fix by cutting, automating, delegating, simplifying, or tying to an existing habit.
- **Drama triangle:** Victim, Persecutor, Rescuer. Turn them into Survivor, Challenger, Coach. "Don't rescue, coach." With an angry client: let them vent, ask "what's the real challenge here?", then "I can't do that, but I could do this instead."

## 4. Health score (effort, not results)

Three behaviours. Green = all 3, Yellow = 1–2, Red = 0.

| Behaviour | Green | Yellow | Red |
|---|---|---|---|
| Replies to us | within 72 hours | only after the second message (3–5 days) | still nothing after 7–10 days |
| 1:1 calls | attends, or warns in advance | keeps rescheduling | no-show without notice |
| Group calls | 1+ every 2 weeks | 1 every 2–3 weeks | none in 4 weeks |
| Training | 2+ hours in 2 weeks | 2+ hours in 3 weeks | under 2 hours in 4 weeks |

What to do:
- **Green:** ask for a review, testimonial or referral, celebrate them, send a gift at milestones, point Doers at the bigger goal.
- **Yellow:** reach out in a way that fits their type. No reply in 72 hours → follow up in the same channel. 48 hours more → WhatsApp or SMS.
- **Red:** call now, framed as a reset, not blame. Follow up in 5–7 days and watch them for 2 weeks.

## 6. Calls

**Onboarding call (20–30 minutes):**
1. Agenda: what this call is for, how long, and that it ends with next steps and the next call booked.
2. Goal: read the closer's notes first. Get the number, what it would mean to them, and why.
3. Expectations: effort × time.
4. Journey map with its ups and downs.
5. Where to take which question.
6. 1–3 actions; book the 14- or 30-day check-in.

**Check-in (30 minutes):**
1. Rapport.
2. Agenda.
3. Wins and problems.
4. Reconnect with the goal and the hours.
5. 1–3 actions.
6. Ask "what was the value of this call for you?", then book the next one.

**Every action:** a confidence score (1–10), a due day, and "message me when it's done".

**Call scoring:** five things, each scored 1/3/5/7, total out of 35: rapport, agenda, energy, fit to their type, action plan. Under 15 = red flag, 15–25 OK, 25–30 good, 30+ excellent. In 2024 the agenda was the weakest part on every call reviewed.

**Things reviewers kept missing:**
- the client's goal on onboarding calls
- celebrating wins
- turning a win into a testimonial
- asking why agreed work wasn't done
- showing the progress map on check-ins

## 7. Reviews, testimonials and referrals

- Ask green clients after 28+ days, or anyone 70–80% of the way to their goal. No big asks in the first 4 weeks unless they're flying.
- The asks get bigger in order: review, then testimonial, then referral. Ask 1:1. If they say no, drop to a smaller ask.
- Name the person to refer ("someone like X who needs Y"), never "anyone you know".
- Give them ready-made wording.
- Reward with gifts, not cash: a skipped instalment, or an extra month, per referral that joins.
- Targets: ask 50% of green clients each month; 5% of active clients refer.
- Ask people who were referred to refer someone too.

## 8. Celebrations and gifts

- **Moments:**
  - day 14 (launch call)
  - end of month 1
  - a results review about 2 weeks before the contract ends
  - 20%, 50% and 100% of the goal
- **Gifts:** about 2–3% of revenue. A book or a handwritten note, sent in a batch every 2 weeks.

## 9. When things go wrong

- Log every pause, cancel and refund request with what happened, and tell leadership.
- **Pause:** up to 2 payments, signed off by leadership.
- **Refund:** through a form that restates the guarantee. Staff promise nothing. If it qualifies, it goes to Pam. After a refund, access ends and they sign a one-page agreement (no use of our material, no sharing, no bad reviews).
- **Missed payment:** a named payments person (not the coach's success contact) emails, retries within 48 hours, phones, retries again, and removes access after 7 days of silence.
- **Feeling mis-sold:** review the sales call.
- **Unhappy:** a 1:1 to find the real reason, then Pam, then possibly a different success contact.

## 10. Monthly client-success report

- **Coaches:** new and lost against last month.
- **Requests:** pause, cancel and refund requests and how each ended.
- **Load:** coaches per success contact, at about 40 each, and spare capacity.
- **Onboarding:** progress by month joined, and the mix of types.
- **Churn:** at 7 days, at 30 days, and rolling over 3 months.
- **Rates:** activation (target 90%) and buy-in (target 85%).
- **Advocacy:** reviews, testimonials and referrals logged.
- **Call reviews:** whose calls were reviewed, and one simple fix each.

## 11. Offer ideas they raised

- A tech self-score of 1/3/5/7 in the joining form; 1 or 3 gets a 15-minute setup call.
- The first programme limited to 6 months, with advanced material saved for the next level.
- The next step is triggered by: revenue goal hit, going full time, or 3–6 clients.
- The closer books onboarding before the sales call ends, and onboarding happens within 48–72 hours.

## 12. Hormozi's onboarding list (LTV workshop, 2026)

Not from Ethical Scaling; added here because it overlaps.
- **Quick win:** something that shows it works, not the end goal.
- **Personal welcome.**
- **Day 1–14 checklist.**
- **Human contact fast:** within 48 hours, ideally the same day. People judge you in the first 24 hours.
- **Proof:** people like them who just won.

**Week 1 feedback (Hormozi, used with new sales staff; we'd use it with new members).** Run once on day 8. Each person answers privately, never in the group: the first answer in a group sways everyone else. Say why: "I don't want anyone influencing each other."
- Start: 1. What didn't we do in the first week that we should? 2. What could be done better?
- Stop: 3. What should we cut from the first week because it wasn't helpful?
- Continue: 4. What did you like that we should keep doing? 5. What did you like that we didn't do enough of?

Read every answer before the next session. Sort them: a pattern (3+ people say it) → fix it for the next group and tell this group what you changed; one person → handle it with them 1:1; praise → note what to protect.

His offer survey has two questions: "If we had to cut everything but one part, which would you fight hardest to keep?" and "Which one part would you be happiest to see gone?" Split the answers by your best and worst customers.
