# Ideal client language patterns

Derived from the 215-document Business Coach Academy avatar corpus (`.ica-research/docs/`, gitignored). Companion to [Ideal Client Avatar schema](./ideal-client-avatar-schema.md) and the avatar step of [First Campaign Setup](./first-campaign-setup.md).

**The headline finding first, because it changes the build:** this corpus is a rich source of *universal* business-owner pain language and a surprisingly poor source of *industry* vocabulary. 113 of 215 files share an identical section skeleton (`Internal Monologue` / `Suspicions` / `Enemies` / `Past Failures`), 63 use the exact heading `Triggering Events: When Something Has to Change` — but only **20 of 215 files contain a single trade-specific noun** (`lads`, `top billers`, `sheds`, `WIP`, `covers`, `debtor days`, `snagging`, `rebate`, `MRR`, `placements`, `fee earners`, `shrinkage`). The playbook's core demand — industry-specific vocabulary — is the thing the avatars almost never deliver. Details in section 7.

---

## 1. Universal pains — the brand-level defaults

Six pains recur across nearly every industry. I've ordered them by how often they appear and given the phrasings that repeat near-verbatim across unrelated sectors.

### 1.1 "Why does everything still come back to me?" (founder as bottleneck)

This is the single most reproduced sentence in the corpus. It appears near-identically in life sciences, insurance broking, construction, architecture, engineering, consumer products, and local services:

> — `Nick-Adams---Ideal-Client-Avatar---Consumer-Products.md`

> "Why does everything still come to me?" / "Everything still comes back to me"
> — `Gordon-Mackay---Ideal-Client-Avatar---Construction-Specialist-Contracting-Services.md`, `Gordon-Mackay---Ideal-Client-Profile---Construction-Specialist-Contracting-Services.md`

> "Too many issues land on my desk" / "I feel like I'm holding it all together"
> — `Gordon-Mackay---Ideal-Client-Profile---Construction-Specialist-Contracting-Services.md`

> "Everything still lands on my desk."
> — `Andrew-Hayward---Ideal-Client-Avatar---Architects-Professional-Services.md`

- **Distrust of own numbers.** "Margins are guessed, not known" and "They don't fully trust the numbers they see" (`Daniel-Mirwis---Ideal-Client-Avatar---Houseware...`); "Forecasting is unreliable ('finger in the air' numbers)" (`Adam-Westbrook---Ideal-Client-Profile---Shropshire...`); "We're busy, but I don't trust the numbers enough" (`James-Fenner---Ideal-Client-Profile---Local.md`); "Decisions made on gut, not numbers" (`Ideal-Client-Profile-and-Avatar.md`).

## 4. Objection patterns

The best-structured source is `Andrew-Hayward---Ideal-Client-Avatar---Architects-Professional-Services.md`, which has an explicit `# Why They Delay Getting Help` section. Its six objections map almost perfectly onto what's scattered across the rest of the corpus, so I've used it as the spine.

### 4.1 "No one understands my industry" — the dominant objection

This is the most frequent and most consequential objection, and it recurs with the industry name swapped in:

Countered elsewhere in the corpus by the owner's own private doubt — "What if I am too close to the business to see what needs to change?" (`Yair-Emanuel...`) — which is the crack to work with.

### 4.6 Fear of exposure (the objection behind the objections)

`Terence-Monaghan---Ideal-Client-Avatar---Manufacturing-Engineering.md` does something no other file does: it translates the stated objections into real ones.

> 👉 Translation:
> - Fear of wasting time
> - Fear of being exposed
> - Fear of change

"Fear of being exposed" is corroborated by:

> "How can I be a coach and advisor to business owners when I'm struggling in my own business!"
> — `Business-Coach-Avatar---v2-Elevate-updated.md`

> "Maybe my product isn't differentiated enough, but nobody is telling me."
> — `Amended-Client-Avatar---SaaS.md`

> "I don't need a cheerleader, I need a commercial edge."
> — `Ideal-Client-Avatar---Cilla---Events.md`

### Does it vary by industry?

**Barely — and that's the finding.** The *structure* is constant across all 215 files: too busy → been burned → you don't know my world → doing okay → can fix it myself. What varies is only the noun slotted into "no one understands ___" and the flavour of past failure (failed marketing agency in agencies/e-comm, failed ERP or systems project in distribution, failed lean initiative in manufacturing, failed merger in construction — "He has experienced unsuccessful mergers in the past that did not create the expected value, leading to skepticism," `Ideal-Client-Profile.md`).

Two genuine industry-specific objections worth noting:
- **Life sciences / regulated**: resistance is *identity-based*, not time-based — "Resistance to structure due to fear of becoming 'too corporate'" (`Allison-Hogg---Ideal-Client-Profile---Life-Sciences.md`).
- **SaaS**: objection is *timing-based against an external clock* — "Maybe the market is saturated, and I've missed my peak window" (`Amended-Client-Avatar---SaaS.md`).

## 7. What surprised me

### 7.1 The corpus is a template, not a research archive — and it fails the playbook's own red-flag test

The numbers:

| Section heading | Files (of 215) |
|---|---|
| `Internal Monologue` | 113 |
| `Suspicions` | 89 |
| `Past Failures` | 83 |
| `Enemies` | 81 |
| `Triggering Events: When Something Has to Change` | 63 |
| **Any trade-specific noun at all** | **20** |

63 files share a *verbatim heading string*. Whole passages are byte-identical across differently-named files — `Avatar-example.md` and `Copy-of-Avatar-example.md`, the four near-identical Paul Grys manufacturing files, three identical Andy Blocke healthcare variants within one file. `Ideal-Client-Avatar---Accountancy.md` contains the same `Triggering Events` list three times with one bullet added each pass.

So the corpus is largely **one ChatGPT prompt run 200 times with the industry name swapped**. The implication is uncomfortable but actionable: the avatars themselves commit the playbook's #1 red flag ("Generic language - i.e. any business coach could write it"). A generator trained naively on this corpus will reproduce generic output at scale. **The industry vocabulary layer has to be authored, not mined.**

### 7.2 The best material is in the ugliest files

The single most useful passage in 3.6MB is the profanity-laden monologue in `Ideal-Client-Day-Vehicle-Sales-Leasing.md` ("I've built a machine that only works if I keep shoveling coal into it"). Second best is the typo-ridden raw session transcript in `Amended-Client-Avatar---SaaS.md` ("Burnign 50,000 a month… RUNWAY!!!", "For chirst sake love I can make you a lot of money why don;t press button"). Both read like actual transcribed human speech.

Conversely, the tidiest files — the ones with clean bullets and emoji headers — are the most generic. **The polish is inversely correlated with the value.** If the generator has a quality signal, "sounds a bit unpolished and specific" should score *higher* than "well-structured".

Corroborating this, the corpus itself flags the register to aim for: "How would they say it to their partner (non business wife or husband) / How would they say it to their 10 year old kid. Somewhere between the two." (`src/knowledge/connection-messages.md`)

### 7.3 Exit is the most under-used trigger in the corpus

`exit|succession|sellable|saleable` appears in **106 of 215 files** — more than cash flow (90) or firefighting (81) — yet almost no avatar leads with it. And the single most devastating trigger quote in the corpus is an exit quote: *"You're too reliant on you. If you stepped away, what's left?"* (`Ideal-Client-Avatar---Accountancy.md`).

This matters because exit reframes the founder-bottleneck pain from a *lifestyle* complaint (easy to dismiss, "I'll sort it when things calm down") into a *balance-sheet* problem with a number attached. It converts objection 4.4 ("we're doing okay") into urgency. An avatar generator should probably treat exit/valuation as a first-class desire axis, not a footnote.

### 7.4 "Growth" is a threat word, not a benefit word

I expected growth language to be aspirational. It's overwhelmingly framed as danger:

> "Growth feels exhausting – more jobs = more stress, not more money" (`Nov-Construction-Profile-Avatar.md`)
> "Growth has added complexity, not calm" (`Gordon-Mackay---Ideal-Client-Avatar...`)
> "Fear that growth is increasing fragility, not strength" (`Gordon-Mackay---Ideal-Client-Profile...`)
> "Growth without control amplifies existing problems" (`Matt-Beevers...`)
> "Business growth feels risky rather than exciting" (`Ideal-client-profile---Develops-Construction.md`)
> "Growth starts to feel risky rather than exciting" (`Andy-Blocke---Ideal-Client-Avatar---Healthcare-Fitness.md`)
> "Scaling sounds like: more hiring / more risk / more stress. So he hesitates." (`Graham-Withe---Ideal-Client-Avatar---B2B---Saas.md`)
> "What if we expand and make the business more complicated?" (`Yair-Emanuel...`)
> "They may have already experienced the pain of uncontrolled growth: More staff / More jobs / More mistakes / More complaints / More cash pressure" (`Derek-Hollingdale---Ideal-Client-Profile---Surrey.md`)

`{main_desire}` lines built around "scale" or "grow faster" therefore risk triggering the fear rather than the want. **"Control", "predictable", and "without" are the safer verbs.** The corpus supports this directly: "I want to feel back in control… without doing everything" (`Spencer-Thomas-Ideal-Client-Avatar---Engineering-Services.md`), "They want to feel in control without being controlling" (`Gordon-Mackay---Ideal-Client-Avatar...`), and `predictab*` appears in 91 files.

### 7.5 They want a plan and a framework — literally those two words

One file steps outside the pattern and reports the actual lexical preferences:

> "They want a plan. A lot of them use the word 'Plan'"
> "They want a framework. Also another highly used word"
> — `Zander-Pam-Woodford-Smith---Perfect-Avatar-Profile.md`

Corroborated in `Daniel-Mirwis---Ideal-Client-Avatar---Houseware...`: "They want efficiency, not reinvention" and "I don't want another project – I just want to run this business properly." And in `Nick-Summers---Ideal-Client-Avatar.md`: "They don't want sympathy. They want solutions that work on the ground."

The mechanism half of the connection message ("used a **simple system** to…") is well-aligned with this already. What the corpus adds is that "transformation", "journey", and "unlock your potential" are actively wrong — they read as the "folder full of fluff" the prospect has already paid for once.

### 7.6 The objection structure is universal even though the objection *content* is industry-specific

This is the most build-relevant asymmetry I found. Every industry runs the same five-objection sequence (too busy → burned before → you don't know my world → we're doing okay → I can fix it myself), but the *only* variable that matters is the noun in "no one understands ___".

That means an avatar generator doesn't need per-industry objection research at all — it needs one canonical objection model plus an industry-vocabulary dictionary. **Effort spent on per-industry objections is largely wasted; effort spent on the vocabulary layer is the whole game.** And the corpus can't supply that layer (only 20 files have any trade noun), so it has to come from coach interviews, trade press, or the coaches' own client conversations.

### 7.7 A few structural notes for the generator

- **Deduplicate before ingesting.** `Copy-of-*` files, `Revised-*` files, and multi-pass files inflate every frequency count. My raw counts above are file-presence counts, which mitigates this but doesn't eliminate it.
- **`*LinkedIn-Marketing*` files are outputs, not inputs.** They contain generated posts and hooks, not client language. Including them will create a feedback loop where the generator learns from its own prior output. Filter to `*Avatar*` and `*Profile*`.
- **Some files are mislabelled by industry.** `Ideal-Client-Avatar.md`, `Client-Avatar.md`, `Ideal-Client-Avater.md` and similar have no industry in the filename and mixed content inside. Industry has to be inferred from body text, not filename.
- **`Big-6-Areas-Of-ICP-Pains-1-10M-SMEs.md` is the closest thing to a canonical universal-pain source** and is a better starting point for brand-level defaults than aggregating the 215.
- **The pain → desire transform is mechanical and worth hard-coding.** Nearly every desire in the corpus is a negated pain: "everything comes back to me" → "runs without me"; "busy but no profit" → "profit that matches the effort"; "stand

ards slip when I step back" → "runs to standard without you in the room."

That last point deserves expanding, because it's the cheapest win available. The desire sections across the corpus are almost never independent research — they're the pain sentence inverted. Compare directly:

| Pain (verbatim) | Desire (verbatim, same or sibling file) |
|---|---|
| "Why does everything still come back to me?" | "The business runs well without me being everywhere" — `Andy-Blocke---Ideal-Client-Profile-Healthcare-and-Fitness.md` |
| "We're busy… but where's the bloody profit?" | "Profit does not reflect effort, risk, or responsibility" → wants profit that does — `Ideal-Client-Profile---Engineering-Manufacturing-Production-Design-Businesses.md` |
| "I can't seem to step back without standards dropping" | "They want to feel in control without being controlling" — `Gordon-Mackay---Ideal-Client-Avatar---Construction-Specialist-Contracting-Services.md` |
| "I don't actually feel in control anymore" | "I just want to know where we really stand - and make decisions without fear" — `Daniel-Mirwis---Ideal-Client-Avatar---Houseware-Tableware-and-Kitchenware.md` |
| "Team keeps bringing problems instead of solutions" | "They want a business that works because of the team, not despite it" — `Joe-Jarrett---Ideal-Client-Profile---B2B-SAAS.md` |
| "I've built a job… not a scalable business" | "I want to feel back in control… without doing everything" — `Spencer-Thomas-Ideal-Client-Avatar---Engineering-Services.md` |

So the generator needs only **one** researched field per industry — the pain, stated in that industry's nouns — and can derive `{main_desire}` from it by negation. That halves the research surface and, more importantly, guarantees the desire line and the pain line share vocabulary, which is exactly what the connection message needs (the hook and the mechanism have to rhyme).

The practical shape this suggests:

1. **One universal pain library** (six pains from section 1, with 5-10 verbatim variants each) as brand-level defaults. This is done — the corpus fully supports it.
2. **One canonical objection model** (five objections from section 4) with a single industry-name slot. Also done.
3. **One trigger library** ranked as in section 5, with the trigger *story* pattern preserved — the resignation reason, the buyer's comment, the doctor's warning — because the specificity is what makes the trigger land.
4. **A per-industry vocabulary record** — the four swap columns plus 2-4 non-generalisable pains. This is the only part that needs new research, and it's the only part that determines whether the output passes the playbook's red-flag test.

Items 1-3 are essentially free from this corpus. Item 4 has to be built from scratch for every industry a coach targets, because 195 of the 215 documents contain no trade vocabulary whatsoever.

The pattern's becoming clear: nearly every desire in the corpus traces back to negating some pain—"everything comes back to me" flips to "runs without me," "busy but no profit" becomes "profit that matches the effort." This transformation is mechanical enough that it's worth hard-coding into the system rather than deriving it each time.
