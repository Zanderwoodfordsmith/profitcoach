## 1. The habit

**On screen:** centred slide. "Confidence looping" / "The habit that wins the call"

> There's one small habit that makes the biggest difference on a sales call.
> It's called confidence looping. Once you know it, you'll hear it in every
> great call.

## 2. What it is

**On screen:** the Script tab, Investment, with "confidence loop" in the line.
**Label:** WHAT IT IS / **Heading:** Answer, then loop it back

> Every time a prospect asks you something, you answer it. Most coaches stop
> there. A confidence loop is the next sentence. You tie your answer straight
> back to what they told you they want: their result, their problem, their why.

## 3. Three moves

**On screen:** slide with the three moves.
**Label:** HOW / **Heading:** Three moves

> It's three moves. One: answer the question, short and clear. Two: loop it
> back. "Which means…" and then their result, in their words. Three: check it
> landed. "Can you see how that gets you to…" and their result again.

## 4. An example

**On screen:** slide with the question and the loop, Mike's result highlighted.
**Label:** EXAMPLE / **Heading:** Sorting out the team

> Say Mike runs a building firm, and he wants to work three days a week. He
> asks, "Do you have a process to help me sort out the team?" Don't spend five
> minutes on it. Explain it in two or three lines, then loop: "And that's what
> buys back your time, which is what gets you to three days a week. Does that
> make sense?"

## 5. Why it works

**On screen:** slide, a row of small steps building up to the investment.
**Label:** WHY IT WORKS / **Heading:** Every answer adds up

> One loop on its own does very little. But do it after every answer, from the
> pitch right through to the investment, and their certainty builds minute by
> minute. Think of waves on pebbles. Each one is tiny, but over time they
> change everything. By the time you share the investment, they've
> heard again and again how this gets them what they want.

## 6. Watch out

**On screen:** slide with three short lines.
**Label:** WATCH OUT / **Heading:** Three traps

> Three traps. First, don't start coaching on the call. Show them you have the
> plan and the tools. The coaching starts once they've signed. Second, keep it
> short. Talk for three minutes on one point and you've lost them. Third,
> listen to how they answer. "Yeah, probably" isn't a yes. Ask them what makes
> them say probably.
