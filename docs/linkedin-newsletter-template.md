# LinkedIn newsletter template (Pam / BCA)

Source material for the AI newsletter editor. Two BCA templates from Google Drive, Pam's
Call 2 coaching guidance from session transcripts, and three real published newsletters
studied for structure.

---

## 1. The generic BCA template ("Susan's LinkedIn Newsletter Template")

A 40-block skeleton built around a four-beat persuasion arc. Reference examples cited in
the doc: [Tara Halliday](https://www.linkedin.com/in/tara-halliday-phd/),
[John McCarthy](https://www.linkedin.com/in/johnmccarthysummit/),
[Daniel Andersen-Tuffnell](https://www.linkedin.com/newsletters/business-mindsets-6948584257497796609/).

Setup decisions made once, not per edition:

- Cadence: weekly, fortnightly, or monthly. More frequent wins more clients.
- Name: say what it is on the tin, e.g. "Law Firm Profit Newsletter".
- Ask alliances to share each edition "to add value to their clients".

Per-edition block order:

| Block | Content |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Topic of this newsletter |
| 2 | Header image |
| 3 | Standard intro (identical every edition) |
| 4 | "This week we're looking at…" |
| 5 | Divider |
| 6–9 | **INTERRUPT / CHALLENGE THEIR THINKING** — subtitle, statement or quote, image, context and story |
| 10–13 | **ENGAGE / CALL OUT PROBLEM** — subtitle, text, image, text |
| 14–17 | **EDUCATE / OFFER SOLUTION** — subtitle, text, image, text |
| 18–21 | **OFFER / CALL TO ACTION** — subtitle, text, image, text |
| 23–25 | Divider, then "What I've loved this week" or "While I was training this week" — pic and text, related to the topic |
| 26–29 | Divider, then "An action step you can do today…" — text and image |
| 30–31 | Divider, then "Do subscribe and share!" |
| 32–38 | Short bio, programme link, quiz or lead magnet, book-a-call link |
| 39–40 | Warm sign-off, first name only |

House rules from the same doc:

- Say "workshop" or "event", never "webinar".
- Put share/like/comment prompt at the bottom.
- After publishing, run posts that point back at the newsletter and remind people to subscribe.

Distribution: LinkedIn sends the first edition to every connection; send to the target
market; run posts, InMails and Top 100 outreach before and after each edition; expand
sub-topics from one edition into future editions.

## 2. Pam's own template ("2025 - LinkedIn Newsletter Template" — Profit Patter)

More prescriptive. This is the one Pam hands to coaches and to her own marketer/BDM.

**Formatting:** Montserrat 14 body, Montserrat 17 headlines. Black text is fixed
boilerplate, red text is what you edit each week.

**Header:** duplicate the Canva header template, swap the small left image, replace the
navy headline with this week's topic, replace the orange line with this week's tagline.

**Fixed intro (verbatim every edition):**

> Hi,
>
> Your weekly edition of simple profit growth strategies to double your profit within
> 6 months, claw back 10 hours a week of your precious time immediately and power up your
> team. All delivered with straight-talking Yorkshire candour to guide you in eradicating
> any areas of chaos and take control of your business, without losing your sanity or life
> in the process, so that your life is fun.
>
> Each edition gives you 5 strategies to do, 3 critical things to avoid, and a 7-point
> action checklist you can use immediately…
>
> I'm Pam Woodford, Globally Awarded Business Profit Coach and Self-Appointed Minister of
> Fun and I'm looking forward to sharing valuable insights with you. 😁

Then a variable line introducing this week's topic, a forward-reference to the next
edition, and "Let's dive straight in…".

**Body sections, in order:**

1. `💥 Headline with relevant emojis 💥` — the week's topic. Short sentences, short
   paragraphs, images interspersed.
2. `👀 Emoji sub-headline` — more of the topic, well spaced.
3. `✅ 5 Strategies You Can Do Right Now to [topic]` — numbered 1–5.
4. `⚠️ 3 Critical Mistakes to Avoid` — numbered with ❌.
5. `🧰 More Tools = More Juice for the Squeeze` — tool, book, video or idea list with emoji bullets.
6. `🧾 7-Point Profit Checklist` — seven ✅ lines.
7. `🤝 Free Workshop: …` — date, time, what you'll learn, bonuses, booking link, phone number.
8. `✍️ Meet Pam Woodford` — fixed bio block.
9. `👉 Subscribe and Share` — fixed.
10. `💬 Over to You` — one engagement question tied to the week's topic.

**Spacing rule that matters most:** no more than 20 lines between images. High energy,
fast paced, fun.

## 3. Pam's coaching guidance (Call 2 transcripts)

Beyond the docs, from `.ica-research/session-transcripts`:

- **One topic per edition.** If you have three or four ideas, write three or four
  newsletters. Never stack them.
- Break text with images so it looks bite-sized — "readable while they're having a cup of
  coffee". A wall of text gets "I'll read that later", which means never.
- Keep the same structure every edition so subscribers recognise it and know they're in
  the right place.
- Always end with action points so they got value and can implement.
- Do the **SEO title and description in newsletter settings before publishing** — the
  newsletter, About section and Experience section are all indexed, and search can route
  strangers to your profile.
- **Draft in Google Docs and paste into LinkedIn**, never compose in LinkedIn's editor.
  Pam has lost finished newsletters to LinkedIn failing to save or publish.
- Repurpose: each edition yields 5 + 3 + 7 = 15 further posts, plus carousels, checklists,
  articles, VIP nurture messages and InMails.
- Newsletters beat articles because LinkedIn notifies subscribers on every publish;
  articles are a static library that showcases expertise.

## 4. Real published examples studied

### Pam — [Profit Patter](https://www.linkedin.com/newsletters/profit-patter-7029113740000165889/)

Weekly. Recent editions almost all titled "💥 Your Weekly Profit Power-Up", occasionally a
themed one ("The Real Balancing Act: Dads, Business and Being Present"). Confirms the
fixed-title-plus-variable-topic pattern from her template. Edition bodies sit behind
LinkedIn's auth wall; the template above is the authoritative structure.

### John McCarthy — Profitable Practice (law firms)

The clearest worked example of the generic template. Observed shape:

- Personal credibility opener (book chapter contribution) rather than a hard hook.
- Explicit promise of what the newsletter series will deliver.
- A results block — five one-line client outcomes with numbers.
- Two long-form client stories, each with an image and a named framework
  (9-Step P.R.O.F.I.T. roadmap, Business Blueprint).
- Forward-reference: "In the next two editions I'm going to cover…".
- Free workshop block with date, time and limited-places scarcity.
- "Do comment, subscribe and share!" → bio → book-a-15-minute-call → awards → "Have a
  successful week! John".

Note his later edition titles use the template's line verbatim: "This week we're looking
at…".

### Adam J. Graham — [The Growth Mindset](https://www.linkedin.com/newsletters/7427074619309797377/) (34K subscribers)

A materially different format worth supporting as a second template. Mirrored publicly at
[adamgraham.kit.com](https://adamgraham.kit.com/posts/when-the-founder-finally-lets-go-the-growth-mindset).

- "Hi Reader / Welcome to this week's edition" + two or three sentences naming the thread
  running through the issue + "Enjoy!".
- **Eight or nine curated items**, not one topic. Each is a headline, a summary of an
  external piece (FT, HBR, Stanford, Edelman, TechCrunch), then a paragraph of *his own*
  read on why it matters to founders and SME owners, then "Find out more here" / "Read it
  here" / "Get the report here".
- `AI prompt of the week` — a long, copy-pasteable prompt with bracketed placeholders and
  a numbered list of required outputs. This is the standout reusable asset.
- A visual reference or framework graphic.
- `Drop me a line` — explicit reply invitation, specific about what makes a useful reply.
- "Cheers! Adam".

The growth is plausibly topic-driven (AI), but the mechanics are repeatable: curation plus
distinctive commentary, one high-value takeaway asset per edition, and a genuine
conversation invitation instead of a sales CTA.

## 5. The 5-3-7 → 16 edition cascade (recommended content plan)

Pam's 5 strategies / 3 mistakes / 7 checklist is not only the structure of *one*
overview newsletter. Once that overview is right, it becomes a **year's content map**:

| # | Kind | Count | Role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Overview | 1 | Full 5-3-7 edition on the lead pain/topic |
| 2–6 | Strategy deep-dives | 5 | One newsletter per strategy |
| 7–9 | Mistake deep-dives | 3 | One newsletter per critical mistake |
| 10–16 | Checklist deep-dives | 7 | One newsletter per checklist item |
| **Total** | | **16** | ~4 months weekly, or ~8 months fortnightly |

Fill toward **26 editions** (a year at LinkedIn's own every-two-weeks cadence) with:

- Profit System / framework editions
- Industry-specific editions from the coach's ICP brain
- Timely POV / myth-busting / client Q&A editions

Getting the overview 5-3-7 right is the whole game — everything else explores those lines
using messaging from First Campaign / the AI brain (pains, hooks, vocabulary, proof).

### Topic ideation (blank-page killers)

- Questions clients ask all the time
- Top-performing LinkedIn posts → deeper dive
- Industry mistakes or myths to clarify
- Prompt: "Based on my business / ICP, what newsletter topics would build authority and attract leads?"

### Length modes

| Mode | Words | Use when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quick insight / timely POV | **400–800** | Default. Scannable, shareable, saveable — a LinkedIn post with more breathing room |
| In-depth guide / case study | **~2,000+** | Only when the value truly warrants length |

Goal: scannable, shareable, saveable. Busy-reader formatting: bold headlines, short
paragraphs, bullets.

### Alternate breezy structure (optional template)

1. Quick personal story or point of view
2. Teach **one** clear takeaway (tip / framework / trend)
3. Link to a resource (lead magnet, YouTube, blog)
4. Soft CTA (comment / reply). Hard offer at most every 3–4 issues

### Every edition also needs

- A **feed promo post** that drives subscribe/read
- SEO title + description before publishing
- Optional **cover image** from editable templates (same family as post quote cards)

## 6. Implications for the AI newsletter editor

- Primary flow: name the newsletter → pick lead topic from brain/ICP → generate overview
  5-3-7 → expand to 16-edition cascade → optionally fill to 26 → write each edition in an
  artifact editor (AI left, live draft right) → copy for LinkedIn + generate promo post.
- Templates: Pam 5-3-7 overview, Pam deep-dive, breezy story/teach, curated roundup.
- Inject First Campaign / `profiles.ai_context` brain on every draft (ideal client, pain
  language, messaging hooks, vocabulary, proof, client results).
- Fixed blocks (intro, bio, subscribe, sign-off) are series settings — AI only edits
  variable blocks.
- Image slots are structural; cover + pull-quote templates fill them.
- Enforce spacing (max ~20 lines between images) as a live warning.
- Copy-for-LinkedIn must preserve headings, bold, lists, emoji bullets and dividers.
