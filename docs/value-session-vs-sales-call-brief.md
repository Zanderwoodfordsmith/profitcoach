# Value Session vs Sales Call: brief for review

Prepared 20 October 2026. This is a self-contained summary of a discussion about how coaches in the Profit Coach app should run their prospect calls. You do not need access to the codebase or Google Drive to read it. Quoted lines are verbatim from the sources unless marked as a draft.

## 1. Context

- **The product.** Profit Coach is an app and programme from Business Coach Academy (BCA). It trains and equips business coaches to win and serve clients who own small and medium businesses (UK, roughly £1M to £5M turnover). The method is the **Profit System**: three pillars (Vision, Velocity, Value) and nine steps.
- **The coaches.** Many are new and not trained salespeople. Right now the focus is getting new coaches to their first booked call and first client as fast as possible (target: 100 new coaches a month).
- **Pam** (Pam Woodford) is the lead coach and the source of the sales training. **Zander** runs the product.
- **The nine steps**, in order: Aligned Vision, Defined Strategy, Disciplined Planning (Vision); Profit & Cash Flow, Revenue & Marketing, Operations & Delivery (Velocity); Financials & Metrics, Infrastructure & Systems, Team & Leadership (Value).
- **The BOSS Score** is the app's business scorecard. It scores the same nine areas plus a tenth, Owner Performance. It's also a public opt-in funnel ("takes about 3 minutes").

## 2. The question

Zander asked: how does Pam's **Value Session Mastery Workbook** fit with the sales call script that's already in the app? Is it a different section before the pitch, part of the pitch, a different call entirely, or a source of questions for discovery? Then a follow-up: is a "value session" really just another name for a 30-minute discovery call? How should the process be structured so coaches convert the most clients without getting confused?

## 3. Sources

### 3a. Value Session Mastery Workbook (Pam, Google Doc)

A live training workbook ("This is not a sit-back session"). It has three tabs:

1. The script
2. Marketing ideas (launch email, nudge email, LinkedIn DMs, Featured section, carousel post, calendar description)
3. Landing page copy for Pam's version, the **Quick Wins Profit Maximiser** ("Free 30-Minute Strategy Call... No Fluff. No Pitch. No Catch.")

**Mini script flow:**

1. **Warm rapport (2 to 3 min):** "Great to meet you, thanks for making the time. I'd love to understand more about where you're at right now and where you'd like to go."
2. **Key question 1, current challenge:** "What's the biggest challenge or opportunity / want to be even better, you're facing right now that's stopping your business from being where you want it to be?"
3. **Key question 2, desired future:** "If we were having this same conversation 12 months from now, what would need to have changed in your business for you to feel genuinely happy with your progress?" (This creates "the golden gap".)
4. **Trust builder 1, personal driver:** "Why is solving this important to you personally - beyond just the numbers?" (family, freedom, pride, legacy)
5. **Trust builder 2, past efforts:** "What have you tried so far, and how has that worked out?"
6. **Bridge to value:** "Based on what you've told me, would you like me to share a couple of insights and strategies that will make a real difference?"
7. *(Insights and the "aha" moment. The workbook doesn't script these. It lists "the dashboard and questions to ask" as a session output but doesn't include them.)*
8. **The magic question:** "Would you like to see how I help my clients solve exactly this and get results faster?" Wait for the yes. Alternatives: "Can I share the framework I use with clients who want the same outcome you've described?"
9. **Bridge into the offer:** "Based on what you've told me about [their challenge] and your vision for [their outcome], here's how I typically work with clients in your situation."
10. **Outline the offer, three parts at most:** benefit 1 tied to their pain, benefit 2 tied to their goal, benefit 3 tied to their personal driver.
11. **Social proof:** "Other clients in your position have [result], and I'm confident we could achieve the same for you."
12. **Gentle close:** "Does that sound like the kind of support that would make a real difference for you?"
13. **Next steps:** "If you're happy, we can get started straight away - would you like me to walk you through the details?"
14. **If "not now":** "No problem at all - the important thing is you've got clarity on your next steps. If it's useful, I can check in with you in [X days/weeks]..."

### 3b. E3 "The Value Session" course (BCA academy lessons, built from Pam's training)

**Planet 1, Value Sessions vs Sales Calls.**
- A value session is "a 30 to 45 minute booked conversation in which you give the prospect a meaningful piece of value, no pitch attached."
- It "typically **replaces** both the discovery call and the roadmap call. One booking, one session, both jobs done."
- Three differences from a discovery call:
  1. Audit questions, not qualifying questions
  2. The coach actually coaches during the call
  3. The prospect leaves with one specific strategy
- BCA's claim: about four times more bookings than discovery calls, with a similar close rate per call.
- Timing: a 45-minute slot. 30 minutes for the session, 5 to 10 minutes for an optional pitch demo, and a buffer.

**Planet 2, How to Run a Value Session.**
- Design the audit around the coach's main strength (profit, teams, systems or marketing).
- Ask 5 to 8 questions, each scored red, amber or green.
- Leave a space for "the biggest issue" and a space for "the one strategy".
- Delivery:

  | Minutes | What happens |
  |---|---|
  | 0 to 2 | Rapport |
  | 2 to 4 | "What made you want to book the session?" |
  | 4 to 7 | Goal: the 12-months-from-now question |
  | 7 to 12 | Challenges: "If one thing was fixed today, which one would make the biggest difference?" |
  | 12 to 27 | Audit, coaching deeper on the answers that touch their pain. Reframe a lot of red as opportunity. |
  | 27 to 30 | Teach one specific strategy |

- At minute 30 the promise is complete. "What you do not do at minute 30 is pivot uninvited into a pitch."

**Planet 4, Presenting Your Offer.**
- "The prospect's question is the permission slip for the pitch. If they ask, you can present. If they do not, you cannot."
- Transition: "If we were to work on this together, we would focus on [the biggest pain point you uncovered]. The goal would be to help you go from [current state] to [their goal]. Does that sound like something you would want help with?"
- The pitch demo is tailored: their words, their order (lead with their pain), their emphasis. Keep it under 10 minutes.
- It covers four things: how the engagement runs, the work, expected results, and how to get started.
- The price goes **inside** the demo, said matter-of-factly: "The investment for that is [number]."
- **No objection handling.** Answer clarifying questions (session length, evenings, cancellation). Defer real objections: "That's fair. Let me send through what we've covered today, and let's catch up in a week."

**Planet 5, Handling Questions & Closing.** Four outcomes:

1. **They say yes:** close fast, send the paperwork the same day, give them one first action.
2. **"I need to think about it":** don't ask why or try to overcome it. Send a report within 24 hours (audit summary, the one strategy, one or two more ideas) and hold a check-in call on day 7.
3. **They never invited the pitch:** send the report anyway and move them to nurture ("VIP 100").
4. **They raise a concern:** answer clarifications; real objections wait for the follow-up.

The day-7 call is where the case for coaching lands: "Most people do not action standalone advice... That's exactly what coaching is for... Want to talk about how that would work?"

### 3c. The current in-app sales call script ("Get Clients → Script")

The code comment says Discovery "follows Pam's value session (Value Session Mastery Workbook). The rest follows BCA's ClickUp call script (Transition & Pitch, Committing / Temp Check, Investment, Taking Payment, Book A Next Steps)." The script has six phases.

**1. Discovery.** Goal: "Get the five facts in their words, then read them back."
- The five facts are their priority, what it's costing them, the result they want, their numbers, and why it matters to them. "What they've tried" is asked but not stored as a fact.
- It opens with an info box: "This is Pam's value session: a free, pitch-free conversation built around your superpower and their main pain. You give real value first, and the sale follows from that."
- The flow is close to the workbook:
  1. Rapport, then "So what made you want to book this today?"
  2. Set the frame: "...I'll share a couple of things that should genuinely help, and if I think I can help further, I'll tell you how. Sound good?"
  3. Biggest challenge, plus "And what's that costing you at the moment? In money, in time, or in how you feel?"
  4. The 12-month question, plus "Is there a number on that?"
  5. Personal why
  6. What they've tried, plus "If nothing changed, and six months from now you were in exactly the same position, how would you feel about that?"
  7. Give them value: the bridge question. **The audit is only an info box:** "On a value session, this is your 15-minute audit: your questions, scored red, amber or green... Give them one or two things they can act on straight away."
  8. The magic question
  9. The read-back: "...the main thing you want to achieve is {result}. At the moment, {problem} is getting in the way... Have I understood that correctly?"
- **There is no "teach one strategy" step.**

**2. The Pitch.** Goal: "Take command, then nine steps or three phases. Every part tied to their problem."
- Transition: "Don't be approval-seeking here. Be definite and take command." Then "...you're exactly the kind of owner I help..." and "Are you happy for me to share my screen?"
- The coach picks one of two pitches. AI can personalise either from the five facts.
  - **Nine steps** ("what we fix"): an intro naming the three pillars, then one tailored paragraph per step, then "Bringing it together".
    - Rule: every step carries "because you told me / what we will do / which means".
    - The nine-step guide says the order never changes.
  - **Three phases** ("what happens after they say yes"):
    1. Bridge, including the three things that stay with the owner: be honest, make the decisions, do what's agreed
    2. Phase 1, get clear (the first month, weekly)
    3. Phase 2, fix what matters (months two and three, a weekly scoreboard)
    4. Phase 3, make it last (from month four, a new plan every 90 days)
    5. Recap

    Use it when the owner is cautious or has been burned before.
  - Rule in the script: "On a follow-up call, use the one you didn't use last time."
- After the pitch: "How do you feel about the structure and approach specifically?" Then **confidence looping**: "what about [that] specifically stands out to you?", "Besides [that], what else...?", "what questions do you have about the process and the structure specifically?" If they ask about price: "More than happy to go through the investment in a moment. I just want to focus on the process first."

**3. Investment.**
- **Temperature check:** "On a scale of 1 to 10... where would you say you land on the process and the structure specifically?" They need "a solid 9, or ideally a 10" before the price.
- An 8 or below gets probed. An unconvincing score gets challenged: "Really? I could be wrong here, but you sound more like a 5 out of 10..."
- Then the price line, "For that, you're getting...", and "Given everything we've mapped out for the next 6 to 12 months, do you want to move forward and do this together?" Five to seven seconds of silence, then "How does that land with you?"
- "Anything other than a clear yes: go straight to Loop 0 of the universal closes."

**4. Objections.** One page each for:
- Time to think. It's treated as "a smokescreen, they don't need time". The response re-ties them to the result, then digs for the real objection.
- Money
- Partner
- Timing
- Fear
- Won't decide today
- Cheaper elsewhere
- Can't find it

**5. Closes.**
- Universal closes are seven loops, starting with:
  - Loop 0: "Do you think this can help?"
  - Loop 1: "What's your main concern?"
  - Loop 2: "That might be the perfect reason"
  - Loop 3: "Hypothetically, if this were perfect"

  Guidance: "Loop three or four times at most, then release."
- The other closes:
  - The 1 to 10 close
  - Deposit close
  - Responsibility close
  - The contract

**6. Next steps.**
- Take payment
- Ask for referrals
- **Book the next call** from inside the call ("Don't say 'we'll follow up'"), with reminder texts between calls

### 3d. Other places that name prospect calls

These don't agree with each other or with the sources above.

- **The academy lead flow map** shows a discovery call as "An optional conversation before the Value Session". Its target is "10 Interested, 5 discovery calls, 4 value sessions, 1 client." And: "After the value session, present the offer and move toward close."
- **Pam's public page** labels her booking "Free profit strategy call".
- **The pipeline board** tags booked calls as "Discovery" or "Value session" based on the calendar title.
- **"Blueprint Call"** is a different thing: BCA's own call with a new coach. It isn't a prospect call.

## 4. Analysis

**How they fit.** The app's Discovery phase is already the workbook's front half, almost word for word. So the workbook isn't a new section to slot in.

**Where they clash.** After Discovery, the app runs a high-ticket closing call, and that contradicts the value session's promise:

| | Value session (workbook and E3 course) | App script after Discovery |
|---|---|---|
| Length | 30 minutes, plus 5 to 10 for an optional pitch | Nine-step pitch, confidence loops, temperature check, investment and closes. Likely 60 minutes or more. |
| Pitch | Only if invited, under 10 minutes, built around their pain | "Take command", full nine steps in fixed order |
| Price | Inside the pitch, said plainly | Its own phase, after a 1 to 10 temperature check |
| "Not now" | No objection handling. Report within 24 hours, then a day-7 call. | Objection pages and loops until "a clear yes or a clear no" |

A coach who advertised "No fluff. No pitch. No catch." and followed the app script would break that promise. The E3 course names this as the thing that burns warm pipeline.

**What's missing on the value session side:**
1. The audit itself: real questions, scored red, amber or green, on screen. It's the core 15 minutes, and in the app it's only an info box.
2. Teaching one strategy before the magic question.
3. The follow-up report and the day-7 check-in call.

**Smaller things worth adding to Discovery:**
- The "or want to be even better" option on the first question, for owners who aren't in pain
- "If one thing was fixed today, which one would make the biggest difference?"
- Storing "what they've tried" as a sixth fact, so the pitch can say why this time is different
- The line that reframes a lot of red as room to grow

**Is a value session just a discovery call under another name?** Mostly, yes. To the coach, it's **the first call**, and discovery is its first ten minutes rather than a separate call. What makes it different is on the prospect's side: the booking promises something useful, the coach keeps the no-pitch promise, and the call includes an audit, live coaching and one strategy. The app's mistake isn't the content. It's that two calls have been merged into one script, and three different call models are taught in different places.

## 5. Recommendation: two calls, two names

The rule for coaches: **the first call gives, the second call decides.**

Coaches only need two call names, **Value Session** and **Follow-up Call**:
- Drop "discovery call", "strategy call", "fit call" and "roadmap call" as prospect call types.
- Each coach markets the Value Session under their own name. Pam's is the Quick Wins Profit Maximiser.
- "Blueprint Call" stays reserved for BCA's call with the coach.

### Call 1: Value Session (draft outline, 45-minute slot)

| Time | Part | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 4 min | Rapport | Rapport, then "What made you want to book this today?" Set the frame without promising a pitch. |
| 4 to 12 min | Discovery | The five facts in their words, plus what they've tried. Workbook questions as now. |
| 12 to 27 min | Audit | 5 to 8 questions scored red, amber or green on screen, coaching deeper on the ones that touch their pain. **Proposal:** build the audit on the BOSS Score / Profit System areas, so the reds map straight onto the nine steps. Optionally the prospect takes the 3-minute BOSS Score before the call, and the session goes deeper on their results. |
| 27 to 30 min | One strategy | One specific thing to do this week, then the reframe if there's a lot of red. |
| ~30 min | Magic question | "Would you like to see how I help my clients solve exactly this, and get results faster?" |
| If yes, ~10 min | How we'd work together | Read back the problem and goal, then the **three-phase pitch** (how the engagement runs, what happens and when). Say the price inside it: "The investment for that is [number]." Close with "Does that sound like the kind of support that would make a real difference for you?" |
| End | Outcome | **Yes:** sign now, paperwork the same day, one first action. **"Let me think", or any objection:** don't handle it. Book Call 2 in the diary before hanging up. **They didn't want to hear about working together:** report within 24 hours, day-7 check-in booked, then nurture. |

### Call 2: Follow-up Call (draft outline, 30 to 45 minutes)

The prospect has agreed to talk about working together, so the no-pitch promise no longer applies. Most of the current script's second half moves here.

1. **Open:** "How did you get on with the strategy?" Most won't have done much, which makes the case for coaching: "Most people don't act on standalone advice. That's what coaching is for."
2. **Read-back**, updated: "Has anything changed since we spoke?"
3. **The nine-step pitch**, leading with their red areas from the audit. It's the other pitch from Call 1, so nothing is repeated.
4. Confidence looping, then the temperature check, then the investment.
5. Objections and closes, as they are in the app now.
6. Next steps: take payment, or book the next call.

### Why this structure should convert best

1. New coaches are usually short of booked calls, not closing skill. The no-pitch promise is what lifts bookings (BCA claims about four times), so it has to be kept.
2. Most owners don't buy on the first call. The money comes from Call 2 actually happening, so it gets booked live rather than "I'll send an invite".
3. The closing tools (temperature check, objection pages, loops) feel like pressure on Call 1 but like help on Call 2, once the prospect has opted in. They're also easier for a nervous new coach to use with someone who has already said yes to the conversation.
4. Coaches never repeat a pitch: three phases on Call 1, nine steps on Call 2.
5. The audit and the pitch share one model. The prospect's own red scores become the order of the plan.

### Where this differs from Pam's E3 course (needs Pam's agreement)

- **The magic question.** The recommendation keeps the workbook's permission question rather than waiting for the prospect to ask, as the E3 course requires. Many owners won't ask on their own, and asking permission doesn't break the promise.
- **Booking Call 2.** It's booked live on Call 1 rather than sent as an invite afterwards.
- **The lead flow map.** Its "optional discovery call" step and its target line would need rewriting.
- **Pitch order.** The course says to reorder the pitch around the prospect's pain. The nine-step guide says the order never changes. Proposal: keep the fixed order on Call 2 but spend the time on their reds, and use the shorter three-phase pitch on Call 1.

## 6. Questions worth checking

1. Is "the first call gives, the second call decides" the right model for coaches selling ongoing coaching (paid monthly or for six months) to SME owners? Or does a one-call close (value session plus full pitch and closes) convert better overall, even though it costs bookings?
2. Is handling objections on Call 2 consistent with the no-pitch promise, given the prospect has opted in to discussing the work?
3. Does the three-phase pitch cover what Planet 4 asks for on Call 1 (how it runs, the work, results, how to start, the price) in under 10 minutes?
4. Is the high-ticket closing toolkit (the "you sound more like a 5" challenge, seven close loops, "time to think is a smokescreen") right for this brand and these coaches? It's meant to be calm and trusted-advisor, and many coaches are new.
5. Should the BOSS Score be the value session audit, or should each coach build their own audit around their main strength, as the E3 course says?
6. What should coaches measure to see where the process leaks? Suggested:
   - booking rate
   - show rate
   - magic-question yes rate
   - Call 1 close rate
   - Call 2 booked rate
   - Call 2 close rate
   - days to first client
